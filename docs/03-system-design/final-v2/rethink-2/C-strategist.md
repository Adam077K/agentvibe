## 1 · The big idea as I read it

Keel is a company of agents on one person's Mac that takes a project and achieves it, across every field a company has, walking relentlessly until the result is right. What it must never lose is the four things no vendor can ship and no competitor can copy because they are made of what happened: **direction** (charters and intents with a done-test, and now an outcome), **the record** (an append-only, hash-chained, provenance-carrying log of what the company did and what the world said back, across ventures), **truth** (anchors with mutation cases, reconciliation against records the company does not write, contact rungs), and **taste** (the founder's, derived from decisions and scored on a held-out slice). Everything else in the plan is an adapter to a surface some vendor has shipped or announced. The thinkers' findings threaten the kernel from four directions at once: the plan reaches no stranger, so the record never holds a market fact (C1, C8); the only test of its central assumption is rigged to pass (C2); it spends the scarcest input, the founder's hours, without a price (C4, C20, B3); and it is about to build the commodity half by hand while the vendors ship it for free (C3, A7, B6). Ambition is not the thing to cut. The thing to cut is the belief that eighty mechanisms in dependency order reach a customer. The fixes below make the kernel the first thing built, put a stranger in the build graph, price the founder's hour, and write down what Keel refuses to build.

### C3 · Vendors ship the plan's mechanisms faster than the plan can build them · (DIGEST: conv. 4)
