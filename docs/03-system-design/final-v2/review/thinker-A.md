### A5 · `bin/egress` as a loopback hop cannot be reached from a sandboxed child — measured

**how we would know** Start any listener on 127.0.0.1 outside the sandbox, `curl` it from a `--restricted` child's Bash (note: `Bash(curl *)` is denied in project settings — use `nc -z`), and read the errno.

## What is right — five things a weaker plan would not have

1. **ABSENT versus WISH, with a path per mechanism and a state table per section.** Every builder knows what does not exist; nothing reads as enforced by being described.
2. **The trifecta as argv and a Sender that holds no model; no approve verb; staged-not-sent as the only channel a silent run has.** This survives a fully persuaded agent, and few plans in this field can say that.
3. **Crash-only instincts throughout:** `StartInterval` not `KeepAlive`, `F_FULLFSYNC`, `run.started` before exec, `orphaned` never `finished`, the Watch/Sender lease. These are the details on-call engineers learn by losing a night.
4. **v73: an anchor that has never failed is unrated.** Splitting the rung-1 share into rated and unrated is the one instrument here that can fall for a good reason, and the plan says the fall is the instrument arriving.
5. **R12 named before building.** Stating that the whole architecture inverts if most company work has no deterministic anchor — and refusing to build the inverted version *or* to assume it cannot happen — is the honest posture almost nobody holds.

## The big vision — from the chair that has to sit in it

**What this should become.** A company that keeps working when the founder's laptop closes, spends the founder's attention as its scarcest input, and can show any number it reports against a record it did not write. The plan has the third of these nailed and the first two by assertion. As built on paper, Keel is a superb *audit* of a harness; it is not yet an *operation*.

**What it optimises for versus what it says.** It says *walk with agents relentlessly until the perfect result*. It optimises for enforceability — every rule names its mechanism — and provenance. Those are the right virtues for a control plane and the wrong ones to put first for a one-person company with no customer: 960 KB, 82 rows, 80 mechanisms, 26 research questions, 72 losing images, and zero venture work has ever run through it (LONG-TERM's own standing note). The practitioner's read is that the plan is the product, and the mechanism-or-WISH doctrine has become the way the plan grows. The doctrine is good; the ratio of mechanism to venture is not.

**What is entirely missing, from the hands' point of view.** (1) *A machine.* A fanless Air on battery that sleeps in a minute is not a runtime for a night; the always-on box is a €10-a-month decision the plan defers behind a measurement it cannot take. (2) *A runbook.* Nowhere does it say what the founder does with their hands on Monday morning — write eight charter lines, an envelope in three lists with numbers, wake-me thresholds, ten agent packs, a managed file that removes their working mode — nor how long that takes. §2.7's table is the founder's *steady-state* list; the *first-month* list is ten times longer and unwritten. (3) *A cold start.* Empty taste store, empty negatives, no instruments admitted, no pack demonstrations: every anchor is rung 4 for the first month by construction, and the plan's own §11 will correctly report that the company knows nothing. Say so and give the first month a different success metric than the rung-1 share. (4) *A customer and money.* Two departments have no shipped precedent and both are the ones that touch a person. (5) *The founder's parallelism.* Five orchestrators at once is how they work today; the plan issues one.

**What is commodity within a year and should not be built.** Terminal pop (`--tmux`, `--worktree`, agent view, `--bg`/`--attach` all shipped this summer); the fleet page (`claude agents`, teams UI, Remote Control); the cost page's arithmetic (`/usage`, `prompt_cache`, `spend_limit`, `modelPricing` — W4); the bell (vendor push); the office, the 3D graph, the canvas (pages 1, 6, 7 are entertainment the plan itself calls a disqualifying control surface, and vendors and MIT projects will keep shipping better ones). A supervisor with leases — the vendor has a daemon with `leases=` in its log already.

**What is the moat.** Everything that is about *this company's truth* and nobody else can ship: the ledger with expiry and forced disposition; the anchors and their mutation cases; the negatives store; the reconciliation against records the company does not write; the consent register; the taste store mined from 3.1 GB of this founder's own transcripts; the Sender that holds no model. Those are eight programs and four files. They are the company. Build them first, on the box, with one agent, and let the fifteen names arrive one per real intent.

**What would make it the best system in the founder's fields.** In engineering: the blind tester plus the reconciliation is already ahead of everything surveyed — ship that pair alone and measure R12 on a *real* venture's first thirty intents, not the harness's. In growth and sales: the world's door and the consent register are the only honest shape for outreach by machines; make the first outward act happen with the founder's tap in month one, not after seven pages. In operations: a night that survives a lid closing. **What would make it merely adequate:** building the seven pages, the packs and the roster before the first non-harness intent, so that in month three the company has a beautiful office and no customer — which is the failure every "AI company in a box" of 2024–2026 shipped.

**If I had the founder's hands for a week:** buy the box; write `bin/log` and `bin/run` with `--restricted --tools --session-id --no-session-persistence --autocompact 1000000`; run one builder against one real venture intent through one anchor; read the transcript in the morning; then decide what the second program is. Everything in this plan that is right will still be right in a month, and it will be cheaper to build against one night of evidence than against 26 open questions.
