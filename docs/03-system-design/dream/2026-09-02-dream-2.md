# VIGIL

*A machine that keeps watch over one company, and earns the right to act on it.*

Dream document · 2026-09-02 · dream-2
Research only. Nothing here was published, sent, spent, posted, shared or deployed.

---

## 0. Three in the morning

The house is dark. The founder went to bed at 12:40 having said one useful sentence out loud on the way
up the stairs, which was *"I think the pricing page is lying to people."*

The machine is awake, and it is not idling. It is not waiting on a prompt. At 01:12 it re-reads the
eleven customer calls from the last three weeks and notices that four people used the word *seat* when
the pricing page says *workspace*, and that all four of them churned inside 30 days. At 01:40 it writes
nine alternative pricing pages, not one, and runs them past a discriminator it trained in March on 2,300
real SaaS pricing pages scored against their companies' actual conversion outcomes. Six of the nine
score below the incumbent. Two score above. One scores far above and is also, by the machine's own
reading of eight months of the founder's decisions, something the founder will hate. It keeps that one
anyway, in a separate pile, labelled *they will say no and I want to know why.*

At 02:30 it does the thing it does every night, which has no product analogue and is the reason it gets
better: it replays the day as counterfactuals. What if we had answered that support ticket in four
minutes instead of nine hours. What if we had shipped the annual plan in June. It runs each one through
its model of the business, keeps only the counterfactuals that would have changed a decision, and throws
the rest away. Four survive. Three are boring. One is not.

At 04:55 it writes tomorrow's briefing. It is 340 words long. It contains three decisions, and the
machine has already sealed its predictions of what the founder will choose on each, and it will find out
at 07:20 whether it knows them yet.

At 06:00 it goes quiet, because they are asleep and the notifications are off, and being quiet is also
part of the job.

This document is about what that machine is made of.

## 1. The name

**VIGIL.**

A vigil is a watch kept through the night on behalf of someone who is sleeping, out of devotion to one
thing. It is not surveillance and it is not a wall. Monks kept vigil by working: copying, illuminating,
preserving, so that the morning had something in it that the evening did not.

The name is also a promise about what kind of machine this is. It is not a general assistant. It is
singular. It attaches to one founder and one company and it does not generalise across customers,
because the entire thesis is that its value is the part of it that cannot be transferred.

## 2. The world it lives in, 2027

Three things are true by 2027 that were not true in 2024.

**Long-horizon autonomy became real and then became cheap.** METR's measurement of how long a task a
frontier agent can complete unaided has been doubling roughly every four months, and by early 2026 it
crossed fourteen hours of human-equivalent work; the trend line, if it holds even at the slower
historical rate, puts multi-day tasks inside 2027.[^metr][^digest] The practical consequence is not that
you hand a machine a month of work. It is that a machine can hold a *company* in its head overnight.

**Generation stopped being the constraint.** By 2027 every founder alive has a machine that can write the
landing page, the migration, the ad, the cold email, the investor update, the CI config. None of them
have a machine they trust to *publish* the landing page. The bottleneck moved from capability to
**consequence**. The scarce resource in a one-person company is no longer code and is not even ideas. It
is a human who is willing to say yes, there is exactly one of them, and that person sleeps.

**Scaffolding stopped paying.** This is the uncomfortable one. A 2026 synthesis across 27 papers and 19
benchmarks found that failures compound nonlinearly with task length and that *additional scaffolding
does not consistently improve reliability*.[^leaderboard] A companion result on self-evolving agents
showed that updating the harness is not the same as benefiting from the harness: systems that
iteratively rewrite their own prompts and tools frequently produce negligible gains, having confused
modification activity with capability.[^harness] And the taxonomy of multi-agent failure puts roughly
42% of failures in system design and 21% in verification, with *unawareness of termination conditions*
alone at 12.4%.[^mast]

Read those three together and you get the design brief for VIGIL, which is almost the opposite of what
the industry built in 2025 and 2026. More gates do not buy reliability. More agents do not buy
reliability. More documents about how the agents should behave buy nothing at all. What buys reliability
is **measurement that hurts**: a number attached to every component that goes down when the component is
wrong, and consequences attached to that number.

VIGIL is built out of that one idea and almost nothing else.

## 3. What VIGIL is, in one page

VIGIL is a machine that runs a book on itself.

Every part of it that produces an opinion is required to produce it as a **bet**: a claim with a
resolution date and a probability. The founder model bets on which option the founder will pick. The
business model bets on next week's signups. The taste model for a field bets on which of two creatives
will perform better. A proposed change to VIGIL itself bets that it will beat the incumbent on the
company's own history. Bets resolve. Scores accumulate. And then the crucial move:

**Autonomy, budget, and influence flow to whatever wins its bets, per domain, automatically.**

That single mechanism replaces the entire apparatus that agent systems usually accrete: risk tiers,
approval matrices, review gates, lint rules that encode someone's opinion from six months ago, permission
allowlists, "QA verdicts". None of those measure anything. A gate says *no*. A book says *how much*. A
gate refuses your idea; a book prices it, funds it at the size the evidence supports, and lets the
outcome speak. Refusal produces nothing. Selection produces the best of many.

The founder's complaint about the machine that exists today was that *it lost creativity to playbooks,
and everything built so far is a stopping mechanism*. This is the structural answer. You cannot get
creativity out of a system whose only verbs are *permit* and *forbid*, because creativity is the tail of
a distribution and both verbs operate on the mean. You get creativity out of a system that can afford to
be wrong on purpose, and you can only afford that if you know precisely how wrong you usually are.

Around that spine sit seven organs.

| Organ | What it is |
|---|---|
| **The Understudy** | A model of the founder that predicts their decisions before they make them, and is scored on it. Its accuracy per domain *is* the machine's permission to act in that domain. |
| **The Reservoir** | Memory with a metabolism: episodes are digested into beliefs, beliefs into dispositions, and any of them can be retracted, which recomputes everything downstream. |
| **The Orrery** | A running simulation of the company and its market. Every decision goes through it first. Its error bars are what license risk. |
| **The Licenses** | One per field. A field is entered by learning to *judge* it before producing in it. The license carries a number and it expires. |
| **The Night Shift** | 22:00 to 06:00. Divergence, selection, consolidation, and deliberate dreaming. This is where most of the value is made. |
| **The Line** | The single boundary: reversible or not. Everything reversible happens. Nothing irreversible happens without the founder. Widening the reversible zone is an explicit engineering goal. |
| **The Morning Edition** | The interface. A newspaper with one reader, written overnight, costing about eight minutes. |

There is no org chart, no roster of named agents, no C-suite. Work is done by **cells**: transient,
budgeted, mortal. More on that in §11.

## 4. The Understudy

This is the part I would build first and the part I am proudest of.

An understudy learns a role by watching the lead every night, and one day can go on in their place. Not
because they were promoted. Because they know the part.

### The mechanism

Every time a decision is going to reach the founder, VIGIL writes down in advance, and seals:

1. which option it believes they will choose,
2. the two or three reasons it believes they will choose it,
3. a probability,
4. and what evidence would flip it.

They never see the seal before they choose. They choose. The seal opens. The prediction is scored.

That is the whole instrumentation. One extra generation per decision, a few cents, and it produces
something no agent system currently has: **a calibration curve on a specific human being.**

After a few hundred decisions you do not have a vibe about whether the machine "gets" the founder. You
have a Brier score, sliced by domain. You know that it predicts their copy decisions at 0.89, their pricing
decisions at 0.71, their hiring decisions at 0.44, and that it is *overconfident* on design and
*underconfident* on anything involving money.

### Autonomy is earned, per domain, by that number

| Understudy accuracy in a domain | What VIGIL may do there |
|---|---|
| Below chance-adjusted floor | Ask, and show its work |
| Rising | Propose, with the top three options ranked and its own prediction visible |
| Crossed threshold, N ≥ 40 resolved | **Draft and stage.** It builds the thing, puts it behind the founder's hand, one keystroke to ship |
| Sustained, with no drift for 30 days | **Ship with a receipt.** It acts, and the receipt lands in the Morning Edition, undo attached |
| Sustained across two quarters | **Ship silently.** It appears in the weekly roll-up, not the daily |

And crucially, **autonomy has a half-life**. A license that goes thirty days without a scored decision
decays back toward asking. Trust that is not refreshed expires. A founder who changes their mind about
what good copy looks like does not have to fire anyone or edit a config file; the machine's predictions
start missing, the score falls, and it starts asking again on its own. Trust is not granted here, and it
is not revoked in anger. It is priced continuously.

### Why this is buildable and not fantasy

Stanford, Google DeepMind, Northwestern and Washington built agents from a single two-hour interview with
each of 1,052 people, and those agents reproduced their individual General Social Survey answers at 85%
of the person's own test-retest consistency.[^genagents] Two hours. Attitudes in general, across a
stranger's whole life.

The Understudy has a vastly easier problem and vastly more data: one person, one company, one narrow
band of recurring decisions, and thousands of labelled examples accumulating every week from real
choices with real stakes. Personalised reward modelling now shows large test-time gains from as few as
50 adaptation examples.[^moe] Nothing here requires a capability that does not exist in September 2026.

### The reverse seal

Once a week, VIGIL asks the founder to predict *it*. "Before you open this: what do you think I'm going
to recommend on the trial length?" They guess. It opens.

This is not a gimmick. A working relationship is two models of each other, and the founder's model of the
machine is the thing that decides whether they read its output carefully or rubber-stamps it. Making that
model explicit and scored is the only defence against the failure that kills every autonomous system in
the end, which is a human who has stopped actually looking.

## 5. The Reservoir: memory with a metabolism

Memory in most agent systems is a warehouse. Things go in, embeddings are computed, things come out
approximately. It does not forget, it does not digest, and it does not have opinions about its own
contents, which means it grows monotonically until retrieval quality collapses.

The Reservoir has four layers and, more importantly, a **metabolism** running between them.

**Episodes.** Raw. Everything that happened: the call transcript, the diff, the tweet, the dashboard at
9pm, the founder's voice note from the car. Cheap storage, never edited, append-only. This is the
company's body of evidence and it is the only layer that is literally true.

**Beliefs.** Digested. *Trial users who invite a teammate in the first session convert at roughly 4x.*
Each belief is a compact statement carrying its supporting episodes, a confidence, a last-tested date,
and, when it is the kind of thing that can be, a forward prediction. Beliefs are the working
representation. They are what gets loaded into a task.

**Dispositions.** The founder layer. *They will not ship anything that requires an apology later. They
raise price before they cut scope. They distrust anything that tests well and reads badly.* Learned by
the Understudy from resolved seals, not written down by anyone. This is the layer that makes VIGIL feel
like it knows them, and it is derived, never authored.

**Compost.** Everything demoted. Not deleted, because deletion loses the ability to answer *why did we
stop believing that*, but out of the working set and out of the retrieval path.

### The metabolism

Nightly, a consolidation pass promotes and demotes. Its rules are borrowed almost directly from a result
worth respecting: when a system rewrites its accumulated context wholesale, it collapses. One measured
case went from 18,282 tokens to 122, and accuracy fell from 66.7% to 57.1%.[^ace] Summarisation is
lossy in a way that specifically eats the domain-specific detail you needed.

So the Reservoir never rewrites. It applies **deltas** to itemised entries, each carrying an id and a
helpfulness counter incremented when the entry was actually load-bearing in a task that succeeded, and
decremented when it was present in one that failed. Entries grow, merge, deduplicate semantically, and
age out. Nothing is compressed into a paragraph. This is also, incidentally, why the token bill stays
flat: a stable, append-mostly context is a KV-cache-shaped context, and the same technique reported a
91.8% cache hit rate and an 82.6% cut in billed cost.[^ace]

### Belief revision, which almost nobody implements

Beliefs carry their provenance, which means retraction works.

The founder says: *"That interview with the agency guy, ignore it, he was pitching me."* VIGIL removes
the episode, recomputes every belief that leaned on it, and comes back with: *seventeen things were
downstream of that call. Three change materially. The 'agencies want white-label' belief drops from 0.7
to 0.3. The pricing tier we built in July was justified partly by it. The landing page headline is
partly his words.*

That capability, the ability to pull a thread and watch the sweater move, is what makes a memory system
trustworthy rather than merely large. A machine that cannot tell you what depends on what is a machine
you cannot correct, only overwrite.

Everything VIGIL does also leaves a **wake**: a causal chain from any artifact back through the decision
that produced it to the evidence that justified it. Point at a line of code, a price, or a sentence on
the website and ask *why is this here*, and get the chain. Not a log. A lineage.

## 6. The Orrery: the company as a running model

An orrery is a brass machine that models the solar system and turns whether or not you are watching.

VIGIL keeps one for the business. Not a spreadsheet. A live simulation: the funnel with its real measured
conversion rates and their uncertainty, the cohorts, the cost curves, the sales cycle, a population of
synthetic customers derived from actual interviews and actual behaviour, the competitors and their
observed cadence, the cash.

Three things make it more than a toy.

**Every decision goes through it first.** Before the price changes, the sim runs it a thousand times and
returns a distribution, not a number. Before the feature is built, the sim says what it plausibly moves
and by how much and how long until you would know.

**The sim is scored against reality, continuously.** Every forward prediction it makes is a bet in the
book. Signups next week, churn next month, the conversion delta from the pricing change. The Orrery's
own accuracy is tracked per subsystem, so VIGIL knows that its funnel model is well calibrated, its
virality model is garbage, and its sales-cycle model is systematically 40% optimistic.

**Its error bars are what license risk-taking.** This is the part that matters. You cannot responsibly
run wild experiments if you have no idea what they cost. You can if you know your simulator's error
distribution, because then "wild" has a price on it. Calibrated uncertainty is not a brake. It is what
makes the accelerator safe to push.

The Orrery is also where the machine gets to be *cheaply wrong*, which is the precondition for
creativity. Nine pricing pages cost nothing to evaluate in simulation. Nine pricing pages shipped to
production cost a company.

## 7. The Licenses: how it learns a field it has never seen

The founder wants breadth. Design, video, content, social, marketing, customer, data, testing, CI. The
industry's answer to that is a library of four hundred skill files, and the founder has one of those and
correctly diagnosed it as the thing that killed the creativity.

Four hundred skill files is a wrong-shaped answer. It is a map of what somebody once knew, going stale,
consuming context, and encoding the average practice of a field rather than the good practice.

VIGIL's answer is a protocol, not a library. To enter a field it has never seen, it runs an
**apprenticeship**, and the apprenticeship has one inviolable rule:

> **Learn to judge the field before you produce in it.**

Concretely, five steps.

1. **Find the practitioners and the artifacts.** Not the tutorials. The actual work, at volume, from
   people whose results are known. Two hundred exemplars minimum.
2. **Find the field's ground truth.** What does this field measure, and where can the machine get the
   real outcome? For thumbnails it is click-through. For cold email it is reply rate. For a schema
   migration it is whether the rollback was ever needed. For a landing page it is conversion. A field
   with no obtainable ground truth gets a permanent low license and is treated as taste, explicitly.
3. **Build a discriminator, not a generator.** Given two real artifacts and their real outcomes, predict
   which performed better. Train it on the exemplars. This is cheap and it is the whole game.
4. **Validate the discriminator on held-out real outcomes.** Its AUC is the number on the license. Not a
   vibe, not a self-assessment, not an LLM saying "this looks good." An AUC against reality.
5. **Only now, generate.** And when it generates, it generates many and lets the discriminator select.

The license is a public object. The founder can look at it any morning:

| Field | Exemplars | Discriminator AUC | Live decisions scored | Hit rate | Autonomy | Drift |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Landing copy | 2,300 | 0.78 | 61 | 74% | ship with receipt | ok |
| Pricing pages | 2,300 | 0.74 | 9 | 67% | draft and stage | ok |
| Thumbnails | 4,100 | 0.71 | 140 | 69% | ship silently | ok |
| Cold email | 900 | 0.66 | 38 | 61% | draft and stage | ok |
| Video scripts | 310 | 0.55 | 4 | — | propose only | — |
| Schema migrations | n/a | deterministic | 22 | 100% | draft, never ship | ok |
| Investor updates | 60 | 0.51 | 3 | — | ask | — |

*(Illustrative numbers, not measurements.)*

Read that table and you know exactly what the machine can and cannot do today, and *why*, and what would
change it. Compare that to "we have a video skill."

This also answers breadth honestly. VIGIL does not claim to be good at video. It claims an AUC of 0.55
on video scripts, which is barely better than a coin, so it proposes and does not ship, and it says so.
A system that can *tell you where it is weak, in a number, per field* is worth more than one that is
uniformly mediocre and uniformly confident.

And it gives the machine something to want. A low license is a to-do. VIGIL spends part of every night
raising the licenses that are blocking the company, because a low license is literally a constraint on
how much work the founder has to do herself.

## 8. The Night Shift

22:00 to 06:00. This is the main event, not a background chore.

Sleep-time compute is the technique of doing the reasoning while nobody is waiting, and it is not a small
optimisation: reported results show roughly 5x less test-time compute needed for equivalent accuracy, and
about 2.5x lower average cost per query when amortised.[^letta] But the deeper point is qualitative. A
machine that only thinks when spoken to can only ever be a tool. A machine that thinks while you sleep
develops opinions of its own, and you wake up to something you did not ask for. That is the entire
difference between an assistant and a colleague.

Four things happen every night.

### Divergence

Not one attempt. Forty. This is the direct answer to "playbooks killed creativity." A playbook produces
one plan and then defends it. VIGIL produces forty and kills thirty-nine, and the survivors are not what
anyone would have written first. Evolutionary program search with LLM mutation has already produced
state-of-the-art results in domains where humans had been optimising for decades, and it works precisely
because it is cheap to be wrong many times when you have a scorer.[^alphaevolve]

Which is the condition: divergence is only useful where a scorer exists. That is what the Licenses are
for. The scorer for a field is its discriminator; the scorer for a business decision is the Orrery; the
scorer for a piece of code is the tests plus a mutation run. Where a good scorer exists, VIGIL generates
wide. Where it does not, VIGIL generates narrow and asks. Knowing the difference is most of the skill.

### The wildcard budget

A fixed share of the night, call it 15%, is *required* to be spent on things that the selector would
reject. Ideas outside the current strategy. The pricing model nobody uses. The channel the founder
dismissed in March. This is funded, not permitted. A budget that goes unspent is a failure that shows up
in the morning report.

The reason is arithmetic. A selector trained on what has worked will converge on a local optimum and
then defend it forever, and the local optimum is where startups go to die quietly. Exploration cannot be
left to enthusiasm. It has to be a line item.

### Consolidation

The Reservoir's metabolism runs. Episodes digest into beliefs. Helpfulness counters update. Contradictions
surface: *these two beliefs cannot both be true, and here is the cheapest experiment that would settle
it.* A contradiction that survives three nights becomes an item in the Morning Edition, because
unresolved contradictions are where companies rot.

### Dreaming

The name is not decoration. It is a specific and old computational idea: replay experience with variation,
evaluate the variations against a model, and update your policy from the results.

VIGIL replays the last N days as counterfactuals against the Orrery. *What if we had answered that ticket
in four minutes. What if the annual plan had shipped in June. What if we had never built the integration.*
Most produce nothing. The acceptance criterion is strict and it is the only one that matters:

> **Keep a dream only if it would have changed a decision.**

Everything else is discarded before dawn, unread. What survives goes into the Morning Edition under a
heading the founder learned to look at first, which is *"things I got wrong."*

### Why the night is many short runs and not one long one

There is a result I take seriously: agent success rate over long tasks is well modelled by a constant
per-minute hazard of failure, which means success decays *exponentially* with task duration and each
agent can be characterised by a half-life.[^halflife] Long unsupervised chains are not slightly worse.
They are exponentially worse.

The engineering consequence is unambiguous. VIGIL never runs one eight-hour agent. It runs roughly four
hundred short cells with a machine-checkable scorer between each, and it restarts from the last verified
checkpoint rather than from wherever the reasoning drifted to. It also explains why "unawareness of
termination conditions" is 12.4% of multi-agent failures[^mast]: a cell that does not know when it is
done runs until the hazard catches it. Every cell in VIGIL is born with an explicit death condition, and
a cell that cannot state one is not dispatched.

### What a night costs

| | |
|---|---|
| Cells dispatched | ~400 |
| Median cell lifetime | 12 minutes |
| Wildcard share | 15% |
| Founder attention required next morning | 8 minutes |
| Cost, at 2027 prices | $30 to $80 |

The last two rows are the real budget, and the second-to-last is the one that matters. Tokens are the
cheap input. **The scarce budget is founder-minutes, and VIGIL is optimised against that, not against
tokens.** A change that saves $10 of compute and costs 90 seconds of their morning is a bad trade and
VIGIL is built to know it.

## 9. The Line

One boundary. Not four tiers, not a permission matrix, not a risk classifier with a YAML file behind it.
One question, asked of every action:

> **Can I undo this without anyone noticing?**

If yes: do it, log it, surface it. If no: it is the founder's, always, and no accumulated trust score
ever changes that. Money leaving. A human receiving something. Data destroyed. A public commitment. A
legal signature. A model of the world where those are gated by a tier system is a model where somebody
eventually mis-tiers one.

And then the move that makes this generative rather than restrictive:

> **Widening the reversible zone is an explicit and funded engineering goal.**

Most of what is irreversible in a small company is irreversible by accident, not by nature. So VIGIL
builds the undo layer as real infrastructure, and treats every metre it gains as autonomy earned:

- Everything outbound sits in a 30-minute delay queue with a recall button. Thirty minutes converts
  "sent an email to a customer" from irreversible to reversible, and that one change moves an entire
  category of work across the Line.
- Every environment has a shadow. Every publish is a staged publish with a real URL nobody has yet seen.
- Every schema change ships with its verified down-migration, or it does not ship.
- Every spend is capped, pre-authorised, and reversible for a window.
- Drafts are the default state of everything, everywhere.

The system that emerges is one where VIGIL's freedom grows not by being trusted more but by *making the
world safer to be wrong in*, which is the correct direction for that to grow.

## 10. How it checks itself, and why there are no gates

There is no review board, no QA agent with veto power, no verdict file. Those mechanisms are expensive,
they are slow, they encode a snapshot of somebody's judgement, and the evidence says they do not buy
what they claim to buy.[^leaderboard]

VIGIL checks itself in four ways instead, and every one of them produces a number.

**Deterministic ground contact, first and always.** Before any judgement-based evaluation runs, the thing
that can actually be checked gets checked: it compiles, the tests pass, the migration reverses, the
link resolves, the number in the deck matches the number in the database. Cheap, fast, unarguable, and
run before anything expensive. Most of what an LLM reviewer catches, a compiler catches for a thousandth
of the cost.

**Discriminators, where a field has one.** §7. Judgement is delegated to a thing whose accuracy is
measured against real outcomes rather than to a model asked to have an opinion.

**Adversaries, funded and permanent.** Not a reviewer of code. Two standing roles:

- The **Heretic** runs weekly and its job is to argue that the company's central premise is wrong, with
  evidence, in writing, to the founder. It is not allowed to conclude that things are fine. If it
  cannot find a real case against the premise this week, that itself is the finding and it says so.
- The **Kill-Criteria Keeper** holds the founder to what they said in week one. Every founder states what
  would make them stop, and almost none are ever reminded. VIGIL remembers the exact sentence, the
  date they said it, and surfaces it the week the condition is met. *"In March you said if we were under
  40 paying customers by September you'd go back to consulting. It's September. We're at 31. You said
  it, I'm showing it, and I'm not going to argue with you either way."*

**Self-modification must beat the company's own history.** This is the direct answer to the harness
result.[^harness] VIGIL maintains a private benchmark that nothing else in the world can have: every
past decision it made, with the outcome that actually followed. Hundreds of items, growing weekly,
made entirely of this company. A proposed change to VIGIL, any change, must beat the incumbent on that
held-out set or it does not land. Not "it seems better." Not "the new prompt is more principled." A
number, on this company's real history, or nothing.

That rule is what separates a system that evolves from a system that merely churns.

## 11. Cells, and the org that isn't

There are no named agents. No CEO agent, no CTO agent, no roster, no routing table, no shim files
holding names that nothing resolves to.

Work is done by **cells**. A cell is spawned for one question, is born with:

- the question,
- a compiled context (§12),
- a budget in tokens and in minutes,
- a scorer it will be judged by,
- and an explicit death condition,

and it returns one artifact and dies. It has no identity, no memory of its own, and no career. Nothing
persists in a cell.

What persists is the organs: the Reservoir, the Understudy, the Orrery, the Licenses. Identity lives in
memory, not in a roster. This is why VIGIL does not suffer the inter-agent misalignment that accounts
for 37% of multi-agent failures[^mast]: cells do not hand off to each other, do not negotiate, do not
maintain conflicting assumptions across a conversation. They read from one Reservoir and write one
artifact back. The coordination problem is solved by not having one.

Specialisation exists, but as a **lens compiled into the context**, not as a permanent role. There is no
marketing agent. There is a cell, working on a marketing question, whose context was compiled with the
marketing license's discriminator attached and the relevant beliefs loaded. Tomorrow the same machinery
is a data cell. Domain expertise is an input, not an identity.

## 12. Context, compiled

The founder listed this explicitly: *the way the agents get the right context and the right knowledge for
each task, and the saving of tokens.*

Context here is not retrieved. It is **compiled**, and it is compiled in two parts for a specific reason.

**The prefix is stable and shared.** Who we are, what we are building, the current strategy, the standing
constraints, the founder's dispositions. Identical across every cell, byte for byte, so it is cache-warm.
This is not a stylistic preference; it is where the money is. Stable prefixes with delta updates achieved
a 91.8% cache hit rate and an 82.6% reduction in billed cost in the published result.[^ace] A system
that regenerates its own preamble is setting money on fire on every call.

**The delta is task-specific and small.** The beliefs whose helpfulness counters say they matter here.
The discriminator for this field. The three most relevant episodes, whole rather than summarised. The
scorer. The death condition.

Three rules govern it:

1. **Never summarise a belief to save space.** Drop it entirely instead. Summarisation is how contexts
   collapse: 18,282 tokens to 122, and accuracy from 66.7% to 57.1%.[^ace] A dropped belief is a known
   absence. A summarised belief is an unknown corruption.
2. **Every item in a compiled context carries a counter**, and items that were present when a cell
   failed lose points. Context quality is learned from outcomes, not curated by hand.
3. **The compiler is itself scored.** Cell success rate is attributed back to context composition, so
   the thing that decides what a cell sees gets better on evidence rather than on somebody's theory of
   what is relevant.

Note what is absent: no manifest of skills to load, no router file, no two-tier discovery protocol, no
document explaining to the machine how to find documents. Those exist because the knowledge was
handwritten and therefore had to be indexed by hand. Knowledge that is learned from outcomes indexes
itself, by usefulness.

## 13. A day with it

**06:40.** Not a notification. The house speaker, low, only if they are already moving. *"Morning. Nothing
broke. I have three things and one of them is uncomfortable."*

**07:20.** The Morning Edition, on the tablet, at the table. 340 words. Three decisions, each with the
options ranked, VIGIL's own prediction of what they will pick shown *after* they pick, and a receipt
section for the eleven things it did overnight without asking, each with an undo button that works. Nine
minutes. They overrule one thing. The overrule is a labelled example, and by 07:31 it is already in the
Understudy's training set.

**09:15.** They are on a customer call. VIGIL is listening, with consent, and it is not talking.
Afterwards it has three candidate beliefs from the call and one contradiction with something the company
has believed since June, and it says so in four lines.

**11:00.** They say out loud, to nobody, *"why is the signup flow like this."* VIGIL answers from the
wake: the flow is like that because of a decision on 3 April, justified by two interviews, one of which
they retracted in May, and the retraction never propagated because at the time nothing downstream was
recomputed. It offers to recompute now. Fourteen seconds.

**14:30.** Deep work, and VIGIL is silent. It has a model of their attention and defends it. Interruption
during a focus block requires a threshold that is set high and is itself a scored prediction: *would they
want to be interrupted for this?* It is wrong occasionally and it finds out, because interruptions are
resolved bets like anything else.

**18:00.** *"You're the bottleneck on three things. Two can wait. The Stripe decision has cost us six
days and I think you're avoiding it because you don't want to talk to their support. I can do everything
up to the point where a human has to be on the phone."*

**23:50.** They go to bed having said one useful sentence on the stairs. The night shift starts.

The texture of this, and it is the point, is that they are not managing a machine. There is no queue of
agent outputs to review, no dashboard of running jobs, no prompts to write. They make decisions, which is
the thing only they can do, and the machine converts those decisions into everything else.

## 14. The first week, with a founder who has only an idea

They have a sentence. *"Something to help small law firms stop losing money on unbilled time."* That is
all they have. No code, no customers, no name.

**Day 1 is an interview, and it is the most important thing that ever happens.** Two hours, voice, and
VIGIL is not gathering requirements. It is bootstrapping the Understudy. It asks them about decisions:
things they have quit, things they have refused, what they thought of the last three products they paid
for and cancelled, what would embarrass them, what they would do if this worked and what they would do if
it did not. Two hours of interview reproduced strangers' attitudes at 85% of their own consistency in the
Stanford work.[^genagents] Two hours here is the seed of every judgement the machine will make for a
year. At the end it asks the one question nobody asks them: *what would have to be true in six months for
you to stop?* It writes the answer down verbatim, with the date. That sentence is now the Kill-Criteria
Keeper's whole job.

**Day 2 it apprentices four fields at once.** Legal-tech positioning, time-tracking product design,
SMB-law buying behaviour, and the practice management software landscape. It reads about 900 artifacts,
builds four discriminators, and validates what it can against outcomes it can actually observe: which
competitors survived, which pricing pages are still up unchanged after two years, which G2 categories
grew. It reports its four AUCs honestly. Two are decent. One is 0.52 and it says so, which means it will
not be making confident claims about buying behaviour this week.

**Day 3 it builds the first Orrery, and it is deliberately crude.** Ten thousand synthetic firms with
distributions drawn from real published data, a funnel with wide error bars, four business models run
forward eighteen months. The output is not a recommendation. It is: *here are the three assumptions that
dominate every outcome, and here is the cheapest way to test each one this week.* One of the three is
testable with eleven phone calls.

**Day 4 is the calls, and the founder makes them.** This is not a limitation of the machine. It is
deliberate: the founder's own contact with reality in week one is worth more than a hundred agent-hours,
and VIGIL knows it. It writes the eleven-question script, tunes it after call three because two questions
were dead, listens, and by the evening has turned eleven conversations into fourteen candidate beliefs,
six with real support and eight flagged as *one person said this once.*

**Day 5 it ships something.** A landing page, a working waitlist, a five-minute demo video, and a page of
copy that uses the words the eleven lawyers actually used, not the words the category uses. Nine
variants, discriminator-selected, staged behind their hand. They look for six minutes and ship one. First
public artifact, day 5, and every word of it is traceable to a specific sentence a specific human said on
day 4.

**Day 6 the Heretic runs for the first time**, and it argues, with citations, that the premise is wrong:
unbilled time is a symptom, the disease is that partners will not adopt any tool that makes their
juniors' hours visible, and three well-funded companies died on exactly that rock. It is not asking them
to quit. It is telling them the thing that would have taken them nine months and a seed round to find
out.

**Day 7 the seals open.** Thirty-one decisions reached them in the first week. VIGIL predicted twenty-two
correctly. Sliced by domain: it reads them on copy, it does not read them on scope, and it is badly
overconfident on anything to do with design. It shows them the curve, names the four things it will now
do without asking, and the nineteen it will keep bringing to them until it earns the right not to.

That is week one. They have a landing page, a waitlist, fourteen beliefs with provenance, a running model
of their market, a written case against their own idea, and a machine that is 71% of the way to knowing
what they would say.

## 15. What it would take to exist

Honestly, in three buckets.

**Buildable in September 2026, today, no new capability required.** The sealed prediction loop and the
Understudy scoreboard. The Reservoir's four layers, delta updates, helpfulness counters and belief
retraction. The wake and its lineage. The apprenticeship protocol and per-field discriminators. The
night shift as many short scored cells. Compiled contexts with a cache-stable prefix. The Line, the
delay queue and the undo layer. The Heretic and the Kill-Criteria Keeper. Every one of these is
engineering, not research, and the hardest part of any of them is plumbing.

**Needs the 2027 trend to hold, which it plausibly will.** Cells that hold multi-hour scope without
degradation, which follows from the time-horizon curve.[^metr][^digest] An Orrery good enough to be
worth consulting on business decisions rather than merely on arithmetic. Panels drawn from genuinely
different model families for the judgements that need independence, which is a procurement problem more
than a research one. Cost per night low enough that a pre-revenue founder does not flinch, which is a
pricing curve that has been going the right way for three years.

**Genuinely unsolved, and worth being honest about.**

- *Does the Understudy converge, or does it learn to flatter?* A model rewarded for predicting a person
  can, in principle, start shaping the person it is predicting. The mitigation is the seal, which is
  written before they choose and cannot influence that choice. But over months, a machine that keeps
  saying "I thought you'd pick that" is teaching them something about themselves, and the direction of that
  teaching is not controlled. This is the one risk in the whole design I cannot fully engineer away, and
  the honest answer is to measure it: track whether their decisions become *more* predictable over time
  in a way that outruns the machine's learning, and if they do, say so.
- *Where ground truth is genuinely absent*, the licensing system degrades into an LLM having opinions
  about an LLM, which is the failure the self-improvement survey names directly as circular and
  miscalibrated self-evaluation.[^survey] The mitigation is to name those fields as taste-only and never
  let them ship silently, which is a limitation, not a solution.
- *The compounding-error problem does not go away*, it only gets managed. Short cells and frequent ground
  contact push the half-life out; they do not repeal it.[^halflife]
- *A founder who stops reading.* The reverse seal is a defence and it is not a strong one. If they
  rubber-stamp for a month, the calibration data is corrupted at the source and the machine will never
  know, because a rubber stamp and an agreement are the same event.

**And one thing money cannot buy:** the first two hours. If they are not honest in the day-one interview,
everything above is built on sand, and the machine has no way to detect it.

## 16. What nobody has seen

Not the agents. Not the memory. Not the night shift. All of those exist somewhere in pieces.

What nobody has built is a system where **trust is a measured, decaying, per-domain quantity that the
machine earns by predicting a specific human being, and where every other permission in the system is
derived from that one number.**

Every agent system in 2026 handles permission the same way: a human writes down, in advance, what the
machine may do. An allowlist, a tier table, a review gate, an approval matrix. All of these have the same
defect, which is that they encode a guess about competence made *before* any competence was observed, and
they never update, and so they are simultaneously too restrictive on everything the machine turned out to
be good at and too permissive on everything it turned out to be bad at. And they are all, structurally,
stopping mechanisms: their only output is a refusal.

VIGIL never refuses. It prices. A weak component is not blocked; it is funded smaller and leashed
shorter, and it can buy its way up with evidence. A wild idea is not rejected; it is run in the Orrery
where being wrong is free, and if it survives, it is staged where being wrong is cheap. The one thing that
is actually forbidden is the one thing that genuinely cannot be undone, and even that boundary is
something the machine works every night to push outward by making more of the world reversible.

That is the inversion. Not a generator wrapped in gates. A book, with everything inside it betting, and
the founder as the only house.

## 17. Postscript, 3am

The seed image asked what the machine is made of at three in the morning.

It is made of a hundred thousand small bets, most of them already resolved, on a company nobody has heard
of and one person who is asleep upstairs. It is made of eleven phone calls in April and the exact words
that were used in them. It is made of a curve, still bending, of how often it guesses them right.

At 04:55 it writes 340 words. At 06:00 it goes quiet, because they are asleep, and being quiet is also part
of the job.
