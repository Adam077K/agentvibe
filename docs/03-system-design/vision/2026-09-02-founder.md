# The company from the chair — a Tuesday in 2028

**Vision round, angle: the founder's chair.** Written 2026-09-02 against `vision/00-BRIEF.md` and
`vision/r0-founder.md`. Full scale, no floor, no year one. Year one is derived backwards in §7.

**The design rule I held to.** Every mechanism below traces to a *moment* — a minute in the founder's day
when it is either there or missed. A mechanism I could not attach to a moment is not here, however clever.
That rule cut about a third of what I drafted, and the cut third is named at the end of §8.

**The one sentence.** *The founder's attention is the only input the machine cannot manufacture, so it is
the resource the whole machine is budgeted against* — metered like tokens, spent deliberately, accounted
for at the end of the week.

Marks: `C2`, `P7`, `W1` cite `expansion/concepts.md`; a repo name cites `open-source.md`; `hands §1.1`
cites `hands.md`. Everything else is **`NEW`**.

# 0 · THE PICTURE

## The night before

At **21:00** the founder's laptop is shut and the company is not on it. The company is a small always-on
host — one machine, roughly the cost of a phone plan — running a supervisor called `chair-supervisor`, and
under it four loop processes, one per venture: `loop-beeond`, `loop-v2`, `loop-v3`, `loop-probe`. Each loop
is a short program with a sleep. None of them thinks. Each one reads the mission graph, computes the next
move with a pure function, prints the arithmetic, and spawns a worker.

A worker is `claude -p` with `--allowedTools` narrowed to exactly the hands its pack was granted, plus, for
two of the four ventures tonight, a second-family process for one judge seat. At 03:00 the process list is
about thirty entries deep and every one of them is traceable to a mission id.

Overnight it produces **≈70 durable artifacts**: sixteen ad variants rendered through higgsfield's batch
pipeline (`hands §1.1`), a rewritten onboarding flow behind a flag, four sourced field notes, a competitor
teardown from an RSS sweep, eleven dead-end records, and one thing nobody asked for, made on the explore
reservation (`C33`). It also produces **eleven cards** for the Deck, seven of which will fuse before the
founder ever sees them, because they do not need to answer those and the machine knows it.

## 06:40 — the Wake

The founder wakes to a **90-second spoken briefing**, four sections in a fixed order that never varies
(`V2`):

> *What changed.* Sixteen ad variants for beeond, three in cells the gallery has never occupied. The v2
> onboarding rewrite is behind a flag and rendered clean.
> *What is blocked.* One thing: v3's payment test needs a card you have to add. It has been blocked eleven
> hours and its block expires Thursday.
> *What needs you.* Four cards. One is irreversible.
> *What I would do next.* Kill the v3 pricing mission. It has spent 40% of its budget, its falsifier came
> back negative on Sunday, and it is holding the slot.

Fixed order is what makes it skimmable by ear. Each section may be "nothing", and "nothing" is spoken —
silence is indistinguishable from failure. The founder says *"more on the second one"* and the briefing
walks one level down (`V3`). They say *"stop"* and it stops mid-word.

The Wake reads a field; it does not summarise a record. Every row carries `say:` — one sentence, fifteen
words, no paths, no hashes — written at emission by the thing that knew what happened (`V1`). There is no
model between what happened and what the founder hears.

## 07:10 — the Table

One screen. Not seven views. **The Table** is the balcony's morning face and it shows **the round, not the
pick** (`C37`).

Across the top, four lanes, one per venture, each with its single in-flight mission and the computed
priority arithmetic printed beneath it — not a ranking, the *working*: `deadline 6d · unlocks 3 · cost est
$140 · falsifier resolved · → 0.71`, and beside it `instead_of: v2-pricing-probe` (`P1`, `EC3`).

Below that, the night's candidates as **objects, not rows**. The sixteen ad variants are sixteen thumbnails
laid out by the two behaviour descriptors their pack declared — density on one axis, register on the other
— with the gallery's previously occupied cells greyed underneath (`C2`). The founder can see at a glance
that eleven of tonight's sixteen landed in cells that were already full, and three landed somewhere new.
Each carries a ≤60-word pitch and nothing else (`C26`).

Bottom right, one number and the company's only headline metric: **founder interventions per surviving
artifact**, this week against the trailing quarter, both components visible so a fall caused by fewer
surviving artifacts cannot read as a win (`SI4`).

## 07:10–07:35 — the taste pass

Twenty-five minutes, and it is the highest-value twenty-five minutes in the company's day. The founder
picks, kills, and promotes.

Every pick writes a **preference pair**: the two artifacts, the choice, and one required sentence of why
(`C21`). Not prose — data. Tonight's picks join about 900 accumulated pairs, and the eight most relevant
are injected into every judge dispatch tomorrow as few-shot calibration. Promoting a *loser* is the single
most informative act available to this company, so it is one click and the shown set is ordered by archive
distance, making the three visible losers genuinely different from the winner rather than three shades of
it. Nothing here is a form. The founder never types a rating. **There is no 0–10 anywhere in this company,
at any layer, by rule** (`A1`).

## 07:35–07:50 — the Deck

Four cards. Each one is the same shape and the shape is the point:

```
CARD  v3-billing-live · irreversible · fuses Thu 09:00 → DEFAULT: do not ship
REVERSIBILITY   Money moves. A refund is possible; a chargeback is not. Cannot be reverted by git.
THE CASE FOR    [4 lines, sourced]
THE CASE AGAINST[4 lines, sourced]
WHAT WOULD CHANGE MY MIND   If the Stripe test-mode run had covered the dunning path. It did not.
COST OF WAITING $0/day. Nothing downstream is blocked.
YOU DECIDE BECAUSE  blast_radius: financial. No configuration can clear this one.
```

**Reversibility is the first line, before the recommendation**, because a founder reading fast reads the
first line. Both sides are argued by different workers with no sight of each other, and the "what would
change my mind" line is arithmetic where priority is computed, not a model's opinion (`E3`).

And every card has **a fuse**: a deadline and a stated default that fires if the founder does not answer.
The default is always the reversible branch. The founder's silence is recorded as a decision with the
reason "fused", not as an unread item.

This is why the Deck is not the empty escalation Inbox. The Inbox has been empty on every project this
company has ever run, and the reason is structural: **a queue with no fuse is a queue nobody has to work.**

## 08:00–11:30 — the making block

The founder makes something themselves. Three and a half hours, and the machine may not interrupt.

This is not a comfort. In a company where a machine does the producing, the founder is the only remaining
source of taste, and **taste that is only ever exercised as judgement degrades into preference.** A founder
who has not made anything in six months starts approving things for reasons they cannot articulate, and the
preference pairs get noisier, and the judges calibrate on noise.

The block is declared in `DAY.yml` and the loop reads it as a hard constraint. During it the founder's
**attention budget is zero** with exactly two named exceptions — a live outbound action that a control
refused and cannot wait, and a security event. Both are enumerated in the file. Nothing else, ever, for any
reason a model finds compelling.

## 11:30 — the accrual

Whatever accumulated during the block arrives at once, ordered, as one interruption rather than nine. The
founder's **attention meter** shows the day's spend: 2 of 6 interruptions used, 0 of 3 questions.

## 12:30 — the walk

The phone. Exactly three verbs: **approve · redirect · stop** (`V5`). Anything richer queues for a
keyboard. Redirect is a choice among candidates wherever the machine can offer one, so the common case is a
tap and not typing.

Voice in, and every voice-originated instruction is read back **in the system's own words** before it binds
(`V4`) — echoing the transcript would confirm the transcription, not the intent. Above `reach: internal`
the read-back is required and the move will not dispatch without `confirmed: true`.

## 14:00 — the conversation

Twenty minutes. **The machine asks its questions.**

It has three per day and it is required to spend them. A machine that never asks is a machine that guesses,
and guessing is invisible until an artifact is wrong for a reason nobody can name. The questions are not
status; they are the specific ambiguities that blocked a judgement:

> *"Two of the sixteen variants use the phrase 'built for teams'. Your taste file says 'no corporate
> register' and I could not tell whether that phrase violates it. Does it?"*

The answer becomes a rule in **the Grain**, this project's taste store, with an expiry. In a quarter the
machine will show it back and ask whether it still holds.

## 16:00 — the world's hour

The **Register** reports. This is the hour that separates this company from every system that measures only
whether it was *right*.

Everything that ever left the building — published, sent, deployed, posted — has a register entry with a
`check_on` date, and on that date an outcome is recorded (`W3`). The screen shows last week's departures
against what the world did, and each is labelled with its **rung on the evidence ladder** (`W2`):

```
0 it renders · 1 a stranger understood it in five seconds · 2 someone clicked
3 someone came back · 4 someone paid
```

Most weeks most rows sit at rung 0–1 and the ladder reads as an indictment. That is the correct reading and
it is more useful than the absence of one. `no data` is a permitted outcome and is distinct from
`not checked`; a run of consecutive `no data` is itself a finding, surfaced as one.

## 17:30 — the Night Order

Four minutes. The founder states **purpose, not tasks** (`P7`): task, purpose one level up, end state, and
constraints. There is no `method` field and the schema refuses one.

> *"Find out whether beeond's onboarding is losing people at the invite step or the import step. Purpose:
> we are about to spend three weeks on the wrong one. End state: a number for each, from real sessions, or
> a written statement that we cannot get one. Constraints: no contact with any user, no spend above $50."*

Then they leave the chair.

## The month, from a distance

**Monday 09:00 — the constraint.** One sentence naming the week's bottleneck (`P2`). Every move the loop
dispatches must relieve it or be logged as an override with a written reason. It is a claim with an expiry,
so it cannot quietly outlive its truth.

**Friday 16:00 — the reckoning.** Forty minutes, and the only recurring meeting the company has. Four
things: the intervention metric with its components; **what the machine learned about the founder's taste
this week, read back for confirmation or rejection**; the retirement queue — everything with zero calls in
90 days (`X2`); and the waiver count, because three waivers on one block is a decision being avoided.

**Quarterly.** The portfolio review: each venture's share of the window with its floor and ceiling
(`CY2`), and one venture's `retire_on` coming due. The taste recalibration: twelve preference pairs older
than a quarter, shown back, kept or dropped. The grant review: every hand's grant expires and must be
renewed with a reason.

**A month at full scale, in numbers.** These are targets, not measurements — §8 says what has to be true
for them.

| | |
|---|---|
| Ventures live · missions in graph / in flight | 4 · ≈30 / 4 |
| Durable artifacts per night · cards to the Deck | ≈70 · ≈11, of which ≈7 fuse |
| Founder time in the chair, per day | ≈70 minutes |
| Founder interruptions, budget per day | 6 |
| Machine questions, budget per day | 3 (and it must spend them) |
| Machine cost, all-in per month | ≈$8,000 |
| Interventions per surviving artifact | target < 0.3, trending down |

# 1 · WHAT IT MAKES

**Artifacts, by class.** Roughly 70 durable artifacts a night and 2,000 a month, but the class distribution
is what matters, because the founder touches these classes at completely different rates:

| Class | Volume/month | Founder touches | Ships behind |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sketches into the gallery | ≈900 | sees ~40 | nothing — they never ship |
| Finished creative (video, page, copy) | ≈120 | picks ~30 | `blocking-human` on publish |
| Code shipped behind a flag | ≈60 | sees the flag flip | the QA gate + the flag |
| Sourced field notes into the Book | ≈80 | never | claim expiry |
| Dead-end records | ≈150 | never | nothing |
| Outbound to a named human | ≈40 | approves each first contact | the contact register |
| Register outcome checks | ≈120 | reads the weekly roll-up | nothing |

**Ventures.** Four live, and the number is a declared ceiling rather than a drift. One flagship carrying
most of the revenue, two growing, one probe that will either graduate or wind down within two quarters.
Intake is a bounded protocol producing exactly three artifacts and then stopping — a taste file, one
mission with a falsifier, one approved done-test — and no move dispatches against a venture missing any of
the three (`CY1`).

**Cadence.** The unit of work is the **mission** and it outlives any session — days to weeks, one in flight
per venture, a blocked one parked with its expiry and its slot freed (`B5`, `B3`). Moves run minutes to
hours. Nothing runs on a session boundary, because the session is a property of the runtime, not of the
work.

**How money comes in.** Through the ventures, never the machine. The machine is a cost centre measured as
**cost per surviving artifact** — spend over artifacts that reached rung ≥ 2 (`EC2`) — and a month of cheap
runs producing nothing has an **undefined** ratio, reported as undefined, never as excellent.

**How money goes out.** Four meters, and only one of them is currently expressible in this repo's
vocabulary:

1. **Tokens** — the rolling window, governed by the Rope (`EC4`).
2. **Credits** — media generation, readable in-band (`higgsfield.balance`).
3. **Compute** — GPU-seconds; the meter runs whether or not anyone is watching, which is the real risk.
4. **Real money** — ad spend, postage, subscriptions. **This is the axis no tier in this repo can express**
   (`hands §8.3`), because it is the only one with a *rate*: "$400 per day" is not a reversibility class
   and not a blast radius.

**A good year.** Two of the four ventures clear a revenue floor that makes the machine a rounding error;
one probe graduates; one venture winds down through the protocol with its learnings kept global (`CY4`);
the intervention metric falls by half while the founder's engagement count holds steady — because a metric
that falls because the founder disengaged is the failure wearing the success's face, and the pair is
reported together (`SI4`).

# 2 · THE FOURTEEN, GROWN

## 01 · Missions & drive

`MISSIONS.yml` per venture: a dependency graph, not a list, with hash-based ids so parallel loops do not
collide (`gastownhall/beads` is the closest verified prior art and its `bd ready` query is the missing
scheduler). Each mission carries commander's intent — task, purpose, end state, constraints — and the
schema **refuses `steps:`, `how:`, `method:`, `implementation:`** (`P7`; the predicate already exists in
`schema-lint.js` for playbook stages). It also carries a `falsifier:` with a cost, and no expensive move
dispatches until the falsifier has an outcome (`C36`). Priority is **computed by a pure function over
declared fields and printed with its arithmetic** (`P1`) — the model's job is to fill fields honestly, not
to pick — and `evidence_of_demand` is sourced through `claim-source` so it cannot be asserted without a URL
and a quote. One mission in flight per venture. **Blocked is authored, stalled is computed** (`B1`): a
worker declares blocked with a `clearable_by:`, the meter computes stalled from output tokens since the
last durable artifact on disk, and neither can author the other. Blocks expire and force one of three
dispositions (`B3`). The week's constraint pointer is the loop's dispatch filter (`P2`).

**Moment it serves:** 07:10, the priority arithmetic on the Table; 06:40, "what is blocked" spoken as its
own section; 17:30, the Night Order writing intent and nothing else.

## 02 · Workers & roster

≈26 **packs**. A pack is a grant and a stop — a named set of hands, a declared artifact type, and a
done-test — and never a procedure. Personas exist separately, argue, and are structurally forbidden from
producing. Trust is **computed, never declared**, and the key is the pair `(pack × field)`, not the worker
(`T1`, `T2`): a pack excellent at short-form video has no standing on regulated claims, and the lookup
fails closed to `supervised` on an unknown pair. Promotion needs N consecutive moves whose artifacts
survived the world's verdict; demotion is the same counter running backwards on an incident, with no
restore and no appeal that skips the count (`T5`). A new pack serves an apprenticeship in shadow: real
moves, real artifacts, ships nothing, graduates on a record (`T4`). Retirement is triggered by **zero calls
in 90 days**, computed from dispatch telemetry, not proposed by anyone (`X2`), and above the declared
ceiling a new pack requires a retired one (`X4`). Retirement is archival with a resolvable stub, never
deletion (`X5`).

**Moment it serves:** Friday 16:00, the retirement queue; and every 03:00, because trust is what decides
whether a card reaches the Deck at all.

## 03 · Hands

**Grant = (worker × hand × scope × budget × expiry)** (`hands §5.2C`) — the same shape as a claim, which
means forced expiry, the hard part, is already built. Every grant declares `reversible: yes|no|partial` and
`blast_radius: self|founder|stranger|public`, and a grant with no risk declaration is invalid (`R2`).
Declared once, at the moment a capability enters the company, rather than hundreds of times at call sites.
Reach is then *derived* from the tools an action used, never asserted about the action. Three things run
daily: a **health oracle** (`claude mcp list` is one line and today nothing runs it), a **reachability
probe** that dispatches a worker whose only job is to call each granted hand once and report (`H2` —
because a grant that exists in a file and does not survive dispatch is worse than no grant), and the
**named-human register**, which fails closed on an unparseable recipient (`R6`). Every outbound hand is
**dry-run by default**: it produces the draft, the unpublished post, the unsigned invoice, and the send is a
separate call carrying the artifact's hash (`R4`). Absolute rate ceilings bind regardless of reasoning
(`R5`), because every reasoning-based control can be reasoned around.

**Moment it serves:** 12:30 on the phone, where "approve" is a hash-carrying second call and not a verb the
machine could have said to itself.

## 04 · Knowledge

**The Book** (`FIELDS/`) is global and holds how a field works. Its core is not rules — it is **three
annotated exemplars per field: a good one, a bad one, a near-miss**, each sourced, with rules derived from
and subordinate to them (`C16`). Learning a new field is a bounded protocol with a checkable exit — five
practitioners named, three canonical artifacts sourced, seven rules extracted, one proposed done-test —
and then it **stops** (`K1`). A field file naming a project fails lint (`K3`), which is Decision 9 enforced
by scope rather than by convention. The 28 mental models with stop rules are injected **by the harness**,
not chosen by the agent, and the return must name which it used and what the stop rule said (`K2`) — today
zero of eighteen agents cite one, and discretionary citation is why. Before ORIENT the harness greps the
Graveyard by field and by tool and injects the actual entries (`C25`).

**Moment it serves:** the founder never sees the Book, and that is the test. If they have to read it, it
failed.

## 05 · Memory

Four stores, and what makes them work is that **they have different physics and the difference is a routing
rule** (`MEM1`):

| Store | Holds | Physics |
|---|---|---|
| `events.jsonl` | what happened | append-only, never expires, never evidence for a belief |
| the claim ledger | what is true | expires, forces a disposition |
| the Book | how a field works | exemplar-first, global, expires |
| the Grain | this venture's taste | preference pairs + extracted rubric, expires quarterly |

**An event is never evidence for a belief without a claim.** Conflict resolves as newer-wins *with the
older retained in place carrying the evidence that moved it* (`MEM2`) — the practice this repo already
follows by hand in every supersession block, made a data operation. Forgetting is a program with four rules
and a stub left under every original heading, never a judgement call (`MEM3`). And **RAG over the 2,936
transcripts is refused as memory** (`A4`): transcripts are full of superseded beliefs stated confidently,
and retrieval cannot tell a corrected belief from a current one.

**Moment it serves:** 14:00, when the machine's question produces a rule that must be findable in a quarter
and expirable after it.

## 06 · Communication

**Star topology: workers never message workers** (`CO3`). A worker's only outputs are its artifact and its
rows; to reach another worker it writes a row the dispatcher reads. This is enforced by grant — no worker
holds a messaging hand — and it exists to stop the org chart growing back through informal channels. A
**pod**, two workers sharing one dispatch, is the named exception for genuine pair work. The baton has a
fixed shape with four required sections and a per-section byte cap (`CO1`, SBAR), and the receiver restates
it in its own words on first return, with large divergence raised as a **finding rather than a block**
(`CO2`) — the receiver may be right and the baton wrong. Parallel workers take **time-limited leases on
path prefixes**, checked at dispatch rather than discovered at merge (`CO4`).

**The founder channel is two channels, and merging them is why steering feels impossible** (`S1`) — the
split is in §4. The founder says which. The system never guesses.

**Moment it serves:** 12:30. "Warmer, less corporate" must not stop the world, and "stop doing that" must.

## 07 · Context & cost

Every injected surface has a declared byte cap that only ever **ratchets down**; exceeding it does not
raise the cap, it forces a two-tier split into ids plus one-line summaries with content on demand (`CT1`).
This is measured twice in this repo already: skills discovery 15,000 → 1,070 tokens, and `session-start.js`
27,069 → 2,941 bytes, at which point the injected content actually reached agent context for the first
time. **Every row carries a real task id, minted at dispatch, from the first commit** (`CT2`), with a
parent/child pair so sub-work has identity. Compaction writes a manifest of what it dropped and where the
full record lives, so an agent knows what it no longer knows (`CT3`).

**Moment it serves:** every moment. The Table, the Wake, the Deck, the Register and the attention meter are
five projections of one log, and without the id they are five logs that disagree.

## 08 · Quality & truth

**A deterministic oracle runs before any model is asked anything.** Then a panel that returns findings and
never a number. Judges compare two candidates blind, with identities stripped, order randomised, and the
swapped twin run — and **a pair that flips resolves `unresolved`, never to a winner** (`C1`, `C19`).
Selection is arithmetic over findings: eliminate anything with a P1, prefer fewest distinct P2s, break ties
by archive distance (`C17`). There is no `total` field anywhere and a check refuses one. The maker decides
which findings to act on and records which it declined and why; a silently dropped P1 is caught by set
difference (`C20`). A second model family holds one seat, fail-closed — exit 0 with empty stdout resolves
`unresolved`, which is the exact shape of Codex bug #19945 (`C22`). Before any of that runs, a
**deterministic embarrassment linter** catches placeholder text, `#` hrefs, unreplaced variables, alt text
equal to the filename, colours outside the palette (`C38`) — named precisely as hygiene, never reported as
taste.

**Moment it serves:** 07:10. The founder's twenty-five minutes must not be spent finding lorem ipsum.

## 09 · Control & safety

**One classifier, two axes.** `scripts/lib/classifier.js` gains `reach: internal · outbound-private ·
outbound-public · financial · human-contact` and the effective tier is the max of the code axis and the
reach axis (`R1`). A second implementation is refused by name — this repo has already paid to learn that
two implementations of risk disagree during the incident (`A5`). Anything with
`blast_radius: stranger|public` or `reversible: no` is **`blocking-human` by type, not by policy**, so no
mode, configuration or reasoning chain can clear it (`R3`); `gates.yml` already refuses to let a `human`
gate carry a `run:`, which is precisely the structural impossibility this needs. **Taint narrows the
grant**: while foreign content — a fetched page, an inbound email — is in a worker's context, outbound
hands become `blocking-human` automatically, as a property of the context and not a judgement about the
content (`H3`). The kill switch is **a file, checked at the top of every dispatch** (`RT3`), created with
`touch`, requiring no service that could be down. The **Cord** stops a running worker at its next durable
artifact — not the next token, not eventually (`S3`). Tool matching is on structured input or on resolved
executable path, never on the command string, because `( npx --version )` already defeats this repo's
blocking hook today (`H4`).

**Moment it serves:** 22:00, when the founder goes to bed and the answer to "what could it do to me
tonight" has to be a list, not a hope.

## 10 · Surfaces

**The Balcony has four faces and one log underneath** (`E5`). The **Table** (morning, taste, the round not
the pick). The **Deck** (decisions, reversibility first, every card fused). The **Wake** (voice, four
sections, barge-in). The **phone** (three verbs). Underneath, three renderers over one event log — founder,
operator, auditor — and a test that all three render from the same query, because CAST's cost figures come
from two pipelines that disagree with a third richer source never read.

**Explanation is a replay, not a generation** (`E1`): given a task id, reconstruct the chain from rows —
what was chosen, by what rule, at what cost, with what verdict, and what the founder did next. The renderer
joins rows and invents no connective tissue. **An explanation with a hole says so**: *"I cannot explain
step 3 — no row was written between the dispatch and the artifact"* (`E2`). Early on most explanations are
mostly holes; that is the surface reporting honestly that the instrumentation is thin, which is what you
want to know before you trust it. Q&A about the company's own behaviour must cite an event id, a file path
or a claim id, or answer *"I don't have a record of that"* (`E4`).

## 11 · Runtime

**The loop does not live on the laptop.** At full scale it is a small always-on host with a supervisor
(`launchd`'s `KeepAlive` is the local version; a pod is the durable one), and under it one loop per venture
with a sleep, each spawning `claude -p --allowedTools <pack grant>` workers. Dual-layer supervision: an
inner watchdog that kills a hung call and records it, an outer daemon that restarts a dead loop — **neither
supervises the other's failure, which is the point** — plus a circuit breaker on the outer one, because the
reference implementation this pattern comes from names a circuit breaker that exists nowhere in its code
(`S4`). Every move is **idempotent or declares `idempotent: false` and takes a lease that survives its
death**; the schema has no default, so the author must choose, and the dispatcher refuses to auto-restart a
non-idempotent move (`RT1`). The control plane still cannot spawn a shell — that ban stays at zero
exceptions, because it closed three RCEs. **Moment it serves:** 03:00, and the fact that the founder's
laptop being shut is not a fact about the company.

## 12 · Self-improvement

**Correction mining.** The highest-value signal in the company is the founder saying *"no, not like that"*,
emitted constantly and captured never. A ~50-line regex classifier over transcripts extracts candidates;
the founder confirms them at the Friday reckoning; confirmed ones bind (`M1`). Take the classifier, refuse
the loop around it. **Transcripts are instrumentation, not memory** (`M2`), with a positive control on
every extractor so format drift cannot silently take a number to zero. Every post-mortem ends in a row with
a **Mechanism** column where `none` is permitted and **counted**, so a class with three `none`s escalates
by count and not by memory (`SI1`). Patterns promote at three independent sightings, to *candidacy*, never
to an automatic write (`SI2`). And **nothing merges without a caller in the same diff** (`X1`) — not a plan
to wire it, the wire — because six of ten things the founder asked for already existed, connected to
nothing, and every existing cure is a detector that runs after the fact.

## 13 · Economics

**The Rope**: the loop stops itself at a declared fraction of the rolling window, and at the ceiling a
safelist still permits landing work — commit, push, check, PR, ledger writes (`EC4`). It never competes
with the founder for their own quota. A hard cost ceiling **downgrades the model rather than stopping the
work**, failing closed when a model has no catalogue price rather than scoring the spend at zero (`EC1`);
the model is recorded on the artifact, because a done-test passed on a downgraded model is a different
fact. **Cost per surviving artifact** is the yield metric (`EC2`), and every dispatch records `instead_of:`
— free when priority is computed — which reveals what the company systematically never gets to (`EC3`).
Portfolio shares carry a floor and a ceiling, reviewed on a cadence, because continuous reallocation is how
the loudest venture wins (`CY2`). And **10% of the window is reserved for work with no requested outcome**
— spent or lost, never banked, never asked to pay off (`C33`).

## 14 · The company itself

Venture intake produces three artifacts and stops (`CY1`). Wind-down is a protocol: stop dispatch, resolve
every open claim, write one dead-end record for the venture, archive with a stub — and **the global facts
stay global**, which is the strongest argument for Decision 9's split, because it means a failed venture
still contributes (`CY4`). A second human is a role with declared rights per decision *type*, exactly one
Accountable, and a `blocking-human` gate names **which** human (`CY3`). For one founder that field is
ceremony, and its value is making the exception visible on the day it arrives.

# 3 · WHAT COMPOUNDS

Seven stores. For each: what feeds it, what reads it, what it changes. **A store nothing reads is a store
that does not exist**, so the "read by" column is the one to audit.

### 1 · The Grain — this venture's taste

- **Fed by** every founder pick at 07:10 as a preference pair with one required sentence (`C21`); confirmed
  corrections mined from transcripts (`M1`); rubrics extracted from references (`C15`); annotations from
  the phone (`S1`).
- **Read by** every judge dispatch as few-shot calibration; every maker prompt as a rubric.
- **Changes** what gets made without the founder saying it again. In year one they say "warmer" six times;
  in year three they say it once and it is a rule with an expiry.
- **The compounding claim.** Taste is the most perishable thing in the company — it lives in the founder's
  head, is applied to one artifact per round, and evaporates at the session boundary. Making it a store is
  the single largest change in this document.

### 2 · The Book — how fields work, globally

- **Fed by** the bounded learning protocol (`K1`), wound-down ventures (`CY4`), the explore reservation.
  **Read by** ORIENT on every move in that field.
- **Changes** the cost of entering the fourth field versus the first. Decision 6 says agents research their
  way in; without the Book they research their way in *every time*.

### 3 · The Gallery — the quality-diversity archive

- **Fed by** every creative round, one elite per occupied cell, with the losers kept (`C2`, `C30`).
- **Read by** novelty scoring, recombination briefs, the anti-inbreeding slot, and the Table (which shows
  occupied cells greyed under tonight's candidates).
- **Changes** the *range* of what the company can make. The only store here that makes creative breadth
  accumulate rather than reset — a tournament returns one winner and throws away five, and the five contain
  the ideas you want in three weeks. **Its weakness:** descriptors that are both proxies for quality fill
  every cell with the same thing, and orthogonality is a judgement someone makes per artifact type.

### 4 · The Graveyard — what failed and why

- **Fed by** every abandoned move (the loop refuses to close `abandoned|failed` without a record) and every
  help request, whose required `tried:` list is written at the one moment a worker wants to write it (`N1`,
  `N3`). Where a command reproduces the failure it becomes a **negative claim** with an expiry, so the
  world moving under it forces *does this still fail?* on a schedule (`N2`).
- **Read by** the harness before ORIENT, injected as actual entries and not a summary (`C25`); and by the
  "we already tried that" tripwire, which refuses a third identical approach hash unless the dispatch
  states a named difference (`C24`).
- **Changes** how much of the night is spent re-walking a path. Fresh context is right for quality and
  wrong for memory; the resolution is to put the memory outside the context.

### 5 · The Register — what the world did

- **Fed by** everything that leaves the building, with a mandatory `check_on` date (`W3`).
- **Read by** trust promotion (`T1`), the bandit allocator across creative directions (`C34`), cost per
  surviving artifact (`EC2`), and the founder at 16:00.
- **Changes** whether autonomy can grow at all. **Almost everything downstream needs an outcome signal and
  there is none today.** Trust cannot be earned without it, so without the Register everything stays
  `supervised` forever and the founder is the bottleneck permanently.

### 6 · The trust matrix — derived, never stored

- **Fed by** nothing directly. **Recomputed at every dispatch** from the event log, so a hand-edited value
  is overwritten and a test asserts a tampered stored level does not change the effective one (`T1`).
- **Read by** the dispatcher, to decide whether an action needs a card. **Changes** the founder's
  interruption rate, which is the whole point.

### 7 · The attention record — what the founder actually did

**`NEW`, and it is the chair-native flywheel.** Every store above learns the founder's *taste*. This one
learns their *attention*.

- **Fed by** every card: was it answered or did it fuse, how fast, and did the founder later reverse the
  fuse. Every Table session: what was looked at, what was skipped. Every Wake: where they said "skip".
- **Read by** the card generator and the attention meter.
- **Changes** what reaches the Deck at all. **A card class the founder has let fuse eleven times running is
  a card class that should stop being a card** — the machine proposes retiring it at the Friday reckoning,
  with the eleven instances shown. Conversely a card class the founder always reverses after fusing is a
  class whose default is wrong, and that is a finding about the machine, not about the founder.
- **The failure to watch.** This store can quietly train the machine to stop asking about the things the
  founder is *avoiding* rather than the things they do not need. The guard: reversals are counted
  separately from non-answers, and a class with any reversal history can never be auto-retired.

# 4 · THE FOUNDER

## A Tuesday, as a budget

The founder's day is a **declared object the machine reads**, `DAY.yml`, and it is the most unusual thing
in this design. **`NEW`.**

```yaml
protected:
  - block: making
    from: "08:00"
    to:   "11:30"
    interruptions: 0
    exceptions: [outbound-refused-and-urgent, security-event]   # enumerated, closed set
  - block: sleep
    from: "22:30"
    to:   "06:30"
    interruptions: 0
    exceptions: [security-event]
budget:
  interruptions_per_day: 6
  questions_per_day: 3        # the machine MUST spend these
  chair_minutes_target: 70
windows:
  table:  "07:10"             # the taste pass
  deck:   "07:35"
  ask:    "14:00"
  world:  "16:00"
  order:  "17:30"
```

The loop reads it as a hard constraint and schedules against it: work whose output the founder must look at
is planned to **land before 07:10**, and work that will produce a card is planned so the card's fuse does
not fall inside a protected block.

The attention budget is metered exactly like the token window — a ceiling, a safelist of things that always
get through, and an override that demands a written reason and logs it with the numbers. The precedent is
`budget-guard.js`, which already does this for tokens, at 0.08s warm latency, and is registered nowhere.

## What they touch

- **Picks.** ~30 a week, each writing a preference pair. The highest-leverage act in the company.
- **Cards.** ~4 a day answered, ~7 fused. Only irreversible, financial and first-contact classes are
  structurally incapable of fusing.
- **The Night Order** (four minutes, purpose only), **the constraint** (one sentence a week), and **three
  answers a day**.
- **Making.** Three and a half hours on their own work, and the machine's only role is to stay out.

## What they never see

- Any priority *argument*. They see arithmetic and its inputs; disagreement means changing an input, and
  the flip point is computed: *"if the deadline moved out four days, goal B wins"* (`E3`).
- Any artifact that failed the embarrassment linter; any sketch that occupied no new gallery cell.
- The Book. Ever. If they have to read it, the knowledge layer has failed.
- Any row without a `say:` field, and any panel score, because there are none.

## How taste enters

Four doors, deliberately different in latency and in cost:

| Door | Latency | Cost to the founder | Where it lands |
|---|---|---|---|
| A pick between two candidates | instant | one click + one sentence | preference pair in the Grain |
| An annotation ("warmer") | never interrupts | five spoken words | the Grain, next artifact |
| A rubric approval | ~10 min, rare | reads an extracted rubric | the Grain, binding, mechanically checkable where numeric |
| A correction, mined | days | zero — it was already said | candidate → confirmed at Friday |

The rubric door is the one worth arguing for. Given references, a model copies them, and a copy teaches the
system nothing reusable. Instead the references feed an **extraction** step whose output is a written
rubric — *"type scale is 1.25 not 1.333; every section has exactly one accent; motion is 120–180ms and only
on enter"* — and **the rubric is what the founder approves**. The maker then works from the rubric and
never sees the references (`C15`). Taste enters once and enters as rules the company owns. The honest
limit: the extractable part of taste is the measurable part, and the measurable part is not the interesting
part. It gets you correct proportions, not a point of view. The point of view comes from the picks.

## How they steer

**Two verbs and a cord.** **Redirect** targets a task id, must be acknowledged, and changes the current
move; on the phone it is one tap on the row it applies to, so the target is implicit and cannot be
mistyped, and a redirect with no task id is refused. **Annotate** targets a field or a project, needs no
acknowledgement, and never interrupts. **The Cord** stops the line at the next durable artifact and reports
where it stopped so the state is resumable (`S3`) — distinct from the kill file, which stops *new* dispatch
and does not touch a running worker, and the two are always described as different switches so nobody
believes the file stops the world.

Latency is bounded by boundary polling: a worker reads `STEER.md` at every phase boundary and the **harness
performs the injection, not the agent**, so it cannot be skipped (`S2`). Inside a single long tool call
there is no boundary, which is why the Cord exists as well.

## What the machine may ask, and how

The question budget is three a day and **the machine is required to spend it**. A question must be:

1. **Blocking a specific judgement**, named. Not "what do you think of this?"
2. **Answerable in under a minute**, with the machine's own best guess stated so the founder can say "yes"
   rather than compose.
3. **Recorded as a rule with an expiry**, so it is never asked twice and never binds forever.

An unspent question budget is reported at the Friday reckoning as a finding about the machine, not as
efficiency. A machine that stopped asking is either done learning or has learned to guess, and the second
is far more likely.

## The week and the quarter

Shape as in §0. What matters here is that **both are report-driven and neither is a meeting**: Monday's
constraint is one sentence that becomes a claim with an expiry and the loop's dispatch filter (`P2`);
Friday's reckoning is a generated document the founder reads and disposes of, carrying the intervention
metric with both components, the taste read-back, the retirement queue, the waiver count, and the unspent
question budget. The quarter's four expiries — grants, taste pairs, portfolio shares, accepted risks — are
staggered at creation so they never all fire in one week.

## The hands with reach at 3am, and what makes each safe by construction

The phrase that matters is **by construction**. Not "the model was instructed to be careful" — a control
that a persuasive plan can talk its way past is not a control at 3am.

| Reach | Live at 3am? | What makes it safe |
|---|---|---|
| **Publish** (TikTok, blog, social) | produces the artifact, **never sends** | `blocking-human` by type on `blast_radius: public`. `gates.yml` refuses to let a human gate carry a `run:`, so there is no code path that clears it (`R3`) |
| **Contact a person** (email, DM) | drafts only | The named-human register is checked at the wrapper and **fails closed on an unparseable recipient** (`R6`). Reply-in-thread is a distinct, narrower permission; first contact always needs the founder |
| **Spend** (ads, GPU, credits) | up to a declared rate | Absolute per-hour and per-day ceilings at the wrapper, counted from the event log, not model-visible in a form it can argue with (`R5`). Overrides log the numbers |
| **Deploy** | behind a flag, yes | The flag is the kill switch for shipped work. The QA gate blocks on a verdict sha256-bound to this exact diff |
| **Inbound** (a webhook, an email arriving) | yes, and it wakes work | **Taint**: anything the world wrote is `foreign` provenance, and while it is in context every outbound hand becomes `blocking-human` automatically (`H3`) |
| **The founder's own Chrome** | **never at night** | It acts as the logged-in human. It is `irreversible` by any honest reading and is granted per-session, in the chair, with the founder present |
| **Remote code execution** (`sandbox_exec`) | not granted | A grant that exists in a file survives dispatch; the way to not have it at 3am is to not grant it |

Three of those rows describe capabilities that are **live and ungoverned on this machine today**:
`tiktok_publish`, Gmail `send_message`, and an authenticated Chrome. The current grant surface is not the
result of a risk decision; it is the result of which OAuth buttons happened to be clicked (`hands §5.2A`).

## What the night makes

- **Sketches into the Gallery** — cheap, short, structural, many; only the finalist gets finishing cost
  (`C30`), so volume goes up and nothing is thrown away.
- **Drafts into the outbox** — every outbound artifact on disk and inspectable before any send call.
- **Cards into the Deck** — case for, case against, and a fuse.
- **Field notes into the Book** (sourced, or `sourced: partial` with the gaps named) and **dead ends into
  the Graveyard** (the loop cannot close a failed move without one).
- **Register outcomes** — including `no data`, which is a real outcome and not a silence.

## What the night learns

- **Which judge seats were right.** Verdicts join to the founder's later disposition; a seat whose PASSes
  are systematically reworked loses its seat (`T3`).
- **Which approaches are circling.** A third identical approach hash is refused at dispatch, not nudged
  (`C24`).
- **Whether it is bored.** The stall ceiling detects *nothing being produced*; the boredom detector detects
  *the same thing being produced*, which reads as healthy progress — commits land, tokens burn, and the
  output is a monoculture (`C23`). Two failures, two detectors, gated on whether the done-test has been
  met, because converging toward a met done-test is good and converging without one is circling.

## What the night refuses to judge

It judges correctness against a deterministic oracle, hygiene against the embarrassment linter, and
relative merit through blind pairwise findings. It **refuses** to judge taste: where the Grain has no rule
covering the case, the night does not guess — it produces both, archives both, and spends one of its three
questions in the morning. Rule 10, applied to the one thing a machine genuinely cannot check.

# 6 · BEYOND THE MAP

Five things the fourteen territories do not contain. The first is the one this whole document is built on.

## 15 · The founder's attention as a governed resource

Territory 10 is *Surfaces* — where the founder looks. It is not *how much they can be asked, by whom, at
what cost, and what happens when the budget runs out.* Those are different questions and only the second
one is a constraint.

Everything in territory 10 assumes an unbounded founder. **`DAY.yml`, the attention meter, the interruption
budget, the question budget, the fuse on every card, and the attention record are territory 15.** They have
one thing in common: they treat the founder's day as a finite, meterable, spendable resource with the same
machinery as tokens — a ceiling, a safelist, a logged override.

The test of whether this territory exists: *can the machine answer "how much of the founder did I spend
this week, and on what?"* Today nothing can.

## 16 · Obligations — what the company owes

Missions are things the company **chose**. Claims are things that are **true**. Neither is a thing the
company **owes**: a reply promised to a customer, a refund due, an invoice, a deadline given to a partner,
a renewal.

An obligation has different physics from both. It has a counterparty, it does not expire when it becomes
inconvenient, and defaulting on one costs reputation rather than money. A machine company with a growing
outbound surface accumulates these fast, and today there is no store that would notice one going unmet. The
Register knows what left; it does not know what is *owed*.

## 17 · Identity — what the company has said, and may never say

The company speaks under a name, at volume, without a human reading most of it. That is a liability which
is neither taste nor risk-tier: **consistency with everything it has already said in public**, plus a list
of things it may never say — claims it cannot substantiate, comparisons it will not make. Not the Grain
(aesthetic) and not the reach ladder (about the act, not the content). It is a corpus of the company's own
public statements plus a refusal list, checked before publication, and it becomes load-bearing the first
time two ventures say contradictory things under one name.

## 18 · Cadence — the world's calendar, not the company's scheduler

Territory 11 covers *when the machine runs*. It does not cover *when things are due in the world*: a
seasonal window, a competitor's launch, a renewal date, the fact that nobody reads email on December 24th.
Priority is computed from declared fields and every one of them is internal. A company that cannot
represent "worth three times as much in nine days and nothing after that" ships good work at the wrong
time, systematically.

## 19 · The bus factor

The whole design has one human in it and no territory names what happens when that human is unreachable for
a month — ill, travelling, or simply not interested this quarter.

Three sub-questions, none of them answered anywhere: what does the machine do when **every** card fuses for
thirty days (it should notice and stop, not proceed); who can pull the Cord; and what a **handover file**
would have to contain for a second human to sit in the chair cold. Territory 14 mentions a second human as
an org-design question. This is a continuity question, and it is the one a machine company is most exposed
to.

# 7 · THE PATH BACKWARDS

*Part II of `STARTUP-OS.md` read at this point, and not before. What follows is written against it.*

## The headline, before the table

**The frame is a genuine slice on ten of fourteen territories, and merely small on exactly the four that
are chair-shaped.** That is a better result than I expected, and it is not a coincidence: no designer was
asked the chair question, so the four things missing are the four things only that question produces.

The frame is also *better than my picture* in three places and I adopt its version. **Star topology is
physics, not a grant** — *"a `claude -p` child has no address"* cannot be widened by an oversight, where my
withheld messaging hand can. **Staging is a stronger dry-run than mine**: `R4` with the second call
performed by a human is safer than a wrapper the machine could learn to chain. And **`EXPOSURES.yml`
belongs at the centre**, not at store five of seven — the outcome signal is the prerequisite for trust,
allocation and the intervention metric, so every other store is downstream of it.

The frame's §17 also states my §8's first assumption more bluntly than I did: *"the measured base rate of
this founder looking is zero."* A chair-first design has to answer that, not restate it.

## Month one, derived from the picture

The chair's month one has one goal: **make one good Tuesday morning happen, once, by day 7.** Not the
loop — the morning. Everything else is downstream and can wait.

That produces four changes to the frame's fourteen landings. Three are reorderings, one is a new field.
None is a new subsystem.

**A · `say:` moves from landing 13 to landing 3, alongside the task id.** For the same reason the task id
moves early: a row emitted without `say:` can never be spoken, and backfilling it is a rewrite of every
emitter, not a migration. The two fields are one landing. Cost: a column in `logEvent` and the lint that
already exists in the frame's §10.

**B · The briefing lands before the loop, not after it.** The frame builds the loop at landings 6–8 (days
6–10) and the balcony at landing 13 (days 17–19). From the chair that is backwards. Its own §17 says the
whole spine rests on the founder looking, and the cheapest test of that is to hand them something to look
at on **day 5**, built from the demand test, the grant census and two hand-run moves. If `BRIEFING.md` is
not opened twice unprompted 48 hours apart in its first week, the frame should shrink *before* days 6 to 19
are spent. The frame already declares that self-test; it just runs it two weeks too late to act on.

**C · Every founder-clearable block gains `default_if_unanswered:` and a `fused` disposition.** This is the
Deck's fuse, and it is one schema field plus one branch in `tick.mjs`. The frame's block already requires
`until:` and already forces one of three dispositions — but all three require the founder to act, so an
absent founder produces an accumulating queue, which is the empty Inbox in a new costume. Adding a stated
default that fires converts the founder's silence from an unread item into a recorded decision, and the
default is always the reversible branch. **This is the single highest-value addition in this section**,
because it is the direct mechanical answer to §17's weakness, and it costs almost nothing.

**D · `notify_per_day: 3` becomes an accounted budget, and the machine gains one question a day.** The
frame already caps interruptions; it does not report the spend. One line in `monthly.mjs` turns a cap into
a meter. And the return schema gains an optional `question:` field, of which `tick.mjs` surfaces at most
one per day into the briefing's NEEDS YOU section. That is the whole of my 14:00 conversation at year-one
scale: near-zero cost, and it is the only mechanism by which the machine's ignorance becomes visible rather
than being resolved by a guess.

Everything else in the frame's fourteen landings, I would build in the order given.

# 8 · WHAT WOULD HAVE TO BE TRUE

Ranked by how much of the picture collapses if the assumption is false. Each names how to find out.

### 1 · The founder will sit in the chair

**If false, nothing above survives.** Every store compounds through a founder act: pairs need picks, the
Grain needs confirmations, the Register needs a tap, the Deck needs answers, trust needs outcomes. A
machine designed around a chair nobody sits in is a machine with no selector. The evidence is bad — an
escalation Inbox empty on every project ever, seven balcony views of which one acts, eleven beeond mockups
made and none looked at. **Measurement, available in week one:** are day 8's three demand-test numbers
recorded without chasing, and is `BRIEFING.md` opened twice unprompted 48 hours apart? If both fail, shrink
to a machine that stages and stops — no register, no rungs, no cards — and say so.

### 2 · A `claude -p` argv actually narrows what a process can touch

**If false, every "safe by construction" claim in §5 is denial rather than absence**, and the night is
governed by a prompt. Measured true for `--disallowedTools` on 2026-09-02, which is the good half.
`--allowedTools` narrowing built-in *and* MCP tools under launchd, outside a session, is the load-bearing
half and is unmeasured. It is also the cheapest measurement in this document.

### 3 · Every row carries a real task id from the first commit

**Not retrofittable**, and the reference case is unambiguous: a system that omitted it needs a 60-second
time-window join to answer "what did this cost", and gets it wrong in exactly the cases you want to
examine. The Table, the Wake, the Deck, the Register, the attention meter and `explain.mjs` are six
projections of one log. Without the id they are six logs that disagree, and the disagreement is discovered
during an incident.

### 4 · Taste extracted from picks generalises into rules a maker can use

**If false, the Grain is a log rather than a flywheel** and the intervention metric never falls, because
the founder re-supplies the same judgement every week forever. The known limit is real and stated in §4:
the extractable part of taste is the measurable part, and the measurable part is not the interesting part.
**Measurement:** after 30 preference pairs, does a maker given the extracted rubric and no references
produce artifacts the founder picks at a higher rate than one given neither? At this volume that will be
suggestive, not significant, and should be reported as suggestive.

### 5 · An outcome signal is obtainable at one-founder volume

**The most likely to be false in a mild and corrosive way.** Trust promotion, bandit allocation, cost per
surviving artifact and the intervention metric all divide by "artifacts that survived the world's verdict."
If most register rows resolve `no data` forever, all four stay undefined, autonomy never grows, and the
founder is the permanent bottleneck — which is precisely the thing the company exists to avoid. Say in
advance that `unresolved` will vastly outnumber `pass`, and treat a run of consecutive `no data` as a
finding about the instrument rather than about the work.

### 6 · A metered attention budget is not simply overridden every day

If the override is exercised daily it is not a budget, it is a suggestion with paperwork. **Measurement:**
count overrides per week in `monthly.mjs` from day one. Three overrides a week means the budget is set
wrong; twenty means the mechanism is theatre and should be removed rather than tuned.

### 7 · The machine's questions are answerable in under a minute

A question budget the machine must spend is a good idea only if spending it is cheap for the founder. If
questions arrive as essays requiring context reconstruction, the founder stops answering, the budget goes
unspent, and the mechanism inverts into a nag. The guard is structural: a question must state the machine's
own best guess so the answer can be "yes."

### 8 · Behaviour descriptors orthogonal to quality can be written per artifact type

The Gallery is the only store that makes creative *range* accumulate, and it fails silently if its two axes
are both proxies for quality — every cell fills with the same thing and the archive is theatre. This is a
human judgement per artifact type and there is no mechanism for it. **Measurement:** cell occupancy
entropy, plus the frame's falsifier — zero founder promotions of a non-nominated cell within 30 days of the
first card means delete the archive and keep the distance function.

### 9 · A second model family is reachable from the harness

Now looks *more* likely than this repo has recorded in four places: `gemini` is installed, the harness runs
outside the sandbox, and the only thing that hid it was `~/.gemini` sitting in `denyRead`. If it holds,
three judge claims and the `risk: high` predicate stop being permanently unresolvable. If it does not, the
single-family risk stands and should keep its exit date rather than being quietly re-accepted.

### 10 · The night is worth more than it costs, and one human is an acceptable risk

Cost per surviving artifact must become *defined* and then must fall; reporting undefined as excellent is
the failure that metric exists to prevent. And the whole design has one person in it — §6/19 names it and
nothing in the picture addresses it. Almost certainly acceptable for several years, and it should be a
written acceptance with a review date rather than an omission.

## What a chair-first frame threw away

The rule at the top — every mechanism traceable to a moment — cut about a third of what I drafted. The
shape of that bias, so the founder can correct for it:

**Defensible cuts.** Forecast tournaments and Brier calibration (`P4`) and A/B on the system's own prompts
(`SI3`), both under-powered by an order of magnitude at this volume. Weighted voting among personas
(`A2`), an average in a costume. A policy handler registry — four controls need no fifth thing.

**A cut I am unsure of.** The thinking board. It produces a *document*, and a document is not a moment, so
my rule cut it — but the founder asked for one, it exists, and it has never convened. "It does not fit my
organising principle" is a weak reason to leave a built thing unwired. It belongs to whoever designs the
*making* of decisions; I was designing the *taking* of them.

**A cut I would reverse.** I kept star topology, allowed a pod as the exception, then used the pod nowhere.
Maker-and-editor iterating is a real quality pattern (`C29`) and a hub makes it slow and lossy. Nothing is
lost in year one, but the picture should say what the pod is *for*, and it does not.
