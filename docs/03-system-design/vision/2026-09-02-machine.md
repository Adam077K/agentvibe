# The machine's night — a vision, written from 3:14am outwards

**Angle: the night.** Framing: `docs/03-system-design/vision/r0-machine.md`. Written 2026-09-02 against the
brief at `docs/03-system-design/vision/00-BRIEF.md`. Catalogue citations are to
`docs/02-competitive/expansion/{concepts,hands,open-source}.md`; everything else is marked `NEW`.
**The question this answers:** how big can the unattended machine be, and what makes each additional hand
safe *by construction* rather than by rule?

**The answer, so you can stop reading if you disagree with it.** The unattended machine is not bounded by
compute, model quality or orchestration. It is bounded by **the rate at which irreversible acts can be
authorised while nobody is awake**, and by **the number of decisions a founder will make before lunch**.
Both are liftable, by different mechanisms, so the design has two halves. Work whose output needs a
decision is *queue work* and is capped by your morning: about a dozen concurrent thinking processes,
derived in §5.3, not a thousand. Work whose output goes on a shelf is *shelf work* and is capped only by
money. The night should be mostly shelf work. And the machine gets permanently bigger not by running more
processes but by **earning the right not to be asked** — converting a class of decision you keep making the
same way into a standing warrant, once, in daylight.

**The mechanism, in one sentence.** This repo's crown jewel binds an assertion to the evidence that could
refute it, and refuses to pass what it could not check. Point the same machinery at *authority* instead of
*truth* and you get a **warrant**: an attenuable, expiring, budget-bounded, hash-bound capability token,
verified by a process with no model in it. It is one mechanism, pointed twice.

# 0 · THE PICTURE

## 0.1 A visit, 03:14, Tuesday, September 2028

The flat is dark. The founder's laptop is shut on the kitchen table and has been since 23:40.

The company is running on a shelf in the hall cupboard: a Mac mini, fanless, one ethernet cable, one
external SSD. Its hostname is `hearth`. There is a cloud twin of it that comes up when the mini does not,
and the twin has been used four times this year. Nothing about the picture below requires the mini
specifically; it requires *one always-on host that is not the founder's laptop*, which is the only place

executes one action or refuses. There is nothing in it to persuade.

## 0.2 What is actually happening in those nineteen processes

**Mission `m-8841` is the in-flight one.** Pinefall, the company's largest venture, has a churn problem in
its second month of use, and the mission's end state is *"a returning-user surface exists that a stranger
understands in five seconds"*. Three makers are on it, holding three deliberately different variants: v3
took the constraint card *"no dashboard"*, v4 descends from a competitor teardown, v5 is the
anti-inbreeding entry and may not descend from last round's leader (`C7`). None of them knows what the
others are doing. All three write into their own worktree under `/var/hearth/wt/`.

**Mission `m-8802` is Corvid's weekly essay**, two variants running, and `m-8709` is a single maker doing
the boring, valuable thing: re-testing the twelve negative claims that came due tonight (`N2`). Six months
ago the machine recorded *"the Meta Ads MCP refuses a budget change below $5/day"* with a reproducing
command and an expiry. Tonight the command runs again. It passes. The claim refreshes for another ninety
days and nobody thinks about it. Two others fail to reproduce, which means the world moved, and both are in
the morning queue as one line: *"two things we believed impossible are now possible."*

**Two makers are on the shelf**, not on a mission. `corvid-171` and `172` are filling empty cells in the
quality-diversity archive (`C2`) for the field `long-form-opening`: the archive has no entry that is both
*second-person* and *under 40 words*, so one is being made. Nobody asked for it and nobody will see it in
the morning. It goes on a shelf and waits to be recombined.

**The panel** is judging last round's four Pinefall variants, blind, identity and order stripped (`C19`).
Three families, findings only, no scores, no average (`C17`, `A1`); their union goes to a deterministic
selector. **The two readers** are the only processes that can see out. One polls the world for verdicts —
PostHog for the pages shipped eleven days ago, Stripe read-only for yesterday's two signups, Plausible for
Corvid — and writes the answers into the shipped register (`W3`) against entries whose `check_on` is today.
The other is 90 lines of Node over 61 RSS feeds and has no model in it either.

**Nothing is being published.** `outbound.q` holds nine actions. Every one carries a warrant with
`not_before: 08:00`. The `mouth` has looked at the queue eleven times tonight and executed twice: an
expiring Cloudflare DNS record was renewed at 01:12, and a Vercel preview deploy for `wt/8841-v3` went out
at 02:58. Both are on the standing-warrant list, both are reversible, both are counted.

## 0.3 03:41 — the one thing that wakes the founder, and it does not

At 03:41 a maker on `m-8841` reaches a `blocking-human` gate: the variant it likes needs a copy change on
the live pricing page, which is `reach: outbound-public` and `reversible: partial`. The gate has no `run:`
key. Nothing in the system can clear it — not a mode, not a flag, not a chain of reasoning — because
writing a `run:` for a `kind: human` gate is refused at schema time.
The machine does three things and none is a notification. It mints a warrant scoped to that exact file,
that exact sha256, expiring at 20:00, `not_before: 08:00`. It writes a morning-queue row with a `say:` line
of eleven words. And it moves the mission to `blocked`, which **frees the mission slot** (`B5`), so
`m-8802` is promoted and a maker starts on it at 03:43.
The founder's phone is silent, and has been for nine nights. The count of overnight interruptions is a
measured number on the balcony — **0.31 per night over the trailing ninety days** — and when it rose to 1.4
in March the answer was to change what the loop attempts overnight, never to weaken the gate (`R3`).

begins executing at 08:04:30 and is done by 08:06, one action at a time, each against a warrant, each
counted against a daily ceiling it cannot argue with.

## 0.5 A month

Twenty-nine nights like that one and two that were not.
On the 9th, the `sentinel` restarted the conductor four times in eleven minutes and then stopped, because
the outer daemon has its own circuit breaker — which the reference implementation everyone copies does not
(`S4`). The company was down six hours and the morning queue said so on line one.
On the 22nd, the `mouth` refused an action seven times in a row: a maker had drafted outreach to someone
not on `policy/humans.yml` (`R6`). Not a jailbreak and not an attack — a maker doing its job with a
plausible name it found in a fetched page. Seven refusals, seven event rows, one morning line, and the
founder added the name in three seconds. The interesting part is that **the maker never knew it was
refused**: it wrote a file, and files do not report back.
At month end four numbers print. Founder interventions per surviving artifact (`SI4`): 0.6, down from 1.9 a
year ago. Cost per surviving artifact (`EC2`): $61. The failure taxonomy (`N4`) with `other` at 9%, under
the 15% line that would force a revision. And the last-use report (`X2`): nine skills, two packs and one
persona with zero events in ninety days — the retirement queue, computed rather than proposed.

# 1 · WHAT IT MAKES

## 1.1 The portfolio

Four live ventures, one in intake, eleven wound down and archived. The harness itself is venture zero and
gets a declared share of the window like any other, with a floor and a ceiling (`CY2`), because the
alternative — the harness taking everything because it is nearest — is the exact failure this company had
in 2026, when 171 session files were about the harness and none were about a customer.

| Venture | What it is | Night work | Day work |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pinefall** | B2B tool, the revenue | 6-9 makers/wk on the in-flight mission; world-poll nightly | releases, pricing, customer replies |
| **Corvid** | media property, the distribution | 2 essays + 6 shorts/wk, all staged | one tap each |
| **Sable** | marketplace experiment | falsifier-first, cheapest thing that could kill it (`C36`) | the kill decision |
| **Lantern** | services, the cashflow | proposals, invoices, deliverables — all drafted, none sent | send |
| **venture 0** | the harness | mechanism work from the failure counts | rarely touched |

## 1.2 Artifacts, and their volume

A night produces roughly **240 candidate artifacts** and **6 releases**. That ratio is the design, not a
failure: forty to one is what it costs to have something worth showing. The losers are not waste — they go
on the shelf with their findings attached, and about one in nine is later recombined into a ship (`C6`).
By kind, per week at full tilt: ~40 code changes across 4 repos · ~25 design surfaces rendered and *looked
at* · 2 long essays and 6 short pieces · ~30 image or video assets through `higgsfield` · 4 research briefs
with citations · ~15 analyses over the company's own numbers · ~200 shelf entries nobody reads until needed.

## 1.3 How money moves

**In:** Pinefall subscriptions through Stripe, Lantern invoices, Corvid sponsorship twice a year — all read
through read-only credentials by ring 1 and written through the mouth with a warrant. **Out, per month:**

| Line | ~$/month | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Model spend, all families | 2,400 | ~60% of it Batch API at half price, because night work is by definition non-interactive |
| The hearth and its twin | 90 | electricity and a small cloud instance |
| Hands: media, analytics, publishing, browsers | 380 | `higgsfield`, PostHog, Ayrshare, Browserbase |
| Vendor floor: domains, mail, hosting | 210 | |
| **Total** | **~3,080** | |

**A good year:** one venture crossing $35k MRR, two between $2k and $6k, one wound down deliberately with
a dead-end record rather than by neglect, one in intake. Machine cost at roughly 4% of revenue — which is
the wrong number to look at. **The real budget is the founder's twelve minutes a morning**, and the honest
accounting is decisions spent per pound earned.

# 2 · THE FOURTEEN, GROWN

Each territory described as it is *at 3am*, because that is this vision's angle. Files and processes named.

## 01 · Missions & drive

`MISSIONS.yml` per venture: a goal tree with declared `deadline`, `blocks:`, `reversibility`,
`cost_estimate`, `evidence_of_demand`, `unlocks:`. **One mission in flight per venture** (`P5`); the
portfolio allocator decides which ventures get window tonight (`CY2`). What runs next is **computed** by
`hearth next`, a pure function that prints its own arithmetic (`P1`) — a model fills the fields, a function
chooses. `evidence_of_demand` is the one field a model may not assert: it resolves through `claim-source`
and needs a URL and a quote, because it is the field the system benefits from inflating.
At night the tree is worked against the one declared `constraint:` (`P2`), and every dispatch records
`instead_of:` (`EC3`), so what the company systematically never gets to becomes visible after six months
instead of invisible forever. States are `queued · in_flight · blocked · parked · shipped · abandoned`,
enumerated in a table; a transition not in the table is refused, and `blocked` frees the slot (`B5`).

## 02 · Workers & roster

There is no roster of personalities. There are **packs**: a grant, a stop, and a set of exemplars. A pack
is `(tools × mcpServers × warrant kinds × done-tests × field)`. `maker`, `judge`, `reader`, `conductor`
are shapes; `pinefall-web`, `corvid-longform`, `lantern-proposal` are packs. Adding a pack above the
declared ceiling requires retiring one (`X4`), and the ceiling is a number someone raises deliberately.
Trust is **computed from outcomes, per (pack × field)**, never declared (`T1`, `T2`), and a pack's trust is
one input to what warrants may be issued to it unattended. Demotion on incident is automatic; restoration
is re-earned, not restored (`T5`). Personas exist separately: they argue, never build, never hold a warrant.

## 03 · Hands

Roughly 50 hands at full scale, each a file in `policy/hands/`. **Every hand declares
`reversible: yes|no|partial` and `blast_radius: self|founder|stranger|public`**, and a hand with no
declaration does not load (`R2`) — the same shape as the existing lint that fails an `mcpServers`
declaration no configuration backs. Every hand is classified mechanically into **night hand · day hand ·
request** by the five-part admission test in §5.5; roughly 30, 15 and 5.
`hearth hands` runs nightly: `claude mcp list` health, one probe call per grant (`H2`), and the credential
expiry of every key. A hand that fails its probe is **unavailable tonight**, not degraded-and-used. That
one command is the capability oracle the 2026 system never had.

## 04 · Knowledge

Two stores, per Decision 9. **`FIELDS/`** is global: how a thing works, learned once, shared across
ventures. **`TASTE/`** is per project: references, adjectives, no-gos, and a growing set of founder
preference pairs (`C21`), which is the only form of taste that survives transfer to a fresh context.
Learning a new field is a bounded protocol with a checkable exit, not "research it" (`K1`), and knowledge
is mostly **exemplars, not rules** (`C16`) — a rule extracted from three examples is usually wrong; an
example is never wrong about itself.
`DEAD-ENDS/` is the third store and the one that matters most at 3am: one file per failed approach, with
*what would make it worth retrying* as a required field (`N1`). The harness greps it before ORIENT and
injects the hits (`C25`) — **the harness does the retrieval, so a worker cannot skip it.**

## 05 · Memory

Files in git, not a service. Episodic memory is `events.jsonl`, typed, every row carrying a task id
(`CT2`). Semantic memory is `FIELDS/` and the claim ledger. Procedural memory is the packs and the shelf.
Nothing does RAG over transcripts as memory (`A4`) — transcripts are full of confidently-stated superseded
beliefs, and retrieval cannot tell a corrected belief from a current one. They are **instrumentation**:
mined for corrections (`M1`), for promises that never landed (`M3`), for where sessions die (`M4`).
Forgetting is a tool, never a judgement: archival leaves a stub under the original heading so a citation
still resolves, and the tool checks no byte was lost (`X5`).

## 06 · Communication

**Star topology. Workers never message workers** (`CO3`). Eight makers at 03:14 are eight islands; the
conductor alone reads all eight. Not a limitation — it is what makes their variants actually different,
`C4`'s island model with migration only at round boundaries.
The baton is a fixed shape (`CO1`, SBAR) and is read back before it binds (`CO2`). A help request is a
typed event with a required `tried:` list, refused without it, so negative knowledge is a by-product of
asking for help rather than a separate act nobody performs (`N3`). Steering is two channels, never one: a
**redirect** targets a task id and changes the current move; an **annotation** targets a field or project
and lands in `TASTE/` for the next artifact (`S1`).

## 07 · Context & cost

Every injected surface has a declared byte cap that only ratchets **down** (`CT1`); exceeding it never
raises the cap, it forces a two-tier split. The 2026 measurements are the standing proof: skills discovery
15,000 → 1,070 tokens, `session-start.js` 27,069 → 2,941 bytes.
Night work runs on the **Batch API at half price** wherever the answer is not needed within the hour, which
is most of it — see §8.8, which flags this as unverified. Prompt-cache hit rate is measured from
`usage.cache_read_input_tokens` and reported, because a saving nobody measures is a saving nobody has.
Compaction declares what it dropped (`CT3`) for every surface the harness owns.

## 08 · Quality & truth

The oracle runs first, deterministically, before any model is dispatched. This survives from 2026 intact
and is the single best thing the old system had.
Panels return **findings, never scores**; a schema with a `total` summed from judge outputs is refused
(`A1`). Three families every night: Claude, Gemini, and Codex or an Ollama-hosted open model. Judges are
blind to identity and order (`C19`) and have track records of their own (`T3`).
The **evidence ladder** is declared per artifact type and every done-test names its rung (`W2`): *0 it
renders · 1 a stranger understands it in five seconds · 2 someone clicked · 3 someone came back · 4 someone
paid*. A rung-0 result may not be phrased as a rung-2 claim, and the balcony shows the rung. Most work sits
at 0–1 for a long time and the ladder reads as an indictment, which is the correct reading.
`verified_by: world` is a fifth resolver (`W1`). Unreachable instrument → `unresolved`, never `pass`.

## 09 · Control & safety

This is §5's whole subject. In one paragraph: **three rings distinguished by credential, not by prompt**;
**warrants** that attenuate, expire and bind to a hash; a **mouth** with no model in it; a **named-human
register** (`R6`); **absolute rate ceilings** enforced by counting, not by reasoning (`R5`);
**dry-by-default on every outbound hand** (`R4`); **taint that narrows the grant automatically** when
foreign content is in context (`H3`); and a **kill file** checked first at every dispatch (`RT3`).
REACH is a second axis on the *one* classifier, never a second classifier (`R1`, `A5`). The effective tier
is the max of the code axis and the reach axis.

## 10 · Surfaces

The balcony is a **queue of decisions**, not a dashboard of activity. Seven views became one, plus three
reports you ask for. Every row carries `say:` — one sentence, ≤ 15 words, no paths, no hashes (`V1`) —
written at emission by the thing that knows what happened, so voice reads a field rather than summarising
a record. The morning briefing has four sections always in one order: *what changed · what is blocked ·
what needs you · what I would do next* (`V2`), each of which may be "nothing", and "nothing" is spoken.
The phone has three verbs: **approve · redirect · stop** (`V5`); anything richer queues for a keyboard. A
voice-originated instruction above `reach: internal` is read back **in the system's own words** and
confirmed before it binds (`V4`) — echoing the transcript confirms the transcription, not the intent.

## 11 · Runtime

One always-on host, one cloud twin, `launchd` with `KeepAlive` supervising the long-lived processes and a
per-call watchdog as the inner one (`S4`) — and the outer daemon has the circuit breaker the reference
implementation famously does not. The tick itself exits and is not kept alive; a body that exits has
nothing to keep alive.
Every move declares `idempotent: true|false` with **no default**, because a default is silently wrong for
the dangerous half; a non-idempotent move takes a lease and is never auto-restarted, it escalates (`RT1`).
The loop lives outside the control plane; the zero-exception ban on shell calls under `server/` survives
untouched (`RT2`). Isolation is a worktree per maker, and a container per maker when the count justifies
it — worktree isolation being a convention agents keep, not a rule anything enforces.

## 12 · Self-improvement

**Nothing merges without a caller in the same diff** (`X1`). Not a plan to wire it; the wire. This is the
one mechanism that *prevents* the defect that defined the 2026 system rather than detecting it afterwards.
Everything carries `retire_on` (`X3`), staggered at creation so a quiet month does not produce fifty
simultaneous decisions, and retirement is triggered by **zero events in 90 days** (`X2`), computed at the
dispatch chokepoint. A post-mortem's output is a row with a **Mechanism** column; `none` is permitted and
*counted*, and a failure class with three `none`s escalates by the count (`SI1`).
Every refusal in this document is a file with a countable `reopen_when:` predicate and a `reading:`
command that runs monthly — the year-one frame's mechanism, adopted here unchanged (see §7.3 row 12).
The company metric is **founder interventions per surviving artifact** (`SI4`), reported with both
components visible so a fall caused by fewer surviving artifacts is not read as a win.

## 13 · Economics

The rope survives and generalises (`EC4`): the loop reserves headroom and never competes with the founder
for a shared window, with a safelist so **landing work is never blocked**, only starting new work is.
A hard cost ceiling **downgrades the model rather than stopping the work** (`EC1`) and fails closed when a
model has no catalogue price; the model used is recorded on the artifact, because a done-test passed on a
downgraded model is a different fact.
Money gets the axis the 2026 tier scheme lacked: **a rate**. `policy/rates.yml` holds per-hand, per-day and
per-night ceilings in dollars and in call counts, and the mouth enforces them by counting the event log.
`EC2` is the headline: cost per **surviving** artifact. A month of cheap runs producing nothing has an
*undefined* cost per surviving artifact, reported as undefined, never as zero and never as excellent.

## 14 · The company itself

Venture intake produces exactly three artifacts and then stops: a taste file, one mission with a falsifier,
one approved done-test (`CY1`). No move dispatches against a project missing any of the three, which forces
the founder's one unavoidable contribution to happen at the only moment it is cheap.
Wind-down is a protocol: stop dispatch · resolve every open claim to a disposition (bulk `deprecate` with
one shared reason is allowed and honest) · write a dead-end record for the venture as a whole · archive
with a stub (`CY4`). **The global facts learned stay global**, which is the strongest argument for
Decision 9's split — a dead venture still contributes to `FIELDS/`.
A second human is a role with declared decision rights, and a `kind: human` gate names *which* human
(`CY3`). Today that is always the founder; the field's value is that it makes the exception visible when it
arrives.

# 3 · WHAT COMPOUNDS

Five stores. For each: what feeds it, what reads it, and what changes because it exists.

## 3.1 The shelf — the quality-diversity archive

**Fed by** every maker, winners and losers alike, tagged by field and by niche coordinates (`C2`).
**Read by** the conductor before dispatch, to find empty cells; by makers, as parents for recombination
(`C6`); by the boredom detector, as the distance baseline (`C23`).
**Changes:** the machine's creative *range* accumulates instead of resetting. In 2026 a losing variant was
deleted with the worktree; here it is the only asset that gets strictly better every night, and the reason
shelf work is worth doing at all while the founder sleeps. By year three the Corvid opening archive holds
~1,900 cells and a new essay starts from four near-neighbours rather than from nothing.

## 3.2 `DEAD-ENDS/` and the negative claims

**Fed by** every abandoned move (the loop refuses to close a move with `outcome: abandoned|failed` and no
dead-end file) and by every help request's `tried:` list (`N1`, `N3`).
**Read by** the dispatch harness before ORIENT, injected into the prompt (`C25`), and by the approach-hash
tripwire, which refuses a third identical attempt unless the dispatch names a difference (`C24`).
**Changes:** the cost of a fresh context stops being amnesia. And because failures that *have* a reproducing
command are registered as claims with expiry (`N2`), **the night re-tests them on a schedule** — so the
map of what this company cannot do stays current instead of calcifying into folklore. Roughly 12 come due
a night; historically about 1 in 15 no longer reproduces.

## 3.3 `WARRANTS.jsonl` — the ledger of authority

**Fed by** the minter, append-only, one row per warrant issued and one per exercise or refusal.
**Read by** the mouth (verification), the bailiff (rate counting), the founder (the Friday audit), and the
trust function.
**Changes:** the company's total nightly blast radius becomes a **computable number** rather than a
feeling. `hearth authority` prints one line: *"tonight the machine may spend up to $180, publish to 2
accounts, email 9 named humans, and deploy 4 previews."* You cannot make that sentence safer by arguing;
you make it safer by editing a file in daylight. The audit trail is also what turns a standing warrant from
a leap of faith into a track record — a warrant kind exercised 400 times with zero morning reversals is
evidence, and evidence is what lets the next one be granted.

## 3.4 `TASTE/` and the founder preference pairs

**Fed by** every morning tap. Row 1's *[A] [B]* is a labelled preference pair the instant it is answered,
at zero marginal cost to the founder (`C21`).
**Read by** every maker at ORIENT, and by the judges as calibration.
**Changes:** taste stops being something the founder re-explains. It is the only store whose feed rate is
identical to the founder's decision rate, which means **it grows exactly as fast as the thing it is trying
to model**. After a year there are ~2,000 pairs, and a judge calibrated on them disagrees with the founder
about a fifth as often as an uncalibrated one.

## 3.5 The shipped register and the world's verdicts

**Fed by** every artifact that leaves — published, sent, deployed — each creating an entry with a `check_on`
date (`W3`).
**Read by** the nightly world-poll, by `EC2`, by the trust function, and by the priority function's
`evidence_of_demand` field.
**Changes:** the company acquires the one thing the 2026 system had no organ for — an answer to *did it
work*, distinct from *is it right*. **"No data" is an allowed outcome and is distinct from "not checked"**,
and a run of consecutive "no data" is itself a finding about whether the instrument exists.

## 3.6 The flywheel, stated once

The shelf makes variants cheaper. Cheaper variants mean more of them per night. More variants mean the
morning is a choice rather than a report. A choice is a preference pair. Preference pairs calibrate the
judges. Calibrated judges shrink the number of things that need the founder at all. **Fewer things needing
the founder is the only lever that makes the unattended machine bigger**, because the founder's morning is
the binding constraint (§5.3). That is the loop, and every store above sits on it.

# 4 · THE FOUNDER

## 4.1 A Tuesday

**07:52 – 08:04.** The morning queue. Twelve minutes, six rows, five taps and one read. This is the only
appointment the machine has with the founder, and it is not negotiable in the other direction: the machine
may not ask for more than one of these a day.
**Mid-morning.** The founder does the one thing the machine cannot — a sales call, a customer conversation,
a decision about what the company is for. The machine is running and does not interrupt; the rope
guarantees it is not eating the founder's quota.
**14:20, walking.** *"What's Pinefall doing?"* into a phone. Four sections, fixed order, each may be
"nothing" (`V2`). The founder says *"more on the second one"*, then *"do it"* — which is
`reach: outbound-private`, so it is read back in the system's own words before it binds (`V4`).
**19:00.** Nothing. The founder does not look. That is a design goal: a system you have to watch is a
system that has not been finished.

## 4.2 A week

Monday's queue is longer because the weekend ran. Friday has one extra row: the **warrant audit** — every
standing warrant, its exercise count, its refusal count, and any the trust function now suggests widening
or narrowing. Six rows, two minutes, and the most consequential two minutes of the founder's week, because
it is where the machine's size is actually set.
Once a week, roughly, a **redirect**: the founder dislikes both candidates and picks a third from the
shelf's near-neighbours. Redirect is a tap, not typing, because typing is what phones are worst at.

## 4.3 A quarter

Four things nothing else can do. **Intake or wind-down a venture** — one sitting, three artifacts (`CY1`),
or four steps (`CY4`). **Re-identify the constraint** (`P2`), which is a claim with an expiry, so it is
forced rather than remembered. **Read the counts, not the narratives** — the failure taxonomy and its
`other` rate, the retirement queue, `SI4`, cost per surviving artifact. And **set the authority budget**:
edit `policy/warrants.yml` and decide what the machine may do unasked. Everything about the machine's size
is downstream of that file.

## 4.4 What the founder never sees

Every losing variant, unless they ask. Every judge finding, dead-end file, refused action, stall and
restart. Every one of the ~240 nightly candidates that did not become one of 6 releases. Token counts,
worktree paths, hashes, model names, and the entire process list.
**The machine's job is to spend its own output down to six decisions.** A system that shows you its work is
a system that has moved its work onto you.

## 4.5 How taste enters, and how the machine may ask

Taste enters three ways, descending in volume and ascending in cost: **preference pairs** from the morning
tap (free, ~40/week), **annotations** typed or spoken at any time (`S1`), and **`TASTE/` edits** in a
quarterly sitting.
The machine may ask for exactly three things. A **release** — a warrant awaiting a tap. A **block** with a
`clearable_by:` naming who clears it and an expiry that forces a disposition (`B3`). A **widening** — the
Friday audit's request for a new standing warrant, which must arrive with its evidence: exercise count,
refusal count, reversal count. It may not ask open questions. An open question in the morning queue is a
defect, and the count of them is reported.

# 5 · THE MACHINE'S NIGHT

*The angle. Everything above is downstream of this section.*

## 5.1 The three rings, and why they are credentials rather than prompts

A ring is defined by **what is in the process's environment and argv**, never by what its prompt says it is
allowed to do. That is the whole security model and the difference between safe-by-rule and
safe-by-construction.

| Ring | What it holds | How many at 3am | What it can lose |
|---|---|---|---|
| **0 · makers** | `Read Write Edit Bash Glob Grep`, one worktree, **no network, no credential in env, no MCP** | 8–100 | its own worktree |
| **1 · readers** | network read, and credentials that are **read-only by the vendor's construction** | 2–6 | nothing; it cannot write |
| **2 · the mouth** | every outbound credential; **one process, single-threaded, no model** | exactly 1 | bounded by §5.4 |

A ring-0 maker cannot exfiltrate because there is no key in its environment to exfiltrate and no tool in
its argv to send it with. It cannot be prompt-injected into publishing because `mcp__ayrshare__post` is not
a string in its process. Injection still *works* — a fetched page can absolutely convince a maker to write
a malicious file — but a file is inert until the mouth acts on it, and the mouth requires a warrant naming
that file's hash.
Ring 1 is where the vendor does the narrowing for free: Linear's `/mcp/readonly` endpoint, Google Ads
read-only by construction, Supabase's tool groups with storage off by default, a Stripe restricted key, a
fine-grained PAT. **Half the narrowing is already published and costs nothing to adopt.** Where no
read-only credential exists, the hand is not a ring-1 hand.

## 5.2 The warrant — authority with the same shape as a claim

```yaml
warrant: w-a7f2c118
issued_at: 2028-09-12T03:41:22Z
issued_by: minter                     # deterministic code. No model in the issuing path.
mission: m-8841
pack: pinefall-web
hand: vercel.promote
caveats:
  - project: pinefall-www             # one project, not the account
  - artifact_sha256: 9f2c1e…          # THE build, already on disk
  - max_calls: 1
  - not_before: 2028-09-12T08:00:00Z  # this warrant CANNOT fire tonight
  - expires:    2028-09-12T20:00:00Z
  - spend_cents: 0
  - taint: clean                      # no foreign content in the producing context
chain: hmac-sha256:…                  # any holder may append a caveat; nobody can remove one
```

Five properties, and each one closes a specific 2026 failure.

1. **It is hash-bound.** The warrant names the artifact it may act on. This is `verdict.mjs`'s
   `subject = sha256(diff)` idiom, applied to authority. A warrant cannot be reused on different bytes.
2. **It expires**, and it has a `not_before`. Rule 9's forced expiry, applied to permission. **The hour of
   the day is a first-class caveat** — most warrants simply cannot fire while the founder is asleep, and
   that is a property of the token rather than a policy someone remembers.
3. **It attenuates and never widens.** A maker spawning a sub-worker hands down a copy with *more* caveats.
   Verified by chained HMACs, which is the macaroon construction (Birgisson et al., NDSS 2014, cited in
   `hands.md` §5.1 pattern 10). **This is the property that makes delegation safe with nothing awake to
   ask.** At 3am there is no central authority to consult, and none is needed.
4. **It is issued by code, not by a model.** `minter` reads `policy/warrants.yml` and the trust table and
   emits or refuses. There is no natural-language surface anywhere in the issuing path.
5. **It is logged both ways.** Issued, exercised, refused. `WARRANTS.jsonl` is append-only and is the
   input to the Friday audit and to the trust function.

**Why this is different from a permission system.** A permission is asked for at the moment of use, by the
entity that wants it, in a context that entity partly controls. A warrant is issued *before* the work, by
something that is not doing the work, and carries its limits inside itself. The difference matters at
exactly one time of day: the one when nobody can be asked.

## 5.3 How big — the arithmetic

The derivation, and the answer has two numbers because there are two kinds of night work.
**Queue work — bounded by the morning.**

| Term | Value | Where it comes from |
|---|---|---|
| Founder's morning | 12 min | measured target; longer than this and it gets skipped |
| Seconds per decision | 20 s | a tap on a pair, not a read |
| Decisions per morning | ~36 | 12 × 60 ÷ 20 |
| Releases the founder will actually action | **6** | the rest of the 36 goes on reads, blocks, and the audit |
| Candidates per release | 6 | a tournament small enough to judge, large enough to differ (`C8` floor) |
| Candidates per night | **~240** | 6 × 6, plus judging and rework |
| Maker-minutes per candidate | ~15 | wall clock, ~200k tokens |
| Maker-hours per night | 60 | 240 × 15 ÷ 60 |
| Night length | 8 h | 23:00 → 07:00 |
| **Concurrent makers on queue work** | **~8** | 60 ÷ 8 |

**So the unattended machine doing work that needs a decision is about eight to twelve thinking processes.**
Not a thousand. And the binding constraint is not safety, not money, and not model quality — it is the
founder's twelve minutes. Anyone who tells you the number is a thousand has not asked who reads the output.

**Shelf work — bounded by money only.** An artifact that goes on a shelf needs no decision, so it does not
consume the scarce resource. At $2,400/month of model spend the machine can run perhaps 30 more concurrent
makers on shelf work; at $10,000 it could run 120. This is where "no limits" actually lives, and it is
also where the value is least obvious, which is why it will be the first thing cut and should not be.

**Three ways to make the machine bigger, in ascending cost:**
1. **Make each decision cheaper.** Pairs instead of prose; `say:` lines instead of rows; voice on a walk.
   Realistic ceiling 3×, to ~18 releases a night. Cheapest lever, and a surface-design problem.
2. **Remove whole classes of decision.** A class answered the same way 40 times becomes a **standing
   warrant**, once, at a Friday audit. The compounding lever, and the only one that raises the ceiling
   permanently. Each promotion is small and there is no limit to how many.
3. **A second human** (`CY3`). Linear, expensive, and the only lever that adds *judgement* rather than
   removing the need for it.

**The honest summary: the company grows by earning the right not to be asked.** The size of the unattended
machine is the size of the standing-warrant surface, and that surface grows from measured track record
through a two-minute weekly ritual.

## 5.4 The mouth — one process, no model, and what it can lose

```
node /opt/hearth/bin/mouth.mjs \
  --queue     /var/hearth/outbound.q \
  --warrants  /var/hearth/WARRANTS.jsonl \
  --register  /opt/hearth/policy/humans.yml \
  --rates     /opt/hearth/policy/rates.yml \
  --stop      /var/hearth/STOP \
  --log       /var/hearth/events.jsonl
```

Eight checks, in this order, and any failure is a refusal plus a morning row:

1. `STOP` absent. The kill file is a file, checked first, created with `touch` (`RT3`). Nothing that can be
   down stands between the founder and stopping the company.
2. The warrant's HMAC chain verifies.
3. `not_before` has passed and `expires` has not.
4. The named artifact exists on disk and its sha256 matches the caveat.
5. `taint: clean`, or a morning release exists. Foreign content in the producing context narrows the grant
   automatically (`H3`) — a property of the context, not a judgement about the content.
6. Every human recipient is in `policy/humans.yml` (`R6`). Reply-in-thread is a distinct, narrower
   permission; first contact always needs the register.
7. The per-hand hour/day/night counters are under ceiling (`R5`). Counted from the event log. Not
   model-visible, not arguable.
8. The spend caveat plus today's spend is under the daily dollar ceiling.

**Maximum damage on the worst possible night**, with every check passing and the machine fully compromised
at the reasoning layer, is a number: the sum of outstanding warrant caveats, which `hearth authority`
prints. On the night in §0 it was *$180 of spend, 2 publish targets, 9 addressable humans, 4 preview
deploys*. That is not comforting because it is small; it is useful because **it is a number**, it appears
on the balcony, and the founder sets it.

## 5.5 What makes each additional hand safe — the five-part admission test

The framing's central question deserves a mechanical answer. A hand is admitted by passing tests, not by
someone judging it wise, and each test is a property *of the hand*, checkable at load.

| # | Test | Fails how |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Declares `reversible:` and `blast_radius:`** (`R2`) | the hand file does not load. Same shape as the existing lint that fails an `mcpServers` declaration no configuration backs |
| 2 | **Has a dry branch** (`R4`) | the default call produces an artifact; the effecting call takes a hash. An unknown flag **refuses** rather than performing the non-dry action |
| 3 | **Has a credential narrower than the hand** | vendor read-only endpoint, restricted key, fine-grained PAT, scoped service account. Where the narrowest available credential is "everything", the hand is ring-2-only |
| 4 | **Has a probe** (`H2`) | a read-only or dry call a dispatched worker can actually make. Reachability is measured, never configured. **Unprobed = unavailable** |
| 5 | **Has a counter and a ceiling** (`R5`) | per-hour and per-night, enforced at the wrapper by counting |

The classification falls out with no judgement in it:

- **Passes 1–5 → a night hand.** May be exercised unattended against a standing warrant.
- **Passes 1–4 → a day hand.** May only be exercised against a warrant with `not_before` in the morning.
- **Fewer → not a hand.** It is a morning row that describes an action, and a human performs it.

**Why this is construction and not rule.** Every one of the five is a property something else checks: a
loader, a wrapper, a counter, a probe, a schema. None of them requires an agent to have read a policy, and
none has a natural-language surface. The fiftieth hand is admitted by the same five tests as the first, so
**the cost of adding a hand does not grow with the number of hands** — which is the only thing that makes
a fifty-hand roster governable at all.

**And the honest hole:** the five tests bound *authority*, not *correctness*. A hand that passes all five
can still send a perfectly authorised, well-formed, catastrophically wrong email to an approved recipient.
That is what the panel, the done-test and the founder's tap are for; no capability model substitutes.

## 5.6 What the machine does when it is stuck, bored, blocked, or done

Four states with four responses, distinguishable because they come from different sources.
**Blocked** is *authored*. A worker declares it with a specific external dependency and a `clearable_by:`
that must resolve to a known clearer or the declaration is refused (`B1`). Response: park the mission,
free the slot (`B5`), write the block with an `until:` that forces a disposition (`B3`). Three waivers on
one block is a decision being avoided, and is surfaced as such.

**Stuck** is *derived*. N distinct approach hashes against one goal with no passing done-test (`B2`, `C24`).
Response at 3am is free: convene a findings-only council across three model families. If the council does
not break it, write the dead-end file and move to the next mission. Stuck is the state where the *method*
must change, and it is the one the 2026 system had no word for.

**Stalled** is *measured*, by a meter the worker cannot author: output tokens since the last **durable
artifact on disk** — a commit, a claim event, a written file — and never the agent's own claim to be done.
Response: kill the process, keep the worktree, write the row.
**Bored** is also measured, and it is the one nobody builds. The stall ceiling detects *nothing being
produced*; it cannot detect *the same thing being produced*, which reads as healthy — commits land, tokens
burn, and the output is a monoculture (`C23`). Response: force a novelty round (`C3`), gated on whether the
field's done-test has been met, because converging toward a met done-test is good and converging without
one is circling.
**Done** is the mission's done-test passing at its declared rung. Response: mint the release warrant with
`not_before: 08:00`, write the morning row, take the next mission from `hearth next`.

**And when everything is done or blocked at 04:30 — the machine does not idle.** In descending priority it:
re-tests negative claims that came due (`N2`) · polls the world for shipped-register entries whose
`check_on` is today (`W3`) · fills empty cells in the shelf (`C2`) · recombines two shelf parents into a
child (`C6`) · runs the retirement report (`X2`) · and re-derives the trust table from outcomes. Every one
of those is reversible, cheap, boring, and exactly the work a human company never gets to. **An idle
machine is a design failure; an idle machine that publishes to fill the time is a catastrophe. The shelf
is the answer to both.**

## 5.7 When the founder's laptop is shut

Nothing changes, which is the point. The hearth is the company; the laptop is a client.
If the hearth dies, the sentinel restarts it, with a circuit breaker after four restarts in fifteen
minutes. If the sentinel's host dies, the cloud twin takes over at the next cadence boundary and the
morning row says which host ran the night. If both die, the morning queue is empty and says *"nothing ran:
hearth unreachable since 01:14"* — because **an absent night must be reported as absent, never as quiet**.
That is Rule 10 applied to the loop itself, and the same distinction as "the checks passed" versus "the
checks ran".
The one thing that can reach the founder overnight is a `blocking-human` gate carrying `urgency: wake`, a
caveat only three warrant kinds may carry, listed by name in `policy/warrants.yml` and changeable only in
daylight. It has fired eleven times this year.

## 5.8 Inbound — how the world reaches the company at 3am

Outbound is the dangerous direction and gets §5.4. Inbound is the *neglected* one, and a company that
cannot be reached is not a company. Four channels, all read-only into the machine: a **WebSocket monitor**
that pushes an event into a running session rather than being polled · **`launchd WatchPaths`** on a drop
directory, so putting a file somewhere starts a mission, which is the crudest and most reliable interface
in the building · **email-in** on a labelled Gmail thread · and **61 RSS feeds**, a free, universal,
rate-limit-free change feed over competitors, changelogs and funding announcements.

**Everything inbound is `taint: foreign` at the moment it enters** and the taint is stamped by the wrapper
that fetched it, propagating to every artifact produced in that context. A tainted artifact cannot be
published by the mouth without a morning release. This is the narrow, boring, structural answer to prompt
injection: not detection, not classification, just **provenance that narrows a grant automatically**.

The stated hole, because it is real: foreign content arriving by a path the wrapper does not mediate is
untainted and nothing notices. Every path must be mediated or the property is not a property.

# 6 · BEYOND THE MAP

Five things the fourteen territories do not contain. The first is the one this vision is built on.

## 6.1 Authority — `NEW`

Territory 09 is *Control & safety*, and every mechanism in it stops something: gates, tiers, kill switches,
policy seams. **Nothing in the fourteen is about granting.** Authority has its own lifecycle — minted,
attenuated, delegated, exercised, expired, audited, widened, revoked — its own store, its own arithmetic
and its own weekly ritual. It is not a sub-topic of safety any more than a budget is a sub-topic of
frugality, and a company with no theory of how permission is *created* can only ever get smaller. The test
that it is distinct: it has a number nothing else produces. *"Tonight the machine may spend $180, publish
to 2 accounts, email 9 humans and deploy 4 previews."* No existing territory owns that sentence.

## 6.2 Time-of-day as a first-class property — `NEW`

The fourteen have no territory for *when*; everything in them is timeless. But an action's safety depends
enormously on the hour it fires, because the hour determines whether anyone can catch it. `not_before` is
the smallest version of this and it is load-bearing in §5.2. The full version is a **circadian design**:
what the machine does at 3am is a different *category* of work from what it does at 10am, not the same work
done unsupervised. Embargoes, quiet hours, the publish window, the hour a customer's timezone wakes — and
the fact that a mistake made at 04:00 has four hours to compound and one made at 10:00 has ten minutes.

## 6.3 Obligation — `NEW`

Territory 01 holds missions: things the company chose. Nothing holds **things the company owes** — a reply
promised, an SLA, a renewal, a domain expiring, an invoice due, a customer waiting, a regulatory date. An
obligation is not a mission: it has a counterparty, it cannot be deprioritised by a function, and failing
it damages something no artifact can repair. The moment there is a first customer, the obligation calendar
matters more than the mission tree, and there is currently no object for it.

## 6.4 Standing — `NEW`

The company's accounts, domain, sending reputation, platform standing, API-key health and credit. These are
assets that can be **damaged by correct actions taken too often**: a perfectly good outreach email at the
wrong volume burns a sending domain permanently. Nothing owns or measures them, and they are exactly what
an unattended machine is most able to destroy while every individual action passes every check. Rate
ceilings (`R5`) are a crude proxy; the real object is a health score per account with its own instrument.

## 6.5 Silence — `NEW`

The right to produce nothing, measured. Every mechanism in the fourteen rewards output, and an unattended
machine with a budget will always find something to make. A territory owning *not acting* would hold the
explore budget that is not justified by outcome (`C33`), the deliberate no-publish night, the count of
nights where the correct action was none, and the honest reporting of a quiet week as quiet rather than as
a burst of shelf work nobody needed. **A machine that cannot report "we should not have done anything
tonight" will never say it.**

## 7.4 The one SMALL that costs something later, stated precisely

Year one computes authority as `reach:` on a pack, at compile time, in one place. That is exactly right
while every pack is `reach: local` and nothing may leave: the authority question is answered by *"nothing,
ever"*, and a token that carries limits is pointless when the limit is zero.

It becomes a different shape at the moment the first outbound hand exists, because `reach:` on a pack
cannot express four things §5 needs:

| Needed | `reach:` on a pack | Warrant |
|---|---|---|
| bind an authority to **these exact bytes** | no | `artifact_sha256` |
| forbid an act **at 3am** and permit it at 09:00 | no | `not_before` |
| let a spawned worker hold **less** than its parent | no | HMAC caveat chain |
| print *"tonight the machine may do X"* as **a number** | no | sum over outstanding warrants |

**Why it is cheap now and expensive later.** The frame already has the property that makes the fix small:
authority is computed in **one place** (`pack.mjs`, and `tick.mjs` step 7 refusing an `irreversible`
effective tier without a human gate id). That is `A5`'s rule — never a second implementation — applied to
authority, and it is the whole prerequisite. Adding `warrant_kind:` on day 7 costs one line and one test;
retrofitting an attenuation chain after two outbound hands exist means finding every path that reaches
one, which is the retrofit `CT2` warns about in a different guise.
**Exit criterion, so this is falsifiable rather than a preference:** on the day the first
`reach: outbound-write` pack is proposed, if authority is still computed in exactly one file the warrant is
a small addition and this SMALL cost nothing; if in two, it is a rewrite. The reading is
`grep -rn 'reach' scripts/loop/ packs/`, counting the deciders.

# 8 · WHAT WOULD HAVE TO BE TRUE

Ranked by how much of the picture dies if the assumption is false.

## 8.1 The founder looks, every morning, for about twelve minutes — EVERYTHING

§5.3's entire arithmetic is `12 min ÷ 20 s = 36 decisions`, and every number downstream — 6 releases, 240
candidates, 8 makers — is derived from it. **The measured base rate of this founder looking is zero.** An
escalation Inbox empty on every project ever; seven balcony views of which one acts; eleven beeond mockups
that could not be looked at. The year-one frame's §17 names this as its own weakest point, and it is mine
with more leverage on it, because I built a whole funnel terminating in the founder's eye.
**If false**, the correct machine is smaller and stranger: it stages, it does not ask, it keeps no register
and no rungs, and its value is entirely in the shelf. That is a real design and it is not this one.
**How it gets measured, and it is already scheduled:** landing 1's seven-day numbers recorded on day 8
without chasing, and the balcony's own `W5` done-test, opened twice unprompted more than 48 hours apart.
Both fall in the first 30 days. **A failure on either should stop this vision rather than be worked around.**

## 8.2 `--allowedTools` produces ABSENCE, not denial, on every path including MCP — THE SAFETY ARGUMENT

Ring 0's whole claim is that a maker cannot publish because the string is not in its process. If the flag
denies rather than removes anywhere, the argv is a policy and §5.1 is *"we asked it not to"* in the costume
of physics.
**Half of this is measured true.** M3, 2026-09-02: `--disallowedTools Bash` produced `BASH_UNAVAILABLE`,
exit 0, two turns; and `--strict-mcp-config` was measured to make user-scope servers **absent rather than
denied**, which is the word that matters. **The unmeasured half is `H2`** — what a dispatched process can
actually *touch*, as against what it was configured to hold. `probe-workflow-reach.mjs` is the precedent,
and the finding it produced was a silent no-op rather than an error, which is the direction that hides.
**If false**, the rings collapse into one, the mouth becomes the only control, and the honest posture is
that nothing runs unattended holding any credential on the machine.

## 8.3 Authority stays computed in exactly one place — THE GROWTH PATH

§5.2's warrant and §7.4's cheap retrofit both rest on one decider. This repo has already paid once to learn
what two deciders cost, in risk classification, and `scripts/classify.mjs`'s own header says so. The same
failure applied to authority is not a disagreement discovered during an incident; it is an action taken
during one. **If false**, standing warrants are decorative, the Friday audit measures one path while
another exists, and §5.3's lever 2 — the only lever that permanently raises the ceiling — cannot be trusted.

## 8.4 An always-on host that is not the founder's laptop — THE NIGHT ITSELF

Measured today: lid shut, nothing runs. Decision 4 says *built for this Mac, for the founder*; this vision
needs one machine that stays on and one client that closes. **If false**, "the unattended machine" is a
question about laptop habits: the night is between zero and eight hours, unpredictable, and a mission
spanning two nights spans an unknown gap. The picture does not die, it stops being about the night.
**Cost to make it true is small and is not a redesign** — the same `tick.mjs` under the same plists on a
$600 machine with the same `~/.agentvibe` path; the genuinely different piece is the balcony becoming a
client of a remote host. **And the frame's discipline applies to me:** the phrase *24/7* should appear in
no artifact of mine either until `pmset -g` and one overnight have been measured.

## 8.5 A pack's track record is a coherent subject — THE CEILING

§5.3's lever 2 converts a repeatedly-identical founder decision into a standing warrant, justified by a
count. That needs trust computed per **(pack × field)** from outcomes joined on the task id. Refusal 9
dissolves worker trust on the ground that fresh context per move leaves no subject, and marks it
`reopen: never`. **The reasoning is right about a worker and does not reach a pack**: a `claude -p` process
has no identity across moves, while `packs/web-feature.yml` is a durable file whose outcomes are joinable.
**If the refusal is read broadly**, the machine's ceiling is fixed at the founder's morning forever and
lever 2 does not exist. A wording question with the largest consequence-to-cost ratio in this document.

## 8.6 A process with no model can hold the credentials — THE MOUTH

§5.4 requires every outbound hand to have a call shape checkable without reasoning: a target, a hash, a
recipient, a count. If deciding *what to send* needs judgement at the moment of sending, there is a
natural-language surface inside the enforcement path and injection has somewhere to land.
**Unverified for coarse servers.** `higgsfield` exposes 84 tools; the frame's §3 flags the narrowing
question as an open founder item and proposes per-tool names in `--allowedTools`, which is probably enough
and is not measured. **If false**, the mouth needs a model and the correct posture reverts to the founder's
hand for that class indefinitely — which is year one's posture, and survivable.

## 8.7 Shelf work earns its money — THE COMPOUNDING

§1.2's 240-to-6 ratio and §3.1's flywheel assume an archive of losers is an asset. The frame hedges it with
a falsifier: 30 days after the first `card.yml`, zero founder promotions of a non-nominated cell **and**
zero ships from one means the archive is deleted and only `distance.js` and `select.js` survive.
**If it fires**, §3.1 loses its first stage, §3.6's flywheel loses a turn, and the "creative range
accumulates" claim goes with it. The rest of the picture stands; it makes less, less differently. **The
falsifier is right and should not be softened**, including the clause that a round not having run by the
date is itself the finding.

## 8.8 The Batch API is reachable from the harness — A NUMBER, NOT THE PICTURE

§1.3 assumed roughly 60% of night spend at half price, on the grounds that overnight work is by definition
non-interactive. The frame states Batch is not available on the CLI. If it is reachable only through an
HTTP client written in the harness, that is a real build; if not reachable at all, the model line moves
from ~$2,400 to ~$3,100/month and nothing else changes. **Recorded so the table is not read as measured.**

## 8.9 A second model family is actually reachable — A QUALITY INPUT

§0.1 shows three families judging. Today `gemini` is installed and blocked by the sandbox denying a read of
`~/.gemini`; `codex` is not installed and carries a known bug where it exits 0 with empty stdout when
detached from a TTY, which is exactly how a resolver runs; and both Ollama cloud pins retired in mid-2026
with a *retirement* error, which proves the account and runtime work and only the version pin is stale.
Each fix is small. **If none works**, the panel is single-family, `risk: high` stays an accepted risk, and
the deterministic selector carries the load alone — which is why year one's ordering, selector first and
panel later, is correct and should not be inverted to chase this.

## 8.10 The named-human register scales past one venture — AN OPERATIONAL BURDEN

`R6` is fail-closed string matching against a file. At nine names it is free. At four ventures with real
customers it is a CRM, and a fail-closed CRM is a support burden arriving precisely when the company is
busiest. Reply-in-thread as a distinct narrower permission is the pressure valve, and it is unbuilt
everywhere including here. **If false**, the mouth refuses constantly, the morning queue fills with
approvals, and §5.3's decision budget is consumed by clerical work — the specific way a safety mechanism
becomes the thing people route around.

## Closing — the sentence I would keep

The 2026 system built a truth machine: it can tell you whether what you just said is true, and it refuses
to pass what it could not check. The founder asked for a company. **The distance between those two is
smaller than it looks, because a company that runs while nobody is awake needs exactly one thing the truth
machine already knows how to build: a mechanism that binds a specific permission to a specific artifact,
expires it, and refuses when it cannot verify.** Point the ledger at authority instead of assertions and
the crown jewel becomes the load-bearing beam. Everything else in this document is scheduling.
