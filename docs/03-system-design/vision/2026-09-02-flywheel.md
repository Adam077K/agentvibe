## THE RECOMMENDATION, AND HOW HARD IT IS TO UNDO

**Reversibility first.** Almost everything below is reversible: a store you stop writing, a pack you retire,
a channel you abandon. Four things are not, and they are the only four that need a founder's signature
rather than a founder's opinion:

| Irreversible | Why `git revert` does not reach it | Who decides |
|---|---|---|
| The task id on every row | CAST could not answer "what did this cost" because the key was never there. Not retrofittable, at any price. | founder, day 3 |
| The exposure register existing at all | An exposure not recorded when it happened cannot be recorded later. The world moved on. | founder, day 1 |
| The company's name on a thing that reached a stranger | A published post, a sent letter, a spent dollar, a named human contacted | founder, forever |
| Owned distribution, started or not started | Audience has the longest lead time of any asset here. A year not spent building it is a year that cannot be bought back. | founder, month 1 |

**The recommendation.** Build the company as an **evidence engine with an audience attached**, not as a
harness with ventures attached. Concretely: make the exposure register the centre, and make the first
year's second job — after the loop exists at all — the acquisition of a channel the company owns, because
the evidence engine cannot spin without someone on the other end of it. Everything else in this document
is downstream of those two sentences.

**What I am arguing against.** Not the year-one frame, which is mostly right and mostly a slice (§7). I am
arguing against two of its specific refusals: that worker trust is "dissolved" and reopens *never*
(Part II §16 refusal 9), and that revenue is a number the founder types (Part II §13). Both foreclose the
mechanisms that take the founder out of the loop, which is the only question this angle was asked.

# 1 · WHAT IT MAKES

## The unit

The company makes **exposures**. An exposure is anything that left the building with the company's name on
it, and the register that holds them (`ventures/<slug>/EXPOSURES.yml`) is the centre of the company in the
same way a factory's centre is its shipping dock rather than its machine shop. Everything else — moves,
artifacts, checks, verdicts — is work in progress. An artifact that never becomes an exposure has produced
no information about the world and has not yet cost anything or earned anything.

This is not the same as "the company makes artifacts." It is deliberately stricter. Eleven mockups nobody
looked at is eleven artifacts and zero exposures, and the difference is the whole of why 2026 taught this
company nothing.

## The portfolio, October 2028

| venture | what it sells | started | ARR | founder hands per month |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `beeond` | subscription tool, $29/mo | 2026-10 | $1.14M | ~2h |
| `northfeed` | paid newsletter $9/mo + sponsorship | 2027-03 | $612k | ~4h (one recorded piece a week) |
| `cutting-room` | subscription, $19/mo | 2027-08 | $448k | ~1h |
| `bramble` | subscription, $49/mo | 2028-02 | $196k | ~2h |
| `arbor` | in validation — killed 2028-10-14 | 2028-09 | — | 40 min, total |

**Twenty-seven ventures have entered intake since 2026. Twenty-three are wound down.** The survival rate
is 15%, and that is a feature, not an embarrassment. The median wound-down venture consumed **$2,400** of
machine time and returned one field note and one dead-end. At that price the company can afford to be wrong
about a market nine times a year, which is the only way a one-founder company gets to be right about four.

## Money in

| line | 2028 |
|---|---|
| subscription revenue | $2,396,000 |
| sponsorship and one-offs | $184,000 |
| **total** | **$2,580,000** |

Revenue is not typed by a human. It is read from Stripe by `scripts/world/revenue.mjs` on a schedule, and
each month's figure enters the ledger as a claim with `verified_by: command` and a `valid_until`. This
matters more than it sounds: **revenue is the highest rung of the evidence ladder, and a rung-4 reading
that a human retyped is a rung-4 reading with a rung-1 provenance.** If the instrument is unreachable the
month reads `unresolved`, and `unresolved` months are counted.

## Money out

| line | 2028 | note |
|---|---|---|

Founder draw and tax sit outside this table. The number the machine is judged on is **cost per surviving
exposure**: $71 in 2028, $340 in 2027, `undefined` for the first four months of 2027 because a zero
denominator prints `undefined`, never a flattering low number.

## Cadence

| period | what happens |
|---|---|
| a night | 8–14 artifacts, 4–7 published, ~$60 spent, one sweep of everything due |
| a week | ~41 exposures: 18 social posts · 6 videos · 3 emails · 8 page or copy changes · 5 product changes · 1 experiment |
| a month | one venture-level decision · one monthly report · one reallocation across lanes · zero or one intake · zero or one wind-down |
| a quarter | one deliberate reopen of a standing refusal, or an explicit note that none fired |
| a year | ~9 intakes, ~8 wind-downs, 1–2 ventures reaching rung 4 |

## What a good year looks like

Not "revenue up". A good year is one where **founder minutes per week fell while exposures at rung ≥ 2
rose.** Those two moving in opposite directions is the only definition of "the founder stopped being the
bottleneck" that cannot be faked by working harder. The bad year that looks good is revenue up, exposures up,
founder minutes up — a services business with extra steps, and it does not compound.

# 2 · THE FOURTEEN, GROWN

## 01 · Missions & drive

A **forest**, not a tree: one `MISSIONS.yml` per venture, each an uncapped tree, each lane with exactly one
mission in flight. `next.mjs` is still pure and still prints its arithmetic, but its input is four lanes
rather than one leaf list, and it ranks across lanes on **cost of delay against a calendar** — because at
this scale the world has dates the company does not control (a sponsorship deadline, a seasonal algorithm
change, a competitor's launch, a VAT quarter). `P3`'s shape, with the honest caveat that both terms are
estimates and the estimator's error is recorded against actuals.

Allocation across lanes is `CY2`: a declared share of the day's capacity per venture, with a **floor** so a
quiet venture is not starved and a **ceiling** so a loud one cannot take everything, reviewed monthly rather
than continuously, because continuous reallocation is how the loudest wins.

Missions can be **woken by the world**. An inbound event — a customer reply, a failed payment, a funnel
spike, a competitor's changelog — creates a leaf with a deadline. This is the largest structural difference
from year one, where nothing outside the machine could start work.

`falsifier:` is still required and still cheap-first (`C36`); `none` is allowed, counted and reported.
`blocked` is authored, `stalled` is computed, `stuck` is counted (`B1`, `B2`), and a fourth state was added
by necessity: **`railed`** — the move is fine, the actor is fine, the day's rate limit is spent. It frees the
slot like `blocked` and clears on a clock rather than on a person.

## 02 · Workers & roster

Fourteen packs across five families: `web-feature` · `design-brand` · `content-copy` · `content-video` ·
`customer-market`, plus `orient` (read-only, the field learner) and `sweep` (the outcome resolver). Zero new
agent files, ever. A pack is still an argv, and the argv is still the policy seam, because a tool absent
from a process cannot be called by any reasoning, injection or error. Physics; it does not change with scale.

What changes is **how a pack's reach grows**, and here I part company with the year-one frame, which
dissolves worker trust on the ground that fresh context leaves no subject. That is right about *workers* and
wrong about *packs*: the pack is durable, versioned, and named on every row.

> **Reach is earned from the world, per (pack × field).** A pair moves from `staged` to `railed-autonomous`
> when N consecutive exposures of that class resolved at rung ≥ 2 with zero incidents, computed at dispatch
> from the exposure register, with no field to edit. `T1`'s recomputation, `T2`'s key, `T5`'s single counter
> running both directions — but the signal is the world's verdict, not an internal judge, which is what
> makes it unforgeable.

New packs run in **shadow** (`T4`): real moves, real artifacts, nothing published, compared to the incumbent,
one move in five. They graduate on a record. Packs retire on `X2` telemetry — zero dispatches in ninety days
— and retirement is archival with a stub (`X5`), never deletion. Concurrency: four lanes, one move per lane
per tick, fan-out inside a move for variation rounds. Workers never hold `Task` and there is no
worker-to-worker edge.

## 03 · Hands

**Four tiers, decided by which process holds the hand**, and the fourth is new:

| tier | who | examples |
|---|---|---|
| **H** — harness, outside the sandbox | `tick.mjs` and its scripts | `git` `gh` `ffmpeg` `sips` `say` `osascript` `caffeinate` `gemini` `codex`, the worktree, the archive and field writes |
| **W** — the pack's argv | a `claude -p` child | Read/Write/Edit/Glob/Grep/Bash where granted; `playwright`; nothing outbound |
| **R** — railed | the harness, after a meter check | publish to an **owned** channel · publish to a **rented** channel · spend · send to an opted-in list |
| **F** — the founder, forever | a human hand | anything irreversible by physics: a signature, a letter, a purchase of a durable asset, a legal statement, contact with a human not on the register, and the first message to any new counterparty |

Tier R is what year one does not have and what full scale cannot do without, because *the founder publishing
by hand caps the whole company at the founder's throughput* — the exact failure this angle was asked about.
Tier F exists because no amount of evidence should ever promote a machine across the physics line: **F is
not a trust level, it is a permanent reserve.** A pack with a thousand clean exposures still may not sign a
contract.

Servers, all narrow, all with a named caller in the same diff as their grant (`X1`): PostHog (the world's
verdict), Stripe read-only via a restricted key, Resend (send and *receive*), Ayrshare (thirteen networks on
one credential — therefore the most dangerous credential in the building, railed per platform), higgsfield
(media; `tiktok_publish` railed rather than refused), Firecrawl (eyes), n8n (inbound webhooks, the only path
the world has in). Refused permanently: `sandbox_exec`, `claude-in-chrome` in any pack argv, any registrar or
postage hand, any unbounded `stripe_api_write`. Daily `claude mcp list` → `hands.json` stays a report, not a
check; an unhealthy server a live pack grants is a balcony row at 08:00.

## 04 · Knowledge

Skills are **injected, never discovered**: `prompt.mjs` inlines the router index plus the pack's one namespace
under a byte cap, and the harness meters what was actually read (`X2`), so a skill nobody reads leaves on the
same schedule as a pack nobody dispatches.

`~/.agentvibe/fields/<slug>.md` holds about **180 field notes** by 2028 — how cold email works, how a YouTube
thumbnail works, how trial-to-paid moves, how VAT registration works in three jurisdictions. Global by design
(Decision 9), because *how a field works* is the same for every venture and *what this venture sounds like*
never is. Each note is three exemplars — good, bad, near-miss — with a source, rules subordinate to them
(`C16`), and **every fact a claim with an expiry**, so a note about a platform's algorithm rots on schedule
rather than quietly becoming folklore.

`ORIENT` is computed by the harness and prepended: matching dead-ends, attempts already made on this leaf,
archive cells occupied, the field's exemplars, one thinking model's stop rule. The maker's prompt carries no
rubric, no scoring axis and no reference URL (`C18`) — divergence and convergence separated in capability,
not merely in instruction. Constraint and far-transfer decks are drawn without replacement per variant
(`C9`, `C13`).

## 05 · Memory

| store | one rule | sole writer | reader |
|---|---|---|---|
| `MISSIONS.yml` ×4 | transitions from a table | `tick.mjs` (state), founder (intent) | `next.mjs` |
| `EXPOSURES.yml` ×4 | append-only, `check_on` required, written **before** the outbound call | `expose.mjs` | `sweep.mjs`, `claim-world`, briefing |
| `outcomes.jsonl` | one row per resolution; `no_data` ≠ `not_checked` ≠ `unresolved` | `sweep.mjs` | priors, trust, P&L, allocator |
| `AUDIENCE.yml` ×4 | channel, size, measured at a date, owned vs rented | `sweep.mjs` | the rung ceiling, the allocator |
| `RAILS.yml` | founder-only, `irreversible` tier | founder | `pack.mjs` at compile time |
| `TASTE.md` ×4 | founder only, ≤ 20 lines | founder | the MAKE prompt |
| `taste/PAIRS.jsonl` | only `promote.mjs` writes | `promote.mjs` | judge calibration, the archive falsifier |
| `fields/` | every fact a claim with `valid_until` | harness, from an `orient` return | `orient.mjs` |
| `dead-ends/` | never expires; `retry_if:` required | `tick.mjs` | `orient.mjs` |
| `archive/` | every candidate kept, rotated by volume, never deleted | `tick.mjs` | `orient.mjs`, balcony |
| `people.yml` | the named-human register; nobody is contacted who is not on it | founder | the `contact` rail |

Retrieval is by construction (the harness injects) and by `Grep`/`mdfind` over an append-only corpus. Still
no vector store. By 2028 there is one index — a DuckDB file over `outcomes.jsonl` and `EXPOSURES.yml`,
because prefix sums over four years stopped being instant — and it is an index, rebuildable from the files,
never a second source of truth. Conflict: newer wins, older superseded **in place** with its reason.
Forgetting is a tool, never a judgement call, and nothing is deleted to meet a cap.

## 06 · Communication

Still a **star**, and still for the reason of physics: a `claude -p` child has no address. Down is the
compiled prompt; up is JSON on stdout parsed into a fixed return. No worker messages a worker, at any scale.

One edge is added: **inbound**. `relay.mjs` holds the socket and the mailbox and does exactly one thing with
what arrives — it writes a leaf. **A received body never enters a producing context.** It enters an `orient`
argv with no Write, no Edit, no Bash and no outbound grant, which is the only structural answer to
injection-to-action that does not depend on a model behaving.

Handoffs are SBAR. Help is the return's `tried:` list, which doubles as the raw material for a dead-end.
Escalation is three rungs with maximum dwell times (`B4`) — retry, block, wake — and the third is capped at
three interruptions a day, inside the notification window only.

## 07 · Context & cost

Every row carries a real task id, `M-####.path#attempt`, from day three of year one, because a join key added
later is a join key that never existed. Cost per mission is a prefix sum; cost per venture, field and pack
are the same sum grouped differently.

The prompt is assembled in **cache order**: stable prefix first (pack preamble, router index, taste), varying
suffix last (board, steer, orient, task). The cache-read ratio is a monitored number, not an assumption,
because caching at ~0.1× input is worth tens of thousands a year at this volume and nothing proves it is
captured except `usage.cache_read_input_tokens`. Everything overnight goes through the Batch API at half
price, which is free money for a company whose whole premise is non-interactive work. Compaction is still
designed out: a process that would need it is recorded `truncated`, and `truncated` is never `done`.

## 08 · Quality & truth

**Oracle first, deterministic, per artifact type**, before any model is asked anything: code is the check
suite; a page is a screenshot at two viewports plus link resolution plus zero console errors; an image is
its pixel dimensions; a video is `ffprobe` streams and duration; copy is non-empty with resolving links.
Plus the embarrassment linter over the artifact — placeholders, TODOs, `#` hrefs, unreplaced template
variables, alt text equal to the filename, off-palette colour. Hygiene, never taste, never reported as
taste — and it converts "no placeholder UI" from a wish into a mechanism.

**The ladder is the spine of the whole company** (`W2`), declared per medium in `LADDERS.yml`: rung 0 *it
renders* (command) · rung 1 *a stranger understands it in five seconds* (human) · rung 2 *someone clicked*
(world) · rung 3 *someone came back* (world) · rung 4 *someone paid* (world). A claim's assertion string is
generated from its rung, so a rung-0 result is structurally incapable of being phrased as a rung-2 one.
**No judge, of any family, ever resolves a done-test.**

Judges exist and only ever produce findings. By 2028 the panel is multi-family, because that question was
settled by measurement rather than by repeating that it could not be. Selection is arithmetic over findings
(`C17`): eliminate any candidate with a P1, prefer fewest distinct P2s, tie-break on archive distance. There
is no `total` field anywhere and a test enforces its absence. Taste enters twice and never as a score:
`TASTE.md` before MAKE, the founder's promote afterwards.

## 09 · Control & safety

The seam is **argv absence plus one blocking hook**, and nothing has been added to it in three years,
because four controls did not need a fifth thing. Risk has **three axes**, not two:

| axis | question | mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| path | what does it touch on disk | `classifier.js`, one implementation, never paralleled |
| reach | how far does the act travel | `classifyGrant({paths, reach})`; `local · outbound-read · outbound-write · spends · speaks-as` |
| **rate** | how much, how often, in what window | **`RAILS.yml` + `meter.mjs`, checked at argv compile time** |

The rate axis is the one thing `hands.md` says no mechanism here can express, and without it no autonomous
outbound is defensible. A rail is per (venture × hand × day). `pack.mjs` refuses to compile an argv whose
hand would exceed its remaining rail, and **refuses when the meter is unreadable** — Rule 10, applied to
money. Kill switches, three, none in a prompt: unload the launch agent; `touch STOP`, reachable from a
phone; `stop:` in `STEER.md`, halting at the next durable artifact rather than mid-write. Residual risks are
written down and re-read monthly, because an unwritten accepted risk becomes an unremembered one.

## 10 · Surfaces

The balcony is a **list of goal-sized rows**, one line each, with a spoken form under fifteen words, and its
own done-test: opened twice unprompted, more than 48 hours apart. Four verbs, and their yields are not
equal, which is the design point:

| verb | what it leaves behind | yield |
|---|---|---|
| **promote** | a preference pair — two artifacts, a pick, a reason | **highest.** Founder attention converted into a reusable judge |
| **annotate** | a line in `TASTE.md` | high, and rare |
| **approve** | a disposition on one exposure | low. It gates, it does not teach |
| **redirect** | a steer on a named task | low, and a symptom — a high redirect rate is a brief defect, not a worker defect |

So the balcony **shows the round, not the pick** (`C37`): the nominated cell plus up to three others, ordered
by distance, each with a ≤ 60-word pitch. A founder choosing between three things produces an asset; a
founder approving one thing produces a signature. The briefing is a generated document with a fixed shape,
money first, and `say` reads its first two hundred words. `explain.mjs <task>` replays the chain from rows
and prints `[no record]` at a gap rather than bridging it with narrative.

## 11 · Runtime

The company left the laptop in 2027. It runs on a small always-on machine plus one cloud lane for burst, and
the Mac is a window. `tick.mjs` is unchanged by the move because it was always **crash-only**: read state
from disk, take one move, write, exit. Recovery is the next tick; there is nothing to keep alive and
therefore no restart loop to break. Models are pinned by id and chosen by job, never globally — Haiku for
probes, oracles and triage; Sonnet for MAKE; Opus for a genuinely novel field and for synthesis; a second
family from the harness for judge seats and nothing else. The model is recorded on the row, so a done-test
passed on a cheaper model is a different fact.

## 12 · Self-improvement

`monthly.mjs` is the only self-improvement mechanism and it is a **report, not a loop**, because every studied
system's self-improvement loop was prose that nothing checked ran. It prints: last-use per governed artifact
with a retirement queue; founder interventions per surviving exposure, paired with an engagement count so
disengagement cannot masquerade as autonomy; archive coverage and promotion rate; corrections mined from
transcripts by regex, not by a model, since a mechanical detector cannot decide something was unimportant;
and **every standing refusal with the command that reads its reopen trigger.**

That last one is what makes this whole document survivable. A refusal with a countable trigger and a monthly
reader is a decision that expires on schedule; a refusal without one is a permanent opinion in a mechanism's
clothes. Post-mortems end in a row with a Mechanism column, `none` allowed and counted, three `none`s against
one failure class escalating by the count. A/B on the company's own prompts stays refused at this volume
except for catching large regressions, because an underpowered comparison read as a result is worse than no
comparison.

## 13 · Economics

`LIMITS.yml` holds the rope and the clock brake; `RAILS.yml` holds the money. **The binding constraint changed
over three years and that change is worth naming**: in year one it was a rolling token window shared with the
founder, so the mechanism was a reserved fraction. By 2028 it is dollars, so the mechanism is a table of
rates. A design that hard-codes the window as *the* constraint gets rebuilt on the day billing changes.

`PL.md` per venture, monthly, machine-generated: model spend from `tasks.jsonl` · outbound spend from the
rails · **revenue read from Stripe as a claim** · exposures made · exposures at rung ≥ 2 · cost per surviving
exposure, `undefined` at a zero denominator · founder interventions per surviving exposure. Two numbers drive
allocation and neither can be gamed by working harder: **cost per surviving exposure** and **cost to
falsify**. A venture whose cost per surviving exposure rises for two quarters is a wind-down candidate
whether or not it is still growing.

## 14 · The company itself

**Intake** produces exactly three artifacts and stops: a taste file, one mission with a falsifier, one approved
done-test at a declared rung. The loop refuses to dispatch against a venture missing any. One sitting, about
forty minutes, machine-drafted and founder-edited — at nine intakes a year, under six hours annually for the
entire top of the funnel.

**Wind-down is a harvest, not a cleanup.** Stop dispatch · resolve every open claim to a disposition (bulk
`deprecate` with one shared reason is allowed and honest) · write one dead-end for the venture as a whole ·
archive with a stub. Field notes and archive cells **survive the venture** and become global, which is the
strongest argument for Decision 9's split: a dead venture still pays. A **second human** is a role with
declared decision rights, not a headcount — `RAILS.yml` and the human gates carry a `who:`, exactly one
accountable per decision type, today always the founder, and the field's value is that it makes the exception
visible on the day it arrives.

# 3 · WHAT COMPOUNDS

This is the section the angle was assigned for. A one-founder company fails when the founder is the
bottleneck and nothing accumulates. Below are the seven stores that accumulate, what feeds each, what reads
each, and what each *changes* — because a store nothing reads is a diary, not an asset.

## The seven wheels

### A · The evidence wheel — exposures and their outcomes

| | |
|---|---|
| **store** | `EXPOSURES.yml` per venture + `outcomes.jsonl` |
| **fed by** | `expose.mjs` writes a row **before** the outbound call; `sweep.mjs` resolves it on `check_on` |
| **read by** | field priors · pack-field reach · the allocator · `PL.md` · the briefing |
| **changes** | what the machine believes about the world, and how far each pack may reach |

This is the load-bearing wheel and everything else is geared to it (`W1`, `W3`). Two details carry most of
its value. First, **the row is written before the act, not after** (NEW): a crash between "posted" and
"recorded" loses an exposure permanently, and write-ahead makes the register a superset of reality rather
than a subset, so a row with no confirmed act is a visible anomaly instead of an invisible absence. Second,
**four terminal values, never three** — `pass` · `no_data` · `not_checked` · `unresolved`. Collapsing
`no_data` into `unresolved` is how registers die; collapsing `unresolved` into `pass` is how instruments lie.

### B · The audience wheel — owned reach

| | |
|---|---|
| **store** | `AUDIENCE.yml` per venture: channel, owned/rented, size, measured at a date |
| **fed by** | every exposure that acquires a subscriber, follower or address; `sweep.mjs` measures |
| **read by** | the rung ceiling; the allocator; the intake protocol |
| **changes** | **which evidence rungs are reachable at all** |

**This is the accelerator, and it is the thing no reference system has, because every reference system is a
harness and this is a business asset.** (NEW; the catalogue's 128 mechanisms contain no entry for it.)

The mechanism is simple and it is the most important sentence in this document:

> An exposure's reachable rung is bounded by the audience that can see it. Publish to nobody and the
> world's verdict is silence; silence resolves `no_data`; `no_data` teaches nothing; and the evidence wheel
> does not turn. **A company with no audience cannot learn, no matter how good its instruments are.**

So audience is not a marketing outcome, it is *epistemic infrastructure* — what converts an exposure from an
act into a measurement. It compounds harder than anything else here, because reach is roughly multiplicative
across exposures: each post that acquires subscribers raises the measurement quality of every future post in
every venture that shares the channel. It is also the asset with the **longest lead time**, which is why
§7's month-one derivation puts it second rather than fifth. Registered as `proposed:c-fly-rung-needs-reach`.

### C · The taste wheel — founder attention, converted

| | |
|---|---|
| **store** | `taste/PAIRS.jsonl` + `TASTE.md` |
| **fed by** | every `promote` at the balcony: two artifacts, the pick, one sentence of why |
| **read by** | judge calibration (few-shot before any panel), the MAKE prompt, the archive falsifier |
| **changes** | how often the founder must be asked at all |

`C21`, plus one rule of my own (NEW):

> **Every founder touch must leave a durable asset behind. A touch that leaves nothing is a leak.**

This reframes the balcony. `approve` gates one artifact and teaches nothing; `promote` costs the same seconds
and produces a reusable calibration example. So the surface is built to make choosing cheaper than approving,
and the company metric is not only interventions per surviving exposure (`SI4`) but **pairs written per
founder-hour**, which should rise while total founder hours fall. Pairs expire, because taste drifts: a pair
from eighteen months ago is a claim about a taste the founder may have moved past. Registered as
`proposed:c-fly-attention-converts`.

### D · The field wheel — knowledge that crosses ventures

| | |
|---|---|
| **store** | `~/.agentvibe/fields/*.md`, global by construction |
| **fed by** | `orient` moves, which have no Write and return JSON the harness writes |
| **read by** | every MAKE prompt, in every venture |
| **changes** | the quality of the first draft, before any iteration is spent |

**This is the portfolio effect and it is the entire reason to run four ventures instead of one.** Venture four
launches knowing what ventures one through three learned about landing pages, cold email, thumbnails and
pricing. The first draft is better, so fewer rounds are needed, so cost per surviving exposure falls — which
is exactly the curve on the flywheel page. It is also the most falsifiable claim here, and it already has a
date: `proposed:c-fly-fields-transfer`, resolved by counting field notes read by a task outside the venture that wrote
them. If that count is zero, `FIELDS/` is a private diary and the portfolio is four small companies sharing a
laptop.

### E · The graveyard wheel — what the dead ventures pay

| | |
|---|---|
| **store** | `dead-ends/` + the field notes and archive cells that survive a wind-down |
| **fed by** | `CY4`'s four-step wind-down, and the loop's refusal to close a failed move without a dead-end |
| **read by** | `orient.mjs` before every make; the intake protocol |
| **changes** | the size of the search space, and the price of the next bet |

Twenty-three wound-down ventures at a median of $2,400 is **$55,000 spent on being wrong**, and it is the
best money the company spent: it bought twenty-three markets' worth of evidence and a functioning appetite
for killing things. What makes it an asset rather than a loss is that the harvest is **compulsory** — the
loop refuses to close `failed|abandoned` without a dead-end carrying `retry_if:`, and the wind-down refuses
to complete with unresolved claims (`proposed:c-fly-kill-is-cheap`). One caution: dead ends **decay**. A dead end from
2026 may be alive in 2028 because the models got better, and `retry_if:` is what converts a prohibition into
a dated hypothesis.

### F · The free wheel — model progress, captured or lost

| | |
|---|---|
| **store** | *nothing.* This wheel is the absence of a store |
| **fed by** | the vendors, on their schedule, at no cost to the company |
| **read by** | every move |
| **changes** | everything, for free, if the company has not spent three years accumulating the wrong asset |

Here is the strategic claim, and it is the deepest answer this angle has to the founder's complaint about
playbooks (NEW):

> **Procedure is a depreciating asset. Evidence and taste are appreciating ones.** Every model release
> writes down the value of accumulated procedure — the step lists, the templates, the rubrics, the
> "how to do X in nine steps" — because the model now does that better unprompted than the procedure
> describes. The same release writes *up* the value of accumulated evidence and taste, because a better
> model does more with a good exemplar, a real outcome and a preference pair than a worse one did.

So the founder's instinct — *push back on super-specified packs; give them the tools, the way and the
understanding on how to learn* — is not a style preference. It is correct **asset allocation**. Three years
spent accumulating procedure is three years buying a depreciating asset with the most expensive input the
company has; three years spent accumulating exposures, outcomes, pairs and exemplars buys an appreciating one
and takes each model release as a free capability upgrade. The mechanism already exists and needs only a new
target: the schema predicate refusing `steps:`, `how:`, `method:` and `implementation:`, pointed at packs,
briefs and field notes alike. A pack is a **grant and a stop**, never a procedure
(`proposed:c-fly-procedure-depreciates`).

### G · The cash wheel — revenue that does not need hands

| | |
|---|---|
| **store** | Stripe, read as a claim |
| **fed by** | subscriptions, which renew without a founder |
| **read by** | `PL.md`, the allocator, the rails |
| **changes** | how many machine-hours the company can buy |

The only note worth making: **the revenue must not require the founder's hands each month.** A services
business's revenue is linear in founder hours and therefore cannot fund a flywheel — every extra dollar
costs an hour that could have been a decision. Subscription and sponsorship revenue funds machine-hours
without consuming attention, which is why the portfolio is shaped the way it is.

## The anti-flywheels

Things that compound *against* the company, listed because a wheel that only spins one way is a story:

| anti-wheel | what it accumulates | the brake |
|---|---|---|
| **Accumulated procedure** | step lists, templates, rubrics that a better model makes worse than useless | the `steps:`/`how:`/`method:` refusal, pointed at packs and fields |
| **Unresolved exposures** | rows past `check_on` with no disposition — the register rotting into a wish | a suite check that fails on a past-due row; runs of `no_data` reported as a finding |
| **Serially waived blocks** | decisions avoided, one legitimate waiver at a time | count waivers per block; three on one block is surfaced as "decision avoided" |
| **Unretired artifacts** | packs, skills, checks and instruments nobody calls | `X2` last-use, zero calls in 90 days, computed not proposed |
| **Reputational debt** | one bad exposure under the company's name, and the asymmetry that it destroys faster than it builds | the physics line; the rails; the embarrassment linter; and the fact that an exposure row exists before the act |
| **Founder attention spent on rework** | hours that produce no asset | the `promote`-over-`approve` bias; redirect rate reported as a brief defect |
| **A second implementation of anything that classifies risk** | disagreement discovered during an incident | one classifier, extended, never paralleled |

# 4 · THE FOUNDER

## A Tuesday

| time | what they touch |
|---|---|
| 07:05 | The briefing reads itself aloud while they make coffee. Money line first. They do not open the laptop. |
| 08:20 | The balcony, 26 minutes. Seven rows. Four promotions, one read, one block noted, one kill. |
| 08:46 | Closed. |
| 14:00 | An email arrives from a real person; they answer it themselves, because that is tier F and always will be. |
| 16:30 | They record one four-minute video for `northfeed`. It is the only artifact this week that is genuinely theirs, and it is the one the audience is actually there for. |

**Total: about 50 minutes of company, and one recording.**

## A week

Eleven hours. Six are decisions at the balcony across five mornings. Five are things only a human may do: the
recording, two customer calls, one signature, one conversation with an accountant. What they never see: the
1,340 moves that ran, the 190 oracle failures, the 61 retried leaves, the 400 archive cells filled, the 14
dead-ends, the 9 field notes updated, every `no_data` a sweep returned. All of it is on disk, all of it
replayable by task id, none of it delivered.

## A quarter

One sitting, about ninety minutes: read the flywheel page with three months side by side · reallocate the
lane shares within the floor and the ceiling · read the reopen-trigger list and decide anything that fired,
reopen or waive with a date · one intake, one wind-down, or explicitly neither · adjust `RAILS.yml` if a rail
has been at cap for a month. **Raising a rail is an act, recorded with a reason, never a drift.**

## How taste enters — three doors, and only three

**`TASTE.md`**, rarely: twenty lines per venture, founder-only, tiered irreversible, the door for *what this
venture is*. **`promote`**, constantly, at the balcony: the door for *which of these is better*, and the one
that compounds, because each pass writes a pair. **The founder's own hand on one artifact a week**, which is
a contribution and not a gate — a founder who never makes anything loses the ability to judge what is being
made for them, and a four-minute video is cheap insurance against that. Taste never enters as a score, never
as a rubric in a maker's prompt, and never as a judge's opinion standing in for the founder's.

## How they steer

Four instruments, in ascending violence: **annotate** (`TASTE.md`, applies to the next artifact, no
acknowledgement) · **redirect** (`steer.mjs <task>`, refused without a task id, changes the current move) ·
**the andon cord** (`stop:` in `STEER.md`, halting at the next durable artifact rather than the next token) ·
**STOP** (a file, reachable from a phone, checked before anything else; if the check errors, refuse).
Redirect and annotate are deliberately separate verbs, because bundling them produces a mechanism too heavy
for the second and too slow for the first (`S1`).

## What the machine may ask, and how

**One interrupt class, narrow by construction:** a founder-clearable block on a lane's in-flight mission,
inside 08:00–22:00, capped at three a day, delivered as a notification carrying the task id. Everything else
waits for the briefing. The machine may never ask the founder to choose a method, review a plan, confirm it
understood, or approve a step. It may ask for four things: a decision on a block, a choice between
candidates, a signature across the physics line, and a disposition on a fired reopen trigger.

# 5 · THE MACHINE'S NIGHT

## 03:14, and what is running

Nine Claude processes, four lane ticks, one relay, one sweep, two transcodes, one meter refresh. Total
concurrency peaks near twenty-four during a variation round and sits at six or seven the rest of the night.

## The hands with reach, and what makes each safe **by construction**

| hand | reach | what makes it safe at 3am with nobody awake |
|---|---|---|
| **publish to an owned channel** (own site, own list, own YouTube) | `outbound-write` | The channel is owned, so removal is possible and cheap. The artifact passed a deterministic oracle and the embarrassment linter. The rail caps uploads per day well under the platform's limit. **The exposure row is written before the call**, so a crash cannot produce an untracked act. |
| **publish to a rented channel** (a social platform, under the company's name) | `speaks-as` | All of the above, plus: the pack-field pair has earned `railed-autonomous` from N clean rung-2 outcomes, and any pack-field pair without that record stages instead. Removal is possible but reputation is not — hence the higher bar. |
| **spend** (paid distribution, media credits) | `spends` | `pack.mjs` reads `meter.mjs` at compile time and **refuses to build the argv** if the day's remaining rail is below this hand's declared maximum single spend. An unreadable meter is a refusal, never an assumption. The spend is bounded before any reasoning happens, so no reasoning can defeat it. |
| **send to a person** | `contact` | The argv holds no address book. `people.yml` is an allowlist the founder writes; a recipient not on it cannot be constructed. First contact with anyone new is tier F, forever. |
| **receive** (webhook, mailbox) | inbound | A received body enters only an `orient` argv: no Write, no Edit, no Bash, no outbound grant. Injection-to-action requires two hops and the second hop does not exist. |
| **sign, purchase, post a letter, make a legal statement** | — | **Not granted to any process, at any trust level, ever.** The physics line. |

The pattern across every row is the same and it is the only thing that scales: **safety comes from the
absence of a capability or the refusal of a compiler, never from an instruction and never from a judgement.**
A tool absent from an argv cannot be called by any reasoning, any injection or any error. A compiler that
refuses cannot be persuaded.

## What the night makes, learns, judges and refuses

**Makes:** eight to fourteen artifacts across four lanes — two or three short videos with thumbnails and
copy, one newsletter draft in three variants, four to six social posts, a page change or two, one product
change behind a flag, one experiment opened or resolved.

**Learns:** `sweep.mjs` at 03:00 resolves everything whose `check_on` came due, ten to twenty rows. Each
resolution updates the field priors for its (field × channel) pair and the reach record for its (pack ×
field) pair. About two thirds return a reading; the rest return `no_data` or `unresolved`, and that ratio is
itself watched — a rising `no_data` rate means the instruments are decaying, not that the work got worse.
`orient` moves, read-only, refresh two or three field notes a night against their expiries.

**Judges:** findings, never scores. Oracle, embarrassment linter, constraint-card checks, then `select.js`
eliminates any candidate with a P1, prefers fewest distinct P2s, and breaks ties on archive distance. Every
candidate is archived, one is nominated, three go to the balcony with their pitches so the founder can
overrule the selector — the single most informative event the company can receive, which is why the surface
is built around it. No model resolves a done-test at 3am or at any other hour.

**Refuses:** merging to `main` · editing `TASTE.md`, `RAILS.yml`, `LIMITS.yml`, mission intent, settings,
workflows or the launch agents · registering a grant · disabling the sandbox · contacting anyone not on
`people.yml` · anything across the physics line. Every one of those refusals is the absence of a tool or a
compiler check. None is a sentence in a prompt.

# 6 · BEYOND THE MAP

Seven things the fourteen territories do not contain. The first two are the ones that decide whether this
is a company or a workshop.

### 15 · Distribution and audience

The fourteen have **Hands** (can I post?) and **Quality** (is it good?) and nothing for *who hears it*. That
absence is why every mechanism in the catalogue is about production and none about reach, and it is the
largest hole in the map for a company whose learning loop depends on being seen. It would contain: owned
versus rented channels and their different risk; the reach-to-rung mapping; acquisition as a first-class
mission type rather than a side effect; platform rate limits as physics (LinkedIn ~100 calls/day/member,
Reddit 1 post per 10 minutes for a new account and $0.24 per 1,000 commercial calls), which kill a "post
everywhere continuously" design and leave a "post rarely and well, per platform" design standing; and the
asymmetry that a rented channel can be taken away overnight while an email list cannot.

### 16 · Money in

Territory 13 is called Economics and is entirely about **spend**. Nothing in the fourteen covers pricing,
offers, billing, dunning, churn, refunds or cohorts — which is to say, nothing covers the only rung-4 signal
the company has. It would contain: price as an artifact that can be varied and measured like any other;
revenue as an instrument reading rather than a typed number; churn as the harshest honest verdict available;
the difference between revenue that needs a founder's hands each month and revenue that does not.

### 17 · The company's name

Legal identity, entity, contracts, IP ownership of machine-made work, liability, privacy, tax. The thing
exposed on every exposure, and nothing in the fourteen governs it. Invisible at one venture, a weekly fact at
four across two jurisdictions, and the clearest instance of the physics line: none of it is undone by a
command.

### 18 · Named relationships as state

`people.yml` is an allowlist in my §2, but a relationship is not a boolean. A customer, partner, supplier or
sponsor each has a history, a consent state, a last-contact date, and a promise kept or not. A 24/7 machine
that can contact people without holding that will approach the same person twice about the same thing, which
is the fastest available way to spend reputational capital.

### 19 · The calendar

The fourteen have no concept of *when*. The world has seasons, launches, quarter ends, algorithm changes,
competitors' announcements and a VAT deadline, and a priority function that cannot see a date ranks a
time-critical move below an important one forever. The machinery is cheap — a dated events file and one term
in the ranking — and its absence is invisible until the quarter it costs.

### 20 · Provenance and disclosure

What a company must disclose about machine-made work is a live question in several jurisdictions and on
several platforms, differing by medium and market. More than compliance: **disclosure is an asset here.**
Being openly and precisely a one-founder company that runs on a machine is a position no incumbent can copy,
and it is available only to a company that recorded provenance from the beginning. Retrofitting it onto three
years of unlabelled exposures is not possible.

### 21 · Succession, and the machine as an asset

The fourteen assume the founder. A company whose durable knowledge lives in files rather than a head is
unusually **transferable**: the exposures, fields, pairs, dead-ends and rails *are* the company and would
still be under a different founder. That is the exit this architecture creates almost by accident, and the
bus-factor answer no amount of documentation gives a normal one-person business.

# 7 · THE PATH BACKWARDS

*Read `STARTUP-OS.md` Part II in full at this point. The month one below is derived from the picture above,
not from Part II's build order, and the table that follows compares them.*

## Month one, derived from the picture

The picture's minimum viable unit is **one full turn of the wheel**, at the smallest radius that still
turns. Not "build the loop" — the loop is a means. The measurement that matters on day 30 is not that the
machine ran; it is that **one exposure went out, one outcome came back at a named rung, one preference pair
was written, and one field note was read by something that did not write it.** Five artifacts. If month one
produces a working loop and none of those five, month one produced a workshop.

That gives a different emphasis, and exactly one different ordering decision, from Part II:

| | derived from the picture |
|---|---|
| **First** | Close the wheel by hand before building any of it. One real page at a real URL with analytics on it, posted once, with the pass threshold and the "uninformative" threshold **written down before it goes up**, and the numbers recorded on day 8 without chasing. This is row 1 of the register and the only reading from outside the system. *Part II has this as step 1 too, and it is the single most important agreement in the document.* |
| **Second — and this is the divergence** | **Start the channel.** Not a page: an *address*. Something a stranger can subscribe to, on infrastructure the company owns. Audience has the longest lead time of any asset in the picture, and everything at rung ≥ 2 is gated by it. Part II's build order puts distribution nowhere in the first 30 days and reaches its first real published artifact at step 14, days 20–30, published by the founder's hand. That is right about the *hand* and, by my reading, one order too late about the *channel*. A month spent building a loop that will publish into silence is a month of instrument-building whose instruments cannot read anything. |
| **Third** | The task id, on every row, before anything else is built. Not retrofittable at any price. Part II has this at step 3 and is right. |
| **Fourth** | The argv compiler and one pack. The seam before the hands. |
| **Fifth** | The register, the ladder, and the sweep — in that order, so the first exposure has somewhere to land and something to be measured against. |
| **Sixth** | The loop, the plists, the first unattended run in daylight with the founder present. |
| **Seventh** | The balcony with `promote` **before** `approve`, so the first founder touch writes a pair rather than a signature. Part II has both from step 13; my only note is on which one the surface is built around. |

Everything else Part II lists in its thirty days I would keep, in its order, for its stated reasons.

## Territory by territory: SLICE or SMALL?

*SLICE = the same shape, smaller; it grows into the picture without being replaced.
SMALL = a different shape; it would have to be replaced, whether or not that is the right year-one call.*

| # | territory | verdict | specifically |
|---|---|---|---|
| 01 | Missions & drive | **SLICE** (data) · **SMALL** (selector) | The tree, the id format, the state table and the falsifier all grow unchanged. `next.mjs` returning the leftmost leaf of the single in-flight mission is a different shape from ranking across four lanes against a calendar — but it is a *designed* replacement with a named trigger ("a second mission in flight"), and a small answer that names its own successor is a slice of the process even when it is not a slice of the shape. |
| 02 | Workers & roster | **SLICE** (packs) · **SMALL and consequential** (trust) | Packs-as-argv is the strongest slice in the frame; more packs is the only change. But refusal 9 dissolves worker trust with reopen `never`, on the ground that fresh context leaves no subject. **The subject is the pack, not the worker, and the pack is durable.** This is the one refusal I would argue with, because evidence-earned reach is the mechanism that takes the founder out of the publishing loop, and `never` forecloses it. Recommend: change the reopen trigger to *"the founder has approved 20 exposures of one pack-field pair with zero rejections"* — a countable predicate, not an opinion. |
| 03 | Hands | **SLICE**, with a fast trigger | Tiers H/W/F, the argv, and stage-only are all the right shape; tier R (railed) is the addition. The reopen — "the founder performs the same outbound act by hand 3×" — is a good trigger and will fire quickly. Worth stating plainly: **while the founder publishes by hand, the exposure register grows at the founder's rate, which caps the entire flywheel at founder throughput.** That is acceptable for thirty days and fatal for a year. |
| 04 | Knowledge | **SLICE** | Injection under a byte cap, metering, the exemplar triad, fields global with expiries, ORIENT computed by the harness. All of it grows without changing shape. `FIELDS/`'s freeze falsifier is exactly the right instrument. |
| 05 | Memory | **SLICE** | One writer per store, append-only, greppable, nothing deleted to meet a cap. The only full-scale addition is a rebuildable index over the same files, which is an optimisation and not a second source of truth. |
| 06 | Communication | **SLICE**, and permanent | The star is physics. The only edge ever added is inbound, and Part II's rule (a fetched body enters only a no-Write argv) is already the right rule for it. |
| 07 | Context & cost | **SLICE**, and the most important one | The task id on every row from day three is the decision that cannot be made later. Cache order, prefix sums, `undefined` never zero: all correct at any scale. Batch API is the only line I would add. |
| 08 | Quality & truth | **SLICE** | Oracle first, ladder 0–4, rung generated into the assertion, no judge resolving a done-test, `unresolved` distinct from `pass`, no `total` anywhere. The panel is deliberately withheld with a named reopen, which is the correct way to withhold something. |
| 09 | Control & safety | **SLICE** (path, reach) · **SMALL** (rate) | Two axes are the right two axes and one implementation is the right number. But `usd_per_day: null` in `LIMITS.yml` is a single global scalar, and the shape the picture needs is a table keyed on (venture × hand × day) with a meter read at compile time. A scalar is the degenerate case of a table, so this is cheap to grow — provided the refusal-while-null stays, which is what makes the year-one version honest rather than absent. |
| 10 | Surfaces | **SLICE**, with one emphasis note | Four verbs, goal-sized rows, `say`, the balcony's own done-test: all correct. My note is the ratio, not the set. `approve` gates and teaches nothing; `promote` costs the same seconds and writes a pair. Build the surface around the second. Part II §17's weakest-point argument — that the human gate rests on a founder whose measured base rate of looking is zero — is answered better by making looking *productive* than by making it mandatory. |
| 11 | Runtime | **SLICE** | Crash-only, exits, recovery is the next tick, no `KeepAlive`. That design is portable off the laptop unchanged, which is exactly what a slice means. | 12 | Self-improvement | **SLICE**, and the mechanism that makes everything else a slice | The monthly report reading every refusal's countable reopen trigger is what stops year one's small answers from becoming permanent opinions. Nothing else in the frame does as much work per line. |

# 8 · WHAT WOULD HAVE TO BE TRUE

Ranked by how much of the picture collapses if the assumption is false. Each names how it would be
measured, because an assumption with no test is a hope.

### 1 · The founder looks, and looking is cheap enough to keep doing

**If false, everything collapses.** The conversion-of-attention thesis, the taste wheel, the promote verb, the
four-verb balcony and the human rungs all rest on a founder who spends twenty-six minutes a day on it.
Part II §17 names the measured base rate honestly and it is zero: an escalation inbox empty on every project
ever, seven mission-control views of which one acts, eleven mockups unlooked at. **Test:** does the day-8
reading of the day-1 demand test get recorded without anyone chasing it? That single event is the assumption
in miniature. If it does not happen, the answer is not more instruments — it is to shrink the company to
**deterministic done-tests only**, which means code and hygiene, and to accept that brand, copy and video are
outside what this machine can do.

### 2 · The world answers

If exposures reach nobody, every outcome is `no_data`, no priors form, reach never earns, the allocator has no
input and the flywheel page shows six `undefined` rows. Trust, allocation, cost per surviving exposure and
every bandit downstream all have an outcome signal as their prerequisite. **Test:** the ratio of readings to
`no_data` over the first fifty exposures. A run of consecutive `no_data` is itself the finding, and it is a
finding about audience, not about quality.

### 3 · Owned distribution is buildable by a machine at all

The audience wheel assumes reach can be acquired without a human doing it by hand, and platforms disagree at
different rates: LinkedIn caps at roughly 100 calls per day per member; Reddit is one post per ten minutes for
a new account, with karma and age rules and $0.24 per 1,000 calls once commercial; several platforms restrict
automated posting outright. **If false**, the accelerator is capped and the company is a slower thing —
probably SEO and email rather than multi-channel. Not fatal; it changes the numbers by an order of magnitude.
**Test:** one channel, ninety days, machine-published inside the stated limits, measured for throttling and
account health.

### 4 · The argv actually narrows the child

Every safety claim in §5 reduces to this. Two legs are measured: `--disallowedTools` narrows built-in tools in
a `claude -p` child (2026-09-02, `BASH_UNAVAILABLE`, exit 0), and the blocking hook fires for `Write` under
`claude -p` (2026-09-02). Unmeasured: the Bash path under launchd, and MCP narrowing under
`--strict-mcp-config` from a scheduled child. **If false**, the safety story is *denial*, not *absence*, and
tier R must wait for something genuinely absent rather than merely refused.

### 5 · A rate can be enforced *before* the spend

`RAILS.yml` is worthless if the meter is readable only after the money is gone. Some vendors expose a balance
in-band (higgsfield's `balance`, Stripe's balance summary); ad platforms generally do not expose today's spend
with useful latency. **If false**, autonomous spend is off the table and paid distribution stays tier F
forever, removing about a quarter of the picture's reach and most of its speed. **Test:** for each spending
hand, the latency between a spend and its appearance on the meter. Anything above an hour is not a rail.

### 6 · Field knowledge transfers across ventures

The portfolio effect is the whole reason to run four ventures rather than one, and it is entirely this
assumption. Already falsifiable and already dated: `X2` shows a field note read by a task outside the mission
that wrote it, by 2026-12-02. **If false**, `FIELDS/` is a diary, the four ventures are four small companies
sharing a laptop, and the right move is to concentrate rather than diversify (`proposed:c-fly-fields-transfer`).

### 7 · Taste is extractable, stable enough to encode, and improves a judge

Pairs are worth writing only if a judge calibrated on them behaves measurably more like the founder. Two ways
it fails: the one-sentence "why" is a rationalisation nothing can check, and taste drifts faster than pairs
accumulate. **Test:** hold out twenty pairs and ask whether a panel given the rest predicts the held-out picks
better than a panel given none. Below a clear margin, pairs are documentation rather than calibration — still
useful, not a flywheel.

### 8 · Model progress continues, and the company is positioned to capture it

The free wheel assumes each release makes accumulated evidence and taste more valuable and accumulated
procedure less. If models plateau, procedure stops depreciating and the rule loses its sharpest argument,
though it stays right for the founder's original reason: a playbook produces the median of the distribution.
**Test:** on each release, re-run twenty archived tasks unchanged and compare oracle pass rates and founder
promotion rates. Cheap, honest, repeatable, and nothing in the catalogue proposes it.

### 9 · The evidence ladder stays honest under pressure

Nobody, human or machine, describes a rung-0 result in rung-2 language. Generating the assertion string from
the rung is the mechanism; the pressure to inflate is strongest exactly when the numbers are worst.
**Test:** count claims whose asserted rung exceeds what their resolver kind can establish. Zero, checked.

### 10 · Sample sizes ever reach significance

Most readings at this volume will be underpowered, and acting on a point estimate below the minimum
detectable effect launders a guess as evidence. **Test:** a power calculation in the resolver; below
threshold, `unresolved`, and count how often. If that is nearly always, the ladder's top three rungs are
aspirational and the company should say so out loud.

### 11 · The runtime holds, and leaving the laptop is affordable

Lid shut, nothing runs. The always-on runtime assumed in §2 costs real money before the revenue justifies
it. **Test:** `pmset -g`, one measured overnight, and a costed comparison — and no artifact says "24/7"
until both are done.

### 12 · Nothing under the company's name blows up

One bad exposure — a false claim, an offensive generation, a second approach to the same person, an
undisclosed machine-made endorsement — destroys reputational capital faster than three years of good ones
build it, and the embarrassment linter catches hygiene rather than judgement. **There is no good forward
test**, which is exactly why this is handled by the physics line and the rails rather than by measurement.
It is the assumption I would most want a second pair of eyes on before any tier-R hand is granted.

## Where this vision is weakest

Three places, named so a reader does not have to find them.

**One.** The audience wheel is the load-bearing new idea and the least mechanised thing here. `AUDIENCE.yml`
and the reach-to-rung ceiling are `NEW`, unbuilt, and rest on a claim that is obvious at the extreme and
unquantified in the middle. I do not know what audience size makes rung 2 reliably attainable for a given
medium, and neither does anything I read.

**Two.** The picture's numbers are internally consistent and externally unsupported. $2.58M, four ventures,
15% survival, $71 per surviving exposure, eleven founder hours a week: a coherent shape, not a forecast. They
are here because the brief asked for concreteness, and every one of them is *illustrative*. The only figures
with provenance are those cited from the catalogue.

**Three.** This vision argues for tier R — machine publishing and machine spending within rails — and tier R
is where a one-founder company gets to be embarrassed at scale. The safety argument is made structurally
(absence and compiler refusal, never instruction), which I believe is the only kind that survives contact
with a 3am process. But it has never been run and the rails do not exist. The gap between "the founder
publishes by hand" and "the machine publishes within a rail" is the gap between a company that cannot outgrow
its founder and one that can be wrong very quickly in public. That is the founder's decision and it is not
close to being an agent's.
