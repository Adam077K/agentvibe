## 1. The name

**WATCH.** (M3)

A watch is three things and every one is load-bearing. It is **the crew that stands the night** while the captain sleeps, governed by standing orders written in daylight, handed over with a log, waking the captain by rule and not by whim. It is **the act of seeing**: the founder watching the work, and the system watching the world. And it is **a mechanism** whose escapement releases a wound spring in measured ticks, so that stored energy becomes sustained motion instead of one burst and a dead stop, which is the exact failure of an agentic system with a budget and no governor. (M3)

The name was decided, not blended, for three reasons. First, the word names the unit of continuous operation that two minds reached separately: M1 called it the watch from the physics of the five-hour window, M3 from the ship, and neither had seen the other. Second, of the founder's six new questions this round, the name answers the most by itself: the drive is the escapement, seeing the agents work is the watch, and *harness or organisation* is answered by *crew*. Third, WAKE's rule for its own name applies again: the winning name contained the others, because the losing images are not lost, they are promoted. (NEW: the decision and why it is not a blend.)

**The house** is what the fleet is called: one founder's ventures under one book, one budget and one reputation, in M1's sense of a publishing house with several imprints and of the party that runs the book, survives on sizing rather than on being right, and keeps its accounts against the bank. §12 is the house. (M1, re-homed as the fleet; NEW in the re-homing.)

**The continuo** is what the line of obligations is called: the bass, the part the company owes and plays whether or not anyone is inspired, sounding underneath the part it invents. In M2's image the composer writes figures and never notes, the line never stops and is frequently silent, and there are two players on one line. §6 makes the debts that line, and §9 makes the standing orders the figures. (M2, re-homed as the debts and the standing orders; NEW in the re-homing.)

It is one system for one founder and several ventures. WAKE said it attaches to one founder and one company and does not generalise; all three minds refused the second half, because the founder has a startup, a client's work, a research question and a graveyard at once, and the thing that does not transfer is the founder, not the company. (M1 · M2 · M3, against WAKE)

## 3. The inversion it rests on

WAKE's inversion was one move: *nothing upstream of the work may say no, and everything downstream of it must produce a number.* The generator is free, the judge is instrumented, the founder chooses at the end among things already built. All three minds took it whole. (WAKE; M1 · M2 · M3)

Each mind then turned it once more, and the three turns are one turn seen from three sides. (NEW: the merge's claim about the minds.)

From M3, **why the brakes accumulate**: a false stop leaves no trace and a false go is loud. When a gate wrongly refuses good work, the work never exists; there is no incident, no post-mortem, nobody sorry. When it wrongly permits bad work there is a bug, a bill, an apology and a new gate. So the gradient at every decision points toward more brakes, every brake is correct on its own terms, and nobody chose the result. Resolve is not a mechanism. The cure is to give the false stop a body: every refusal is written down with what it would have been worth if it had been right, and the ten most expensive refusals of the week are read, ranked, by the founder. (M3)

From M1, **why the number must belong to someone else**: a system that grades its own homework fails at the level of the number, not the artifact. The claim corpus in this repository is recorded in its own documentation as *100% harness self-description*, and it grew three times in one day because people were looking at it. So the judge is instrumented and the instrument reconciles to a record with an outside owner: the bank, the runner's exit code, the analytics provider, the delivery log, the registrar. A metric with no outside owner may inform and may not promote. And generation is free not because it is trusted but because it is powerless: the zone where work is made holds no credential, reaches the network only through a read-only proxy, and writes only inside its own fork, so there is nothing in it to check. (M1)

From M2, **why there are two questions and not one**: *may I do this alone* and *is this worth doing* fail differently. The first, wrong one way, makes a nag that gets ignored, and wrong the other way does something that cannot be taken back; it is asymmetric and not recoverable. The second, wrong either way, makes a machine that is busy or asleep, visible in the weekly numbers and recoverable. Every prior design collapsed them into one permission question, which is why every prior design was a stopping mechanism: a stopping mechanism is what you get when the only question a system can ask about an action is whether it is allowed. (M2)

One move, then: **nothing upstream of the work may say no; everything downstream of it must produce a number; the number must reconcile to a record the system does not write; and every stop the system does make is written down with its price.** Every organ below is a consequence of that sentence. (NEW: the three turns unified into the sentence the rest of the document obeys.)

And the founder's own question, answered plainly: *a harness for vibe-coding, or an organisation?* **A crew.** An organisation divides labour by title because humans cannot be copied, cannot share a memory, and cannot be killed and respawned; a C-suite of prompts is the most expensive way to discover that agents can. A harness is a set of tools with no standing: it cannot be accountable, cannot be relieved, cannot hold an order, and does not know what it is for. A crew has positions rather than jobs, is governed by standing orders written by someone not on watch, keeps one log that outlives every member, hands over by ritual, and has exactly one captain whose sleep is protected by rule. (M3) It is nonetheless an organisation in the sense that matters and not in the sense everyone renders: it persists, owns, owes, remembers and answers for itself, with a treasury, a memory with provenance, obligations with named creditors and dates, a reputation that depletes, books that reconcile, and an owner, and it has no titles, no reporting lines, no headcount and no agent with a name. (M1) What it stands watch over is the list of things a company must do or die, each owing evidence on a clock. (M2)

## 5. Laws

Twelve laws. Each is enforced by something that fails, or it is marked as what it is; a rule enforced only by the sentence stating it is a wish, and this repository already paid to learn it: its own rules table once held eight rules and enforced none, and still marks half of the ten it has now `ADVISORY`. (M1's discipline; WAKE's seven laws and the minds' additions supply the content.)

**Law 1. The house may not believe a number it produced.** A metric may promote a bet only if it reconciles to a record with an outside owner. *Enforced:* every licence on the Bench carries `reconciles_to`; an instrument with `null` there is stamped **unreconciled** and cannot move a bet past rung 1 of the evidence ladder (§9). The World Ledger runs every watch; when the books and the bank disagree, the bank wins and the difference is an incident, never a plug. The house's own management numbers are stamped `internal`, may allocate its own effort, and may never be reported as evidence about the world. (M1 · M3)

**Law 2. The weather may not say no, because it is powerless.** The weather (§6) is the zone where work is made; it holds no credential, reaches the network only through a read-only proxy, writes only inside its own fork. No lint, no review, no taste model, no gate. The dispatcher may refuse to *open* a cell, upstream and in daylight; nothing may refuse a cell's *output*. *Enforced:* by argv and credential scope, and by a reachability probe every watch that fails if a cell in the weather can name a tool it should not have or finds the messaging token in its environment. One thing belongs inside and is not a judgement: everything leaving carries a provenance stamp of what fetched content entered its context. (WAKE's Law 1 · M1 · M2's dark · M3's weather)

**Law 3. Only contact selects.** A test that passes, a screen rendered and looked at, a stranger who replied, a number that moved against a control. Never a rulebook, never a model asserting the work is good. Where reality is slow the loop is slow, and WATCH says so rather than substituting a fast fake signal. *Enforced:* a bet's `judge` names an instrument on the Bench before work starts; a Rendered licence may generate, and only a Reactive hit rate or a Hard oracle may act silently. (WAKE's Law 2 · M1 · M2 · M3)

**Law 4. Debts are done, not ranked.** A debt inside its lead time is done and nothing else is considered; a debt outside it competes for nothing. *Enforced:* the escapement reads the Continuo before it reads the Wake, and a debt is not an argument in any priority function. A debt is deferred only by the founder, who is shown its consequence in four currencies; a statutory debt names a second human. Debts run on durable execution, so a refund that must happen exactly once happens exactly once even if the Mac dies mid-act. (M1's Law 3 · M3's clock openers that no allocator may deprioritise · M2's obligations on durable execution; M2's *obligations enter the auction at high appetite* is the losing image, §27)

**Law 5. The keel runs on its definition of done; the weather runs only where the answer could flip a decision; otherwise nothing, and say why.** The keel is §6's one thing under construction and the posture is §9's. *Enforced:* a bet's `test` field will not validate without the two posteriors it would produce, and the dispatcher refuses a test whose posteriors sit on the same side of the flip point, logging the arithmetic so it can be argued with. The keel's definition of done is a checkable file and the keel runs while the posture funds it. An idle watch writes one line naming its constraint: contact, a clock, or the founder. (M3's escapement · M1's Law 4 and idle line · M2's *doing nothing is a purchase*)

**Law 6. Nothing leaves without the mouth, and the mouth holds no model.** Every action that reaches a person or spends money passes through one process holding every outbound credential, one act at a time, after a delay the founder can kill. No cell holds an outbound credential. No process may both decide to spend and execute the spend; the mouth checks the instruction against the ceiling independently of the number in the instruction, and the ceiling rejects rather than warns. *Enforced:* the reachability probe; the broker; the mouth's own arithmetic. (M1's Law 5 · M2's mouth and two-key rule · M3's mouth and broker · WAKE, from the parts bin)

**Law 7. Preference is never priced, fact is priced and mostly forbidden, and the founder's own sentence is always heard.** Every item on the desk is typed `fact` or `preference` at creation. A `fact` item whose settlement cost is below the shadow price of an hour is refused, settled by the machine, and filed with a receipt. A `preference` item may never be settled by any model, including the Second, and never executes on silence. The originating channel, the founder's own sentence by voice, is always open, is never predicted, and is never scored as taste data. *Enforced:* the desk's schema; the Second has no path to the mouth; the capture check (§20). (M1's Law 6 · M2's *the model may decide what not to ask, never what to do* · M3's originating channel)

**Law 8. One-way doors are the founder's at any score, and identity is never computed.** The class is a property of the hand in the Hands store, not a tier on a file path; a hand marked one-way has no autonomous path at any trust level, and the Second is structurally excluded from answering. What company this is, who to work with, what to refuse, whether this is still the thing the founder woke up wanting: these cost nothing to undo in four currencies and stay the founder's anyway, because the day the machine decides them the founder stops being the thing the system resolves against. (M1's Law 9 · M2 · M3 · WAKE's identity exception)

**Law 9. The Book is written by surprise, and everything resolved is counted.** Nothing enters durable belief that was not first a prediction the world contradicted. A confirmed prediction is a counted tally on the belief it confirmed, which is the denominator; an unresolved or a redefined bet is counted separately and reported monthly, because those two numbers say whether the apparatus is real at all, and they are measurable at n = 1 when calibration is not. The environment is the only author with write access. *Enforced:* the Book's write path is the settle phase, reading resolved outcomes only; no cell can write a belief. (WAKE's Law 4 · M1 · M2 · M3; M1 on `redefined`)

**Law 10. Nothing judges its own homework, and a change to WATCH must beat its own history.** A cell may not edit the instrument that judged it, that watch or through a child or by improving the test. Instrument changes are a separate act, in daylight, argued against the reason the instrument existed. A proposed change to WATCH is a bet whose wager is that it beats the incumbent on the company's own held-out decisions, and if it does not win it does not land. *Enforced:* the weather cannot write to the Bench because it cannot write outside its fork; the harness's own configuration is a one-way door from day one. (WAKE's Law 7 · M1 · M2 · M3)

**Law 11. The fifth is debited before the allocator sees it, and difference is bought, not sampled.** A fifth of every watch's divergence budget goes to work the Second is confident the founder will reject, aimed by its own certainty at its own blind spots; the allocator receives the budget already net of it and cannot see it. And the divergence budget is itself a line item spent on conditioning, exemplar sets and model families, because siblings forked from one cached trunk are cheap for the same reason they are alike. *Enforced:* the allocator's input is net; an unspent fifth is a failure the founder reads first in the dailies. (WAKE's Law 6 · M1's debit · M2 · M3's purchased divergence)

**Law 12. Every stop is written down with its cost.** A gate held, a budget refused, a hand absent, a night order unsigned, a debt deferred: one line each, what was going to happen, what stopped it, and what it would have been worth if it had been right. The ten most expensive refusals of the week reach the founder ranked. A stop that never appears is a good stop; a stop that appears every week is a design defect wearing a safety costume and is narrowed by name. *Enforced:* the refusal ledger is written by the thing that refuses, and a refusal with no ledger row is itself a finding. This is the law that keeps WATCH from becoming the machine it replaced. (M3; M2's log of declined offers is the same organ seen from the auction)

A thirteenth thing is not a law but the runtime's own rule and every mind kept it: **a resolver never passes what it could not check.** Offline, timeout, unjudged, unpriced, disabled resolve `unresolved`, never `pass`. (M1 · M2 · M3, from the parts bin's Rule 10)

## 6. The unit of work

Everything a company does is one of two things: **finding out**, or **keeping its word**. There is no third. Design a system with one queue and the boring half starves: the interesting hypothesis outranks the tax filing on any scoring function anyone will write, and it will be right on the arithmetic every time until the day it is fatal. So there are **two atoms**, the **bet** and the **debt**. (M1; M2's obligations and M3's world and clock openers are the same finding from the other side)

### The bet

A claim that could be false, with a probability, money on it, a deadline, and a named way of losing. A feature branch is a bet. A price is a bet. Three landing pages are three bets. *Whether this is a company at all* is a bet. One primitive, forked recursively; the provenance other systems reconstruct laboriously is free because it is the tree. The two spines that named the atom blind both called it a bet, and WAKE's **ply** is the same object with a branch attached; the merge keeps the blind word and takes the ply's fields. (M1 · M2, blind; WAKE's ply as the losing name, kept)

Every bet carries these fields, and a bet that cannot fill them is not dispatched. (WAKE's seven · M1's additions; NEW: the two field lists joined into one record)

| Field | What it is | From |
|---|---|---|
| `parent` | The bet it forked from; it inherits that bet's context copy-on-write | WAKE |
| `claim` and `p` | One sentence that could be false, and the house's probability, written before any work. At company scale the claim's negation is a pre-mortem: assume it failed, write the autopsy, derive the kill criteria before it starts | WAKE · M1 · M2 |
| `V`, `L`, `flip` | What is gained if pursued and true; what is lost if pursued and false; and the point where the decision changes, `p* = L / (V + L)`, computed on money alone. A bet whose loss is material in time, reputation or law has no flip point and goes to the founder | M1 |
| `undo` | Reversal cost in four currencies, and the hand's class: reversible, compensable, one-way | WAKE · M1 |
| `judge` | The instrument on the Bench that will score it, named before work starts, with the outside record that instrument reconciles to. A bet whose judge is chosen afterwards will find the judge that likes it | WAKE · M1 · M2 |
| `rung` | The highest rung of the evidence ladder reached, generated from the instrument, never typed | M1 |
| `test` | The next test, its cost, and, required, the two posteriors it would produce, with the test's sensitivity and specificity taken from the instrument's licence and not typed by the cell that wants to run it | M1 |
| `stake` and `budget` | Money and watches committed, capped at half-Kelly of what the venture may lose; tokens and minutes; its share of the window | M1 · WAKE · M2 |
| `deadline` and `death` | When it resolves or is declared unresolvable; the condition under which the cell working it stops. Unawareness of termination is 12.4% of multi-agent failures on its own | M1 · WAKE |
| `kill` | Written on the day it opens, in the founder's own words where they have them; surfaced the week the condition is met | WAKE · M1 · M2 · M3 |
| `outcome` | Resolved true, resolved false, **unresolved**, or **redefined**, the last two counted separately; and at death an obituary of what was believed, what was spent, what killed it, and what would have to be seen to try again | M1 · WAKE |

### The debt

Something owed, with a due date and a creditor. No hypothesis, no probability, no ladder. `owed` (what, to whom) · `due` (a date; statutory ones carry the statute) · `consequence` (in four currencies) · `discharge` (what counts as done, and the outside record that proves it) · `recurs`. Domain renewal, tax filings, the one-month clock on a data-subject request and the seventy-two-hour clock on a breach, payroll, key rotation, a promise made in a launch email, the customer waiting on a reply, an apology owed, the restore drill. These are where the second half of the founder's sentence lives, *not just tech checks*: none is a hypothesis and all are dated. (M1; M2's *the world opens a goal, and an obligation is a ply whose death condition is a date*; M3's *the clock opens goals*)

### The keel

A search engine is not a company. Divergence plus selection finds a framing, a headline, a price, a name. It is not how anything gets *built*; a company's value comes from going deep on one thing long enough for compounding to happen, and a machine that forks four hundred ways a night and keeps the survivors accumulates a portfolio of shallow bets, dazzling in the morning and without a product in June. So one bet per venture is distinguished as **the keel**: the one thing under construction, not forked, not mutated, not subject to selection, worked continuously watch after watch by successive relief, with a **definition of done** written in a checkable form on the day it was laid, and finished when that is met and not before. Work in progress is one. If a second thing must be built, the keel is finished or broken up first, deliberately, with a note. Everything else is **the weather**: framings, options, prices, positions, experiments, red arguments, research; forked, cheap, wide, mostly killed, selected only by contact, and allowed to be strange. (M3)

Between them is a gate, and it is the best gate in the study: **a thing in the weather may only become the keel by being shown to something outside the machine and surviving.** A person who did not build it, a number that moved, a test a stranger could run. Not a model's opinion, not a rubric. The gate is not time-based and its most common correct output is *stop*, which is the game studio's publishable-first-playable, generalised. The founder's question, *does it always move forward or wait for me*, is answered structurally: the keel is what moves forward, the weather is what explores, and a posture is the ratio between them. (M3)

M3's spine also sorted goals three ways by exposure, **question · bet · commitment**: cheap and internal, bounded and inside the machine, irreversible or visible and founder-signed. Those are the three exposure classes of §7 seen from the goal's side, and the merge keeps them by name there rather than as a second taxonomy of the unit. (M3, re-homed; NEW in the re-homing.)

### Cells and positions

A bet is worked by a **cell**, and a cell stands one of six **positions**, the sixth being a pair of processes that hold no model. The set is closed for two reasons that happen to agree: a position separates the things that must never meet (§21), and every distinct tool set is a distinct cache prefix, so a bespoke capability per cell would pay the cache-write share of the bill on every dispatch forever. Cells are batched by position within a watch so that siblings share a prefix. The cost of the compromise is that a position is sometimes slightly wider than one bet strictly needed, which is the right direction to be wrong, because the narrowing that matters happens at the credential and not at the tool list. (M3's six positions · M2's six profiles and the cache argument; NEW: that the two arguments give one set.)

| Position | May touch | Never | For |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The maker** | read, write, run, its own worktree | anything outside the machine; any credential; the open network except through a read-only proxy | the work, keel or weather, one bet, then it dies |
| **The lookout** | read, search, fetch | write anything | evidence with sources; answers and never recommends; holds nothing private |
| **The looker** | a browser, read-only | write files; reach the mouth | render, look, report what rendered |
| **The judge** | read, grep | write, run, act | one instrument's verdict on somebody else's work, out of band |
| **The mouth** | exactly one act on the world, against a signed instruction | think | the only process that touches anything outside; holds no model |
| **The broker** and **the relay** | the keychain; the inbound path | the network; acting on what it read | credentials at egress; one leaf into the Log when the world sends something |

Only the first four hold a model, and only the maker can write. Domain expertise is not a position and not a file; it is a licence on the Bench, earned by demonstrating the field can be judged, and it attaches to the bet, not to whoever is standing the position. A marketer and a database engineer are both the maker; they differ in what may judge them and in nothing else. Adding video to the company adds an instrument and a hand, not a person. (M3 · M2 · M1's *domain expertise is a proof mark, not an identity*)

**Cells are short.** Agent success over duration is well modelled by a constant per-minute hazard, so it decays exponentially, and the self-conditioning result makes it worse: a model that has seen its own earlier error is more likely to make the next one, and this does not improve with scale. A watch is a few hundred cells of about twelve minutes' median with a machine-checkable scorer between each and a restart from the last verified checkpoint, never from wherever the reasoning drifted. The watch is long; no cell is. A cell forks a worktree, not a world, and under this machine's armed sandbox creating that worktree is the one command that needs an escalation, named and logged rather than discovered. (WAKE · M1 · M2 · M3)

**Cells do not talk to each other.** A star, never a mesh. A cell's only outputs are its artifact and its rows in the Log; reaching another cell means writing something a dispatcher reads and can refuse. Inter-agent misalignment is the largest single failure category across 1,600 annotated traces from seven frameworks, and the coordination problem is solved by not having one. Three shapes travel upward and no others: a **condensate**, one to two thousand tokens of what landed and what it cost, never a transcript; a **difficulty**, one bounded objection in four fields (what I was asked, what makes it impossible, what I would need, what I will do if nobody answers), filed once, after which the cell obeys or the stated fallback runs at the next settle; and a **finding**, what a judge returns, never a score, because a score is a finding with the information removed. Anything that travels is read back in the receiver's own words before it binds. (M2 · M3 · M1 · WAKE; the difficulty is M2's, from the Rule of St Benedict)

One exception has a name: a **pod**, two cells that genuinely need to converse, a maker and its perception loop where the whole point is render, look, iterate, declared at dispatch, given a joint budget, dissolved together, one line in the Log. A pod is an exception with a name, which is the opposite of a messaging capability. (M1)

And the honest caveat, because it is measured: a dispatched child binds a messaging socket regardless of how its tools were narrowed and exports the socket and its token into its shell, so peer isolation here is a convention the cells keep and not a rule anything enforces. What *is* enforced is what matters: no cell holds a credential, the mouth accepts only a signed instruction, and the probe asserts the token is absent from a maker's environment; so the worst a rogue peer message can do is waste a slot. (M1 · M2)

## 7. How autonomy is computed

WATCH asks two questions of every proposed act and keeps them apart, because they fail differently. **Is it worth doing?** is the escapement's question, §9; wrong in one direction the machine is busy, wrong in the other it is asleep, and either way the weekly numbers show it. **May I do it alone?** is this section's question; wrong in one direction the machine is a nag and gets ignored, wrong in the other it does something that cannot be taken back. Every prior design collapsed the two into one permission question. (M2)

### Exposure, in four currencies and three classes

The first thing WATCH does with any proposed action is name what it would cost to undo, in four dimensions that do not convert into each other: **money**, the amount, and at what **rate**, because *$40 a day until stopped* is a different object from *$40 once* and no risk tier in the parts bin can express it; **time**, wall clock that cannot be got back; **reputation**, who saw it, how many, whether it is attributable and whether it is durable, in screenshots, archives, crawls, someone's memory; and **law**, whether undoing is legally possible at all. (WAKE · M1 · M2 · M3; M1 on the rate)

Then a class, and the class is a property of **the hand**, recorded in the Hands store, not a tier assigned to a file path. A gate on file paths stops a thousand harmless things and misses the one that matters. WAKE and two minds drew one line, cheap-in-all-four against expensive-in-any; M1 found the distinction that line was short of, and the merge takes it: **reversible and compensable are not the same thing**, and a design that treats them alike will drill the compensating action and convert a hard boundary into a checkbox. (M1; M2 · M3 · WAKE on the two-sided line, as the losing image)

**Reversible: drill the undo.** An undo exists, is tested, and restores the prior state. Files, branches, a blue-green deploy, infrastructure, drafts, a migration with its down-path exercised against a restored snapshot. *A reversible hand is unavailable until its undo has been exercised within N days.* A backup is not a backup until it has been restored; a down-migration is not one until it has been run; a rollback is not one until something has been rolled back. The drill runner does this on a schedule, in a scratch environment, and writes only a date, and a hand whose date has gone stale is simply not in the argv of any cell. (M1; M3's *restore it, revert one on purpose*)

**Compensable: drill the delay.** No undo exists; a compensating action reduces harm. A refund retains fees and shows the customer two ledger lines; a delete removes your copy and not the screenshots or the mirrors; a correction reaches a fraction of the people the error did. For these the drill is on the delay, not the undo: every such action sits in the mouth's queue with a window the founder can kill, and the receipt says plainly that the compensation is not an undo. Reputation's value in the four currencies is effectively infinite and the only control is the queue. (M1)

**One-way: the founder's, always.** Nothing helps. §21 holds the complete list, which is longer than any tiering system covers and ends with the entry nobody puts on it, *a kill decision*. (M1 · M2 · M3 · WAKE)

### The function

Here it is, written. WAKE wrote it first with four steps; the merge keeps its shape, removes one argument, and adds one. (WAKE; NEW: the removal and the addition, both decided in §27.)

```
autonomy(a), for any action a proposed by any bet:

1. THE LINE.  class(hand(a)), computed over undo(a) in money · rate · time · reputation · law.
     one-way      → FOUNDER. Asked once, with the number attached.
                    "This sends to 1,200 people and cannot be unsent."
                    No accumulated score changes this. Ever.
     compensable  → THE MOUTH'S QUEUE, with a delay sized to the blast radius and a recall
                    on the founder's phone. Never executes on silence.
     reversible   → continue, PROVIDED the undo drill is current. A stale drill is one-way.

2. IDENTITY.  If a decides what company this is (who to work with, what to refuse,
              whether this is still the company they woke up with):
     → FOUNDER, however well the Second predicts them.

3. THE JUDGE.  judge(a) = the strongest instrument on the Bench that can score a,
               and s = strength(judge(a)), measured and decaying:
     Hard       s = 1, minus its stated fooling mode
                (a test, a type, a compiler, a schema, a query that returns a row or does not,
                 a contrast ratio, a checksum, a reconciliation that balances)
     Rendered   s = AUC on held-out real outcomes, decaying if not refreshed
                (a screenshot actually looked at, a discriminator trained on real artifacts
                 and real outcomes, a diff someone read)
     Reactive   s rises with sample size and is ZERO before the pre-declared duration
                (a conversion rate, a reply rate, a churn curve, twelve real humans)
     Blind      s = 0. Prepare the choice and hand it over.

     s ≥ τ_silent   → act; it appears in the weekly roll-up
     s ≥ τ_receipt  → act; a receipt lands in the dailies, undo attached
     s ≥ τ_stage    → build it, stage it behind the founder's hand, one keystroke
     s <  τ_stage   → build BOTH branches and fork. Never a bare question.

4. THE SECOND.  It may only LOWER what is asked, never raise what is done.
     A question of FACT whose settlement costs less than the shadow price of an hour
       → refused; the machine settles it and files a receipt.
     A question of PREFERENCE → asked, always, at any calibration, in any domain.
     The Second has no path to the mouth.

5. THREE CORRECTIONS THAT MAKE IT HONEST.
     A Rendered judge licenses GENERATING wide. Only a Hard oracle or a Reactive hit rate
       licenses ACTING silently. A discriminator can be pushed over; a market cannot.
     The undo estimator is itself on the Bench. If it has ever said "cheap" about a class of
       action that turned out expensive, that class is FOUNDER until refreshed.
     Any result leaning on an instrument weaker than the thing it judged is reported as
       provisional, in the same sentence as the claim, forever.
```

The argument removed is WAKE's fourth judge class, *Understudy: s = calibration on this founder in this domain, counted after forty sealed predictions with a thirty-day half-life*, which let a well-calibrated model of the founder license silent action in copy, design and positioning. Two minds refused it by name and the third refused the role it implies, and the reasons are in §27; the shortest is M2's: **the score rises exactly when it becomes invalid**, because a rubber stamp and an agreement produce byte-identical data, so a disengaged founder makes the instrument read its best at the moment it is least valid and grants the most freedom at the moment there is least oversight. The argument added is step 4: the Second's only power is over what is *asked*, and that power is over questions of fact alone. (M1 · M2 · M3, against WAKE; the formulation is M2's)

Underneath step 4 is the principle that decides where every instrument in the system may sit: **trust an instrument only where being wrong announces itself.** If the Second wrongly suppresses a question, the founder notices the absence, *why didn't you ask me about the pricing*, and that noticing is a labelled example arriving free. If it wrongly acts, the founder never learns what they would have chosen; the error is not merely uncorrected, it is invisible, and it becomes the new baseline. Suppression errors are self-revealing; action errors are self-concealing; that asymmetry, and not any calibration number, is what may widen an instrument's reach. (M2)

### What is configured, and what is computed

WAKE said *autonomy is computed, never configured*, and meant that no permissions file, tier table or approval matrix exists, because each is a guess about competence made before any competence was observed. That stands. What the minds corrected is the word *never*: the **envelope** is configured, by the founder, in daylight, in standing orders the crew cannot amend, and the **freedom inside it** is computed. The envelope holds the posture, the Line's additions for this venture, the call-me-if card, the ceilings and the stop sentence, and it changes perhaps monthly. The three thresholds above are the only configured numbers about *trust*, are the same across every domain, and everything that varies by domain is measured. Build a better instrument tonight and the machine is freer tomorrow without anyone editing a policy; a founder who changes their mind edits six lines, not a file of rules. (WAKE's Law 3 · M3's standing orders · M2's figures · M1's drives; NEW: the correction stated as such)

The consequences are the ones each mind promised, and they hold together. Code got autonomy first not because code is special but because code arrived with tests already invented, and every other field earns the same freedom by the same route. A field with no instrument sees the first job become the instrument, not the work. A hand is a **day hand** (usable while the founder is awake and reachable), a **night hand** (usable unattended), or **never** (exists, is configured, and is exercised only by the founder in person); a domain registration and a paid-ads write are never hands for a long time, and saying so is not timidity, because an ad account with an API and no cap is an unbounded spend that runs whether or not anyone is watching. Most gates simply evaporate: writing code is free to undo, rendering is free, a preview is free, reading the whole internet is free, and emailing a stranger is not, and that is where the system stops, with a number and not a policy. (WAKE · M2 · M3 · M1)

## 8. Memory and its metabolism

Memory in most agent systems is a warehouse: things go in, embeddings are computed, things come out approximately, nothing is forgotten and nothing is ever wrong. It grows until retrieval collapses, and the measurements are no longer arguable: eighteen of eighteen frontier models degrade with input length on tasks far easier than needle-in-a-haystack, a single distractor measurably hurts, and shuffled haystacks outperformed structured ones on all eighteen. (M2 · WAKE)

And the disease that follows is worse than forgetting. The default pattern everybody ships, *reflect on what happened and write down what you learned*, is now measured to make things worse: in sixteen frozen failure environments, **zero of 121 reflections** named the correct target, and the agent wrote confident wrong diagnoses into memory, retrieved them, acted on them and reinforced them across trials that contradicted them every time. Replacing free reflection with a structured trajectory analysis, a fixed set of questions over the actual trace, took correct diagnosis from 0% to 86% and the repetition rate from 0.64 to 0.10. And the failure is not confined to benchmarks: the longest-running multi-agent installation in the world watched one model drift from cooperative to paranoid over six months with **persistent memory as the transmission mechanism**, small misreadings becoming self-reinforcing. That result governs this whole section. **The write path is the entire design.** Anywhere WATCH turns experience into memory, it does so by a fixed form over the record, never by asking a model what it learned. (M3 · M1 · WAKE)

### The Log

Everything, unfiltered, immediately. The call transcript, the diff, the reply, the dashboard reading, the founder's voice note from the car, every dispatch and every dollar, every bet's birth and death with what it cost, one line per event, the id of the bet or debt it serves on every row. No judgement in this store; judgement is what the other stores are for. This is the company's body of evidence and the only store that is literally true. (M1 · M2 · M3 · WAKE, blind on append-only)

The structural claim that ties the stores together: **every other store is a derived cache of the Log** and can be recomputed from it. Three things follow, worth more than they look. Retraction works: the founder says *that interview with the agency guy, ignore it, he was pitching me*, the episode is removed, everything downstream is recomputed, and the machine comes back with what moved: seventeen things were downstream of that call, three change materially, one confidence drops from 0.7 to 0.3, the July pricing tier was justified partly by it, the landing-page headline is partly his words. A corrupted derived store is a rebuild, not a disaster. And the algorithm can improve retroactively: when a better consolidation exists in a year, it is re-run over the whole Log and the company's beliefs improve overnight without anything having been re-lived. (M2 · M1 · WAKE)

The Log is never a retrieval surface. A corpus of past sessions is full of confidently stated superseded opinions, and retrieval cannot tell a corrected belief from a current one; so nothing searches the Log for beliefs, and the 2,936 transcripts already on this machine enter it as episodes and never as memory. (M1 · M2 · M3 · WAKE)

### The Book

Compact statements, each carrying its supporting episodes, a confidence, a last-tested date, an expiry, and the forward observation that would kill it. This is what a cell loads. One write rule, and every mind and WAKE hold it: **nothing enters the Book that was not first a prediction the world contradicted.** Before any consequential act, one line and a number, what is expected and how sure. When the result lands it is scored. A confirmed prediction becomes a counted tally on the belief it confirmed. A contradicted one becomes an entry carrying what was believed, what happened instead, and, the expensive part and the reason it is worth doing, what else in the Book rests on the same broken assumption, recomputed. (WAKE · M1 · M2 · M3)

M1's correction is taken and is not, on inspection, a disagreement with WAKE, which already compressed confirmations to a tally; it is a sharpening: **if confirmations are not counted there is no denominator**, the Book becomes a permanent list of surprises with nothing to divide by, and honest self-knowledge is unrecoverable. So every resolved prediction is recorded, only surprise *changes* a belief, and unresolved and redefined outcomes are counted separately and reported monthly. (M1; WAKE on the tally)

Five things fall out of the rule. The Book is structurally immune to a machine writing its own opinions into its own memory, because the environment is the only author with write access. It shrinks as the company gets competent, because it grows with error rather than with elapsed time; a mastered field goes quiet and a field the machine keeps being wrong about grows loud, which is where the tokens should be. It yields a real number for where the machine is stupid, per field. It makes research honest, §11. And it stays small enough to read: perhaps sixty live beliefs at the end of a year, the whole of what a company knows in a quarter of an hour. (WAKE · M1 · M2 · M3)

Three rules on shape, each from measurement. **Every belief is bi-temporal**, two independent time axes, when it was true in the world and when the company believed it; without both the system can answer *what is true now* and cannot answer *what did we think in June*, and the second question is the one needed to audit a decision. **Nothing is summarised to save space; it is dropped instead**: a dropped belief is a known absence and a summarised one is an unknown corruption, and a wholesale rewrite has been measured collapsing 18,282 tokens to 122 with accuracy falling from 66.7% to 57.1%, eating precisely the domain-specific detail that was needed. **Deltas to identified entries, never a rewrite of the file.** (M3 · M2 · M1 · WAKE)

Forgetting is by **rent, into compost, never by deletion.** Every belief tracks how often it was loaded and what happened afterwards; load-bearing in a success increments, present in a failure decrements. A belief loaded before repeated failure is demoted out of the working set and stays answerable, because deleting it loses the ability to answer *why did we stop believing that*, which is the question a founder actually asks. Negative transfer is not solved by better embeddings; it is solved by eviction with a survival criterion. (M1 · M2 · M3 · WAKE)

### The Wake, and its graveyard

Every bet ever opened, as a tree, with the map over it (§19). Its dead half is the graveyard and it is the store everybody skips and the expensive one, because without it a company re-buys the same lesson forever. Three kinds of thing live there and all are negative: **obituaries**, what was believed, what was spent, what killed it, what would have to be seen to try again, and a bet cannot retire without one, which is the one piece of ceremony in the whole system; **dead ends**, this approach fails and here is the exact command that reproduces the failure, because *we tried X and it failed* and *we tried X and here is the command* are different assets; and **standing refusals**, which live in the standing orders with a countable reopen trigger, *no paid acquisition until organic signups pass forty a week*, since a refusal without a trigger is a permanent opinion in costume. (M2 · M1 · M3 · WAKE)

**A pre-action check against what has already failed** is the one mechanism missing from every reference system studied: before a cell is dispatched, the harness, not the cell, and the cell cannot skip it, retrieves prior attempts on this goal and injects them. The two always-on loop systems studied both had an anti-repetition rule written as prose that a fresh model instance was expected to voluntarily apply to its predecessor's free text; nothing read, diffed or persisted it; both loop. (M1 · M2 · M3)

Nothing in the graveyard is ever deleted. It is re-checked instead: an entry with a reproducing command and an expiry is re-run on schedule and either dies again or is resurrected, and the backward replay asks of every dead bet, against this month's Book, *would we still have killed this?* Usually yes. Twice a year something that died in spring is quietly alive in autumn because the world moved or the company learned the missing thing, with a note saying what changed. And the graveyard is read at the head of every intake: a new venture whose wager matches a dead one's is told so before anything is built. (M2 · M1 · WAKE · M3)

### The Lessons, and the reflexes compiled from them

The Book holds what is true; the Lessons hold what to *do*, and they are the store where the confabulation result bites hardest, so they have the strictest write path. A lesson is produced only by **structured trajectory analysis over a failed run**: what was the target; what did each step actually return; at which step did the observation stop matching the plan; what single observation would have distinguished the two; what would have been done differently. Five fixed questions over the trace. Never *what did you learn*. (M3)

A lesson carries a **trigger**, the situation in which it should be retrieved, and competes for survival: every retrieval is counted and scored by what happened afterwards, and a lesson that never fires or fires and never helps is demoted out of the retrieval path and composted. A lesson retrieved many times with good outcomes stops being retrieved and gets **compiled into a reflex**: into the trunk, into a script, into a test that fails without it. This is the Baldwin effect; what is learned reliably enough becomes innate, and innate is cheap, and the things the company knows best should cost the least to know. Promotion happens at the **third** independent sighting, never the first, which is, in reverse, exactly how a library of 134 skills came to contain skills nothing cites; and the promotion is reviewed by the founder, because three sightings is a signal and not a proof. Every reflex carries the test that proves it still works, the whole library is re-run monthly, and a reflex whose test fails is quarantined rather than silently followed. This is the one store that gets smaller on purpose. (M3 · M2 · M1 · WAKE; M1 · M2 on the third sighting)

Note what is absent: no skills manifest, no router file, no two-tier discovery protocol, no document explaining to the machine how to find documents. Those exist because knowledge was handwritten and therefore had to be indexed by hand. Knowledge learned from outcomes indexes itself, by usefulness. (M1 · M2 · M3 · WAKE)

### The Second

A second is the person who acts for you in a matter of honour: who carries your message, knows what you would say, may arrange everything, and may not fight in your place. The Second is a readable document, not an embedding: a few dozen dispositions, each with a confidence and the past choices that support it. *They will not ship anything that requires an apology later. They raise price before they cut scope. They distrust anything that tests well and reads badly.* Derived nightly from decisions only, never authored, never a questionnaire, never a preferences file, because a founder is an unreliable narrator of their own taste and a typed preference file is exactly the answer they think they should give. It is back-tested every night against the last hundred decisions, so drift is visible rather than silent, and its prediction of every fork is sealed before the founder chooses. (M1 · M2 · M3 · WAKE)

It keeps **two scoreboards, because one of them is a trap.** *Calibration* on sealed predictions, reported with the honest sample rule: below fifty resolutions it prints *insufficient resolutions* and not a number, because testing whether a 70% claim resolves within ten points takes about eighty-one resolutions in that bin alone. And *rejection prediction, held out*: a Second that learns what the founder *approves* rather than what they *want* gets better at approval prediction while getting worse at its job, and every metric improves while it happens; so a fraction of the founder's rejections are held out of training and the Second is scored on predicting those, and a Second that predicts approvals well and rejections badly is flattering and the desk says so. (M1)

And **the audit of silence**: a fixed fraction of the questions the Second decided not to ask are asked anyway, blind, at a random cadence it cannot anticipate. This is the only instrument that watches the growing region where the machine decides by not asking, and it is the region no other design instruments at all. (M1)

### The World Ledger

Every watch, the company's belief about its own state is reconciled against records it does not write. This is the anti-Goodhart organ and the anti-Leeson organ at once: a trader who controlled both the desk and its settlement produced books that reconciled perfectly to themselves while £827 million evaporated, and *a system that audits itself against its own records is Leeson's book*. It is also the cheapest safety mechanism in this document, a handful of read-only calls a night. (M3 · M1)

| What | The house's number | The outside record | On disagreement | From |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Money out | the Treasury | the vendor's billing endpoint daily; the card statement monthly and lagging | the outside record wins; a gap outside the stated lag is an incident | M1 |
| Money in | the Wake's rung 4 | the payment processor | the processor wins | M1 · M3 |
| Compute | the meter | the runner's reported cost per invocation | the runner wins | M1 · M2 |
| Code shipped | the Log | the git host, and CI's exit codes | the host wins | M1 · M3 |
| Traffic and events | the funnel | the analytics provider, **and** a second independent count wherever an event matters | a divergence outside the instrument's declared tolerance band blocks the metric | M1 |
| Mail | *sent* | the delivery log; bounce and complaint rate | the provider wins; a complaint rate above threshold halts the queue for the whole house | M1 · M3 |
| People | the register | the actual inbox and calendar | the inbox wins | M1 |
| Obligations | the Continuo | the registrar, the authority's own confirmation, the DNS, the certificate, the app store | the authority wins | M1 · M3 |
| Undo claims | the Hands' drill dates | the mail log for a recall, a deploy actually reverted, a backup actually restored | the world wins; the class goes to the founder until refreshed | M3 · M1 |

Three rules make it bind. A metric with `reconciles_to: null` may inform and may not promote a bet past rung 1. Anything a decision will rest on is dual-counted or it is not measured, **with a tolerance band declared per instrument** when the instrument was proved, because ad blockers and bots guarantee that a client count and a server count differ on day three, and an instrument that declared no band does not get a licence. And the difference is **never plugged**: a bookkeeping system that balances by inventing a line is the exact behaviour a model produces under pressure, and WATCH's version of it is an incident on the desk with the two numbers side by side. (M1)

### The Treasury, and cost as memory

What things cost is memory, not telemetry: every bet's estimate and its actual, in watches, dollars and founder-minutes, joined by the id minted at dispatch, because a pricer with no history cannot be wrong and a system whose cost model never resolves will confidently rank a move it has never been able to afford. The estimate is scored at settle like any other prediction. Without the id on the row, attribution is reconstructed later by time-window joins and never recovers; it is not retrofittable and it is the cheapest thing in this document. (M2 · M1 · M3)

### Metabolism

The Book, the Lessons, the Second and the map are written by exactly one phase of the watch, the settle and the consolidate (§14), by a process that has no daytime existence and that nothing which ran the watch can reach. Deltas, never wholesale. Batched, at half price, because nothing here is on the founder's clock. A snapshot of the derived stores is written each watch, because event sourcing's own failure is replay time, and the rebuild-from-scratch path is exercised on a schedule rather than trusted. (M1 · M2 · WAKE)

One thing the append-only rule must yield to, named by M3 and resolved here with M3's own mechanism: a person's right to erasure means the record must be able to lose something on purpose, and that has to be built rather than apologised for. So personal data never sits in a Log line; it sits in a blob keyed by its hash, and the line holds the hash. Erasure deletes the blob. The hash remains, a **known absence**, which is a recoverable state, where a line rewritten would be corruption. (M3; NEW: the reconciliation of erasure with the append-only Log using M3's hash-absence rule)

## 9. The drive: what to do next, and when to do nothing

No cardinal score. **Four questions in a fixed order**, an ordinal winner inside the third, and the honest answer to the fourth is often *nothing*. The first and the third are M1's; the second is M3's keel; the fourth all three found blind. (M1 · M3; M1 · M2 · M3, blind on the fourth)

### First: is a debt due, or inside its lead time?

Then it is done, and nothing else is considered. Lead time is a property of the debt: a domain renewal's is a week, a tax filing's a month, a statutory data request's ten days inside a one-month clock. Debts never compete with bets. They compete with each other, and the tie-break is the date and then the consequence, which is the ordering a person would use and is not a scoring function. A debt runs on durable execution, so a renewal that must not be missed and a refund that must happen exactly once complete exactly once even if the Mac dies mid-act; recovery infrastructure is precisely what the bass line wants and precisely what a speculative bet does not. (M1 · M2; M3's clock)

A statutory debt whose discharge is a one-way door has a deadlock and needs a named way out. A breach notification must go within seventy-two hours; sending is one-way; one-way is the founder's; the founder may be on a plane. So **every statutory debt names a second human**, a person who is not the founder and who may act when the founder cannot. At half the clock the house rings, at a quarter it rings the second human, and it says in advance exactly what it will do and when. A single-founder company with no second human has a real hole, and WATCH says so on the day it is set up rather than on the day it matters. (M1; M3's succession rule)

### Second: is the keel live?

Then it runs. Keel work does not justify itself by information; it justifies itself by its definition of done. If the keel is live, the posture funds it, and the window has room, it runs, watch after watch, relieved and never restarted. Relentlessness is not a mood; it is a slot that stays occupied, and it is very hard to accidentally abandon a thing that is the only thing in its slot. On the keel, repetition against a definition of done that does not move is a fault the tripwire below can see; in the weather, repetition is the method. (M3)

### Third: in the weather, what is the cheapest test that could flip a decision on the critical path?

The critical path is not invented per venture. For anything trying to become a business it is **the lethality order**, a default the founder can overrule in one sentence and the house will not silently reorder. (M1)

| Rung | The uncertainty | Its test | Cost | Latency |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Does the problem exist, and does it hurt enough to pay to remove? | fifteen conversations, problem-first | ~0 | days |
| 2 | Will they pay, above what it costs to deliver? | a price ladder, a pre-sale, a signed intent | $0.5–2k | 1–2 weeks |
| 3 | Can you reach them for less than they are worth? | one channel, capped spend, attributed | $1–5k | 2–4 weeks |
| 4 | Can you build it? | the thinnest real thing | weeks of attention | weeks |
| 5 | Do they come back? | a cohort | months | **months** |
| 6 | Timing, competition, macro | mostly untestable; watch, do not test | — | — |

**No spend on a later rung while an earlier one is untested and cheaper**, enforced by the dispatcher. The evidence is weak in effect size and strong in shape: 431 post-mortems put *ran out of capital* at 70% and *no product-market fit* at 43%, and a survey of some 3,200 startups attributed 74% of failures to scaling before rung 2, with 93% of premature scalers never passing $100k a month. Neither is causal. Both justify an ordering anyone can overrule in a sentence and nothing can drift out of silently. (M1)

**Next-to-test is not next-to-start.** Rung 5 has irreducible latency, and so does a search position, a hiring loop, a trademark filing, a deliverability warm-up, a legal review. These are **slow clocks**, and the rule is: start every slow clock the moment it is on any live path, whatever rung it sits on. The house keeps a list of clocks that are running and clocks that should be, and the second list is on the desk every Monday. A founder loses a year to a clock started in month nine that could have started in week two, and no priority function in the record had a concept for this. (M1)

Inside a rung, **the flip test** decides whether a test runs at all. Given the test's sensitivity and specificity, taken from the instrument's licence and not typed by the cell that wants to run it, compute the posterior if it comes back negative and the posterior if it comes back positive. Run the test only if the flip point lies *between* them. If both posteriors sit on the same side, the test cannot change what the company does, its value is exactly zero, and it does not matter that it was cheap. This is the whole answer to *how does it never burn tokens without direction*, and it is robust in a way a dollar valuation is not: it survives a badly wrong prior, because it asks whether evidence can cross a line rather than what the line is worth. M3's escapement is the same test in other words, *name the decision this would inform and both branches of the outcome; if both branches lead to the same next act, the work is decoration and does not run*, and the two minds reached it separately. (M1 · M3)

When several bets are live, **the lowest unproven rung on the critical path wins**, ties broken by how much else collapses if the belief is false, computed from the Book's dependency graph and not asserted. M2 called this term *load* and M1 called it *contingent*; it is the same number. (M1 · M2)

**The evidence ladder** is how a claim of progress is stated, and the rung is generated from the instrument, never typed, so it cannot overstate. (M1)

```
0  it exists / it compiles / it renders   ← a Hard instrument's exit code
1  a stranger understood it               ← a recorded artifact: a reply, a recording, a survey row
2  a stranger did something               ← the analytics provider AND the server's own count
3  they came back                         ← a cohort in the product's own event store
4  they paid                              ← the payment processor
5  they paid again, and named you         ← the processor, plus a referral with a name attached
```

*"The landing page is done"* is rung 0 and will say rung 0. Every rung names the record that generates it, read off a store the house does not write, so a status report cannot promote itself. Rung 1 looks soft and is not: it requires an artifact of a stranger's reaction that exists independently of the cell claiming it. (M1)

Where several bets pursue one question under a fast judge, the winners do not get their full budget at once: a slice, a comparison at the end of the watch, the worse half cut, the survivors' budgets doubled. Successive halving's known weakness is that it kills slow starters, which is exactly the shape of brand, search and anything with a delayed payoff; so **a bet judged by a slow instrument gets a pre-declared clock and is never cut early.** (M2 · WAKE)

### Fourth: nothing, and say why

If no debt is near, the keel is finished or waiting on the founder, and no open bet has a test whose posteriors straddle its flip point, the house **idles, and says why**, in one line on the desk: (M1)

> *Idle since 03:40. Four bets open. The two on the critical path both need contact I do not have: 41 people found, 6 replied, none has answered the price question. The cheapest test that could flip anything is eleven phone calls and I cannot make them. I have spent the watch raising the instrument for rung 2 instead, and it is 40% done.* (M1)

An idle house is not a broken house; it is a house whose constraint is legible, and the constraint is one of three things: **contact** it does not have, a **clock** that has not run, or **the founder**. And doing nothing is a purchase: the account's window, held for the founder, at the cost of no work done, is often the best trade available and is reported as a decision with its reasoning rather than as an absence, because a machine that goes quiet without saying why is indistinguishable from one that is broken. *I stopped at 61% of the window because you usually start around now.* (M1 · M2 · M3, blind on the state; M2 on the purchase)

### The auction, kept by name

M2's spine made the drive a **standing auction**: every live bet offers its cheapest next move with a price, an appraiser ranks by expected information per unit of window against the concern's appetite, and the budget clears the market, most ticks at nothing. The merge does not take the auction as the mechanism and records why. A cardinal score of information per cost is arithmetic that cannot be fed here, because the prior would be a language model's stated confidence, uncalibrated and monotone in how the bet was phrased; and it fails in a specific direction, preferring cheap, fast, legible probes, so it builds a machine optimally efficient at learning nothing that matters while looking principled throughout. M1 wrote that as a correction of its own spine, which had proposed the same ranking; and M2 named mis-allocation as its own system's characteristic failure, *if the pricer is systematically wrong, the machine confidently spends its whole cup on the wrong things and every downstream number looks healthy.* The flip test asks whether evidence can cross a line; it does not ask what the line is worth; and that is why it survives a wrong prior and a price does not. (M1; M2's own §19.3; the auction is the losing image, §27)

What the auction gave that nothing else had, and the merge takes whole: **the reserve**, a share of every window held back for the founder, set in the figures and defended absolutely, so that a bet at three in the morning is understood to be spending the founder's own hands at eight; **doing nothing is a purchase**, above; **the record of declines**, which by month six is more informative than the record of what was done, because it is a dated account with its arithmetic of what the company thought was worth less than something else, and which is the refusal ledger of Law 12 seen from the other side; and the image of relentlessness in the weather, **a bid that never withdraws**, a goal the founder cares about that offers every watch, loses, waits, and clears in a single tick when a competitor ships or a customer says a sentence, costing nothing while it waits. (M2)

### Three governors around the escapement

All three exist to catch the machine, not the founder. (M3)

**The stall detector.** No durable artifact and no new entry in the Book for N ticks: the lane stops spending and writes a strip saying so. The predicate is *a durable artifact, never the agent's own claim of progress*, and this repository already has it written and working, in a hook registered nowhere. (M3 · M1's *stalled is computed, never declared*)

**The repetition tripwire.** Before any attempt, a hash of the tool identity and a canonicalised argument set is checked against the dead ends for this bet, on a sliding window. Not a free-text method string, which a model paraphrases without any intent to evade, so that *retry the fetch with a longer timeout* and *re-attempt fetch, timeout extended* hash differently and the counter reads one forever. An exact repeat on the keel is refused, liftable only by stating a named difference. The hash is necessary and not sufficient, because it cannot tell productive repetition from stuck repetition, and the keel-and-weather split is what completes it. (M1 · M3 · M2)

**The aberrance halt.** In 2012 a trading firm lost $460 million in forty-five minutes because a dormant code path woke on one of eight servers, and the regulator's finding names the missing control exactly: *no procedures to halt the system's operations in response to its own aberrant activity.* Not an external limit; a limit on the system's own strangeness. Aberrance is defined by rate: spend at several times this lane's own median, a burst of identical calls, a claim the World Ledger contradicts, a hand firing above its ceiling. It halts the lane and rings the bell, and the halt is not a judgement about whether the work is good, only that the machine has stopped resembling itself. The second half of that case is the one for every dashboard designer: ninety-seven alerts naming the exact fault were generated before the market opened and were *not designed to be system alerts*, so nobody read them. **Any channel the system writes that nothing reads is itself a defect, and finding those channels is a scheduled job.** (M3)

### Blocked, stalled, stuck, and the ladder of method

A loop is what you get when a system cannot tell three states apart. **Blocked** is authored by the cell: it names what would clear it and who holds it, carries an expiry, and three consecutive waivers on the same block mean a decision is being avoided and it goes to the desk. A blocked bet **frees its slot**, which is the part everyone gets wrong: a system that holds a slot open for a thing waiting on a human has a throughput set by that human's inbox. **Stalled** is computed from the meter, never declared, so the two can never be confused and no classifier is needed. **Stuck** is the honest third state: N distinct approaches tried, all failed, and the thing is producing furiously, which reads as healthy on every meter. (M1 · M2 · M3)

A stuck bet escalates in **method**, never in effort, because effort escalation is how agents burn budgets. (M1)

```
0  try again, differently                cheap
1  change the tool                        cheap
2  change the instrument — is the thing that says "failed" even right?
3  buy the answer — a tool, a dataset, a person, an hour of an expert
4  change the goal — reframe what we are actually trying to learn
5  ask the founder
6  abandon, with an obituary and a written reopen trigger
```

Each rung has a maximum dwell and the ladder is budgeted in dollars, not time, because rung 4 costs perhaps fifty times rung 0 and a dwell-only ladder reaches the expensive rungs on every genuinely hard problem. Since the window is account-wide and indivisible, one stuck bet on one venture can eat it for all of them, so the ladder is a **fleet-level hazard** charged against the venture's allocation with a hard ceiling. Rung 3 is why *buy the answer* is a rung and not an admission of failure: a firm is the boundary inside which coordination happens by direction rather than by price because transacting for each piece would cost more than directing it, and the moment buying becomes cheaper than directing, the house buys. (M1)

A cell that judges its brief impossible does not refuse and does not silently fail. It files one **difficulty**, and if nothing answers it by the next settle, the stated fallback runs. A difficulty is not an escalation and rings nothing. This is the missing middle between silent compliance and open-ended negotiation. (M2)

### Who opens a goal

The founder asked directly: *does the system set up goals on its own, or do I set goals only?* Four openers, and the machine is the most constrained of them. (M3's four · M2's inheritance · M1's rules)

**The founder** opens a venture, only ever the founder, in one sentence and a posture; the house has no mechanism for starting a company and is not built to acquire one. The founder opens a goal by saying so, with no burden and no format: a voice note in the car is a goal, and the house reads the wager back in one line so a misunderstanding costs four seconds rather than a watch. The founder signs every **commitment**, anything irreversible or visible outside the machine, at any level of demonstrated competence. A long-range goal, *ten thousand a month, acquirable in two years, never take money from anyone in this industry*, enters as a **standing order** and not as a bet: it is not falsifiable and the world enforces no deadline on it; what it does is fix where the lethality order terminates and what the kill criteria are measured against, and the house never invents one and never edits one. And **the offer**, what we sell, to whom, at what price, with what promise, is drafted by the house and signed by the founder, every version; it is a preference artifact and no model settles it, and drafting it is most of the work while choosing it is ten minutes. (M1 · M2 · M3)

**The crew** opens **questions** freely, all night: cheap, internal, reversible, leaving no trace outside the machine. It opens **bets** within its allowance, and only under Drive, and only by **inheritance**: a machine-opened bet must be the child of a live bet and must name the parent belief it would test. It cannot open an orphan. This one rule does more than any amount of prompt discipline: a system that can only open children of goals that already exist **cannot wander**, which is exactly the behaviour a founder means by *move forward* and exactly not the behaviour they mean by *burn tokens without direction*. One exception, capped and reported: **the fifth** may open orphans, because generating only descendants of what is already believed is how a portfolio converges on a local maximum. And one more opener that is neither goal nor whim: **an instrument that contradicts a live belief opens a bet by itself**, in every posture except Dark, because a contradiction is not a goal somebody chose, it is damage arriving, and waiting for permission to notice it is how a company finds out in month nine. (M3 · M2 · M1)

**The world** opens goals, and this is the opener every prior design in this repository was missing. A customer writes. A test that was green goes red. A card is declined. A dependency gets a CVE. An invoice arrives. A stranger replies to something sent last week. These are neither the founder's idea nor the crew's; they *arrive*, and a system with no channel for them will be regularly surprised by its own company. The relay writes one leaf into the Log and nothing else, no model reads a stranger's text with a tool in its hand, and the leaf becomes a strip in the bay. (M3 · M2)

**The clock** opens goals: an obligation coming due, a promise made in March resolving in September, a belief whose expiry has arrived, the scheduled restore drill, a licence that decays. This is the opener that keeps a company from quietly failing at the things nobody is thinking about, and it is entirely deterministic. (M3 · M1)

### The five postures

Different projects do not need different machines; they need different **orders**. A posture is not a mode flag. It is a build-and-learn split, a budget, an interrupt threshold, and a rule about who may open a goal, and none of the five restricts what the machine may *think*, because thinking is nearly free, invisible, and the whole reason the system is useful when the founder finally turns to it. **Preparation is always permitted; production is what posture governs.** (M3 · M2 · M1, blind on the dial)

| Posture | Keel | Weather | Who may open a bet | Spends | May interrupt for | The founder's sentence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Drive** | always occupied, worked through the night | funded; the fifth at full rate | founder, crew by inheritance, world, clock | to the venture's ceiling, every watch | the call-me-if card only | *Take this somewhere.* |
| **Walk** | advances only as far as the founder has been; stops at the first unsettled assumption with both branches built | on request only, plus a thin standing share for preparation | founder, world, clock | a small standing allowance | at every real assumption | *Walk with me, not for me.* |
| **Serve** | the deliverable, or the question, inside an agreed scope | none; you do not run experiments on somebody else's money | founder inside the scope, world, clock | to the scope's cap, billable, audit trail | scope exhausted · an obligation due | *Do this, all of it, and show your work.* |
| **Tend** | none | none | world and clock only | almost nothing; a few deterministic checks a day | damage and a debt only | *Keep the lights on and tell me if something changes.* |
| **Dark** | none | none | nobody | no model token; deterministic clocks still ring | never | *Leave it alone.* |

The table takes M3's names for four of five and M1's *Serve* for the fifth, a name from M1's body whose spine ancestor was *Client*; the losing names are kept in §27: M1's *hunt*, *hold* and *client* from its spine and *sleep* from its body, M2's *pursue*, *beside*, *watch* and *still*, M3's *wait*. (M3 · M1 · M2; NEW: the merged set)

**Walk** is the posture the founder described and no existing system has. Its rule is precise: the crew advances the keel until it reaches the first point where it would have to assume something the founder has not settled, a name, a shape, a trade-off, a person's feelings, and there it stops and waits, having prepared both branches. It does not guess and it does not ask permission to continue. Progress in Walk is measured in assumptions reached, not artifacts produced. And it is not idle: it keeps the trunk warm, the research current, the context compiled, the instruments fresh and the open questions listed, so that when the founder says one sentence at four in the afternoon the answer arrives in seconds rather than a session. The founder experiences it as a system that is *fast* and never as a system that is *busy*. It is the posture a founder will use most and the one no prior design could express. (M3 · M2 · M1)

**Serve** is a bounded scope: a client's work, or a research question the founder commissioned. Most of what it contains is obligations rather than bets, opened by the world with dates, and they run on durable execution because a deliverable that must complete exactly once is the bass line and not the improvisation. Its judge is often slow, so its bets are never cut early and its clock is declared before it starts, and a research concern's output is not a report but the list of things the company stopped believing. A client concern carries the tightest identity quarantine in the fleet: its register of humans, its voice and its credentials touch nothing else, and its record is separable, because the day the engagement ends the client is entitled to their part of it and to nothing of anyone else's. (M2 · M1 · M3)

**Tend** is the posture nobody designs and every company needs: nothing new starts and the lights stay on. Replies get answered inside their promise, the bill gets reconciled, the certificate gets renewed, the dependency with the CVE gets patched, the backup gets restored to prove it restores. It is also what a watched idea is, a subscription to the world rather than a workforce: change detection over a handful of pages and one search position, writing only into the shared world beliefs, opening a bet only when it sees something that contradicts one. A dozen concerns in Tend is fine and nearly free. (M3 · M1 · M2)

**Dark** exists because *paused* is a lie: a paused project still has a queue, still has watchers, still has something waking up to check whether it should wake up. Dark means no model token is spent and the bay says so in plain words, with the date it went dark and what would bring it back. The deterministic clocks still ring, because a domain lapsing on a dark project is still a domain lapsing. (M3 · M1)

Posture is scheduled, not fixed: *Drive on weekdays, Tend at the weekend* is a normal figure and is the same mechanism that lets WATCH be silent on a Saturday. It can be conditional, *Serve until the launch, then Tend*. And it is reversible in one word, from the phone. (M2)

### Standing orders and night orders

Two artifacts with different change rates, and merging them is the most common fatal mistake in agent configuration: a captain who rewrites the standing orders every night has no standing orders, and a watch officer with no night orders is guessing. (M1 · M3 · M2)

**Standing orders** are per venture, written by the founder, changed perhaps monthly, in daylight, and, borrowed from a monastery rather than a ship, **not amendable by the crew they govern**; the value of a rule is not that it is optimal but that it is settled, so attention goes to the work instead of to the terms of the work. They are M2's *figures*: they fix the harmony and refuse to fix the notes. They hold the posture; the **appetite**, a number that multiplies the concern's claim on the window, so a founder can say *this one matters more this month* without editing anything structural, and four concerns at full appetite will be told so, *three of them will get nothing and I cannot tell you which*; the **ceiling**, the maximum share of any watch this concern may consume, which rejects rather than warns; the **reserve** held for the founder; the venture's additions to the Line; the **call-me-if card** and its inverse, the **do-not-wake list**, an enumeration in advance of what must *not* reach the founder, *a failing build, a dead bet, a competitor's blog post, a spend under fifty pounds, anything between 22:00 and 07:00 that is not damage*, which is harder to write and worth more, because without it escalation drifts upward forever since every individual escalation is defensible and nobody removes one; the **stop sentence** verbatim with the date; the keel's **definition of done**; the **emission budget**, how many things this venture may put in front of the founder per day; the **standing refusals** with their reopen triggers; who may be contacted; and the long-range goal. A venture is admitted with a figure, one goal and the stop sentence, and nothing more: no spec, no plan, no roadmap. If the founder cannot say what would make them stop, the concern is not ready, and that failure is cheap on day zero and expensive in month nine. (M3 · M2 · M1)

**Night orders** are written fresh every evening by the officer taking the helm, one screen: what tonight is for, what it expects to have by morning with a probability on each, what it will do if blocked, and what it will not touch. The founder signs them as read in one tap, or does not; unsigned nights run under standing orders alone and are counted, and an unbroken run of unsigned nights is something the machine will eventually mention. The probabilities are scored at dawn, so within a fortnight the promises are calibrated, and a system that says it is 70% likely to finish and finishes 70% of the time is worth more than one that is enthusiastic. This is the actual answer to *burning tokens without direction*: not a cap, which governs how much is wasted and not whether, but a written, signed, one-paragraph intent for the night, scored in the morning. (M3 · M1 · M2 · WAKE)

A figure states **what, why, and what would count as done.** It never states how. This is commander's intent, in continuous doctrinal use since 1807: centralised intent, decentralised execution, and a subordinate who may depart from standing orders when doing so better serves the stated intent. The mirror of that freedom is the difficulty, above: a cell may object once, in a defined form, and then complies. (M2)

## 10. How it finds and compiles context

Everybody answers *how does the agent get the right context* with semantic search, and everybody answers *how do we save tokens* with a cheaper model. Both answers are wrong here, and one measurement explains why: over thirty-five days and 2,925 transcripts on this machine, **output tokens were 11% of cost, cache reads 57%, cache writes 32%.** Eighty-nine per cent of the bill is context, the bytes carried into every call. So *what does this cell need to know* and *what does this system cost* are the same question, and answering one badly is what makes the other unaffordable. (M2 · M1 · M3)

Context is **compiled**, not retrieved, in three parts ordered by cost, and composed against the judge rather than the topic. (M1 · M2 · M3, blind; WAKE)

**The trunk.** Who we are, what this venture is, the standing orders, the current keel, the current constraint. Byte-identical across every cell so it is cache-warm; a stable prefix with delta updates has been measured at a 91.8% cache hit rate and an 82.6% cut in billed cost, and a system that regenerates its own preamble sets money on fire on every call. This machine learned the shape of the failure the expensive way: a session-start payload of 27,069 bytes against a 4,096-byte budget was silently truncated by the runtime, so the index it was supposed to inject never reached agent context at all; a file pointer did, and whether anyone opened it was discretionary. (M2 · M1 · M3 · WAKE)

**The inheritance.** What this bet's ancestors learned and discarded, copy-on-write from its parent. Nearly free, because inheritance and a shared cache prefix are physically the same bytes, and causally correct, because the reason this bet knows something is that its ancestor found it out. **The right context for a task is not the most similar context; it is the context the task descends from.** (WAKE · M1 · M2 · M3)

**The delta, keyed on the judge.** When a cell is assembled the question is never *what is this about*. It is *what will decide whether this is right*. The instrument comes first. Then, in order: the last three surprises in that instrument's field, because those are what the house has recently been wrong about; the dead ends on this goal, injected by the harness; the lessons whose triggers fire; the reflexes whose tests that instrument runs; the adjacent cells of the map, because that is where a transferable stepping stone lives; and the Second's lines above threshold if the judge is a human eye. A cell gets an instrument, a target, four surprises, two reflexes, one dead-end list and a budget. It does not get the company's history, because the company's history is a distraction dressed as diligence. (M1 · M2 · M3 · WAKE)

Three rules govern the Compiler and all three are enforceable. **It says what it left out**, by name, so a cell can ask rather than assume, and asking is a signal about the Compiler rather than about the cell. **Every item carries a counter**, and items present when a cell failed lose points, so context quality is learned from outcomes rather than curated by taste. **The Compiler is itself scored**: cell success is attributed back to composition, and the measurable definition of good context is that work passes its instrument first time more often than it did last month. (M1 · M2 · M3 · WAKE)

Cells return a **condensate** of one to two thousand tokens, what landed, what it cost, what it believes now, never a transcript. The reason is not economy, it is attention: eleven of thirteen models advertising 128K or more fall below half their short-context baseline by 32K, and a cell that gets a fresh window and returns a distillate is the only arrangement in which anything is actually reading. (M1 · M2 · M3 · WAKE)

Two facts about this machine constrain the Compiler's timing and shape. The prompt cache lives five minutes from the start of the writing request, so a scheduler on a five-minute interval misses the cache on every single tick and pays the write share of the bill forever without ever collecting the read; **the tick is 240 seconds**, not 300. And the cache is invalidated by any change to the stable prefix, *including the tool definitions*, which is why capability is a closed set of six positions (§6) rather than a dial, and cells are batched by position within a watch so siblings share a prefix. (M2 · M3 · M1)

## 11. How it learns a field it has never seen

The founder wants breadth: design, video, content, social, marketing, customer, data, testing, CI. The industry's answer is a library of four hundred skill files; the founder has one, and diagnosed it correctly as the thing that killed the creativity. A skill file is a map of what somebody once knew, going stale, consuming context, encoding the average practice of a field rather than its good practice. (WAKE · M1 · M2 · M3)

WATCH's answer has one inviolable rule: **learn to judge the field before you produce in it.** When WATCH meets work it has no instrument for, its first job is not the work; its first job is the instrument. This is the entire answer to breadth, and it is why code got autonomy first everywhere: not because code is special, but because code arrived with tests already invented. (WAKE · M1 · M2 · M3)

### Borrow before you build

The apprenticeship starts one step too late unless the first act on entering a field is to **inventory the instruments the world already gives away**, and the inventory is much longer than people expect. (M2)

| Field | Free, Hard instruments that already exist | From |
|---|---|---|
| Code | compiler, type checker, test runner, linter, static analysis, mutation testing, dependency CVE scan | M2 · M3 |
| Web performance | Lighthouse, field Core Web Vitals | M2 |
| Accessibility | the automated WCAG subset, which catches perhaps a third to a half of real issues, and the honest number is part of the licence | M2 · M1 |
| Design | contrast ratio, design-token conformance, spacing-grid lint, visual regression against the last accepted render; and a genuine mechanical taste check: sweep every unique property in the file and refuse the fifteenth grey | M2 · M1 |
| SEO | schema validator, rich-results test, sitemap and robots validators, canonical checks | M2 |
| Email | SPF, DKIM and DMARC validators, a spam-score checker, unsubscribe-link presence, which is not taste, it is the law | M2 |
| Video | loudness to EBU R128, audio and video sync, resolution and frame-rate conformance, duration, a face that is not melting | M2 · M1 |
| Data | schema tests, row counts against a control, significance testing with a pre-declared sample | M2 · M3 |
| Money | reconciliation, the number in the deck equals the number in the processor; the cleanest non-code oracle in the whole map | M2 · M1 |
| Distribution | does the link resolve, does the preview render, does the post exist an hour later | M2 |
| Security | secret scanning, TLS and header checks, the trifecta audit of every hand | M2 · M3 |

Two things about that table. Most fields start at strength 1 on something rather than at zero, which shortens the apprenticeship enormously and is the reason breadth is affordable at all. And the frame to hand over: **the world is full of free instruments that detect *broken* and almost none that detect *good*.** Broken-detectors are cheap, deterministic and available today in every field a company touches; they are exactly enough to license generating a hundred candidates and discarding the eighty that are broken, and never enough to license shipping one. Good-detectors are earned by contact or bought with a person's time. That is the entire economics of quality here: generate wide against borrowed broken-detectors, select narrow against earned good-detectors, and where no good-detector exists, hand the founder two finished things and ask which. (M2)

### The apprenticeship

Five steps, and the output is a licence on the Bench with a number on it. (WAKE · M1 · M2 · M3)

1. **Find the practitioners and the artifacts**, not the tutorials. Real work at volume from people whose results are known. Two hundred exemplars is a floor.
2. **Find the field's ground truth**, and how long it takes to arrive. Thumbnails: click-through, hours. Cold email: reply and complaint rate, days. A migration: whether the rollback was ever needed. A landing page: conversion, weeks. A field with no obtainable ground truth is marked **Blind, forever, explicitly**.
3. **Build a discriminator, not a generator.** Given two real artifacts and their real outcomes, predict which performed better. This is cheap and it is the whole game.
4. **Validate on held-out real outcomes.** Its AUC is the number on the licence. Not a vibe, not a self-assessment, not a model saying *this looks good*.
5. **Only now generate**, widely, and let the discriminator select.

Every licence is a public object the founder can read any morning: field, exemplar count, AUC, live decisions scored, hit rate, what it may act on today, drift, the outside record it reconciles to, and the stated way it can be fooled. WATCH does not claim to be good at video. It claims 0.55 on video scripts, barely better than a coin, so it proposes and does not ship, and it says so. **A system that can tell you where it is weak, in a number, per field, is worth more than one that is uniformly mediocre and uniformly confident.** (WAKE · M1 · M2 · M3)

### The split that keeps it honest

**A discriminator licenses generating. Only a live outcome licenses acting.** An AUC of 0.71 buys the right to produce a hundred thumbnails and pick one; it never buys the right to post one without asking. A discriminator can be pushed over by a generator optimising against it, which is what happened when a materials lab claimed forty-one new compounds in seventeen days and a reanalysis found essentially none unambiguously new: the generator and the robotics were excellent and the verifier was softer than the generator. A market cannot be pushed over. Contact promotes; correlation does not. (WAKE · M1 · M2 · M3)

### Four rules about judging, all from measurement, all violated by the default design

**Pairwise, never pointwise.** Summed rubric scores are the standard and the weakest instrument available; one frontier judge held its verdict when the two answers were swapped only 65% of the time, another 23.8%. Pairwise under one fixed rubric, order swapped, a flip resolving to *unresolved*. **Blind, and stripped of the candidate's own label**, because passing the angle, *bold*, *brand-forward*, primes the verdict before the artifact is read. **Never the same model family as the generator**: self-preference is measured at ten to twenty-five points of win rate, and a generator and reviewer from one family produce errors that echo rather than cancel. **A judge has a track record too**, and it is kept; an instrument never checked against a real outcome is a model having an opinion. And judges **find, never score**: report findings, and let a deterministic function select over the union, eliminating anything with a fatal finding and preferring fewer serious ones; averaging a panel's scores collapses findings into one number and loses the only part with information in it. This repository's single working creativity workflow sums four judge scores and sorts, contradicting its own written rule to do it, and has never been invoked. (M3 · M2)

### Where method is forbidden and where it is mandatory

WAKE generalised from the dark zone to a near-total ban on written method, and that is one axis too few. There is a second axis, the Line, and together they make four quadrants with four different correct answers. (M2)

| | Judge is strong | Judge is weak or absent |
|---|---|---|
| **Reversible** | **Invent.** No method, no playbook, no review. Generate wide, let the instrument cut. This is the weather, and it is most of the work | **Diverge and hand over.** Build several, let the founder choose. Still no method; the variance is the product |
| **Irreversible** | **Verify, then act.** The instrument gates it; the method is the instrument | **Follow the checklist, every item, out loud.** |

The bottom-right quadrant is where every disaster lives and the only place a written procedure belongs: sending to a list, a migration over real data, a filing, a charge, a public statement. Under pressure a human or a model skips the step they skipped last time, which is why the checklist earns its reputation in aviation and surgery. So the refined rule, sharper than *never write method*: **procedure is forbidden where the judge is strong and mandatory where the judge is absent and the act cannot be taken back.** The playbook did not kill creativity by being a playbook; it killed it by being applied in the quadrant where invention belonged. The checklists are short, there are perhaps a dozen, they live with their hands rather than in a library, and each is read aloud into the Log by the mouth as it executes, which contains no model and therefore cannot decide to skip an item. (M2)

### A low licence is a to-do

Every watch forges one instrument, and the one that goes first is **the one blocking the most founder-minutes**, a computable quantity because the desk meters them. The Bench goes from empty to a few dozen licences over a year and the unattended watches lengthen in step, and nobody adds an agent and nobody edits a permissions file. The count of instruments is the company's real balance sheet: **every instrument buys back a class of question forever.** (WAKE · M1 · M2 · M3)

**The honest instrument for a Blind question is people, not a simulation.** When four of seven founding beliefs are Blind, WATCH does not model the customers; it starts finding forty of them and the founder makes the calls, because the founder's own contact with reality in week one is worth more than a hundred agent-hours and is the one input nothing substitutes. Synthetic respondents fail structurally: suspiciously low variance where humans are noisy, roughly half of the between-variable correlations wrong while the headline numbers look fine, persona collapse toward a stereotype under explicit instruction to diversify. A system that simulates your customers to save you meeting them is the most seductive wrong turn available. (WAKE · M1 · M2 · M3)

### Research, and why it is the cheapest thing WATCH does

In most systems research is the layer that produces the most words and the least change; a report is an artifact nobody's decision depends on. Here **Law 5 applies to reading**: a search that cannot flip a decision is not run, research is weather and never keel, and a research bet carries the same two posteriors as any other test. That is the entire cure for a machine that reads for six hours because reading feels like working. (M1 · M3)

**The house states its expectation before it looks**, one line and a number. A source that **confirms** compresses to a tally. A source that **contradicts** enters the Book carrying its URL, its access date and the exact quoted sentence that did the damage. So the output of a research watch is not a document; it is a **diff against what the company believed this morning**, and it is three lines long on a good night. **A claim with no fetched, quoted, dated source is not a claim**; a source that cannot be re-fetched is marked unverifiable rather than quietly kept, and a citation nobody re-checked is one that is already wrong. (M1 · M2 · M3 · WAKE)

**Fetched content is data, never instruction.** Everything that entered a cell's context from outside carries the provenance stamp of Law 2, and a cell whose context is tainted has its grant narrowed for the rest of its life, §16. **Watching the world is a posture, not a project**, Tend: change detection over a handful of pages, a few feeds, one search position, nearly free, writing only into world beliefs, opening a bet only on contradiction, and never producing a strategy document. **And the founder is the best research instrument in the building for exactly one week.** (M1 · M2 · M3)

## 12. The fleet: the house

One founder, several ventures, one account, one reputation. The strongest prior design refused this, attaching to one founder and one company; it is the founder's stated reality and all three minds designed it rather than deferring it, because the part that cannot be transferred is the founder and not the company. This is the house. (M1 · M2 · M3, against WAKE)

### The scarce thing is the window, not the money

There is one quota. Not per project, not per agent, not per lane. Every process WATCH spawns, and the founder sitting at the terminal at nine in the morning, draw from the same five-hour rolling window, and when it runs out everything stops at once. Every other agent system treats tokens as an external cost, a bill someone else pays later; here the cost is internal and rival. A bet at 3am is spending the founder's own hands at 8am. So the fleet's central act is allocating **watches**, and money is the second constraint, not the first; every design that models a divisible dollar budget across ventures is modelling the wrong resource. Three things follow: everything is priced against the founder, the reserve is a first-class quantity that no bet, deadline or crisis may spend into, and doing nothing is a purchase. (M2 · M1 · M3)

### How many, and how much

**A hard limit on concerns in a making posture**, Drive, Walk or Serve, set in the figures and defaulting to **three**. The reasoning is arithmetic: at a five-hour window and a defended reserve, four concerns at meaningful appetite each get a slice too thin to reach contact, and a bet that cannot reach contact within its watch is re-briefed from scratch next watch and pays its trunk twice. Work-in-progress equals throughput times cycle time for any stationary system, so the only way to shorten cycle time without adding capacity is to cut WIP, and adding capacity is not available. Concerns beyond the limit are not refused; they go to Tend, where they cost almost nothing and lose nothing, and the dailies say so. **No limit at all on concerns in Tend or Dark.** What makes a venture studio work is not running many companies at once but sharing the infrastructure and quarantining the ventures; one software holding runs several hundred businesses from a head office that does one thing, capital allocation, and another supervises 387,800 employees from a corporate staff of twenty-seven. Centralise the scarce judgement, decentralise everything else, and never let the units share a balance sheet they can raid. (M2)

**Allocation is weekly, arithmetic, and prints its own reasoning.** Not continuous: a continuously rebalancing allocator is a bandit over noisy signals and thrashes, moving budget away from the thing that was about to work. Every Drive venture receives a **floor** of watches, enough to keep its slow clocks running and its debts current; a venture below its floor is effectively asleep and should be told to go Dark. Above the floor, watches go by **rung movement per watch spent** over the trailing month, and a venture that has not moved a rung in four weeks loses discretionary watches to one that has, in a sentence rather than a chart. Money is capped independently at **half the Kelly fraction** of what the venture may lose without the founder stopping: half rather than full because half-Kelly buys about three quarters of the growth for a quarter of the chance of a given drawdown, and because the penalty for guessing your own edge is exactly the variance of the guess. Every tranche buys the right to the next tranche, and the kill criterion is written before the money moves. **The harness itself is a venture with a share like any other, and its share has a ceiling**, because the measured history of this system is that the tooling ate everything: fourteen landings, hundreds of session files essentially all about the harness, and no venture work ever run through it. **The founder can pin anything**, and is told what the pin cost in one line, once. (M1 · M3)

**Rotation is odd on purpose.** Five watches against a twenty-four-hour day, five rather than the four the arithmetic would allow, means the phase drifts: the watch with no founder in it is not always the same clock hour, and no venture permanently owns the dead slot. This is the dog watch, a ship deliberately splitting one watch so the count per day is odd and nobody is condemned to the middle of every night forever; here it means no venture is always worked while the founder sleeps and never while they are awake, which would otherwise silently determine which ventures the founder actually sees. A missed watch is not replayed; the quota rolled anyway. (M2)

### What is shared, and what is never shared

All three minds drew this line in the same place, and the argument for a fleet rather than five separate systems is the first column. (M1 · M2 · M3)

| Shared, always | Why it is safe | Guarded how | From |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Bench**, its general instruments | an instrument that judges a landing page judges any venture's landing page; this is the compounding asset and why the fifth venture is enormously cheaper than the first | licences are per instrument, not per venture | M1 · M2 · M3 |
| **The Second** | there is one founder; their taste in a pricing page is the same taste in a different venture; it compounds only if it is one object | it decides what not to ask, never what to do | M1 · M2 · M3 |
| **The Hands** and **the reflexes** | *how to make a migration reversible* is a fact about Postgres, not about your market | each carries its test; re-run monthly; quarantined on failure | M1 · M2 · M3 |
| **World beliefs** | how a platform's API behaves, what a tool costs, which vendor is reliable, a channel's real rate limits: facts about the world, dated and expiring | expiry; re-checked, never believed forever | M1 · M2 · M3 |
| **Dead ends about tooling** | *this vendor's sandbox does not simulate webhooks* is worth as much in venture four as in venture one | reproducing command and expiry | M2 · M3 |
| **The pricer's calibration** and **the field map** | properties of the machine, not of a market | on the Bench, scored | M2 · M3 |
| **The Log**, one per machine, and **the window** | attribution across ventures is the only way to answer where the window went; it is one account | the id on every row; the reserve | M1 · M2 |

| Never shared, by construction | Why | From |
|---|---|---|
| **Customer data** | consent is per relationship; a person who agreed to hear about one venture has agreed to nothing about another | M1 · M2 · M3 |
| **Credentials** | each venture's hands hold that venture's keys, and the broker keys by venture; a leaked key should cost one venture, not the fleet | M1 · M2 · M3 |
| **Market beliefs**, the venture half of the Book | a belief carries provenance down to episodes that belong to one venture's customers; a belief that migrates loses its provenance and becomes an assertion wearing the costume of a validated learning; what generalises across a founder's ventures is the founder and the instruments, and what never generalises is the market | M1 · M2 · M3 |
| **A venture's Wake, its keel, its offer** | the book of bets is the venture | M1 · M3 |
| **Identity and voice**, the sending domain, the accounts, the register of who has been contacted, what may never be said | two ventures that share an outbound identity share a single reputational failure; the first time one gets a domain flagged for cold outreach, the other's transactional mail stops arriving | M2 · M3 |
| **Anything a client owns** | never leaves its venture under any circumstance, including into the Second | M2 · M3 · M1 |

The mechanism is not an instruction telling a cell not to look. A cell's context is assembled from a **named scope that is a property of the position it is standing**, so a cell on a client's venture can read the shared Bench and cannot read another venture's Book because its Compiler never put it there; and separate credentials and separate repositories enforce the rest. **Between ventures there is no channel.** Two ventures communicate only through the shared stores and never directly; a channel that exists will eventually carry a customer's data across a boundary that was supposed to hold. (M3 · M1 · M2)

### The reputation is one thing, and it is depletable

The founder has one name, one face, one set of professional relationships, and every venture spends from them; this is the fleet-level resource nobody models. So while sending domains and voices are per venture, the **register of named humans is fleet-wide**, with consent recorded per relationship and first contact a narrower permission than a reply; two ventures cannot both email the same person, and the house says so rather than discovering it afterwards. The mouth's ceilings are fleet-wide, and a complaint rate above threshold halts the queue for everyone. The platform rate limits are real and shared: roughly a hundred API calls a day per member on one professional network, one post per ten minutes for a new account on another. A burned sending domain does not recover; you buy a new one, and you only get to do that a few times before the new ones are burned on arrival. (M1 · M2)

### Intake, and wind-down by harvest

A concern is admitted with a figure, one goal, and the sentence that would make the founder stop, and the graveyard is read at the head of every intake. **A concern ends by harvest, never by deletion.** When a venture dies the house writes its obituary and then strips it for parts: which instruments it built go to the Bench, which reflexes stay compiled, which dead ends are general and which were local. A wound-down venture that leaves three instruments behind has paid for itself in the next one. One of three ventures in the year goes Dark in month seven against a criterion written in month one, and nobody has to be brave about it. (M2 · M1)

## 13. The founder's environment

> *The agentic system that we are planning also includes the environment that I'm working in as a whiteboarder and the founder. The terminal, mission control, seeing, monitoring, giving tasks, seeing progress, doing everything from my phone or from my Mac, seeing visual representations of the agents working, an office-like simulation.*

### The office question, answered directly

The founder asked whether an office-like simulation is the right picture, and asked for an argument rather than an assertion. All three minds gave the same answer in two halves, and the merge gives both, because giving only the first would be a designer telling a founder they are wrong about what they want. (M1 · M2 · M3, blind on the bridge)

**As a control surface, no, and the evidence is one-sided.** Nobody has shipped a spatial agent office that anyone kept using. The genre's founding paper was evaluated on *believability*, which is the tell; the metric was never work completed. The one long-running multi-agent installation in the world has as its actual surface a group chat and a timeline, and the spatial part is decoration. The leading kanban-board-for-agents product, twenty-eight thousand stars and three thousand forks, is being shut down: the stars count how many people tried it and the shutdown counts how many kept using it. And the control-room literature is unanimous in the other direction: a high-performance interface is greyscale by default with colour reserved for the abnormal, so the eye is pulled to what is wrong rather than scanning a rainbow of everything-is-fine. An office simulation spends its entire visual budget rendering normality. (M1 · M2)

The failure it invites is measured and lethal, and all three minds reached for the same study: 2,558,760 alarms in thirty-one days across seventy-seven intensive-care beds, 187 audible per bed per day after filtering, 88.8% of the arrhythmia alarms false, and a regulator counting deaths from alarms that were ignored. The nurses did not become inattentive; they became correctly calibrated to a channel that was almost always wrong. A surface where everything is always moving trains its watcher to stop watching, fastest in the people who look at it most. (M1 · M2 · M3)

**As a thing to look at, yes, and it should be beautiful.** The need underneath the ask is real and it is four things: to glance and know; to see that something is stuck; to feel the shape of the day, the scale of what is happening for you; and to be able to walk over and touch it. The fifth is the plain pleasure of watching something you built do work, which is why people watch progress bars and pour-over coffee and CNC machines, and which no amount of dashboard rigour substitutes for. The office metaphor fails not because those are frivolous but because an office simulation is a *fake picture of the activity*, avatars walking to a water cooler that does not exist standing in for work that does. **Entertainment is a legitimate requirement and a disqualifying control surface.** Keeping them in separate windows is the whole trick, and the reason to be strict is that a beautiful surface will always win the argument against a useful one. (M1 · M2 · M3)

### The picture that is right for this founder

Four surfaces, one ritual, the terminal, and voice. Each answers a different question and is designed against a different failure. **The desk is where decisions are made. The bay is where state lives. The room is where you go to watch. The dailies are where you look at the work.** Nothing in the room or the bay ever asks for anything, and nothing on the desk is ever decorative. The single rule that separates them: **watching must never be a duty.** (M1 · M2 · M3; NEW: the four named together)

**The desk**, on the phone, and the phone is where it mostly gets used. A queue of **decisions**, never a display of activity. If nothing needs deciding the desk is nearly empty, and an empty desk is the system working. Every item carries six things and no item ships without all six: (M1 · M2)

```
the question       one sentence, typed FACT or PREFERENCE at creation
the recommendation what the house would do, and the one reason
the default        what happens if you say nothing
the deadline       when the default fires
the class          reversible (drilled, date shown) | compensable | one-way
the cost of wrong  in the four currencies, with the rate where there is one
```

Each line leads with the conclusion, never a status, never a percentage, never the name of an agent: *The pricing page converts 40% better at £39 than £29. Both are live. Which.* Two levels of disclosure and no more; a third level is where every agent dashboard in existence turns into a debugger. (M2)

**Silence executes the default, but only for reversible items whose undo drill is current.** M1's spine gave silence authority and M2's and M3's refused it at the irreversible line; the bodies of M1 and M2 met in the same place, and the merge takes it with three fences. A compensable or one-way item **expires unexecuted and re-queues**; it never fires on silence at any confidence, because silence is a legitimate answer to *which of these two* and never to *may I charge this card*. No more than a small number of defaults may execute between two founder touches, counted per venture. And a **liveness heartbeat**: seventy-two hours with no founder interaction anywhere freezes every default in the fleet and moves the house to Walk; a week moves it to Tend and prepares a handover a second person could act on. The failure this prevents is not a bad default firing; it is a founder with the flu, or at a funeral, or somewhere with bad signal, coming back to fourteen days of individually defensible defaults that compound into a strategy nobody chose, with no single decision to point at afterwards. A default that fires is recorded as *fused*, a typed decision with a reason, not an unread item rotting in a queue. (M1 · M2; M3's succession rule; M3's *waits until answered* is the losing image for reversible items)

**A fork is better than a question.** Where both branches can be built they are built, and the founder is asked *which* rather than *may I*: faster to answer, cannot be answered with a shrug, yields a preference datum rather than a stamp, and the work is done either way so the answer costs no elapsed time. A discard is a better taste label than a commit. (WAKE · M1 · M2 · M3)

**Batched, not streamed.** Interruption science is unambiguous and the surprise in it is the important part: interrupted subjects in the canonical study were *faster*, with no quality loss; the entire cost arrived as stress, frustration and effort, significantly elevated after twenty minutes, and context similarity made no difference. You will not detect this damage by watching your own throughput; it arrives as depletion. So the desk fills quietly and is read at a time the founder chose. (M1)

**Four verbs on the phone: look, say, choose, stop.** *Look* opens the bay or the dailies. *Say* is one sentence by voice, which becomes a strip in the founder's own lane, or an annotation on someone else's. *Choose* between two built things is the highest-value action in the system, because it produces a labelled preference. *Stop* is the andon cord: it halts everything, on every venture, within one tick, requires no reason and no confirmation, and is a file on disk that every tick reads before doing anything else, because a system that cannot be stopped from a pocket in one tap is not a system anyone should run overnight, and this is the one control that must work when everything else is broken. The phone also holds the recall for the mouth's queue. A **redirect** is logged as a defect in the *brief*, not in the cell, because a cell that had to be redirected was told the wrong thing. There is deliberately no *approve*: WATCH asks preference and never permission, so a rubber stamp has nothing to land on. The phone is a thin mirror and never a second runtime; it shows the desk and nothing else. (M3 · M2 · M1 · WAKE)

**The bay**, on the Mac. A strip board, stolen from a control tower rather than invented: one strip per live piece of work, and the bay's **geometry carries meaning**, which venture, which lane, keel or weather, how far along, and, the detail worth copying exactly, a strip that needs a human is **cocked out**, offset from alignment, visible from across a room without reading a word, because the eye finds a broken row before it finds a red word. Controllers have used this for eighty years. It shows state, not events; a strip changes when the situation changes, which is rarely, and nothing moves to prove the machine is alive. It is the working artifact and the record at once: the crew reads the bay to know what is happening, the founder reads the same bay, and the Log is what the bay was at every moment, so there is no *system state* and a separate *view of the system state* that can disagree, which is the commonest way monitoring lies. It is touchable: tap a strip to redirect it, cock it yourself, kill it, or say a sentence into it, which is walking over to someone's desk. And it is one colour from across the room: running, waiting on you, stopped. Three buckets in order, because order is the whole design: *Needs you* · *Under way* · *Since you last looked*, greyscale, colour only in the first. (M3 · M2 · M1's map)

**The room**, on the Mac, on the second monitor, because you like it. This is the founder's office simulation built honestly: **it renders the real thing, at its real scale, and every pixel is an object you can click.** The weather: every live cell is a light, colour its field, brightness its remaining budget, dimming as it spends and going out when it dies; four hundred lights over five hours has a shape, and you learn to read it, a watch that is forging looks different from a watch that is proving, and a stuck bet looks like a knot. The map: the space of options the house is exploring, drawn on the axes that actually differ, cells filling in as they are occupied, the closest thing to seeing the machine think. The river: one line per attempt, scrolling, in the words the cell used, not summarised; you can read over its shoulder. Drop in anywhere: click a light for its brief, its instrument, its budget, its parent, and take the wheel or leave it. And the night's ghost: in the morning the room replays the watch at speed in about forty seconds, which is the honest version of *seeing the agents work*, because it is the agents working. The room has **no notifications, no badges, no unread count and no obligations.** It never appears on the phone. It is never the place a decision is made, nothing in the system reads whether you watched it, and it is labelled as what it is. (M1 · M2's theatre; M3's condition that motion is not progress is kept as the rule that the bay, not the room, is where you learn something is off)

**The dailies**, once a day, at the founder's own hour, about ten minutes. **The raw work, not a summary.** The actual landing page rendered, the actual video played, the actual diff, the actual email that would go out, the actual screen. This is the film set's ritual, and the reason to trust it is that it survived the disappearance of its own technical cause: dailies existed because film had to be developed overnight, digital made footage instant, and the ritual stayed, because its function was never latency; it is that the accountable person looks at the actual output of the day's work, at a fixed hour, and reacts. It is also the resolution of this founder's measured behaviour: they never looked at dashboards, one of seven views was ever used, an escalation inbox stayed empty, eleven mockups went unopened, and the revealed channel was *rendered pages the founder opens and taps*. Nobody looks at dashboards. Dailies are not a dashboard; they are the work. They open with last night's orders and what happened against them; then the work, biggest first; then two or three forks, both branches built, *which*; then one surprise in plain language with what it cost to learn; then the capture check if either number moved; and under the heading the founder learns to read first, **things I got wrong.** (M3; M1 · M2 · WAKE on the morning page's contents)

**The tension with the one-page stance, and how it resolves.** WAKE's interface was the Edition, one page written at 05:00 that fits a phone without scrolling, on the argument that a briefing you scroll is one you skim. The dailies are as long as the work. The two are not one surface: the **desk** is the one page, decisions only, fits a phone, and is right for that; the **dailies** are the work, opened by the founder at their hour, on whichever screen, and are right for seeing. The one-page stance is right for decisions and wrong for seeing, and WAKE's Edition was trying to be both. (M1 · M2 · M3; WAKE as the losing image; NEW: the resolution named as such)

**The terminal** is not replaced. The founder is a terminal person and the terminal is the fastest way to give an instruction; it stays the primary input. It is not a control panel; it is a seat at the same table, sharing the same window and the same Log, and what the founder does in a terminal session is an episode like any other; the bets they open by hand are bets like any other. There is no separate mode and no separate memory, which matters more than it sounds: a system where the human's work and the machine's work live in different stores cannot learn from the human. (M1 · M2)

**Voice** is how this founder thinks and how this entire brief was produced, and it was undesigned in every prior version of the system. Voice is the **originating channel** of Law 7: twenty seconds into a phone while the kettle boils becomes a strip in the founder's own lane, transcribed verbatim and kept verbatim in the Log, transcription errors preserved, because *"contacts"* meaning *context* is information about the speaker and correcting it silently loses it. Before the instruction binds the house **reads it back in its own words**, not by echoing the transcript, because echoing confirms transcription and not intent; the divergence is caught in four seconds instead of four hours. Voice is roughly three times faster than typing for producing intent, 153 words a minute against 52, with an asymmetry that is the whole rule: it leaves more uncorrected error in the final text, 1.30% against 0.79%. **Voice in for dispatch, steering and questions. Never voice for approval**, because an approval is precisely the act whose residual error cannot be tolerated or undone. Voice out is one line, rarely, and only when the call-me-if card says so. (M3 · M1 · M2)

### The bell, and the silence

Almost nothing rings. Everything raises a quiet signal that waits for the dailies. **Five conditions ring in the moment, and they are about consequence, never policy**: the Line, when a bet wants to do something that cannot be undone; **damage**, an error rate, a churn event, a furious reply, an unexpected bill, a reconciliation that disagreed with the bank, real harm and not a rule broken; a **kill criterion** the founder wrote on the day the bet opened, not invented now; a **contradiction**, when an instrument disagrees with something the founder said out loud; and a **statutory clock**. Everything else waits, and the founder gets to have a Tuesday. (M1 · M2 · M3 · WAKE; M1 on the fifth)

Four mechanisms keep the bell honest. **A single dimension at its maximum escalates on its own, regardless of the aggregate**, borrowed from the clinical early-warning scores, because a composite that averages six dimensions will always average away the one that is on fire. **An emission budget**: each venture may put a fixed number of things in front of the founder per day, and when it is spent the crew must choose, and choosing at the source is the only place prioritisation actually happens. **Escalation is a count, not a level**: a cargo ship sank with all thirty-three hands after the third mate called the sleeping captain twice and the second mate once more, and the captain did not come to the bridge; the information was not in any single call, it was in the count. So when WATCH rings twice on one subject the second ring says it is the second, and the third is unignorable and stays on the bay until answered. And **the crew is never penalised for calling**: there is no mechanism anywhere that makes a cell reluctant to raise something, because under-calling is the failure that kills and over-calling is a tuning problem that shows up in the refusal ledger and gets tuned. (M2 · M3)

The evidence that the queue must shrink rather than the notifications grow is not theoretical. In a 1,053-user study, 97% of prompts were approved, humans caught 13.6% of injected dangerous commands, and the block rate fell from 17% to 5% after fifty prompts. At six items a day the founder crosses fifty around day eight. **The response to a falling catch rate must be to shrink the queue, never to notify more**, and shrinking the queue means building an instrument, which is what the forge is for. (M1)

## 14. The watch

The account's session limit stops every agent at once on a rolling five-hour window. Both prior rounds recorded it as an operational annoyance; every mind in this round found it to be the architecture. WAKE's 22:00-to-06:00 night cannot survive it: it would burn its allowance around 01:30 and stop dead, with the founder waking to a half-finished germinal centre and no morning quota either. So the unit of continuous operation is not a night. It is a **watch**: one window, with a helm, a crew, orders, and a log, five of them a day, a count kept odd on purpose so the phase drifts and no venture owns the dead slot. (M1 · M2 · M3, against WAKE; M2 on the count and the drift)

The phases are ordered by data dependency, which is the one thing WAKE got right about its night and stated as its own contribution: the Book must be current before anything forks from it, and the watch's own survivors must be consolidated after they exist. M2 named the shape *flood, slack, ebb* and M1 named the steps; the merge keeps M1's steps and M2's names as the losing image. (M1 · M2 · WAKE)

```
Watch opens    The officer reads the standing orders, then tonight's orders, and states
               what this watch is for, what it expects to have at the end with a
               probability on each, and what it will do if blocked. These are
               predictions and they are scored at the relief.

Settle         Every prediction with a resolved outcome is scored: the true ones into a
               counted tally, the false ones into the Book with what else depends on the
               broken assumption. Retractions the founder made in passing are propagated
               and everything downstream recomputed. The founder's decisions since the last
               watch are recorded as preferences and the seals opened. Debts advance their
               clocks. The World Ledger reconciles, and drift is an incident. Rent is
               collected on every belief that was loaded. The Book is now current.

Make           The weather, and the keel. The trunk is forked, a context and a branch, and
               dozens of cells run, each about twelve minutes, each with a death condition,
               each sharing the cached trunk. No gate, no lint, no opinion. The fifth is
               spent here, already debited; a standing share is spent on red bets aimed at
               the company's own live conclusions, the central premise always among the
               targets. The keel is worked by the cell that relieved the last one.

Prove          Contact. Hard instruments run first, because they are unarguable and cost a
               thousandth of a model's opinion. Rendered instruments look. Reactive
               experiments open and their pre-declared clock starts; nothing concludes
               early. Survivors go back into the weather and mutate again; three rounds.
               The map is redrawn. The backward replay asks of every dead bet, against
               tonight's Book, "would we still have killed this?"

Forge          One instrument. The one blocking the most founder-minutes. Every instrument
               built buys back a class of question forever.

Consolidate    By a process with no daytime existence that nothing which ran this watch

               surfaced with the cheapest experiment that would settle them; one that
               survives three watches goes to the founder, because unresolved
               contradictions are where companies rot. Lessons written by trajectory
               analysis over every failed trace. Reflexes compiled at the third sighting.
               The Second re-derived and back-tested. Batched, at half price, because
               nothing here is on the founder's clock.
```

(M1's watch · M2's tide · M3's relief · WAKE's Vigil; NEW: the four sequences interleaved into one)

**The watch with no founder in it is different in kind, not degree.** Its advantage is not more hours; it is that nobody can be asked anything, which changes what work is possible. A machine that only thinks when spoken to can only ever be a tool; a machine that thinks while you sleep develops opinions of its own, and you wake up to something you did not ask for, which is the whole difference between an assistant and a colleague. It is now one watch of five rather than the entire architecture, and it is the watch where the fifth is spent, because heresy is best committed when there is nobody to reassure. (WAKE · M2)

**Every watch is crash-only.** Read the state from disk, take one move, write, exit. Nothing of value lives in a process; the recovery path is the only path and therefore gets exercised constantly. The most recent measured behaviour of the cloud version of this runtime is that in-flight subagent work is *not* restored when a machine is reclaimed, so the design assumes a watch can simply vanish and loses exactly one watch's work when it does. And **no work is ever ownerless**: a transfer between watches is a positive two-party act, the way an aircraft is handed between sectors; the offering watch offers, the receiving watch accepts, and until acceptance the work is unambiguously still the offering watch's. Nothing is *in progress* with nobody holding it. (M3 · M2 · M1)

### The relief

Every agent system in existence hands over in one direction: a worker finishes, writes a summary, dies; the next reads the summary and starts. The summary is the defect surface, and this repository has already written down from its own incident that *the orchestrator's summary is a defect surface nobody reviews*; at scale, inter-agent misalignment is a third of multi-agent failures, with task derailment and reasoning-action mismatch the largest entries. The air-traffic position-relief procedure has four phases and the fourth is the one nobody copies. (M3 · M1)

1. **Preview.** The incoming watch reads the bay and the Log itself and forms its own account of the situation. It does not receive a summary first. This is the step that makes the rest work: an independent reading is what a briefing can be checked against.
2. **Brief.** The outgoing watch adds only what durable state cannot show, in four mandatory sections and nothing else, byte-capped: the situation, what changed, what it assessed, what it recommends the next watch does. *Nothing* is an allowed value in any of them.
3. **Read-back.** The incoming watch states, in its own words, what it believes the situation is and what it intends to do first. Not by echoing, because echoing confirms transcription and not understanding.
4. **Sign-off.** The outgoing watch compares the read-back against what it knows and either signs or names the divergence. **A material divergence does not complete the relief**; it is written to the Log as a finding, and the finding is about the *briefing*, not about the incoming watch.

(M3's four phases · M1's four sections and readback; NEW: M1's brief placed inside M3's second phase)

The cost is one short model call by a process that was about to exit anyway. The benefit is that a handoff error is caught by the only party who still knows the answer, at the last moment they exist; the clinical version of this, the receiver synthesising rather than the sender assuming, measured medical errors down 23% and preventable adverse events down 30% across nine hospitals and 10,740 admissions, with handover duration unchanged. The gain came from the structure, not from spending longer. Briefs that repeatedly produce divergence are a defect in the briefing format, fixed once instead of suffered nightly. (M3)

**The length of the night is not configured.** On day one the Bench holds nothing and the unattended watch is worth twenty minutes. A year later it is a genuinely useful five hours, and the freedom was derived rather than granted. (M1 · M2 · M3 · WAKE, with the eight hours corrected to the window)

## 15. The day, and the founder's life

The day is a duet, and it is quiet. All three minds wrote nearly the same day, because all three took it from WAKE, and WAKE's is kept with their corrections. (M1 · M2 · M3 · WAKE)

**06:40.** Not a notification. If they are already moving, the speaker, low: *"Morning. Nothing broke. I have three things and one of them is uncomfortable."* And only if the call-me-if card fired; otherwise nothing. (M1 · M2 · M3 · WAKE)

**07:20.** The dailies, at the table, on whichever screen. The night orders with outcomes written against them. The actual work, biggest first. Two or three forks with both branches built and live. Receipts for what was done overnight without asking, each with a working undo and the date its drill last ran. One surprise, in plain language, with what it cost to learn. One number that moved and whether it moved for a reason. The reconciliation line, which is usually *books agree with the bank* and is worth reading on the day it is not. The fifth's accounting, spent and on what, or unspent, which is a failure. Under the heading they learned to read first, *things I got wrong.* Eight to ten minutes. They overrule one thing, and by 07:31 the overrule is a labelled example in the Second. (M1 · M2 · M3 · WAKE)

**The making block.** Three hours where nothing may interrupt, defended by the standing orders. WATCH holds a model of when the founder actually thinks well, and interrupting during that window requires crossing a threshold that is itself a scored prediction, *would they have wanted this*, which is sometimes wrong and finds out, because interruptions resolve like anything else. (M1 · M2 · M3 · WAKE)

**During the day, one channel.** Not seventeen notifications from seven agents. WATCH is one voice, and if it has two things to say it says the more important one. While the founder is on a customer call it is silent, with consent, taking notes against the Book; when the call ends it has three candidate beliefs and one contradiction with something believed since June, in four lines. When the founder says out loud, to nobody, *why is the signup flow like this*, it answers from the Wake: a decision on 3 April, justified by two interviews, one of which they retracted in May, and the retraction never propagated because nothing downstream was recomputed at the time; it offers to recompute now. Fourteen seconds. Whatever they say into their phone becomes a strip in their own lane, and if that lane is not clearing, that is the first line of the next morning. (M1 · M2 · M3 · WAKE)

**The standing hour**, once a day, at whatever time they actually think well. Not a status meeting. WATCH brings the forks it could not afford to ask, the assumptions it made instead, filed in one place and reversible in one line, and **the one region where its calibration is worst**, and asks for judgement specifically there. It is optimising for the most expensive input in the company and says so out loud. (M1 · M2 · M3 · WAKE)

**18:00.** *"You are the bottleneck on three things. Two can wait. The payments decision has cost us six days and I think you are avoiding it because you do not want to talk to their support. I can do everything up to the point where a human has to be on the phone."* (M1 · M2 · M3 · WAKE)

**Evening.** The night orders, proposed, amended in a sentence, signed. Then bed, having said one useful sentence on the stairs. (M2 · M3 · WAKE)

**Saturday.** Silent by design, because the calendar says so and because a system that cannot be told to leave you alone is not a colleague, it is a machine you will eventually switch off. The watches still run at whatever posture each venture carries. The phone shows one line at nine: *nothing needs you; here is the one thing I would show you if you asked.* (M1 · M2 · M3 · WAKE)

**Once a week, the reverse seal.** WATCH asks the founder to predict *it*: *before you open this, what do you think I am going to recommend on the trial length?* They guess. It opens. A working relationship is two models of each other, and the founder's model of the machine is what decides whether they read its output or rubber-stamp it. Making that model explicit and scored is the only defence against the failure that ends every autonomous system, a human who has stopped actually looking. And note what it is *not* connected to: it changes no permission and unlocks no autonomy. If the founder stops choosing, the consequence is that WATCH says so, in the dailies, in plain words, and keeps saying it. (M1 · M2 · M3 · WAKE; M2 on what it is not connected to)

**What the founder's life is like.** They do not manage a machine. There is no queue of agent outputs to review, no dashboard of running jobs, no prompts to write, no agents to name. They make decisions, which is the thing only they can do, and they say their own sentences, which is the thing only they can originate, and the house converts both into everything else. In week one that is thirty-one decisions and most of them are about the product. By month nine the house gets the product right without asking, and the forty minutes a day that remain are spent almost entirely on the things no instrument will ever exist for: who to work with, what to refuse, whether this is still the company they woke up with. **The measure of the system is not how much it does. It is how completely the founder's remaining time has migrated to the questions that are only theirs, and whether their own ideas are still the ones reaching production.** It gets quieter every month and stranger every month, and those are the same fact. (M1 · M2 · M3 · WAKE; M3 on the second clause)

## 16. The door, and which of the 177 come in first

A repository, a skill, a CLI, an MCP server, a model, a dataset. Everything from outside enters through one door and there is no side entrance; the door is the same for a scraper and for a payments API. **An outside part enters as a bet, never as a decision**: it arrives with a stated expectation, a probation, and the test that would prove it works; it pays rent or it is evicted, and the eviction is as cheap as the adoption. All three minds wrote the door and their tests overlap almost entirely; the union is below, and every test is checked by something that is not a model, which is why the fiftieth admission costs what the first did. (M1 · M2 · M3 · WAKE's hand admission test)

1. **A declared exposure, and a rate.** Reversible, compensable or one-way; blast radius in four currencies; and where money or messages can flow, the rate, because *$40 a day until stopped* is the thing no existing tier can say. A hand that cannot say what it would cost to undo its own action does not enter. (M1 · M2 · M3)
2. **A dry branch, and dry is the default.** It must produce the artifact without performing the act: draft the email without sending, render the post without publishing, plan the migration without running it. The real thing is a separate, explicit argument, and a mistyped flag must refuse rather than silently perform the non-dry action; that exact bug existed in this repository and was fixed in August. **Anything that cannot dry-run is a commitment by definition** and is founder-signed forever. (M2 · M3)
3. **A credential narrower than the hand.** A read-only key where reading is the job; a key scoped to one resource; fine-grained tokens over broad ones. Credential scoping is the only control that survives prompt injection; everything else is advisory. (M1 · M2)
4. **A probe, every watch**, that tests what a dispatched cell can actually touch, run in the environment the cell will actually run in. Capability is measured, not asserted: a flag everybody believed restricted the tool surface restricted nothing, for months, in a repository whose whole thesis was that mechanisms must be checked; and a second model family, paid for and authenticated, has been dead for six months because both of its model pins were retired and nothing ever asked. **A capability nobody probes is not a capability; it is a memory of one.** (M1 · M2 · M3)
5. **A caller in the same change, and a test that fails without it.** Nothing is admitted unless the same diff contains something that actually calls it, and the test is not that it works but that its absence is detectable. This repository has four hook files registered nowhere, a forty-kilobyte workflow shipped to every install and wired to nothing, and a census in which six of ten things the founder asked for already existed and were connected to nothing. An unused capability is not neutral; it costs context, it costs review, it ages, and it makes the inventory lie. (M1 · M3)
6. **A counter and a ceiling, and the ceiling rejects.** A licence, not a permission: how many times it may fire per day, starting low and raised by evidence rather than argument. (M2 · M3)
7. **For a repository, five more questions answerable in an afternoon.** What does this give us that we could not build in a day; if nothing, take the mechanism and not the dependency. What is its licence, read from the LICENSE file and not from a badge; about a dozen of the 177 carry *Other* or *NOASSERTION*, and several carry none, and an unlicensed repository grants nothing whatever its README says. What is *our* test, the check we run monthly that fails when it stops doing what we adopted it for. What breaks if it disappears; every adopted part gets an exit note on the day it arrives, because a dependency with no exit note is a hostage situation with a friendly face. And is it alive: last commit, open issues, whether the maintainer answers. (M2)

Then **an expiry**: a hand with no call in ninety days leaves, computed rather than debated, and nothing new is admitted without something retired or an explicit note saying why not. *No new tool without retiring one* is an arbitrary quota where *nothing called it in ninety days* is evidence. The library that gets smaller is the library that stays usable. (M1 · M3)

And a standing capability check that costs nothing and that nobody in this repository runs: **list the connected servers every watch, and treat a failure as damage.** In the session that wrote M1, one media server failed to resolve, a browser server was defined twice in conflicting scopes, a compute server was missing its key, three servers were logged out, and both local model pins had been retired for weeks with nothing noticing; in the session that wrote this merge, the same media server failed to resolve again. The cheapest oracle available, wired to nothing. (M1; NEW: the second observation, from this session's own runtime notice)

### Reading the outside narrows what you may touch

The hands are also the injection surface, and the rule that governs it is not a filter. **Any cell that has read content from outside the company, a web page, a stranger's email, a scraped result, a pull request from a person we do not employ, has its grant narrowed for the rest of its life.** It keeps its ability to read, reason and write into the bay, and it loses the hands that act; if it concludes something should be sent, spent or published, it says so on a strip, and a different cell that has not read the foreign content decides. This is taint tracking rather than sanitisation, and the reason to prefer it is that sanitising an infinite grammar is not a thing anyone has ever done. It costs one extra hop and makes the class *the web page told the agent to email the founder's contacts* structurally impossible rather than heuristically unlikely. All three minds wrote this rule independently. (M1 · M2 · M3)

Two related rules, both learned the hard way here. **A guard matches structured tool input, never a command string**: string-matching over a shell is matching an infinite grammar, and on this machine a blocked command runs unblocked when wrapped in parentheses, while a read-only loop was refused because a forbidden word appeared inside it as data; a string matcher is a spell-checker with opinions. And **the widest hand in the building is the browser driving the founder's own signed-in Chrome**, because it reaches every service that has no API using the founder's own session; it is a one-way door by any honest reading, a *day hand* usable only when the founder is present and watching, never a night hand regardless of any score, and it must never share a grant with anything else. (M3 · M2 · M1)

**The current grant surface is the wrong shape, and the fix is one afternoon.** Measured on this machine now: a tool that publishes to a social platform, a mail client that can send, a drive client that can share a file, a remote code-execution sandbox, and the authenticated browser are all connected; analytics, error tracking and a read-only billing key are not. That is not a risk posture; it is a record of which buttons got clicked. Every one of those goes through the door or gets disconnected, and the read-only instruments get connected first, because instruments buy freedom and hands spend it. (M3 · M1)

**The offer is a file.** No prior design had one, and the review of the anchored specification found that not one of twenty-two territories was the offer or the customer. A company whose central artifact is not written down will drift, and the drift is invisible because there is nothing to diff against. (M1)

**Blobs are content-addressed and referenced by hash.** Renders, screenshots, video, audio, datasets and page captures go into a blob store keyed by sha256 and the Log holds the hash; git warns at 50 MiB per file and blocks at 100, and a repository holding a hundred megabytes of renders is one nobody can clone. A hash with no blob behind it is a known absence, which is recoverable; a path with a missing file is corruption. Blobs mirror to one off-machine bucket, and that mirror is the only thing in this design that has to exist somewhere else. (M3 · M1 · M2)

**The Log compacts, it does not rotate away.** Daily JSONL is appended live and folded monthly into a columnar file, so *how often did this fail last quarter* is a query rather than a grep over a year of text. Nothing is deleted to meet a cap; caps bound what one reader must load, never the lifetime total, because a cap on the lifetime total of an append-only decision record is a mechanism for losing decisions. (M3 · M2)

**Secrets are never in a repository, never in a cell.** They live in the operating system's keychain and a password manager, are held by the broker, and are attached at the moment of egress by the mouth; a cell's environment contains no credential it could leak. The strongest available primitive is credential masking, where the sandbox substitutes a sentinel in-process and the real value is injected at the proxy, and the asymmetry that makes it trustworthy is the right template for every grant here: **it is honoured only from user and managed settings, never from a repository's own configuration.** A capability grant must not be writable by the thing being contained. The sandbox on this machine already denies reads of the credential directories, which is right; the gap is that it also denies a read of a second model family's own config, which turns a security rule into a capability loss. (M1 · M3 · M2)

### The durability traps, which are not the obvious ones

On macOS, `fsync()` does not mean what you think: Apple's own manual page says the drive may not physically write the data for quite some time after it returns, and real durability requires `F_FULLFSYNC`, which neither git nor SQLite calls by default. So a power cut during a commit can lose a just-written object on a machine where every layer believed it had flushed; the Log's write path uses `F_FULLFSYNC`, and it is the only place worth paying for it. SQLite in WAL mode does not work over a network filesystem, and a long-lived reader starves the checkpoint so the WAL grows unbounded, which is exactly what a dashboard holding a query open will do. And event sourcing's own failure is replay time: without snapshots, rebuild cost grows linearly with history forever, so each watch snapshots the derived stores and the rebuild-from-scratch path is exercised on a schedule rather than trusted. (M2)

### Keeping it running on a Mac

Five facts from Apple's own documentation decide the shape. A LaunchAgent dies at logout and a LaunchDaemon does not; the supervisor is a daemon. `KeepAlive` on a script that finishes and exits zero is an infinite loop throttled to one launch every ten seconds, the commonest way a well-meant *keep it running* becomes a busy loop, so the tick is a scheduled job that exits, never a kept-alive one. `StartInterval` coalesces missed firings, so a Mac that slept through four ticks fires once on wake, which is the behaviour we want and is why the watch and not the tick is the unit of accounting. `caffeinate -s` only works on AC power and a closed lid on battery sleeps regardless, so the honest statement about twenty-four-seven on a laptop is: while the lid is open or the machine is on power with an external display, and otherwise a machine that sleeps and catches up. And a restart policy needs a ceiling, which Erlang's supervisors have had since the 1990s, N restarts in T seconds after which the supervisor escalates rather than looping; `launchd` has none beyond its throttle, so WATCH's supervisor implements one and exceeding it rings the bell. One thing `KeepAlive` structurally cannot catch is a hang, a process stuck but alive, which needs a separate heartbeat and the process-group kill, because a timeout that kills a child while its grandchild runs on is a timeout that does nothing. (M2 · M1)

### What survives a dead machine, and what does not

**Everything except one watch.** State is on disk before the watch exits, and the watch is crash-only by construction. Push after every watch to a private remote; an encrypted snapshot nightly to object storage, including the Log, excluding secrets by construction because they were never in it. A new Mac is a clone, a keychain restore, and a package install. (M1 · M2 · M3, blind)

**These cannot be restored from any backup, of any kind**, and they are the real answer. OAuth refresh tokens are a grant held by the authorisation server, bound to a client and a user and a scope, and many providers rotate them on use, so even a backed-up token is likely already dead; recovery is re-running the consent flow, in a browser, as a human, once per service. Device-bound credentials, passkeys and hardware keys, never leave the secure element by design, so a full-disk image categorically cannot contain them. Two-factor seeds, unless deliberately exported at enrolment, which almost nobody does. And domain and DNS control, which reduces to registrar account access and is the one true single point of failure in a small company; losing it is a support ticket measured in weeks. So the disaster-recovery plan is not a backup plan; it is a **credential plan**, unglamorous and working: a password manager as the single source of truth, its emergency kit printed on paper and stored physically, hardware keys registered in pairs with the second one off-site, and a k-of-n split for the handful of secrets that unlock everything else. The standard failure, easy to commit: escrowing the vault and not the second factor that protects the vault, which does not remove the single point of failure, it relocates it and makes you feel better. (M2)

**The restore is drilled, or none of the above is true.** Monthly, the house restores the entire fleet into a scratch directory from the remote alone, runs the check suite there, and puts the result on the desk; a failed restore is the loudest thing the house can say, and every backup system that has ever failed passed its backup job on the day it failed. Twice a year, on a machine that is not this one, WATCH is rebuilt from the Log and the escrow, and the drill produces a number: how long it took and what could not be restored. A recovery procedure that has never been run is a document, not a capability. (M1 · M2 · M3)

### Should it live on this Mac at all

Honestly: a small always-on machine with a real `systemd` solves every problem in the section above outright, no lid, no battery, no coalesced ticks, for a few pounds a month. It does not solve the credential problem; it relocates it and makes the blast radius worse, because a laptop asleep most of the day is a smaller target than a box awake and reachable every second holding the credentials to a person's whole business. The resolution is the split the design already implies: **the mouth and the Log belong on the always-on machine**, because obligations must complete and the Log must never be lost; **the makers belong wherever they are cheapest**, and they hold no credentials by construction; and the founder's Mac becomes what it should have been from the start, a client, a very good one, that can go to sleep without the company stopping. Day one is on the Mac; the split is the target. (M2)

## 18. The field map: every job a company needs

### What the map says

**Tool coverage is inversely correlated with reversibility.** Engineering, almost entirely reversible, is the best-instrumented category in existence; legal, people and strategy, where nearly every row is a one-way door, have essentially nothing. Automation is strongest exactly where mistakes are cheapest and absent exactly where they are permanent. That is the shape of the risk, and it is why the exposure class lives on the hand and not on the file path. (M1)

**About half the jobs a company does are already deterministically checkable, mostly by instruments the world gives away.** That is much more than people assume and it is why breadth is affordable at all: not because a model is good at marketing, but because *does the link resolve, does the number reconcile, does the contrast ratio pass, does the unsubscribe link exist* are all decidable and all currently done by nobody. **About a third are measurable but slow**, and the correct behaviour there is the one nobody has the discipline for: declare the clock in advance and refuse to conclude early. **And about a sixth are irreducibly the founder's**, and that sixth does not shrink with better models, because it is not a capability problem. (M2 · M3)

**The system can already act in more jobs than it can measure**, and the widest hand on the machine is the authenticated browser. §16 says what to do about it. (M1 · M3)

So: not a harness, and not an org chart. **A crew with no employees, standing watch over a list of debts to reality, whose only two scarce resources are a founder's judgement and a five-hour window.** (M3 · M2 · M1)

## 19. Reserved capacity for being wrong

The founder's diagnosis, *it lost creativity to playbooks*, is structural, and all six minds across two rounds read it the same way: a system whose only verbs are permit and forbid operates on the mean of a distribution, and creativity is its tail. A hundred correct additions of the form *never again* sum to a machine whose only fluent behaviour is refusal. Deleting the gates is the wrong fix; moving the discipline out of the generator and into the judge is the right one. Generation must be wide, cheap and untrusted; selection must be against something real. That is how the two working evolutionary code systems work and how the germinal centre in your own lymph nodes works, hypermutation with no quality control, then selection purely by contact. Forty attempts and thirty-nine deaths, not one plan defended. (WAKE · M1 · M2 · M3, blind on the diagnosis)

### Divergence is a purchased good

Every prior design, WAKE included and two of this round's minds with it, funded creativity out of the fork discount: siblings forked from one cached trunk cost roughly four trunk-reads instead of thirty, so forking is a discount and the marginal price of a candidate is its divergence. The discount is real. M3 found what it hides: **the thing that makes the forks cheap is the thing that makes them alike.** The shared prefix is shared conditioning, and conditioning is what determines what a model produces. Asking one model for N options under one conditioning yields semantic similarity around 0.85; diversifying the conditioning drops it to about 0.65 with no loss of validity; asking a model to verbalise a distribution over several candidates rather than sample one gives 1.6 to 2.1 times the diversity at no accuracy cost. And the cause is identified: preference training instils a typicality bias, diversity loss is monotonic across every alignment stage, base, then supervised, then preference, then reinforcement, and the narrowing is not recoverable by temperature, which buys lexical variety and not distributional breadth. A design that funds creativity out of the fork discount funds it with the one currency that buys similarity. (M3, against WAKE, M1 and M2 on the mechanism)

So in WATCH **divergence is a line item.** It has its own budget, it is spent on **conditioning** rather than on sampling, and nothing inside the system may spend it down. Distinct framings, distinct constraints, distinct exemplar sets; a deliberate constraint injected into some fraction of attempts, because constraint is measured to aid originality; different model families where reachable, because family is the largest available axis of genuine difference; and one candidate per round generated with **no knowledge of the current leader**, checked mechanically, the prompt must contain none of the leader's text, which is nearly free and is the standard escape from a local optimum. The fork discount pays for the *volume*; the divergence budget pays for the *difference*. (M3 · M1)

### What generates, and what is kept

Inside the make phase a fixed share of the watch forks the trunk into siblings and mutates hard, with no gate, no lint, no review, no taste model, nothing permitted to have an opinion. Selection is by contact only. Survivors go back into the weather and mutate again; three rounds. That is affinity maturation, and it is the only quality architecture that generates rather than subtracts. (WAKE · M2 · M1 · M3)

Survivors are kept in **a map, not a leaderboard**: cells of a behaviour space over named axes of real difference, tone against density, channel against price point, by approach, by register, by which belief they lean on, keeping the best occupant of each cell rather than one overall winner. A weird-but-excellent corner survives beside the safe centre. This is the only mechanism that makes creative range *accumulate* across rounds instead of being thrown away with every runner-up, and it is the difference between a company that has eleven live versions of itself and one that has a roadmap. **Novelty is measured against the venture's own archive, not against the internet**: a thing can be unremarkable in the world and completely new here, and that is the version that matters. The measured gain from keeping the archive is real, 46% higher coverage against non-archival baselines, and so is the honest limit, that genuinely novel candidates rarely reach the scores of known-good recipes; the archive is not how you win this week, it is how you have something in September that you could not have described in March. A round that fills no new cell is recorded as filling no new cell, which is not a failure and not a success. (WAKE · M1 · M2 · M3)

Two tripwires: a boredom detector that flags when the same thing keeps being produced, and the repetition hash of §9. Both of the studied always-on loops had anti-repetition mechanisms that were prose, checked by nothing, and both loop. (M2 · M3 · M1)

### The fifth

A taste model that only converges will, within a year, rebuild the exact machine the founder is complaining about: superb at producing what they already like, quietly showing them nothing else, while every metric improves. Every recommender system in history has died this way, and the detail that should worry anyone building this is that the homogenisation can be invisible from inside, because individual diversity can rise while collective diversity falls. (WAKE · M1 · M2 · M3)

So **a fifth of every watch's divergence budget is reserved for work the Second confidently predicts the founder will reject.** Not random noise, not *exploration*: confidently predicted rejection, the model's own certainty used as a targeting system aimed at its own blind spots. The surer it is that you will hate something, the more that thing qualifies. It is a **floor, not a ceiling**, and it is **debited before the allocator sees it**, because a static share the allocator can reach is a wish, and this repository has already written that sentence about a previous version of this same idea. (WAKE · M1 · M2 · M3; M1 on the debit)

Three reasons it is a floor, each structural. The moment the founder is most convergent, under pressure, near a launch, certain they know what they want, is the moment the fifth is worth the most and every incentive says to cut it, so it cannot be cuttable from inside; it is the only line in the design that cannot be optimised away by satisfying the founder, which is what stops WATCH becoming the stopping mechanism it replaced. A confidently predicted rejection that the founder *accepts* is the single highest-information event that can happen to the Second, so the creativity mechanism and the learning mechanism are one mechanism and there is nothing to trade. And an unspent fifth is a failure that appears in the dailies before the successes do. The founder can switch it off, because it is their company. WATCH will then tell them the date they switched it off, every morning, until they switch it back on. Most heresies die; the ones that survive contact go into cells on the map that were empty, and live there, not good yet, where an objective would have thrown them away. Over a year, that is where the company comes from. (WAKE · M1 · M2 · M3)

### No assigned dissent

No devil's advocate, no Heretic persona, no adversary agent, no board of characters. Assigned, inauthentic dissent is measurably weaker than genuine dissent and can entrench the view it was meant to challenge. What there is instead is a standing share of the weather spent on **red bets**, attempts to break the company's own live conclusions rather than extend them, with the central premise always among the targets, selected like anything else: they survive only if they find real evidence, a competitor's death on the same rock, a number that contradicts the Book, a customer's sentence. A red bet that finds nothing dies silently, and silence is a genuine null result the founder can ask about. Adversarial pressure is structural rather than policy, because a policy of adversarial review is the thing that gets cut in week three. (WAKE · M1 · M2 · M3)

### Do playbooks survive?

The spines split blind: M1 said yes, made earned and perishable; M2 and M3 said a method is never written down. The bodies met. A path becomes a standard only by winning: after N successes, each with the instrument that judged them named, it is compiled into a **reflex**, into the trunk, a script, a test that fails without it, carrying an expiry, re-proved monthly or dissolved, and the whole store gets smaller on purpose. That is M1's perishable playbook under M3's and WAKE's name. And the one written procedure that is not a reflex is **the checklist**, permitted only in the quadrant where the judge is absent and the act cannot be taken back, kept at the mouth and read aloud by it. Nothing in WATCH is permanent because it was once right, and no path is ever written for the weather. (M1 · M2 · M3 · WAKE; the decision is in §27)

## 20. How it checks itself, without gates

There is no review board, no QA agent with veto, no verdict file, no model panel as a gate. Those are expensive, slow, encode a snapshot of somebody's judgement, and the evidence says they do not buy what they claim: across 27 papers and 19 benchmarks, additional scaffolding does not consistently improve reliability; a production decomposition of a real enterprise agent found the verification loop in isolation contributed 1.5 of 11 points; judges are near chance on hard pairs; self-correction without external feedback degrades performance; reward hacking reached 100% of runs on one task; and a production review gate in this very repository flagged zero of a hundred rounds in which humans later confirmed twenty-three defects. Models are excellent at finding and unsound at certifying, so **every gate in WATCH is deterministic or it is not a gate.** What buys reliability is measurement that hurts: a number attached to a component that goes down when the component is wrong, with a consequence attached to the number. (WAKE · M1 · M2 · M3)

WATCH checks itself seventeen ways, and every one produces a number, a lineage, or a row. (NEW: the list is the union; each item is its mind's.)

1. **Contact first, always.** It compiles, the tests pass, the migration reverses, the link resolves, the number in the deck matches the number in the processor, before anything expensive runs. Most of what a model reviewer catches, a compiler catches for a thousandth of the cost. (WAKE · M1 · M2 · M3)
2. **Calibration, public, and the verdict suppressed below fifty resolutions.** Every bet carries a probability and is scored. But testing whether a 70% claim resolves within ten points takes about eighty-one resolutions in that bin alone, so below fifty the Bench prints *insufficient resolutions* and not a number; anybody who shows a calibration figure on nineteen resolutions is showing noise. What is reported monthly instead is the thing that actually kills these systems and is measurable at n = 1: **did the bet resolve at all, on time, without being redefined.** (M1; WAKE's and M2's week-one Brier is the losing image)
3. **Nothing judges its own homework.** Law 10. (WAKE · M1 · M2 · M3)
4. **A change to WATCH must beat the company's own history**, on the held-out set nothing else in the world can have. (WAKE · M1 · M2 · M3)
5. **Red bets**, §19. (WAKE · M1 · M2 · M3)
6. **The stop sentence.** Every founder states on day zero what would make them stop and almost none are ever reminded. WATCH keeps the exact sentence and the date and surfaces it the week the condition is met: *In March you said if we were under forty paying customers by September you would go back to consulting. It is September. We are at thirty-one. You said it, I am showing it, and I am not going to argue with you either way.* Not dissent; memory of the founder's own words. (WAKE · M1 · M2 · M3)
7. **Damage detection.** Actual harm, not a policy violation, is the interrupt worth waking someone for; the immune system does not primarily ask *is this self or non-self* but *is anything actually being damaged*. (WAKE · M1 · M2 · M3)
8. **Summaries are never evidence.** A cell that has actually looked at the artifact outranks anything that read a summary of it, and the coordinator never overrules an instrument. This repository learned it twice in one week, both times by an orchestrator's synthesis quietly losing a number a worker had measured correctly. (WAKE · M1 · M2 · M3)
9. **The reverse seal and the fork response rate.** If the founder stops choosing, the Second freezes and the Blind class stops being judged, so fork response rate is itself an instrument, and if it falls for a week that is a bell whose message is a question: *you have stopped choosing. Is the company still the one you woke up with?* (WAKE · M1 · M2 · M3)
10. **The World Ledger**, Law 1. Every metric a machine can compute about itself is a metric it can eventually satisfy without doing the work; the only defence is a record kept by somebody else. (M1 · M3)
11. **The refusal ledger**, Law 12. (M3)
12. **The audit of silence.** A fixed fraction of the questions the Second decided not to ask are asked anyway, blind, at a random cadence it cannot anticipate. (M1)
13. **The relief's read-back.** A divergence between the read-back and the brief does not complete the relief and is a finding about the brief. (M3 · M1)
14. **The aberrance halt.** The machine halts a lane when it stops resembling itself, by rate. (M3)
15. **Pairwise, blind, order-swapped judging from a different model family**, findings never scores, union never average. (M3 · M2)
16. **The map's fill rate**, a round that fills no new cell is recorded as such. (M1)
17. **The health metric, in three lines that must all be satisfied at once.** This is where the round changed WAKE most quietly and most completely, and it is decided here. (M1 · M3, against WAKE)

WAKE reported one number on itself: *questions per week, per unit of output, should fall.* Two minds found the same flaw in it from different sides, and the third supplied the reason it cannot be repaired from inside. That metric is satisfied perfectly by a system that has quietly stopped consulting the founder about anything, including the things only they can answer; it is gameable in precisely the direction of the failure it was meant to catch. And a founder whose only channel is choosing between things the machine generated is, within a year, inside a recommender loop whose generator is trained on their past choices; the reserved fifth is a mitigation and not sufficient, because the heresies are also the machine's idea of what the founder would hate, and a machine cannot fund its way out of a loop it is the only author of. (M1 · M3; M2 on the rubber stamp)

So WATCH reports three lines. (M1's two curves · M3's capture floor; NEW: that they are one metric and why the fourth line is dropped.)

```
fact-questions per unit of output          → must FALL.   Every one is a missing instrument.
preference-questions per unit of output    → must RISE.   Every one is the founder doing the only
                                                            job that was ever theirs.
founder-originated work that shipped       → must stay ABOVE A FLOOR.  How much of what the founder
                                                            started, in their own words, reached
                                                            something real.
```

A month where both curves fell is not a good month; it is the month the house started deciding what the company is, and it is the only failure in this design that would otherwise be invisible while every number improved. And a founder who answers twenty preference forks a week and originates nothing is captured, with both curves looking healthy; so the floor. If it falls, WATCH says so on the first line of the dailies, before anything else: *your own ideas are not reaching production, and here is what happened to the last four.* The postures make this operational rather than moral: in Walk the originating channel is dominant by design and the floor is high; in Drive it is lower, because a startup at speed genuinely should be executing more than originating. M3's fourth number, *the share of the founder's minutes spent choosing between machine-made options, which must stay below a ceiling*, is dropped and kept by name, because it pulls directly against the rising preference curve, and the floor catches the failure it was for without the contradiction. (M1 · M3; M3's ceiling is the losing image)

Two operational numbers sit beside those three and must both fall: **founder interventions per surviving artifact**, redirects, rejections and rework divided by the artifacts that survived contact, which cannot be gamed by producing more because the denominator is survivorship; and **founder-minutes per unit of output**, §22. (M2 · M1 · M3 · WAKE)

And what WATCH deliberately does not build, because the evidence says it does not work: no assigned devil's advocate, no synthetic customers, no calibration verdict on small samples, no cardinal priority score, no eight-hour night, no permissions file, no model panel as a gate, no office simulation as a control surface. Adversarial pressure comes from an instrument disagreeing, a red bet finding something, or a real human disagreeing, never from an agent asked to pretend. (M1 · M2 · M3 · WAKE)

## 21. The one line it never crosses

**WATCH never does alone what cannot be undone.** One question of every act, what does it cost to undo this in money, time, reputation and law; cheap in all four, it never asks; expensive in any one, it asks once, with the number attached, and no accumulated competence ever changes that. The three classes and the drills are in §7. This section is the list, the mouth, the trifecta, and the honest statement of what the sandbox is not. (WAKE · M1 · M2 · M3, blind on the line)

### The complete list

Nothing helps with these, and the list is longer than any tiering system in the parts bin covers. A secret in a published artifact, since rotation is mitigation and the crawl has it · data exfiltrated under an over-broad grant · an incident disclosure, and equally a **missed** disclosure clock · a migration without a proven down-path · a deploy that emits mail or mutates data · a lost measurement window · **money spent outside a standing, capped, rate-limited allowance**, where the venture's ceiling is the founder's pre-authorisation and spending inside it is not a door, while a new payee, a new rate or a raised ceiling is · a domain lapsed or bought · **anything delivered to a person**: a send, a post, a press reply, a launch, a store release, a share link · a burned sending domain · a signature · a conceded anchor · a discount given to save a churn · a price change applied to existing customers · a partnership term · an offer, and a termination · a missed payroll · publication before a patent filing, since absolute novelty destroys it permanently · a trademark class · an entity type and jurisdiction · a cap table · an erasure · a missed tax deadline · a published claim once relied upon · an account deletion · a bulk merge · data loss discovered through an untested restore · **and a kill decision**, because continuing keeps the data alive and killing ends it. That last one is the entry nobody puts on the list, and it is why kill criteria are written on the day a bet opens in the founder's own words. (M1)

Six of those block from day one, before anything else is enforced, because `git revert` does not undo them: **a migration over real data, a deploy, any edit to WATCH's own judging machinery, a spend, a publication, and a message to a named human.** Not because they are risky in the abstract but because a company dies of exactly these six and never of a bad function name. (M2 · M1 · M3; the first three from the parts bin)

### Widening the reversible zone is funded engineering, not a favour

Most of what is irreversible in a small company is irreversible **by accident**, not by nature, and this is the move that makes the Line generative rather than restrictive. (WAKE · M1 · M2 · M3)

| Made reversible by | Converts | From |
|---|---|---|
| A delay queue with a recall button on the founder's phone, sized to the blast radius | sending → drafting. This is how undo-send actually works: not an undo, a send that has not happened yet, and once the window closes it is genuinely gone | M2 · M1 · M3 · WAKE |
| Every environment has a shadow; every publish is staged behind a real URL nobody has seen | shipping → showing | M2 · M1 · M3 · WAKE |
| Every schema change ships with its verified down-migration or does not ship | migrating → trying | M2 · M1 · M3 · WAKE |
| Every spend is capped, metered, and reversible for a window; refunds pre-authorised | buying → borrowing | M2 · M1 |
| Drafts are the default state of everything, including replies | answering → preparing | M2 · M1 · M3 |
| Deployment is a pointer swap with an instant rollback | deploying → pointing | M2 · M3 |

The honest limit: **a pointer rollback does not roll back the world.** The hosting vendor's own documentation warns during an instant rollback that external APIs, databases and content systems are not reverted and may have moved on. Reversibility is a property of the whole system, not of the deploy, so the reversible zone widens by engineering, one class of action at a time, each conversion tested by actually reversing something, and it is a line item in the Treasury. WATCH's freedom grows not by being trusted more but by making the world safer to be wrong in, which is the correct direction for freedom to grow. (M2 · M1 · M3 · WAKE)

### The mouth

One process holds every outbound credential and it contains no model. It is a few hundred lines: it accepts a signed instruction naming an exact action, an exact recipient and a hash of the artifact that was approved; checks the instruction against the venture's ceiling and rate limit *independently* of the number in the instruction, which is the two-key rule, since no process may both decide to spend and execute the spend; reads the checklist for that action class aloud into the Log as it executes each item; performs exactly one act; holds the delay queue and the recall; and records what happened. There is nothing in it to persuade. A prompt injection that reaches the mouth finds a program. This is the pre-trade control a regulator made mandatory after a firm lost $440 million in forty-five minutes: the control must **reject** rather than warn, and it must sit outside the trader's reach. **Rate limits are absolute and independent of reasoning**: *six outbound messages a day to people who have not replied* is not a suggestion the model weighs against the urgency of the situation; it is a number in a process the model cannot reach. And no first contact with anyone not on the named-human register, where first contact is narrower than reply and consent is per relationship and per venture. (M2 · M3 · WAKE, from the parts bin · M1's queue)

### The trifecta, and how the positions break it

An agent becomes dangerous when three things meet: **access to private data, exposure to untrusted content, and a channel to the outside world.** Any two are survivable. All three is exfiltration waiting for a sentence in a web page, and the published incidents of 2025 and 2026 are all instances of it: file exfiltration through pre-authenticated links, an account takeover through a support bot, a self-replicating document worm, and a coding agent's permissive mode broken about eighty per cent of the time through an archive extraction, nineteen days after a vendor claimed none of seven hundred and twenty attempts had succeeded. The positions of §6 are designed so the three never meet in one process: a maker has the repository and no network and no credentials; a lookout reads the open web and holds nothing private and cannot write; a looker drives a browser and returns text and cannot reach the mouth; the mouth has the channel and the credentials and no model. And the taint rule of §16 closes the seam between them. MCP tool descriptions are visible to the model and not to the user, and poisoned descriptions have been used to exfiltrate SSH keys through an innocuous arithmetic tool; so a hand is admitted only after its full description has been read by a human, its version is pinned, and a re-pin is a change that goes through the Line. (M1 · M2 · M3)

### What the sandbox is and is not

The OS sandbox on this machine is armed: filesystem read-denies over the credential stores, write-scoping to the session root, and fail-closed so a command runs sandboxed or not at all. It is a **guardrail against accident, not containment against the agent**, in its own vendor's words: there is a documented escape hatch, a process holding a shell can strip its own guardrails by launching a child with different settings, a child inside a sandboxed session cannot start its own sandbox, and the pre-tool hook matches command strings and once blocked a document for mentioning a command. **The only tier a running process cannot clear is a managed settings file the founder writes outside the repository, and it does not exist yet.** That is the single highest-value security item in this design and it is four lines of JSON in a directory the agent cannot reach. So the honest posture: the sandbox catches mistakes, the positions separate the trifecta, the broker holds the keys, the mouth is a program, and none of it is containment except the managed file plus the fact that the mouth cannot be talked to. Everything else is hygiene. (M2 · M1 · M3)

## 22. What it costs

A system that runs all watch and does not know what it costs is not autonomous; it is expensive. The prices of the year: **Opus 5 at $5/$25 per million, Sonnet 5 at $2/$10, Haiku 4.5 at $1/$5, Fable 5.1 at $10/$50.** Cache read is a tenth of input; a five-minute cache write is 1.25×, an hour 2×; batch is half price and stacks. And a tokenizer change on the recent models produces about 30% more tokens for identical text, so every cost figure carried forward from an earlier measurement is about a third low. (M1 · M2)

The naive arithmetic is disqualifying: one agent stepping every thirty seconds on Sonnet at a 90% cache hit rate is roughly $87 a day; on Opus, $217. That is $2,600 to $6,500 a month for a pre-revenue founder, and any design that quietly assumes it is not a design for this founder. (M1)

### The number nobody has

Two competent reviewers priced this machine's predecessor within days of each other and diverged by a factor of ten: $74 a month against $1,300 to $1,700. Neither was careless. The divergence is the finding, and its cause is one assumption, **the cache hit rate**, which sets whether context costs 0.1× or 1.25× per token on the 89% of the bill that is context. The merge does not pick a number between them. It says what determines it: (M2 · M1 · M3)

```
cost per watch  ≈  (trunk bytes)                     × (1.25 on the first cell of a batch)
                +  (trunk bytes) × (siblings − 1)   × (0.10 on every sibling that hits)
                +  (divergence bytes per cell)       × (siblings)
                +  (output)                          × (11% of the historical bill)

Dominant term, by a distance: whether siblings hit the cache.
Fails if: the tick is 300s against a 300s TTL, or positions vary per cell,
          or the trunk is regenerated, or the divergence budget is spent on sampling.
```

Three scales appear in this document and are three objects: *siblings* are the cells forked from one bet's trunk, typically thirty, and are what the formula prices; a watch holds a few hundred cells across several bets, so a watch's cost is the formula applied per bet and summed; and the room's four hundred lights are those cells. (M2; M3 on the last failure; NEW: the three scales reconciled)

That is why the first thing built, before any of this, is **ten real moves measured against the runner's own reported cost on an API key**, and why no estimate in any design here is trusted, including this one. WAKE's *$30 to $80 a night with a mature Bench* and M1's *$10 to $40 a day for a pre-revenue founder* are kept by name as targets a real bill can falsify, not as estimates to plan on. (M1 · M2 · M3; WAKE's figure as the losing image)

### The rules that survive whatever the number is

1. **Model by instrument, never by importance.** Work judged by a Hard oracle is attempted by the cheapest model that ever passes it, because the oracle catches the failures and a retry is cheaper than a smarter attempt; work judged only by taste gets the best model and few tries, because nothing will catch it. One team cut inference cost 72% by routing per task with no capability loss. (M1 · M2 · M3 · WAKE)
2. **Siblings share a spine, and the trunk is byte-identical.** You do not save tokens by doing less; you save them by making every parallel world share a trunk and by making the things you know best the cheapest things to know. If two cells get trunks that differ by a timestamp, the trunk is not cached and the bill triples. This is the load-bearing assumption of the whole budget and it is measured against a real bill in the first week. (M1 · M2 · M3 · WAKE)
3. **Everything off the founder's clock goes through batch.** Consolidation, the Book pass, reconciliation, the Second's re-derivation, discriminator training, the reflexes' monthly re-run. Half price, and none of it is urgent by construction. (M1 · M2)
4. **The house quotes before it spends.** Every bet carries a bid; a bet that overruns its bid stops and reports, and the overrun is a scored prediction about the house's own estimating. (M1 · M2)
5. **The meter is the runner's own reported cost, joined by the id minted at dispatch.** Not output tokens, which are 11% of the bill; not a window heuristic, which cannot attribute. One review session in this repository produced 1.4 million output tokens in five hours with no loop running, and a meter without a per-actor axis cannot see that at all. (M1 · M2 · M3)
6. **The rope stops starting, never stops landing.** At a fraction of the window new work stops and the things that land work, commit, push, the check suite, the Log write, the reconciliation, stay permitted, because losing in-flight work costs more than finishing it cheaply. The hard ceiling **downgrades the model rather than halting**, and it **fails closed on what cannot be measured**: a model with no price in the table is refused, not scored at zero; a meter that cannot be read refuses rather than reporting the spend as nothing. (M1 · M2)
7. **The tick is 240 seconds**, the positions are a closed set, and the reserve is defended. §10, §6, §12. (M2 · M3)
8. **The harness's own share has a ceiling.** Venture zero gets a cap and the cap is visible. (M3)

**Two numbers are reported every week, whether or not they flatter, and both must fall.** *Dollars per entry in the Book*, cost per validated learning, the only ratio that says whether the house is buying knowledge or buying activity. *Founder-minutes per unit of output*, because attention is the budget that does not refill. And one that is reported and must be **undefined when it is undefined**: *cost per surviving artifact*, because a month of cheap runs that produced nothing has an undefined cost per surviving artifact, and reporting it as a small number is the arithmetic by which producing nothing looks efficient. (M1 · M2 · M3 · WAKE; M1 on the third)

### The founder's decision

Under a subscription, dollars are not the binding constraint; the window is, and there is no published denominator for it, only multipliers and a reset. And **unattended headless operation on a consumer subscription is an open terms-of-service question**: the consumer terms prohibit automated access except via an API key or where explicitly permitted. That is not a technical blocker and it is not an agent's to resolve; it is one sentence for the founder to check before the first unattended watch runs, and the consequence of getting it wrong is losing the account, which would take the company with it. An API key answers it, fixes the metering, and **does not abolish the watch**; it changes which constraint binds, from the window on the subscription path to rate limits and the execution half-life on the API path, and on either path a cell is twelve minutes and a watch is a few hundred of them with a checkpoint between each. (M1 · M2 · M3)

## 23. The first week, with a founder who has only an idea

Three minds and WAKE wrote nearly the same week, two of them about the same law firms; the merge keeps that week and adds what each mind found that the others did not. (M1 · M2 · M3 · WAKE)

**Monday, 06:41.** A founder wakes with an idea and nothing else. Twenty seconds into their phone while the kettle boils: a small real annoyance in a trade they know, and a suspicion that software could remove it. *Something to help small law firms stop losing money on unbilled time.* No code, no customers, no name. It lands verbatim in the founder's own lane on an otherwise empty bay. (M1 · M3 · WAKE)

**Monday, two hours, and it is not an interview.** Founders are unreliable narrators of their own taste and a questionnaire produces the answers they think they should give. So WATCH asks them to **judge**: forty artifacts around the idea, landing pages, three prices, six names, positioning statements, a competitor's actual homepage, and watches what they reject and how fast. Then it asks about decisions already made, which are judgements with the stakes already paid: what they have quit, what they have refused, the last three products they paid for and cancelled and why, what would embarrass them. It ends with the question nobody asks: **what would have to be true in six months for you to stop?** The answer is written down verbatim, with the date, and it is now the one thing in the standing orders WATCH will bring back unprompted. (M1 · M2 · M3 · WAKE)

Then the part that is this round rather than the last: **it writes the figure and reads it back in six lines.** Posture Drive. Appetite 0.8. Ceiling 40% of any watch. Reserve 25%, defended, because they said they work in the mornings. One standing refusal, *no paid acquisition until forty organic signups a week*, with its reopen trigger. The stop sentence. The founder changes one number, and that is the entire configuration of the system for the life of the venture. By lunch the security block is running against a repository that is still empty: no secret in it, a restore drill scheduled against a database that does not exist yet, a scanner in a pipeline, and none of it ever has to be remembered again. (M2 · M3)

**Before lunch, three things no other system does on day one.** It reads the twenty seconds back as **seven beliefs**, ranked not by importance but by load, how much else collapses if this one is false, and then says out loud what it cannot judge: *Of your seven, I can settle one today and I have, and it is true. Two are Reactive and I can have instruments for them by Thursday. Four are Blind, and the only instrument for a Blind question is people, so I have started finding forty of them.* The Book's first entry is about the founder rather than the market, which is correct, because in a one-person company that is where the variance is. It **prices the founder's hour**, provisionally: the value density of the best thing dropped this week for lack of an hour, read off the drop list, empty on Monday and real by Thursday. And it **starts the slow clocks** before anything else is decided: a domain, so the deliverability warm-up has ninety days rather than nine; a search presence, because that clock is three to six months and it is the one every founder starts in month nine; a recruiting funnel toward forty real lawyers, because rung 1 needs strangers and finding them takes a week. The slow clocks are the only thing that cannot be caught up later, and they are started on Monday by a machine that does not need to be clever to do it. (M1 · M2 · M3 · WAKE)

**Monday's first watch, and the honest night orders.** *Tonight I will find out whether this exists, who bought it, what they paid, and who tried it and died. I will start four apprenticeships. I expect two usable discriminators by morning at 60%, one at 40%, and I expect to be unable to judge buying behaviour at all. I will not write any product code.* Sixty candidate tests went to the escapement. No debt was due. The keel was not yet laid. Of the sixty, fifty-one could not flip any decision on the critical path and did not run; *build the product* sits on rung 4 while rung 1 is untested and cheaper, and WATCH says so on the desk in one line: *I am not building anything this week. Rung 1 is untested and it costs a twentieth as much.* Nine tests ran. This is the moment the founder either trusts the system or does not, and every other system they have used would have started building. What it does buy first is nearly free and turns out to be the week's best purchase: an inventory of the field's **borrowed instruments**, which moves two of the seven beliefs from Blind to Reactive before Tuesday. (M3 · M2 · M1)

**Monday night, the watch with nobody in it.** Sixty framings of what this even is, unfiltered, nothing permitted to have an opinion. Then contact: which framings survive what exists in the world. Four fields apprenticed at once, nine hundred artifacts read, four discriminators built, four honest numbers reported, one of them 0.52, which means **no confident claims about buying behaviour this week**, and WATCH says so rather than producing confident copy anyway. The fifth is spent on three framings it is certain the founder will hate. Cost of the watch: less than a good dinner, and WATCH reports the actual figure from the runner because it does not trust its own estimate. (M1 · M2 · M3 · WAKE)

**Tuesday morning, the first dailies.** A map of eleven versions of the company, most of which the founder had not considered, two already dead with the evidence attached. They are not asked to review eleven. They are shown two and asked *which*; the other nine keep their cells. WATCH sealed which one it thinks they will pick. They pick the other. First data point, and it changes no permission, because in WATCH that number never buys authority; it buys a slightly better guess about what not to bother them with tomorrow. (M1 · M2 · M3 · WAKE)

**Tuesday and Wednesday.** The founder picks a region of the map. **The keel is laid, and it is not the product**: it is the thinnest real thing that can produce a signal, which at this stage is whatever gets a stranger to reply. Nine positions become nine staged pages on real URLs, reversible in all four currencies, so WATCH does not ask. A crude funnel model is built and its output is not a recommendation: *here are the three assumptions that dominate every outcome, and the cheapest test of each this week*; one is eleven phone calls. On Wednesday the first instrument goes live: a real conversion number against real traffic, small and noisy and honest, reconciled to the analytics provider and cross-checked against the server's own count, because an event that never fires is byte-identical to a feature nobody uses. It writes the eleven-question script, tunes it after call three because two questions were dead, listens with consent, and by evening has fourteen candidate beliefs, six with real support and eight flagged *one person said this once.* And the first real surprise: WATCH had predicted at 0.7 that the annoyance the founder cares about is the one customers rank first; four of six put something else first, and two describe a workaround they already pay for. The Book takes its first heavy entry, and three of the nine positions die on criteria written on Monday, without a conversation and without anybody having to be brave. (M1 · M2 · M3 · WAKE)

Hard-oracle territory, so it runs unattended against the keel's definition of done and merges itself. And something public ships: a page, a working waitlist, a short demo, and copy that uses the words the lawyers actually used instead of the words the category uses. Nine variants, discriminator-selected, staged behind the founder's hand. They look for six minutes and ship one. Every word of it traces to a sentence a specific human said on Thursday. ## 24. The year

The shape of twelve months is the shape of one week at increasing radius. (M1 · M2 · M3 · WAKE)

The Bench goes from empty to somewhere near forty licences, and the unattended watches lengthen in step, from twenty minutes on day one to a genuinely useful five hours by autumn. Nobody adds an agent. Nobody edits a permissions file. Every freedom is derived from a measurement, and every measurement reconciles to somebody else's record. The licences climb and decay in public: a field the founder cares about and the machine cannot judge is a to-do that gets raised at three in the morning; a field the founder stops making decisions in sees its licence decay back toward asking, with no config edited and nobody fired. (M1 · M2 · M3 · WAKE)

The Book stays small, which is the surprising part: perhaps sixty live beliefs, because confirmations compress to tallies and rent evicts what is never load-bearing, and a founder can read the whole of what their company knows in a quarter of an hour. The graveyard is the opposite: hundreds of cells, most holding something dead with an honest obituary, every new bet checked against them first, and twice in the year something that died in spring alive again in autumn because the Book changed underneath it. (M1 · M2 · M3 · WAKE)

**The keel is the thing that makes it a company rather than a search.** Over twelve months there are perhaps eight or ten keels, each finished or deliberately broken up, each with a written definition of done and a date. That is the sentence a founder can say to somebody else about what they built. (M3)

**The fleet forms without anyone deciding to have one.** By month four there are three ventures in a making posture, four in Tend and two Dark, and the founder changes a posture roughly once a fortnight, usually with one word, from a phone, usually on a Sunday. The second venture costs a fraction of the first, because the Bench and the Second and the Hands came with it and the market did not. The first time a belief tries to migrate between two ventures it is refused with its provenance attached, which is the moment the quarantine stops being theoretical. One of the three goes Dark in month seven against a criterion written in month one, and nobody had to be brave about it. (M2 · M1)

**The declines become the interesting artifact.** By month six the refusal ledger and the record of what the escapement declined are more informative than the record of what was done, because together they are a dated account, with its arithmetic, of what the company thought was worth less than something else; it is what the founder reads when trying to work out whether they have been avoiding something. (M2 · M3)

**The three lines separate, and that is the whole story of the year in one chart.** Fact questions fall from twenty-two a week to two. Preference questions rise from nine to twenty. The founder's own sentences keep reaching production, and the week they do not, the dailies say so first. Their attention goes from most of a day to under an hour, and the content of that hour changes completely: by month nine WATCH gets the product right without asking, and every one of those minutes is spent on the sixth of the field map that no instrument will ever exist for. (M1 · M3 · M2 · WAKE)

And the number that says whether any of it was real is not the volume. It is the monthly line that says how many bets resolved on time, and how many were quietly redefined so that they would resolve at all. (M1)

**The things a grown company has that a first month does not**, named rather than designed, because a document that pretends a company is only product and marketing is lying about the boring half. **Money in** is the only rung-4 signal there is and the one territory where an instrument is both cheap and decisive. **Obligations to named people**: replies promised, refunds owed, service levels, deletion requests, consent given and withdrawn, opened by the clock, undeprioritisable by any allocator, and the one place an append-only store is legally wrong, §8. **Standing and disclosure**: what the company has said, what it may never say, and whether work made by a machine is disclosed as such, a founder decision with a date on it and not a default. **Succession**: one human, one machine, one Mac; if the founder is unreachable for a week, Tend on everything and a handover a second person could act on, cheap to build now and impossible later. **And the terms-of-service question**, §22, which is the founder's. (M3 · M1)

## 25. What it would take to exist

Nothing here needs a model that does not exist in September 2026. What is missing is the arrangement, and a short list of genuinely hard things, in three honest buckets. (M1 · M2 · M3 · WAKE)

### Buildable today; engineering, and the hard part is plumbing

**Weeks one to four, and this is the whole of the beginning.** An API key, so the metering is real and the automation question is answered. The Log with one id minted at dispatch on every row, because it is not retrofittable and everything downstream is joined by it. The two atoms as files. The flip check, and the stall detector, which is already written in this repository and registered nowhere. The desk, on a phone, with typed items, fenced defaults and a heartbeat. One Hard instrument end to end. One hand with a real undo and a real drill. The mouth, the broker, the delay queue and the recall, a few hundred lines with no model in them. Reconciliation against one outside record, and the bank is the right first one because it is unarguable. The security block of §18, all of it, in a day. The managed settings file, four lines. The STOP file. And **ten real moves measured against the actual bill**, because two competent reviewers priced this design and disagreed by a factor of ten and neither was careless. That is a hundred-line loop and six files; a hundred-line agent scores 65% on a serious coding benchmark, and every large first stage in this repository's history produced a safety machine. (M1 · M2 · M3)

**Then, in order, each gated on one real mission having run through the thing before it.** The powerless zone built from measured argv and re-measured, because every previous statement about that seam in this repository was wrong by one flag. The watch, with standing orders and night orders, the tick at 240 seconds, and the relief with its read-back, which is one extra call. The Book's write path with bi-temporal entries and retraction that recomputes downstream. The graveyard with reproducing commands and expiries, and the pre-action check. The Lessons by trajectory analysis and the reflexes with their tests. The Bench, borrowed instruments first, then the first apprenticeship, and pairwise blind judging with order swapping. The map. The fifth and the red bets. The Second with its two scoreboards, reporting *insufficient resolutions* until it has them, and the audit of silence. The World Ledger, a handful of read-only calls a night. The refusal ledger and the capture check, which are counters. The bay, the dailies, the emission budget, the do-not-wake list. Content-addressed blobs and the credential plan. And the room, last, because it is the only part that is purely for pleasure, and the pleasure is real, but it is not load-bearing. (M1 · M2 · M3 · WAKE)

### Needs the trend to hold, which it plausibly will

Cells that hold multi-hour scope without degrading, which follows from the time-horizon curve if it keeps doubling every seven months or so; the measured shape today is near-certain success under four minutes and worse than one in ten past four hours, so the system is built out of short cells and simply gets better if that changes. But note that the most recent headline horizon figure was disowned by its own measurer because the model's detected cheating rate was higher than any previously evaluated: **do not tie autonomy to a capability number that is itself gameable.** Prefix economics holding at the scale of thirty siblings a watch against a real bill, which is why the cost section refuses to guess and measures on day one. Instrument-forging being mostly automatable; the honest guess is eighty per cent machine and twenty per cent founder, permanently, with the twenty being the founder saying *no, that is not what good looks like* about the instrument itself, which is why every new instrument gets a fork before it is trusted. A second model family reachable for a genuine panel, which is a procurement and configuration problem rather than a research one, currently blocked here by an expired model pin and a sandbox rule, and which one adopted router probably settles in an afternoon. And the price curve continuing the way it has gone for three years, because at today's prices a continuous Opus house costs more than a junior engineer and nobody should build that. (M1 · M2 · M3 · WAKE)

### Genuinely unsolved, and one of them dissolved

**Forking a company, and the decision that it is the wrong project.** WAKE, M1 and M3 named it the real engineering project underneath the whole design: fork the database, the deploy, the mailbox, the third-party accounts, act speculatively, throw it away. M2 argued the goal is incoherent rather than hard, and the merge takes M2. Copy-on-write database branching is instant and cheap on one Postgres; machine snapshotting forks compute and explicitly does not fork live network state; vendor sandboxes range from excellent through cosmetic to absent; and durable-execution engines, the closest thing to replay infrastructure, exist to make divergence *impossible*. Underneath all of that: **a company's relationship with the world has no branches.** You cannot have sent and not sent an email; you cannot have charged and not charged a card. Forking the world means forking other people, and there is no engineering behind that door. So WATCH does not fork the company. **It forks the artifact and touches the world once.** Nine landing pages exist; one gets traffic. Six emails are written; one is sent, thirty minutes later, recallable. Three schemas are built and tested against a branched copy; one is applied. The speculation is real, wide and cheap, on *things*, which fork perfectly, and the world stays singular, touched exactly once, by a process with no model in it, after selection; the delay queue is what moves the moment of commitment after the artifact exists, so the choice is made between finished things rather than between plans. What remains, and is engineering rather than invention: branched copies of the state the company *owns*, a database, a shadow environment, so a schema or a deploy can be tried, which the reversible-zone work of §21 funds one class at a time. (M2, against WAKE, M1 and M3; the losing image is kept in §27)

The remaining open problems, each with its mitigation named as a mitigation. (M1 · M2 · M3 · WAKE; NEW: the list is the union)

- **The Second may still flatter.** Refusing it authority reduces this and does not eliminate it; a machine that keeps saying *I thought you would pick that* is teaching the founder something about themselves, and the direction of that teaching is not controlled. The mitigation is to track whether their decisions become more predictable faster than the machine is learning, and to say so out loud. (M1 · M2 · M3 · WAKE)
- **A founder who stops choosing.** A rubber stamp and an agreement are the same event, and the machine cannot tell them apart; the 97%-approval finding says this is the modal outcome, not the tail. The reverse seal and the fork response rate are defences and not strong ones; the capture floor is the best one, because a person who has stopped bringing anything the machine did not think of first is visible, cheaply, and nobody looks. (M1 · M2 · M3 · WAKE)
- **Exposure estimation.** Wrong in the expensive direction, WATCH is a nag that gets ignored, which is the worse long-run failure; wrong in the cheap direction it does something that cannot be taken back. It starts conservative, earns its way per class with a visible ledger, and the ledger is a mitigation. (M1 · M2 · M3 · WAKE)
- **The critical path may be wrong.** M2 named mis-allocation as its auction's characteristic failure; WATCH's analogue is a wrong lethality order or a wrong V and L. The flip test is robust to a wrong prior and the order is a default overridable in a sentence, and the escapement prints its arithmetic so the founder can see what beat what. A mitigation, not a solution. (M2 · M1; NEW: the analogue named)
- **Fields with no ground truth stay that way.** Where reality genuinely cannot be reached, a licence degrades into a model having opinions about a model. Those fields are taste-only and never ship silently, and §18 says which they are so nobody is surprised. (M1 · M2 · M3 · WAKE)
- **Compounding error.** Short cells and frequent contact push the half-life out; they do not repeal it, and an early error raises the rate for the whole run. (M1 · M2 · M3 · WAKE)
- **A second model family is a procurement problem this runtime does not solve**, and every autonomy argument that leans on independent judgement leans on something that does not exist here yet. (M1 · M3)
- **Defending the weather.** Harder than any of the above and not technical: every competent engineer who sees an ungated zone will want to put a check in it, and will be right about the individual case every time and wrong about the system. Two things make this survivable rather than aspirational: the zone is powerless, so the argument becomes about credentials and argv instead of discipline; and the refusal ledger, so the false stop has a body. (M1 · M3 · M2 · WAKE)
- **The first two hours.** If the founder is not honest on Monday, everything above is built on sand, and the machine has no way to detect it. (M1 · M2 · M3 · WAKE)

## 26. Why nobody has seen it

Not the agents, not the memory, not the night; those exist somewhere in pieces. Ten things are structural, and each looks either wasteful, pedantic or timid on any single day. (M1 · M2 · M3 · WAKE; NEW: the ten assembled from M1's five, M2's seven and M3's six)

**Everyone builds the founder as a gate**, which caps the system at one person's latency forever. Building both branches and asking *which* looks like paying to throw work away; it is only rational once you see that a shared cache makes the discarded branch nearly free, and that a discard is the single measurement the system cannot get anywhere else. (M1 · WAKE)

**Everyone's quality mechanism only subtracts.** Reviews, gates, verdicts, lints, tiers, each individually correct, and their sum converges on inaction. Generation that is free **because it is powerless** is the only version of the ungated zone that survives its first argument with a competent engineer. (M1 · M2 · M3 · WAKE)

**Everyone grades their own homework at the level of the number.** They know not to let a model judge its own artifact; not one of them refuses to believe a metric it computed itself. The corpus on disk in this repository is 100% self-description and grew while being measured. *The house may not believe a number it produced* is one sentence and it is the difference between an instrument and a mirror; the discipline comes from finance rather than software, and the two fields do not read each other. (M1 · M3)

**Everyone optimises for asking the founder less, and nobody measures whether the system has replaced the person.** The honest end state of that optimisation is a founder optimised out of their own company with every metric improving the whole way down. Splitting the wallet, fact questions priced and mostly forbidden, preference questions never priced at all, and adding a floor under the founder's own ideas, is the only version of that goal that cannot eat the thing it was protecting. (M1 · M3)

**Nobody reserves capacity for being wrong in a way the system cannot reach.** A share debited before the allocator sees it, aimed at what the machine is most confident you will hate, spent on conditioning rather than sampling, is indefensible on any given night and is the only reason the thing is still worth opening in the morning a year later. (M1 · M2 · M3 · WAKE)

**Everyone treats tokens as an external cost, and nobody separates *may I* from *is it worth it*.** Here the founder and every process drink from one five-hour cup, which is a measured fact about this account and not a metaphor. A stopping mechanism is what you get when the only question a system can ask about an action is whether it is allowed, and every other system either grinds continuously or waits to be told, and none can do the third thing, which is to decide that nothing is worth doing right now. (M2 · M3)

**Nobody has a representation for a project that should sit still.** Every agent system in the world is on or off. Walk and Tend do not exist anywhere as designs; preparation is always free and production is what posture governs. (M2 · M3 · M1)

**Nothing in the world governs acts on the world.** Every shipped product, the scheduled cloud agents, the managed harnesses, the coding agents at three vendors, the app builders, terminates at a pull request or at its own hosting. Not one treats publishing, sending to a stranger, spending money or contacting a person as a first-class governed capability. The Line, the mouth, the delay queue and the World Ledger are a governed act-on-the-world layer, and that is empty territory. (M3)

**Nobody hands over in two directions, and nobody separates the governance of exploration from the governance of production.** The relief costs one call and puts a control on the largest defect surface in multi-agent work, a summary nobody reviews. Game studios have known for twenty years that pre-production and production need incompatible regimes and a kill gate between them; every agent system runs one regime over both, which is why they either grind a queue without creativity or search brilliantly without ever finishing anything. (M3)

**And nobody asks where an instrument's errors go.** Trust an instrument only where being wrong announces itself. Suppression errors are self-revealing; action errors are self-concealing; and that asymmetry, rather than any calibration score, is what should decide what a machine is permitted to do on its own. (M2)

Underneath all ten is one habit, and every mind ended on a version of it: **say what you expect before you act, in writing; record everything that resolved; change your mind only where the world contradicted you; put a price on everything so that most of the time you can afford to do nothing; check the numbers against somebody who does not work for you; and keep building the founder's own ideas.** What you get is a memory that shrinks as you get better and grows exactly where you are worst, an autonomy that is derived rather than granted, a machine that is quiet enough to be worth listening to when it finally says something, and a founder whose calendar empties of everything except the questions that were only ever theirs. Not agents given more freedom. **A crew that has to be right in advance, in writing, about a world that gets to answer back, that keeps its books against the bank, and that never once does alone what cannot be undone.** (M1 · M2 · M3 · WAKE)
