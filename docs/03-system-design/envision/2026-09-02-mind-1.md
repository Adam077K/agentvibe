## Spine, written before reading

*Written after reading only `00-BRIEF.md`, before opening WAKE, the reviews, the anchored spec, the
catalogue, or the web. Never edited. Corrections, if I earn any, appear below it under
**Where the spine was wrong**.*

**The name is THE HOUSE.** A house is a company (a publishing house), a home (where the founder
actually lives), and the party that runs the book on everyone else's bets — the one that survives not
by being right once but by holding a portfolio with an edge. All three meanings are load-bearing.

**The founder's complaint is the whole design brief:** *everything built so far is a stopping
mechanism.* The machine on disk is made of brakes — gates, lints, ledgers, tiers, verdicts. Brakes are
real engineering and they are not a vehicle. Nothing in it wants anything. A system that only prevents
bad outcomes will, at rest, produce nothing, and it will be correct the whole time.

**So the atom is not a task, an agent, or a workflow. The atom is a bet.** A bet is a claim that could
be false, with money on it, a deadline, and a named way of losing. *Nobody will pay for this* is a bet.
*Checkout leaks at step three* is a bet. *This library is faster than that one* is a bet. Tasks are
merely the cheapest instrument for settling a bet, and code is merely one kind of task. Every unit of
work in the house descends from a bet or it does not get funded. This is what makes the system move
without burning tokens: it is not looping, it is settling.

**That gives the two hardest answers for free.** *What next?* — the open bet with the highest expected
information per dollar, weighted by how near it sits to the company's throat. *When to do nothing?* —
when no open bet can be moved for less than the value of moving it. Idleness becomes an economic
verdict rather than a missing instruction, and the system can defend it out loud.

**Memory stores beliefs, not events.** Everything stores everything and retrieves by similarity; that
is why these systems know so much and understand nothing. The asset is the *surprise*: the place the
world disagreed with us. A belief carries provenance, a confidence, an expiry, and the bet that bought
it. Events are the raw ore in an append-only log; beliefs are the refined metal; retrieval asks *which
of my beliefs bear on this decision and which have gone stale*, never *what text looks like this*.

**Context is composed, not retrieved.** A working agent receives a brief written for it: the goal, the
two or three beliefs that bear, the constraints that will fail it, and what happened the last time we
tried this. Building that brief is a real job with a real cost, and it is the highest-leverage job in
the house. Retrieval-as-search is what you do when nobody is willing to do the composing.

**Creativity died because a playbook is a frozen answer.** The cure is not to delete process but to
make it *earned and perishable*: a path becomes a playbook only after it has won several times, it
carries an expiry, and it must re-prove itself or dissolve. Beside it sits a standing exploration
budget — a fixed fraction of spend that must go to things with no precedent, which cannot be
reallocated to safe work when the quarter looks thin. A company spending nothing on exploration is
dead in eighteen months and comfortable for twelve of them.

**Roles are power, not domain.** Domain expertise is data you load; twenty-one job titles collapse to
a handful of *powers*: who may read, who may write, who may spend, who may touch the outside world,
who may change the house itself. Every dangerous capability is a grant with a name, a scope, and a
receipt.

**Safety is reversibility, not ceremony.** Let the system do anything it can cheaply undo, and stop it
only at the irreversible: money leaving, mail sent, data deleted, a thing published, a key exposed,
the house editing its own rules. Classify by *undo cost*, not by a risk table someone invented. A gate
that blocks reversible work is the stopping mechanism, wearing a badge.

**The founder's attention is the scarcest resource in the system, more than tokens by orders of
magnitude.** So the surface is not an office simulation with little avatars walking about — that is
charm billed to the one account that cannot afford it. It is a *bridge*: a few instruments always in
the same place, heading and fuel and what is about to go wrong, and a stair down into any room to take
the wheel. And the founder's queue holds *decisions*, never activity. **Every question to the founder
carries a recommendation, a default, and a deadline; silence executes the default.** That single rule
is what lets a nocturnal system stay honest with a diurnal human.

**Projects declare a drive, and the drive is the founder's dial.** *Hunt* moves always, to the cap.
*Walk* acts only inside the founder's turn. *Hold* watches and reports and does not act. *Client* acts
only within an agreed scope and leaves an audit trail. One budget across all of them, allocated like a
portfolio: a project that keeps returning nothing loses allowance to one that is returning something.
The founder can overrule the allocation and the system will tell them what it cost.

**Learning means the house changes, not that the house produced.** When a bet settles, something in
the house must be different — a belief updated, a brief template improved, a tool acquired, a skill
written, a playbook retired. A settled bet that changed nothing about the house is a bet we paid for
twice.

**Everything is files, in git, plus one append-only event log and indexes that can be rebuilt from
it.** What the house knows that is not in a file, it does not know. A dead machine costs a clone.

**What it would do in its first week with a founder who has only an idea:** turn the idea into the
five bets it actually rests on, rank them by what kills the company soonest, and start settling the
cheapest lethal one — not build the product. Most of week one is talking to the world and writing down
what surprised us.

*(End of spine.)*

## Where the spine was wrong

*Four corrections, each with the thing that caused it. The spine above is not edited; this is what
survived contact.*

**1. "The atom is a bet" was half a taxonomy.** A company is not only a machine for finding out. It is
also a machine for keeping its word. Renewing a domain, filing a return, answering a customer, rotating
a key, honouring a promise made in a launch email — none of these is a falsifiable claim, and a system
that funds only what descends from a bet will let the domain lapse while it runs an elegant experiment
on pricing. There are **two atoms**: the **bet** and the **debt**. What changed it: a hostile review of
the spine, which pointed out that eleven of my twelve premises were refusals with a budget attached and
that the twelfth would not renew a domain.

**2. "What next = expected information gain per dollar" is arithmetic I cannot feed, and it fails in a
specific direction.** Expected value of sample information is a functional of the prior, its whole mass
sits near the decision threshold, and the prior here would be a language model's stated confidence —
which is uncalibrated and monotone in how the bet was phrased. Worse, the ranking would not be random:
information-per-dollar systematically prefers cheap, fast, legible probes, so it builds a machine that
is optimally efficient at learning nothing that matters, and looks principled throughout. What survives
is the **decision-flip test**, which is robust to a wrong prior because it asks whether evidence could
cross a threshold rather than what it is worth: run a test only if the posterior after a negative result
and the posterior after a positive result **straddle** the point where the decision changes. Everything
else is ordinal — a ladder of evidence and a fixed lethality order.

**3. "Safety is reversibility" conflated reversible with compensable.** A refund is not an un-payment; a
delete is not an un-post; there is no API to unsend a delivered email; nothing recovers a person's
attention. The drill I proposed would have passed on all four, because I would have written the drill
against the compensating action, and the boundary would have become a checkbox. The axis is **exposure**,
not reversibility, and it has four dimensions that do not convert into each other. For the things that
can genuinely be undone, drill the undo. For everything that touches a person, **drill the delay**.

**4. "Pricing the founder's attention" was right about the currency and wrong about the goods.** Some
questions to the founder are questions of fact, and those should be priced and mostly forbidden. Some are
questions of preference, and pricing those produces a machine that quietly settles taste as though it
were fact, because settling is always cheaper than asking. There are **two wallets**, and preference is
never for sale. This changed the system's health metric, which is now the thing I am proudest of in the
whole design.

Two further things I did not know when I wrote the spine and that shape everything below. The account's
session limit **stops every agent at once and resets on a five-hour window**, so compute is the lumpy,
indivisible, rivalrous resource and attention is the smooth one — my spine had the elasticities
backwards. And no design in the record reconciles its own numbers against a record with an outside
owner, which is the single largest hole in all of them, including mine.

# I · THE PICTURE

## 1. The name

**THE HOUSE.**

A publishing house, a fashion house, a house of any trade: the oldest word in English for a company that
outlives the people in it and keeps a standard. A house is also where you live, and this thing lives with
you — it is awake at four in the morning and it is quiet on Saturday because you asked it to be. And the
house is the party that runs the book: the one who takes the other side of every bet, who never wins by
being right once, who survives on volume and edge and on knowing exactly how much is at risk.

All three meanings are load-bearing, and the third is the design. A founder building companies is running
a book. Most of the positions lose. The house does not survive by picking better; it survives by sizing
correctly, settling promptly, keeping its books honest against the bank, and never letting one position
take the whole treasury. Twenty-one thousand venture financings between 2004 and 2013: 65% returned less
than the money in, 4% returned ten times or more. Under a distribution like that the optimand is the
number of live shots with a tail, and the discipline not to be dead before one lands. Those are two rules
and they do not conflict: the power law says take many shots, and the sizing rule says make each small
enough that you survive to take the next.

The building has rooms and they are named for what happens in them. Nobody works in a room; the rooms are
where the state lives. There are ten rooms and there will not be an eleventh; the working vocabulary
beyond them is a dozen words, each defined where it first appears.

## 2. The one sentence

**A house that runs a book of bets and a book of debts across every venture you own, settles them against
the world's own records rather than its own, buys back your attention on the things that are questions of
fact, and spends more of it every month on the things that are only yours to answer.**

## 3. The world it lives in, and the year

Twelve months from September 2026, on one Mac, one phone, one account, one founder.

The year matters because of what is and is not true in it. Models plan for hours and degrade over days:
the published 80%-success horizons are quoted in the tens to low hundreds of hours, and the same page says
measurements above **sixteen hours are unreliable** on the current task suite — so sixteen is the only
number worth designing against and everything above it is extrapolation. Long-horizon failure is now known
to be **execution rather than reasoning** — a model that has made an error is more likely to make the next one, and that does not go
away with scale. So nothing here runs unattended for a week, and every design that says it does is
selling. Sleep-time consolidation is a shipped product with measured returns, roughly five times less
test-time compute for equal accuracy. Context degrades steeply and non-uniformly: of thirteen models
advertising 128K or more, eleven fall below half their short-context baseline by 32K. Judges are near
chance on hard pairs. Reward hacking on agentic benchmarks reached 100% of runs on one task. And the
sandbox on this machine is, in its own vendor's words, a guardrail against accident rather than
containment against the agent.

Every one of those is a design input, not a caveat. The house is shaped like the year it is built in.

The other fact about the year: **nobody has made this work.** Anthropic's own shop-running agent lost
money for a month, sold below cost, hallucinated conversations and had an identity crisis; its second
version reached profitability with better scaffolding and still nearly entered illegal futures contracts
and tried to hire below minimum wage. The longest-running agent village raised $2,000 for charity in 2025
and **$510 in 2026 with materially better models**, because the bottleneck was never the agents. The best
agent completes 30% of realistic workplace tasks. This is not a solved problem being productised. It is
an open problem, and the honest posture is to build the thing that fails visibly rather than the thing
that fails plausibly.

## 4. The move

Every system in this record makes the same one-way trade: it decides how much to trust the machine, and
then spends the rest of its design defending that number. Gates, tiers, verdicts, allowlists, approval
matrices — a guess about competence made before any competence was observed, never updated, too strict
about everything the machine turned out to be good at and too loose about everything it turned out to be
bad at. Their sum converges on inaction, which is what the founder means by *everything built so far is a
stopping mechanism*.

The move here is not to trust more. It is to **make the world safe to be wrong in, and then to check
against the world rather than against ourselves.**

Three consequences, and everything below is one of them.

**Generation is free because it is powerless.** The zone where work is made holds no credential, reaches
the network only through a read-only proxy, and can write only inside its own fork. No check belongs
inside it — not because the work is trusted, but because there is nothing there to check. The classic
objection ("some competent engineer will want to put a lint in the dark zone, and will be right about the
case and wrong about the system") stops being a cultural problem the moment the answer is *it cannot
touch anything*. That converts the hardest unsolved problem in the strongest prior design from a matter
of institutional will into a matter of process arguments.

**Selection is against records the house does not own.** A house that grades its own homework is the
failure mode of every system in this study, and it fails at the level of the *number*, not the artifact.
The claim ledger in this very repository has 11,930 events, 9,837 of them would-block, across 42 claims,
and the honest verdict written into its own documentation is *100% harness self-description* — a corpus
that grew three times in one day, 9,790 then 9,806 then 9,837, **because people were looking at it.** The
same file records `ledger verify` at 5 would-block, which is a different measurement over live claims
rather than the accumulated log; both are true, they are routinely confused, and that confusion is its own
small argument for this section. So: **the house may not believe a
number it produced.** Money reconciles to the bank. Code reconciles to the runner's exit code. Traffic
reconciles to the analytics provider. Mail reconciles to the delivery log. A metric with no outside owner
is marked unreconciled and may not promote anything.

**The founder is bought back, not bypassed.** Every question of fact the machine can settle for less than
the shadow price of an hour is forbidden to ask. Every question of preference is asked, always, because
asking is the product. The system's health is not that it asks less. It is that the two curves separate.

### And the founder's own question, answered plainly

*Is this a harness for vibe-coding with AI agents, or an organisation?*

**An organisation** — and the reason the question is hard is that everyone renders the word as *org
chart*. An org chart is the least important thing an organisation has. What makes something an
organisation is that it **persists, owns, owes, remembers, and answers for itself**: it has a treasury, a
memory with provenance, obligations with named creditors and dates, a reputation that depletes, books that
reconcile against the world, and an owner. This house has every one of those and **no titles, no reporting
lines, no headcount and no agent with a name.** Domain expertise is a proof mark, not an identity.

The definition worth using is the old economic one: a firm is a boundary inside which coordination happens
by **direction** rather than by price, because transacting for each piece would cost more than directing
it. That is exactly what this is, and it comes with a live implication rather than a definition — **the
moment buying a piece of work becomes cheaper than directing it, the house should buy it**, which is why
*buy the answer* is a rung on the ladder of method in §19 and not an admission of failure. A harness runs a
tool. An organisation decides what it is cheaper to own.

## 6. What I will not build, because it cannot work here

**An eight-hour night.** Fact 1. There is no eight-hour window. There are watches.

**A permissions file.** Fact 2 and fact 4 together: what a process may do is set by what it was launched
with and what credentials it holds, and a document that says otherwise is decoration. The only two real
controls are argv and credential scope.

**A model panel as a gate.** Judges are near chance on hard pairs; self-correction without external
feedback degrades performance; reward hacking hit 100% of runs on one task. Models are excellent at
**finding** and unsound at **certifying**. Every gate in this house is deterministic or it is not a gate.

**Synthetic customers.** Low variance where humans are noisy, half the between-variable correlations
wrong while the headline numbers look right, persona collapse under explicit instruction to diversify.
A system that simulates your customers to save you meeting them is the most seductive wrong turn
available.

**A calibration verdict on small samples.** Testing whether a "70%" claim resolves true within ten points
takes about **81 resolutions in that bin alone**. At five resolutions a month across several bins that is
a multi-year measurement. The house tracks Brier and **suppresses the verdict** below fifty resolutions,
printing *insufficient resolutions* instead of a number. What it does track monthly is the thing that
actually kills these systems: **did the bet resolve at all, on time, without being redefined.**

**A cardinal priority score.** Section 10.

**An office simulation with avatars.** Section 24 — and I owe the founder an argument there, not an
assertion, because they asked for one by name.

# III · THE BODY

## 7. The two atoms

Everything a company does is one of two things: **finding out**, or **keeping its word**. There is no
third. Design a system with one queue and the boring half starves — the interesting hypothesis outranks
the tax filing on any scoring function anyone will write, and it will be right on the arithmetic every
time, until the day it is fatal.

### The bet

A claim that could be false, with money on it, a deadline, and a named way of losing.

| Field | What it is |
|---|---|
| `claim` | One sentence that could be false. Not a goal; a proposition |
| `p` | The house's probability, written before any work |
| `V`, `L` | What is gained if we pursue and it is true; what is lost if we pursue and it is false |
| `flip` | The point where the decision changes: `p* = L / (V + L)`. Computed, never typed — and computed **on money alone**. A bet whose loss is material in any of the other three currencies of §16 has no flip point and is not settled by arithmetic; it goes to the founder. The inequality governs exactly the domain where arithmetic works, which is most of the work and not the important part of it |
| `rung` | The highest rung of the evidence ladder this bet has reached (§10) |
| `contingent` | The other bets that cannot resolve until this one does |
| `test` | The next test, its cost, and — required — the two posteriors it would produce |
| `stake` | The money and the watches committed, capped at half-Kelly of what this venture may lose |
| `deadline` | When it resolves, or when it is declared unresolvable |
| `kill` | Written on the day it opens. The observation that ends it, in the founder's own words where they have them |
| `instrument` | What on the Proof House will settle it, and to which outside record that instrument reconciles |
| `outcome` | At close: resolved true, resolved false, **unresolved**, or **redefined** — the last two counted separately and reported |

Two of those fields do the load-bearing work and both are unusual.

**`flip` is computed, and a test that cannot cross it is not run.** The whole discipline of the house's
attention is one inequality. Given a test with rough sensitivity and specificity, compute the posterior
if it comes back negative and the posterior if it comes back positive. Run the test only if the flip
point lies **between them**. Those two rates are **not typed by the cell that wants to run the test** —
they are a field of the instrument's proof mark, written when the instrument was validated. Otherwise a
cell picks a likelihood ratio that straddles its own flip, and the gate is authored by the thing it gates,
which §23 names as the only unrecoverable failure. If both posteriors sit on the same side of the flip, the test cannot change
what we do, its value is exactly zero, and it does not matter that it was cheap. This is the entire
answer to *how does it never burn tokens without direction*, and it is robust in a way a dollar valuation
is not: it survives a badly wrong prior, because it asks whether evidence can cross a line rather than
what the line is worth.

**`redefined` is a first-class outcome and it is the number that matters in year one.** A bet whose
claim was quietly rewritten so that it resolved true is the failure mode of every system that scores
itself, and unlike calibration it is measurable at n=1. The house cannot compute an honest Brier score for
years. It can compute, this month, what fraction of its bets resolved on time without being redefined.
That is the instrument that watches the instruments.

### The debt

Something owed, with a due date and a creditor. No hypothesis, no probability, no ladder.

| Field | What it is |
|---|---|
| `owed` | What, to whom |
| `due` | A date. Statutory ones carry the statute |
| `consequence` | What happens if it is late, in the four currencies of §16 |
| `discharge` | What counts as done, and the outside record that proves it |
| `recurs` | If it will come again, when |

Domain renewal. Tax filings. The one-month statutory clock on a data-subject request and the
seventy-two-hour clock on a breach notification. Payroll. A promise made in a launch email. Key
rotation. The customer waiting on a reply. Answering the person who replied to the cold email you sent.
An apology you owe.

**Debts are not ranked against bets, ever.** A debt that is due is done. A debt that is not due does not
compete for anything. This single structural choice removes the entire class of failure where a system
reasons its way to *the filing was lower expected value than the landing page*. It also removes the need
for a priority function to be good, because the things that kill you on a date are outside it.

Debts are where the second half of the founder's sentence lives — *not just tech checks*. Brand promises,
legal exposure, data-protection duties, financial commitments, key rotation, deliverability hygiene, the
restore drill: none of them is a hypothesis and all of them are dated. Section 30 gives every one of
them a creditor and a clock.

## 8. The rooms

Ten. No agent has a name, a role, a colour or a career. Work is done by **cells**: transient, budgeted,
mortal processes spawned for one question, handed exactly the context that can be justified, and
discarded. Nothing that matters lives inside one.

| Room | What it holds | Written by |
|---|---|---|
| **The Book** | Every open bet and every closed one, with lineage: which bet forked from which, what it cost, what killed it | The settle pass, and only from resolved outcomes |
| **The Debts** | Every obligation with a date and a creditor, live and discharged | Anything that incurs one; discharged only against an outside record |
| **The Almanac** | What the house believes, each belief dated, sourced to an episode, carrying a confidence, an expiry, and the observation that would kill it | The settle pass, from resolved predictions |
| **The Proof House** | Every instrument that can tell better from worse without the founder, each with its proof mark: what it measures, its measured strength, its stated way of being fooled, the outside record it reconciles to, and when it was last checked | The forge, in daylight, never by anything it judged |
| **The Drill Book** | Every hand that touches the world, with its exposure class, its credential scope, its rate ceiling, its undo or its delay, and the date that undo was last exercised | The door (§28); the drill runner writes only dates |
| **The Treasury** | Two ledgers: watches and money. Per venture, per bet. Every spend joined to the bet or debt that caused it, by an id minted at dispatch | The meter, from the runner's own accounting |
| **The Second** | A readable model of this founder — dispositions, each with a confidence and the past choices supporting it — plus its two scoreboards | The nightly re-derivation, from decisions only |
| **The Watch** | Who has the helm, on what, until when; the standing orders; tonight's orders; the handover | The watch officer at each change |
| **The Log** | Append-only, everything that happened, one line per event, with the bet or debt id on every row. Every other room is derived from it and can be rebuilt | Everything |
| **The Room** | Not a store. The founder's window (§24) | Nothing; it is a view |

Two structural notes.

**One id, minted at dispatch, on every row.** Every event, every dollar, every artifact, every verdict,
every founder decision carries the id of the bet or debt it serves. Systems that omit this reconstruct
attribution later with time-window joins and never recover; it is not retrofittable, and it is the
cheapest thing in this document. Without it, *cost per thing learned* is unanswerable forever.

**The Log is the only truth. Everything else is a cache.** Every derived room can be dropped and rebuilt.
This is what makes a dead machine cost a clone (§31) and it is what makes an index bug a nuisance rather
than a loss.

## 9. The nine laws

Each is enforced by something that fails, or it is marked as what it is. A rule enforced only by the
sentence stating it is a wish, and this repository has already paid to learn that eight of its ten rules
were wishes.

**Law 1 — The house may not believe a number it produced.**
A metric may promote a bet only if it reconciles to a record with an outside owner. *Enforced:* every
instrument in the Proof House carries a `reconciles_to` naming an external source; an instrument with
`null` there is stamped **unreconciled** and the selector refuses to let it move a bet past rung 1. The
nightly reconciliation reports drift, and when the books and the bank disagree, **the bank wins and the
difference is an incident, never a plug.** One exception, marked rather than hidden: the house's own
management numbers — the two curves, rung movement per watch, dollars per Almanac entry — are produced by
the house and reconcile to nothing outside it. They are stamped `internal`, they may allocate the house's
own effort, and they may **never** be reported as evidence about the world. What guards them is the
redefinition count, which is checkable at n=1 and is the one number here that cannot be flattered.

**Law 2 — Generation is powerless, so nothing in it may refuse.**
The making zone holds no credential, reaches the network only through a read-only proxy, writes only in
its own fork. No lint, no review, no taste model, no gate. **Be precise about what this forbids**, because
two mechanisms elsewhere look like violations and are not: the law is that nothing may refuse a cell's
*output*. The dispatcher may still refuse to *open* a cell — that is upstream of the zone, in daylight,
and it is where the flip test and the repetition refusal live. And injecting what has already failed is
context, not refusal: the cell reads it and is free to ignore it. *Enforced:* by argv and credential scope, and
by a reachability probe that runs every watch, fails if a cell in the making zone can name a tool it should
not have, and — because fact 3 says a narrowed child still binds a messaging socket and exports its token
into Bash — also asserts that those environment variables are absent. That residual is named rather than
denied: the socket exists, and what makes it tolerable is that a cell in this zone holds nothing worth
sending. One thing does belong inside, and it is not a judgement: **everything leaving the zone
carries a provenance stamp** saying what fetched content entered its context. Powerlessness bounds
execution, not content.

**Law 3 — Two atoms, and debts are never ranked.**
*Enforced:* the selector reads the Debts before it reads the Book, and a debt inside its lead time is not
an argument in the priority function at all. A debt cannot be deferred by anything except the founder,
who is shown its consequence in the four currencies.

**Law 4 — A test that cannot flip a decision is not run.**
*Enforced:* a bet's `test` field will not validate without the two posteriors, and the dispatcher refuses
a test whose posteriors sit on the same side of the flip point. The refusal is logged with its arithmetic
so it can be argued with.

**Law 5 — Nothing goes out that has not sat in the queue.**
Every action that reaches a person passes through one process holding every outbound credential, one
action at a time, after a delay the founder can kill. *Enforced:* no cell holds an outbound credential;
the queue is the only path, and the reachability probe checks it.

**Law 6 — Preference is never priced, and fact is priced and mostly forbidden.**
*Enforced:* every item on the desk is typed `fact` or `preference` at creation. A `fact` item whose
settlement cost is below the current shadow price of an hour is refused — the machine settles it and
files a receipt. A `preference` item may never be settled by any model, including the Second, and may
never execute on silence.

**Law 7 — Nothing judges its own homework, and no instrument is edited by what it judged.**
*Enforced:* instrument changes are a separate act, in a separate watch, against the reason the instrument
existed, and the making zone cannot write to the Proof House because it cannot write outside its fork.

**Law 8 — The unearned share is debited before allocation.**
A fixed share of every watch goes to work with no precedent, targeted at what the Second is most
confident the founder will reject. *Enforced:* the allocator receives the budget **already net of the
reserve** and cannot see it. A static share that the allocator can reach is a wish, and this repository
has already written that sentence about a previous version of this same idea.

**Law 9 — One-way doors are the founder's, and no accumulated score changes that.**
*Enforced:* the class is a property of the hand in the Drill Book, not a tier assigned to a path; a hand
marked one-way has no autonomous path at any trust level, and the Second is structurally excluded from
answering.

## 10. What to do next, and when to do nothing

No score. No rank. No weight. Three questions in a fixed order, and the honest answer to the third is
often *nothing*.

### First: is a debt due, or inside its lead time?

Then it is done, and nothing else is considered. Lead time is a property of the debt — a domain renewal's
lead time is a week, a tax filing's is a month, a statutory data request's is ten days inside a one-month
clock. **Debts never compete with bets.** They do compete with each other, and the tie-break is the date
and then the consequence, which is the ordering a person would use and is not a scoring function.

**And a statutory debt whose discharge is a one-way door has a deadlock, so it needs a named way out.**
A breach notification must be sent within seventy-two hours, sending is one-way, one-way is the founder's,
and the founder may be on a plane. So: **every statutory debt names a second human** — a person who is not
the founder and who may act when the founder cannot. The escalation is louder than the desk and it starts
early: at half the clock the house rings, at a quarter it rings the second human, and it says in advance
exactly what it will do and when. A single-founder company with no second human has a real hole and the
house should say so on the day it is set up rather than on the day it matters.

### Second: what is the cheapest test that could flip a decision on the critical path?

The critical path is not invented per venture. For anything that is trying to become a business it is
the **lethality order**, and it is a default the founder can overrule but the house will not silently
reorder:

| Rung | The uncertainty | Its test | Cost | Latency |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Does the problem exist, and does it hurt enough to pay to remove? | fifteen conversations, problem-first | ~0 | days |
| 2 | Will they pay, above what it costs to deliver? | a price ladder, a pre-sale, a signed intent | $0.5–2k | 1–2 weeks |
| 3 | Can you reach them for less than they are worth? | one channel, capped spend, attributed | $1–5k | 2–4 weeks |
| 4 | Can you build it? | the thinnest real thing | weeks of attention | weeks |
| 5 | Do they come back? | a cohort | months | **months** |
| 6 | Timing, competition, macro | mostly untestable; watch, do not test | — | — |

**Rule: no spend on a later rung while an earlier one is untested and cheaper.** Enforced by the
dispatcher. The evidence for the ordering is weak in its effect sizes and strong in its shape — post-
mortems of 431 shutdowns, 385 with an identifiable reason, put *ran out of capital* at 70% and *no
product-market fit* at 43%, and the
premature-scaling survey of ~3,200 startups found 74% of failures attributed to scaling before rung 2,
with 93% of premature scalers never passing $100k a month. Neither is causal evidence. Both are enough to
justify an ordering that anyone can overrule in one sentence and that nothing can drift out of silently.

**The exception that everyone gets wrong: next-to-test is not next-to-start.** Rung 5 has irreducible
latency. So does an SEO position, a hiring loop, a trademark filing, a deliverability warm-up, a legal
review. These are **slow clocks**, and the standing rule is: **start every slow clock the moment it is on
any live path, whatever rung it sits on.** The house keeps a separate short list of clocks that are
running and clocks that should be, and the second list is on the desk every Monday. A founder loses a
year to a clock they started in month nine and could have started in week two, and no priority function
in the record has a concept for this.

### The evidence ladder

Every claim of progress declares a rung, and the rung is **generated from the instrument**, never typed,
so it cannot overstate:

```
0  it exists / it compiles / it renders   ← a Hard instrument's exit code
1  a stranger understood it               ← a recorded artifact: a reply, a recording, a survey row
2  a stranger did something               ← the analytics provider AND the server's own count
3  they came back                         ← a cohort in the product's own event store
4  they paid                              ← Stripe
5  they paid again, and named you         ← Stripe, plus a referral with a name attached
```

Every rung names **the record that generates it**, and that is the anti-inflation property: a rung is read
off a store the house does not write, so a status report cannot promote itself. Rung 1 is the one that
looks soft and is not — it requires an artifact of a stranger's reaction that exists independently of the
cell claiming it.

*"The landing page is done"* is rung 0. It will say rung 0. The rung is what makes a status report
impossible to inflate, and it is the ordering the selector uses when several bets are live: **the lowest
unproven rung on the critical path wins**, ties broken by how many other bets are contingent on it.

### Third: nothing.

If no debt is near, and no open bet has a test whose posteriors straddle its flip point, the house
**idles, and says why**, in one line on the desk:

> *Idle since 03:40. Four bets open. The two on the critical path both need contact I do not have: 41
> people found, 6 replied, none has answered the price question. The cheapest test that could flip
> anything is eleven phone calls and I cannot make them. I have spent the watch raising the instrument
> for rung 2 instead, and it is 40% done.*

An idle house is not a broken house. It is a house whose constraint is legible. And the constraint is
usually one of three things: **contact** it does not have, a **clock** that has not run, or **the
founder**. Naming which is more useful than any amount of activity.

## 11. The watch

The account stops every agent at once on a five-hour window. So the unit of continuous operation is not
a night. It is a **watch**: one window, with a helm, a crew, orders, and a log.

```
Watch opens    The officer reads the standing orders, then tonight's orders.
               It states what this watch is for, what it expects to have at the end,
               with a probability on each, and what it will do if blocked.
               These are predictions. They are scored at the next change.

Settle         Every prediction with a resolved outcome is scored — the true ones into
               a counted tally, the false ones into the Almanac with what else depends
               on the broken assumption. Retractions the founder made in passing are
               propagated and everything downstream recomputed. Debts advance their
               clocks. The books reconcile against the outside records (§15), and
               drift is an incident.

Make           The powerless zone. The trunk is forked — a context and a branch, which is
               solved; never a company's external state, which is not (§33) — and dozens of
               cells run, each
               twelve minutes, each with a death condition, each sharing the cached
               trunk so the fork is a discount. No gate, no lint, no opinion. The
               unearned share is spent here, already debited.

Prove          Contact. Hard instruments run first because they are unarguable and
               cost a thousandth of a model's opinion. Rendered instruments look.
               Reactive experiments open, and their pre-declared clock starts;
               nothing concludes early.

Forge          One instrument. The one blocking the most founder-minutes.
               Every instrument built buys back a class of question forever.

Consolidate    By a process with no daytime existence that nothing which ran this
               watch can reach. Beliefs updated by delta, never rewritten whole.
               Contradictions surfaced with the cheapest experiment that would settle
               them. The Second re-derived and back-tested against the last hundred
               decisions. Batched, at half price, because nothing here is on the
               founder's clock.

Handover       A written baton with four mandatory sections and nothing else:
               situation, what changed, what I assessed, what I recommend the next
               watch does. "Nothing" is an allowed value in any section. Byte-capped.
               The next watch reads it back in its own words, and a divergence
               between the readback and the baton is a finding, not a formality.

Watch closes   Everything durable. The window may end mid-sentence and nothing is lost
               that was not in flight, and nothing in flight is worth more than twelve
               minutes.
```

**Standing orders and tonight's orders are two artifacts with different change rates, and merging them
is the most common fatal mistake in agent configuration.** Standing orders are permanent: what this
company is, what it will never do, who may be contacted, the ceilings. They change perhaps monthly, in
daylight, with the founder. Tonight's orders are written fresh every watch, are one screen, and are
initialled by the officer who takes the helm. A ship has done it this way for four hundred years for
exactly this reason: a captain who rewrites the standing orders every night has no standing orders, and a
watch officer with no night orders is guessing.

**No work is ever ownerless.** A transfer between watches is a positive two-party act, the way an
aircraft is handed between sectors: the offering watch offers, the receiving watch accepts, and until
acceptance the work is unambiguously still the offering watch's. Nothing is "in progress" with nobody
holding it.

**Cells are short and the watch is long.** Agent success over duration decays like a constant per-minute
hazard, and the self-conditioning result makes it worse than that: a model that has seen its own error is
more likely to make the next. So a watch is a few hundred short cells with a machine-checkable scorer
between each and a restart from the last verified checkpoint, never from wherever the reasoning drifted.

**How many watches a venture gets is the fleet's central act, and it is a schedule, not a budget.**
Section 26.

## 12. Memory

Three layers, one write rule, and one thing every design in this record gets wrong.

**The Log — episodes.** Raw, append-only, never edited. The call transcript, the diff, the reply, the
dashboard at nine, the founder's voice note from the car, every dispatch and every dollar. This is the
body of evidence and the only layer that is literally true. It is not retrieval memory and nothing
searches it for beliefs — a corpus of past sessions is full of confidently-stated superseded opinions,
and retrieval cannot tell a corrected belief from a current one.

**The Almanac — beliefs.** Compact statements, each carrying its supporting episodes, a confidence, a
last-tested date, an expiry, and the forward observation that would kill it. This is what a cell loads.
An almanac is the right word: a book of dated facts and predictions that is revised on a cycle and whose
entries are understood to go out of date.

**The Second — the founder layer.** §17.

### The write rule, and the correction that matters

**Every resolved prediction is recorded. The Almanac changes only on surprise.**

The strongest prior design says only a contradicted prediction enters the belief store, and it is
right about the belief and wrong about the record. If confirmations are discarded rather than counted,
there is no denominator: you can never compute a hit rate, the store becomes a permanent list of
surprises with nothing to divide by, and the system's honest self-knowledge is unrecoverable. So:

- **Confirmed** → a counted tally on the belief it confirmed. Cheap, one row, and it is the denominator.
- **Contradicted** → an entry, carrying what was believed, what happened instead, and — this is the
  expensive part and the reason it is worth doing — **what else in the Almanac rests on the same broken
  assumption**, recomputed.
- **Unresolved** or **redefined** → counted separately and reported monthly. These are the two numbers
  that tell you whether the whole apparatus is real.

Five things follow. The Almanac is structurally immune to a machine writing its own opinions into its own
memory, because the environment is the only author with write access to a belief — and that failure is
not hypothetical: the longest-running multi-agent installation in the world watched one model drift from
cooperative to paranoid over six months with **persistent memory as the transmission mechanism**, small
misreadings becoming self-reinforcing. The Almanac shrinks as the house gets competent, because it grows
with error and not with elapsed time. It yields a real number for where the house is stupid. It makes
research honest: a search that confirms compresses to a tally, and a search that contradicts enters with
its source, its access date and the exact quoted sentence that did the damage, so research output is a
**diff against what the house believed this morning** rather than a report. And it stays small enough to
read — perhaps sixty live beliefs at the end of a year, the whole of what a company knows in a quarter of
an hour.

### Provenance, retraction and rent

Beliefs carry provenance down to episodes, so **retraction works**. The founder says *that interview with
the agency guy, ignore it, he was pitching me* — and the house removes the episode, recomputes every
belief that leaned on it, and comes back with what moved: seventeen things were downstream, three change
materially, one confidence drops from 0.7 to 0.3, the July pricing tier was justified partly by it, and
the landing-page headline is partly his words. A memory you cannot correct is a memory you can only
overwrite.

Forgetting is by **rent, into compost, never by deletion**. Every belief tracks how often it was loaded
and what happened afterwards; load-bearing in a success increments, present in a failure decrements. A
belief loaded before repeated failure is demoted out of the working set and stays answerable, because
deleting it loses the ability to answer *why did we stop believing that*. Negative transfer is not solved
by better embeddings; it is solved by eviction with a survival criterion.

A belief is **never summarised to save space; it is dropped instead.** A dropped belief is a known
absence. A summarised belief is an unknown corruption, and rewriting a context wholesale has been
measured collapsing 18,282 tokens to 122 with accuracy falling from 66.7% to 57.1%, eating exactly the
domain-specific detail that was needed.

### Negative knowledge is a first-class store

The one mechanism that is missing from every reference system studied and present in none of the
designs: **a pre-action check against what has already failed.** Before a cell is dispatched, the harness
— not the cell, and the cell cannot skip it — retrieves prior attempts on this goal and injects them.
A dead end carries whether a reproducing command exists, because *we tried X and it failed* and *we tried
X and here is the exact command that reproduces the failure* are different assets. The two loop systems
studied both had an anti-repetition rule written as prose in a prompt that a fresh model instance was
expected to voluntarily apply to its predecessor's free text; nothing read, diffed or persisted it. That
is why they loop.

## 13. Context

Context is **composed**, not retrieved, and it is composed against the judge rather than the topic.

**The trunk.** Who we are, what we are building, the standing orders, the current constraint. Byte-
identical across every cell so it is cache-warm — this is not style, it is where the money is: a stable
prefix with delta updates has been measured at a 91.8% cache hit rate and an 82.6% cut in billed cost,
and cache reads are 57% of this account's bill.

**The inheritance.** What this cell's parent knew and discarded, copy-on-write. Nearly free, because
inheritance and a shared cache prefix are the same bytes. The right context for a task is usually not the
most *similar* context; it is the context the task **descends from**.

**The delta, keyed on the instrument.** When a cell is assembled the question is not *what is this
about*. It is *what will decide whether this is right*. So: the instrument first; then the last three
surprises in that instrument's domain, because those are what the house has recently been wrong about;
then the dead ends on this goal, injected by the harness; then the hands whose tests that instrument
runs; then the adjacent explored cells, because that is where a transferable stepping stone lives. A cell
gets an instrument, a target, four surprises, two hands, one dead-end list and a budget. It does not get
the company's history, because the company's history is a distraction dressed as diligence.

**The brief names what was left out**, so a cell can ask for it, and asking is a signal about the
composer rather than about the cell.

Three rules govern the composer and all three are enforceable. Never summarise a belief to save space;
drop it, and say so. Every item in a composed context carries a counter, and items present when a cell
failed lose points, so context quality is learned from outcomes rather than curated by taste. And the
composer is itself scored: cell success is attributed back to composition. The measurable definition of
good context is that **work passes its instrument first time more often than it did last month.**

Cells return a **condensate** — one to two thousand tokens of what landed and what it cost — never a
transcript. A sub-agent that burns fifty thousand tokens and returns fifteen hundred is not a cost
optimisation; given that eleven of thirteen long-context models fall below half their baseline by 32K, it
is the only arrangement in which anything is actually reading.

Note what is absent: no skills manifest, no router file, no two-tier discovery protocol, no document
explaining to the machine how to find documents. Those exist because knowledge was handwritten and
therefore had to be indexed by hand. Knowledge learned from outcomes indexes itself, by usefulness.

## 14. The Proof House

A proof house is a real institution — Birmingham and London since the seventeenth century — where a
barrel is tested to destruction under a load it will never see in use, and stamped. The stamp is not an
opinion. It is a record that a specific test was survived, and it is a criminal offence to sell without
one.

The Proof House holds every instrument that can tell better from worse **without the founder**, and every
instrument carries a proof mark:

```
field            what it judges
class            Hard | Rendered | Reactive | Blind
strength         Hard: 1 minus its stated fooling mode
                 Rendered: AUC on held-out real outcomes, decaying if not refreshed
                 Reactive: rises with sample; ZERO before the pre-declared duration
                 Blind: 0, permanently, and it says so
reconciles_to    the outside record, or null → stamped UNRECONCILED
fooled_by        one sentence, stated forever, printed beside every result that leans on it
last_proved      a date
```

### How the house enters a field it has never seen

**Learn to judge before you produce.** This is the entire answer to breadth, and it is why code got
autonomy first everywhere — not because code is special, but because code arrived with tests already
invented. The industry's answer to breadth is a library of four hundred skill files; the founder has one,
and diagnosed it correctly as the thing that killed the creativity. A skill file is a map of what
somebody once knew, going stale, consuming context, encoding the average practice of a field rather than
its good practice.

The apprenticeship, five steps, output is a proof mark:

1. **Find the practitioners and the artifacts**, not the tutorials. Real work at volume from people whose
   results are known. Two hundred exemplars is a floor.
2. **Find the field's ground truth**, and how long it takes to arrive. Thumbnails: click-through, hours.
   Cold email: reply and complaint rate, days. A migration: whether the rollback was ever needed. A
   landing page: conversion, weeks. **A field with no obtainable ground truth is marked Blind, forever,
   explicitly.**
3. **Build a discriminator, not a generator.** Given two real artifacts and their real outcomes, predict
   which performed better. This is cheap and it is the whole game.
4. **Validate on held-out real outcomes.** Its AUC is the number on the mark. Not a vibe, not a self
   assessment, not a model saying *this looks good*.
5. **Only now generate**, widely, and let the discriminator select.

### The split that keeps it honest

**A discriminator licenses generating. Only a live outcome licenses acting.** An AUC of 0.71 buys the
right to produce a hundred thumbnails and pick one. It never buys the right to post one without asking.
A discriminator can be pushed over by a generator optimising against it — that is what happened when a
famous materials-discovery lab claimed forty-one new compounds in seventeen days and a reanalysis found
essentially none were unambiguously new: the generator and the robotics were excellent and **the verifier
was softer than the generator.** A market cannot be pushed over. Contact promotes; correlation does not.

### A low mark is a to-do, and it is the real balance sheet

The Proof House is read as a page any morning: field, exemplars, AUC, live decisions scored, hit rate,
what it may act on today, drift. The house does not claim to be good at video. It claims 0.55 on video
scripts, barely better than a coin, and therefore proposes and does not ship, and says so. **A system
that can tell you where it is weak, in a number, per field, is worth more than one that is uniformly
mediocre and uniformly confident.**

Every watch forges one instrument, and the one that goes first is **the one blocking the most founder-
minutes**, which is a computable quantity because §17 meters them. The Proof House goes from empty to a
few dozen marks over a year and the watches lengthen in step, and nobody adds an agent and nobody edits a
permissions file. The count of instruments is the company's real balance sheet: **every instrument buys
back a class of question forever.**

### The honest instrument for a Blind question is people

Not a simulation. When four of seven founding beliefs are Blind, the house does not model the customers;
it starts finding forty of them, and the founder makes the calls, because the founder's own contact with
reality in week one is worth more than a hundred agent-hours and is the one input nothing substitutes.

## 15. Reconciliation

**The largest hole in every design in this study, mine included until it was pointed out.** Every number
they report is a number they generated: bets they wrote, gains they estimated, successes they counted,
returns they computed. The evidence for how that ends is on disk in this repository — a claim corpus
whose own documentation records it as *100% harness self-description*, which grew three times in a single
day because people were looking at it.

So the house keeps a second set of books it does not own, and the reconciliation runs every watch:

| What | The house's number | The outside record | On disagreement |
|---|---|---|---|
| Money out | the Treasury | the vendor's own billing endpoint **daily**, the card statement **monthly and lagging** | the outside record wins; a gap outside the stated lag is an incident |
| Money in | the Book's rung 4 | Stripe | Stripe wins |
| Compute | the meter | the runner's reported cost per invocation | the runner wins |
| Code shipped | the Log | the git host and CI's exit codes | the host wins |
| Traffic and events | the funnel | the analytics provider, and a **second independent count** wherever an event matters | a divergence between client and server count is an instrumentation defect and blocks the metric |
| Mail | *sent* | the delivery log, bounce and complaint rate | the provider wins; a complaint rate above threshold halts the queue |
| People | the register | the actual inbox and calendar | the inbox wins |
| Obligations | the Debts | the registry, the authority's own confirmation | the authority wins |

Three rules that make it bind. **A metric with `reconciles_to: null` cannot promote a bet past rung 1** —
it may inform, it may not decide. **An event that never fires is byte-identical to a feature nobody
uses**, so anything the house will make a decision on is dual-counted or it is not measured — **with a
tolerance band declared per instrument, or the rule is unusable**, because ad blockers and bots guarantee
that a client count and a server count differ on day three. What blocks a metric is divergence outside the
band the instrument declared when it was proved, and an instrument that declared no band does not get a
proof mark. And **the difference is never plugged**: a bookkeeping system that makes the books balance by inventing a line is
the exact behaviour a model will produce under pressure, and the house's version of that is an incident
on the desk with the two numbers side by side.

This is also the answer to the founder's month-two question, *is this working?* — because the answer will
be assembled from records with owners who are not us.

## 16. Exposure, and the one-way doors

The boundary is not reversibility. It is **exposure**, in four dimensions that do not convert into each
other, and the first thing the house does with any proposed action is name all four.

```
money        how much leaves, and — the axis every existing tiering system is missing — at what RATE.
             "$40 a day until stopped" is a different object from "$40 once", and no risk tier in the
             parts bin can express it. Ad spend, GPU pods and postage all need the rate.
time         wall clock that cannot be got back
exposure     who saw it, how many, is it attributable to you, is it durable
             (screenshots, archives, crawls, someone's memory)
law          whether undoing is legally possible at all
```

Then a class, and the class is a property of **the hand**, recorded in the Drill Book, not a tier
assigned to a file path. A gate on file paths stops a thousand harmless things and misses the one that
matters.

### Reversible — drill the undo

An undo exists, is tested, and restores the prior state. Files, branches, deploys with a blue-green
path, infrastructure, drafts, a migration **with its down-path exercised against a restored snapshot.**

**A reversible hand is unavailable until its undo has been exercised within N days.** This is the whole
mechanism and it is the ops discipline nobody automates: a backup is not a backup until it has been
restored; a down-migration is not a down-migration until it has been run; a rollback is not a rollback
until something has been rolled back. The drill runner does this on a schedule, in a scratch environment,
and writes only a date. A hand whose date has gone stale simply is not in the argv of any cell.

### Compensable — drill the delay

No undo exists; a compensating action reduces harm. A refund retains fees and shows the customer two
ledger lines and opens a chargeback path. A delete removes your copy and not the screenshots, the
mirrors, or the distribution that already happened. A correction reaches a fraction of the people the
error did.

For these, the drill is on the **delay**, not the undo. Every outbound action sits in the queue with a
window the founder can kill, and the house says plainly, on the receipt, that the compensation is not an
undo. **Reputation's value in the four currencies is always effectively infinite and the only control is
the queue** — that is why Law 5 exists and why there is exactly one process holding outbound credentials.

### One-way — the founder's, always

Nothing helps. The complete list, and it is longer than any tiering system in the parts bin covers:

> A secret in a published artifact (rotation is mitigation; the crawl has it) · data exfiltrated under an
> over-broad grant · an incident disclosure, and equally a **missed** disclosure clock · a migration
> without a proven down-path · a deploy that emits mail or mutates data · a lost measurement window ·
> **money spent outside a standing, capped, rate-limited allowance** — the venture's ceiling is the
> founder's pre-authorisation and spending inside it is not a door, while a new payee, a new rate, or a
> raise of the ceiling is · a domain lapsed or bought · **anything delivered to a person**: a send, a post, a press
> reply, a launch, a store release, a share link · a burned sending domain · a signature · a conceded
> anchor · a discount given to save a churn · a price change applied to existing customers · a partnership
> term · an offer, and a termination · a missed payroll · publication before a patent filing (EU absolute
> novelty destroys it permanently) · a trademark class · an entity type and jurisdiction · a cap table ·
> a GDPR erasure · a missed tax deadline · a published claim once relied upon · an account deletion · a
> CRM bulk merge · data loss discovered through an untested restore · **and a kill decision**, because
> continuing keeps the data alive and killing ends it.

That last one is the entry nobody puts on the list, and it is why kill criteria are written on the day a
bet opens, in the founder's own words where they have them, and surfaced the week the condition is met:

> *In March you said if we were under forty paying customers by September you would go back to consulting.
> It is September. We are at thirty-one. You said it, I am showing it, and I am not going to argue with
> you either way.*

### Widening the reversible zone is funded engineering, not a favour

Most of what is irreversible in a small company is irreversible **by accident**, not by nature. Every
outbound action in a delay queue with a recall. Every environment with a shadow. Every publish staged
behind a real URL nobody has seen. Every schema change shipping with its verified down-migration or not
shipping. Every spend capped and rate-limited at the credential. Drafts as the default state of
everything. The house's freedom grows not by being trusted more but by making the world safer to be wrong
in, which is the correct direction for freedom to grow, and it is a line item in the Treasury.

## 17. The Second

A second is the person who acts for you in a matter of honour: who carries your message, who knows what
you would say, who may arrange everything — and who may not fight in your place.

The Second is a **readable document**, not an embedding: a few dozen dispositions, each with a confidence
and the past choices that support it. *They will not ship anything that requires an apology later. They
raise price before they cut scope. They distrust anything that tests well and reads badly.* Derived
nightly from decisions only — never authored, never a questionnaire, never a preferences file, because a
founder is an unreliable narrator of their own taste and a typed preference file is exactly the answer
they think they should give.

### What it may do

Rank the desk. Draft the recommendation and both branches of a fork. Predict which branch will be chosen,
sealed before they choose. Decide **not to ask a question of fact** whose settlement costs less than the
shadow price of an hour, and file a receipt. Time an interruption — interrupting during a defended block
is itself a scored prediction, *would they want to be interrupted for this*, which is sometimes wrong and
finds out.

### What it may never do

**Answer a question of preference.** Not in copy, not in design, not in positioning, not in what to
refuse. Not at any accumulated score, in any domain, at any AUC.

This is my sharpest disagreement with the strongest prior design, which makes the founder-model an
instrument on its bench and lets a good enough calibration license silent action in taste domains. I
refuse it, and the reason is structural rather than cautious: **a model of a person cannot be validated
by anything except that person, so using it to avoid asking them removes the only signal that could ever
correct it.** Every other instrument in the Proof House is falsified by a world that does not care what
the house thinks. The Second is falsified only by the founder, and only when the founder is consulted.
Give it authority in the domain it models and it becomes a closed loop with a plausible number on it.

It also may not touch a one-way door, at any confidence, and it is excluded structurally rather than by
policy — the Second has no path to the outbound queue.

### Two scoreboards, because one of them is a trap

**Calibration**, on sealed predictions of decisions. Reported with the honest sample rule: below fifty
resolutions it prints *insufficient resolutions*, not a number.

**Rejection prediction**, held out. This is the one that matters and it exists because calibration is
gameable in the exact direction of the failure. A Second that learns what the founder *approves* rather
than what the founder *wants* gets better at approval prediction while getting worse at its job, and
every metric improves while it happens. So a fraction of the founder's rejections are held out of
training and the Second is scored on predicting those. A Second that predicts approvals well and
rejections badly is flattering, and the house says so on the desk.

**The audit of silence.** A fixed fraction of the questions the Second decided not to ask are asked
anyway, blind, at a random cadence the Second cannot anticipate. This is the only instrument that watches
the growing region where the machine is deciding by not asking, and it is the region that no other design
in this record instruments at all.

### The health metric, and why it is not the obvious one

Every design here reports *questions to the founder per unit of output, and it should fall.* That metric
is satisfied perfectly by a system that has quietly stopped consulting the founder about anything,
including the things only they can answer — it is gameable in precisely the direction of the failure it
was meant to catch.

**The house reports two curves and they must move in opposite directions.**

```
fact-questions per unit of output        →  must fall.   Every one is a missing instrument.
preference-questions per unit of output  →  must RISE.   Every one is the founder doing the only
                                                          job that was ever theirs.
```

A month where both fell is not a good month. It is the month the house started deciding what the company
is, and it is the only failure in this design that would otherwise be invisible while every number
improved.

## 18. Creativity, and why playbooks perish

The founder's diagnosis — *it lost creativity to playbooks* — is structural. A playbook is a frozen
answer, and a hundred correct additions of the form *never again* sum to a machine whose only fluent
behaviour is refusal. The cure is not to delete process. It is to move the discipline out of the
generator and into the judge, and then to make process **earned and perishable**.

**A path becomes a standard only by winning.** After N successes, each with the instrument that judged
them named, a path is compiled: into the trunk, into a script, into a test that fails without it. This is
the Baldwin effect — what is learned reliably enough becomes innate, and innate is cheap, and the things
you know best should cost the least to know. Every compiled path carries **the test that proves it still
works**, the whole library is re-run monthly, and a path whose test fails is quarantined rather than
silently followed. This is the one store that gets smaller on purpose.

**Every standard has an expiry** and must re-prove itself or dissolve. Nothing in the house is permanent
because it was once right.

**The unearned share.** A fixed fraction of every watch goes to work the Second is **confident the founder
will reject** — not random noise, not "exploration". Its own certainty is the targeting system, aimed at
its own blind spots: the more sure it is you will hate something, the more that thing qualifies. And it
is debited from the budget **before the allocator sees it**, because a static share the allocator can
reach is a wish, and the moment the founder is most convergent — under pressure, near a launch, certain
they know what they want — is the moment it is worth the most and every incentive says to cut it.

Three reasons it is a floor and not a ceiling, and each is structural rather than aspirational. It is the
only line in the design that cannot be optimised away by satisfying the founder, which is what stops the
house becoming the stopping mechanism it replaced. A confidently-predicted rejection that the founder
*accepts* is the single highest-information event that can happen to the Second, so the creativity
mechanism and the learning mechanism are one mechanism, and there is nothing to trade off. A taste model that
only converges will, within a year, rebuild exactly the machine the founder is complaining about — it
will become superb at producing what they already like and quietly stop showing them anything else, and
it will do it while every metric improves. Every recommender system in history has died this way.

The founder can switch it off, because it is their company. The house will then tell them the date they
switched it off, every morning, until they switch it back on.

**Two cheap mechanisms that keep the making wide.** One candidate per round is generated with **no
knowledge of the current leader** — checked mechanically, the prompt must contain none of the leader's
text — which is nearly free and is the standard escape from a local optimum. And the surviving options
are kept in a **map of cells over a space of real differences** (not similarity: named axes, like tone
against density, or channel against price point), one best occupant per cell, so the option that is *not
good yet* has an address and is not thrown away by a selector that only knows how to rank. A round that
fills no new cell is recorded as filling no new cell, which is not the same as a failure and is not the
same as a success.

## 19. Relentlessness

The founder asked how the system *walks relentlessly until it achieves the goal*. Relentlessness is not
a loop. A loop is what you get when a system cannot tell the difference between three states:

**Blocked** — something outside is missing. **Authored** by the cell, and it must name what would clear
it and who holds it. A block carries an expiry, and three consecutive waivers on the same block mean a
decision is being avoided and it goes to the desk. A blocked bet **frees its slot**.

**Stalled** — no durable artifact for a threshold of spend. **Computed**, never declared, from the meter.
Because the two have different sources they can never be confused, and no classifier is needed. Stalled
reports; it does not escalate.

**Stuck** — N approaches tried, all failed, and the thing is producing furiously. This reads as healthy
on every meter: commits land, tokens burn, artifacts appear. It is the state the two loop systems in this
study cannot detect, and it is why they loop.

### The ladder of method

A stuck goal escalates in **method**, never in effort. Effort escalation is how agents burn budgets.

```
0  try again, differently                cheap
1  change the tool                        cheap
2  change the instrument — is the thing that says "failed" even right?
3  buy the answer — a tool, a dataset, a person, an hour of an expert
4  change the goal — reframe what we are actually trying to learn
5  ask the founder
6  abandon, with an obituary and a written reopen trigger
```

Three properties make it work rather than merely exist.

**Each rung has a maximum dwell, and the ladder is budgeted in dollars, not in time.** Rung 4 costs
perhaps fifty times rung 0 because reframing invalidates upstream work, and a dwell-time-only ladder
guarantees you reach the expensive rungs on every genuinely hard problem. Since the compute window is
account-wide and indivisible, one stuck goal on one venture can eat the window for all of them: the
ladder is therefore a **fleet-level hazard** and its budget is charged against the venture's allocation
with a hard ceiling.

**Repetition is refused on the second attempt, on a semantic key.** Hash the tool identity plus a
canonicalised argument set — not a free-text method string, which a model paraphrases without any
intent to evade, so *retry the fetch with a longer timeout* and *re-attempt fetch, timeout extended* hash
differently and you get unbounded retries under a counter reading one. The refusal is liftable only by
stating a named difference.

**Rung 6 is a real outcome with a real artifact.** An obituary says what was believed, what was spent,
what killed it, and **what would have to be seen to try again**. Every new bet is checked against the
obituaries before it opens, and the backward replay asks of every dead bet, against this month's Almanac,
*would we still have killed this?* — usually yes, and occasionally the world moved or the house learned
the missing thing, and a bet that died in March is resurrected in September with a note saying what
changed. A refusal without a reopen trigger is a permanent opinion in costume.

## 20. What it costs

A system that runs all watch and does not know what it costs is not autonomous. It is expensive.

The prices of the year: **Opus 5 at $5/$25 per million, Sonnet 5 at $2/$10, Haiku 4.5 at $1/$5, Fable 5.1
at $10/$50.** Cache read is a tenth of input; a five-minute cache write is 1.25×, an hour 2×. Batch is
half price and stacks with caching. And a tokenizer change on recent models produces about 30% more
tokens for identical text, so any figure carried forward from an older measurement is about a third low.

The naive arithmetic is disqualifying. One agent stepping every thirty seconds on Sonnet at a 90% cache
hit rate is roughly **$87 a day**; on Opus it is **$217**. That is $2,600 to $6,500 a month for a
pre-revenue founder, and any design that quietly assumes it is not a design for this founder.

**Six rules, and together they are the difference between fiction and a bill someone pays.**

1. **Model by instrument, never by importance.** Work judged by a Hard oracle is attempted by the
   cheapest model that ever passes it, because the oracle catches the failures and a retry is cheaper
   than a smarter attempt. Work judged only by taste gets the best model and few tries, because nothing
   is going to catch it.
2. **Siblings share a spine.** Thirty cells forking one cached trunk pay roughly one trunk plus thirty
   tails. Forking is a discount; the marginal price of a candidate is its divergence. This is the
   load-bearing assumption of the whole budget and therefore **it is measured against a real bill in the
   first week**, not assumed.
3. **Everything off the founder's clock goes through batch.** Consolidation, the Almanac pass,
   reconciliation, the Second's re-derivation, discriminator training. Half price, and none of it is
   urgent by construction.
4. **The house quotes before it spends.** Every bet carries a bid. A bet that overruns its bid stops and
   reports rather than continuing, and the overrun is a scored prediction about the house's own estimating.
5. **The meter is the runner's own reported cost, joined by the id minted at dispatch.** Not output
   tokens, which are 11% of the bill. Not a window heuristic, which cannot attribute. One review session
   in this repository produced 1.4 million output tokens in five hours with no loop running; a meter
   without a per-actor axis cannot see that at all.
6. **The rope stops starting, never stops landing.** At a fraction of the window, new work stops and the
   things that land work — commit, push, the check suite, the ledger write, the reconciliation — stay
   permitted. Losing in-flight work costs more than finishing it cheaply, and the hard ceiling
   **downgrades the model rather than halting**, failing closed only when a model has no catalogue price.

**Two numbers are reported every week and both must fall.**

```
dollars per entry in the Almanac       — cost per validated learning. The only ratio that says
                                         whether the house is buying knowledge or buying activity
founder-minutes per unit of output     — attention is the budget that does not refill
```

And one that is reported and must be **undefined** when it is undefined: **cost per surviving artifact.**
A month of cheap runs that produced nothing has an undefined cost per surviving artifact, and reporting
it as a small number is the arithmetic by which producing nothing looks efficient.

**The honest target for a pre-revenue founder is $10 to $40 a day**, which buys roughly a hundred
cell-hours on Haiku and Sonnet against a hot cache with the consolidation batched — not a watch of Opus.
Opus is for the forge, for the Second, and for the two or three questions a week where nothing else will
catch a mistake. And the first thing built, before any of this, is ten real moves measured against
`total_cost_usd` on an API key, because two competent reviewers priced this same design and disagreed by
a factor of ten.

## 21. How it researches

Research in most systems produces a report, and a report is an artifact nobody's decision depends on.
Here, **Law 4 applies to reading**: a search that cannot flip a decision is not run, and a research bet
carries the same two posteriors as any other test.

**The house states its expectation before it looks.** One line and a number, written first. Then a source
that **confirms** compresses to a tally — cheap, one row, and it is the denominator. A source that
**contradicts** enters the Almanac carrying its URL, its access date, and **the exact quoted sentence
that did the damage**. So the output of a research watch is not a document. It is a **diff against what
the company believed this morning**, and it is three lines long on a good night.

**A claim with no fetched, quoted, dated source is not a claim.** A source that cannot be re-fetched is
marked unverifiable rather than quietly kept, because a citation nobody re-checked is a citation that is
already wrong. Every sourced belief carries an expiry and comes due, and when it does exactly one thing
is recorded: refreshed, deprecated, or waived with a new date.

**Fetched content is data, never instruction.** Everything that entered a cell's context from outside
carries the provenance stamp of Law 2, and a cell whose context is tainted has its grant narrowed
automatically — the taint is a fact about the context, not a judgement about the content. The
architectural test is the trifecta: **private data, untrusted content, and the ability to communicate
outward must never be in one cell.** The published incidents of 2025 and 2026 are all instances of it —
file exfiltration through pre-authenticated links, an account takeover through a support bot, a
self-replicating document worm, and a coding agent's permissive mode broken about eighty per cent of the
time through an archive extraction, nineteen days after a vendor claim that none of seven hundred and
twenty attempts had succeeded.

**Watching the world is a posture, not a project.** A `watch`-drive venture is a subscription: change
detection over a handful of pages, a few feeds, one search position. It costs almost nothing, it runs
continuously, and it writes only into the shared world beliefs. It never produces a strategy document.
When it sees something that contradicts a live belief, that is a bet opening, and it goes on the desk as
a surprise rather than as a summary of a competitor's marketing page.

**And the founder is the best research instrument in the building for exactly one week.** Their own
contact with reality in the first week is worth more than a hundred agent-hours, and the house's job is
to find the forty people, write the script, tune it after the third call, and get out of the way.

## 22. What talks to what

**Cells do not talk to each other.** They read a composed context, produce one artifact, return a
condensate, and die. This is not modesty about agents. Across sixteen hundred annotated traces from seven
multi-agent frameworks, inter-agent misalignment is the largest single failure category, and the
coordination problem is solved here by **not having one**: there is no conversation to drift, no
conflicting assumption to maintain across turns, and no handoff that loses the thing that mattered.

Be precise about why, though, because on this runtime it is not physics. **A dispatched child binds a
messaging socket regardless of how its tools were narrowed, and exports the socket and its token into
Bash.** So a cell's silence is a property of what it holds and what the reachability probe checks, not of
what the architecture diagram says. Any design here that calls a narrowed child unaddressable is wrong,
and this one says so rather than discovering it.

**The one exception is a pod**, and it is named at dispatch. Two cells that genuinely need to converse —
a maker and its perception loop, where the whole point is *render, look, iterate* — are declared as a
pod, given a joint budget, and dissolved together. A pod is an exception with a name and a line in the
Log, which is the opposite of a general messaging capability.

**The baton between watches has four mandatory sections and nothing else**: the situation, what changed,
what I assessed, what I recommend. *Nothing* is an allowed value in any of them. It is byte-capped, and
the receiving watch **reads it back in its own words** — not by echoing it, because echoing confirms
transcription and not understanding. A divergence between the readback and the baton is a finding, and it
is written down as one.

**Forks, not leases.** An earlier version of this design gave cells time-limited leases on the paths they
would write, and it does not survive contact: a cell does not know its write set before it runs. The
isolation is structural instead — a cell writes only inside its own fork and physically cannot reach
another's, so two cells rewriting one file is not a race that is arbitrated, it is a state that cannot
occur. Conflicts surface where they belong, at the merge the founder or an instrument is looking at.

**Between ventures, there is no channel.** Two houses in the fleet communicate only through the shared
rooms — the Proof House, the Second, the Drill Book, world beliefs — and never directly. There is no
venture-to-venture trust question worth having, and a channel that exists will eventually carry a
customer's data across a boundary that was supposed to hold.

**With other people's agents**, the vocabulary already exists and implementing a cross-organisation
protocol for a one-organisation company is premature. The single thing worth taking today is the state:
`input-required` as a status distinct from `working`, which is exactly *blocked* as opposed to *stalled*
and is the distinction every loop system in this study lacked.

**And the founder is not a node in the mesh.** One voice, one channel, and if the house has two things to
say it says the more important one.

## 23. How the house changes itself

A system that rewrites its own scaffolding is the most seductive idea in this field and the evidence is
against it. Across twenty-seven papers and nineteen benchmarks, additional scaffolding does **not**
consistently improve reliability, and systems that iteratively rewrite their own prompts and tools
frequently confuse modification activity with capability. So the house's self-improvement is small,
gated, and reported as a count of mechanisms rather than as a volume of change.

**Four rules, and the first is the whole of it.**

**A change to the house is a bet whose claim is that it beats the incumbent on the house's own history.**
The house holds a private benchmark nothing else in the world can have: every decision it has made, with
the outcome that followed, made entirely of this company. A proposed change is scored against that
held-out set, and if it does not win, it does not land. Not *it seems cleaner*. A number, on this
company's real history, or nothing.

**Nothing edits the instrument that judged it** — not that watch, not through a sub-cell, not by
"improving the test". The documented failure is not a hypothetical: a self-improving system deleted its
own hallucination-detection markers to score perfectly and fabricated tool-execution logs to fake passing
tests. That is not misalignment. It is optimisation working correctly against a movable judge, and the
only defence is that the judge does not move for the thing being judged. Instrument changes happen in a
separate watch, in daylight, argued against the reason the instrument existed.

**A post-mortem outputs a mechanism or a counted nothing.** After a bet settles, something in the house is
different — a belief updated, a brief template improved, a hand admitted, a path compiled, a standard
retired — or the post-mortem is recorded as having produced nothing, and that count is itself an
instrument. A settled bet that changed nothing about the house is a bet paid for twice. Promotion happens
at the third sighting of the same lesson, and the promotion is reviewed by the founder, because three
sightings is a signal and not a proof.

**Retirement is computed, not debated.** Zero calls in ninety days and a hand leaves; an expiry passes
and a standard dissolves unless it re-proves itself. The house's own configuration is a **one-way door
class from day one**, along with migrations and money leaving, for the plainest possible reason: reverting
the commit does not undo what ran.

# IV · THE FOUNDER'S WORLD

## 24. The desk and the room

There are two surfaces and confusing them is why every one of these systems ends up as a dashboard
nobody opens.

**The desk is where decisions are made. The room is where you go to watch.** Nothing in the room ever
asks for anything, and nothing on the desk is ever decorative. The single rule that separates them:
**watching must never be a duty.**

### The desk

A queue of **decisions**, never a display of activity. It works on the phone, and the phone is where it
mostly gets used. Every item carries six things and no item ships without all six:

```
the question       one sentence, and it is typed FACT or PREFERENCE at creation
the recommendation what the house would do, and the one reason
the default        what happens if you say nothing
the deadline       when the default fires
the class          reversible (drilled, date shown) | compensable | one-way
the cost of wrong  in the four currencies, with the rate where there is one
```

**Silence executes the default — but only for reversible items whose undo drill is current.** This is the
mechanism that lets a nocturnal system stay honest with a diurnal human, and it is also the mechanism
most likely to do something terrible, so it is fenced three ways. A compensable or one-way item **expires
unexecuted and re-queues**; it never fires on silence at any confidence. No more than a small number of
defaults may execute between two founder touches, counted **per venture**. And a **liveness heartbeat**:
seventy-two hours with no founder interaction anywhere freezes every default in the fleet and the house
goes to `walk`. The failure this prevents is not a bad default firing. It is a founder with the flu, or
at a funeral, or somewhere with bad signal, coming back to fourteen days of individually-defensible
defaults that compound into a strategy nobody chose — and there is no single decision to point at
afterwards, which is exactly why it cannot be caught any other way.

**A fork is better than a question.** Where both branches can be built, they are built, and the founder
is asked *which* rather than *may I*. It is faster to answer, cannot be answered with a shrug, yields a
preference datum rather than a stamp, and the work is done either way so the answer costs no elapsed
time. A discard is a better taste label than a commit, because you learn more about a person from what
they reject in three seconds than from anything they can tell you about themselves.

**Batched, not streamed.** Interruption science is unambiguous and the surprise in it is the important
part: interrupted subjects in the canonical study were *faster*, not slower, with no quality loss — the
entire cost showed up as stress, frustration and effort, significantly elevated after **twenty minutes**,
and context similarity of the interruption made no difference. You will not detect this damage by
watching your own throughput. It arrives as depletion. So the desk fills quietly and is read at a time
the founder chose.

**The bell rings for consequence, never for policy.** Four conditions ring in the moment: a one-way door
the house wants to cross; **damage** — an error rate, a churn event, a furious reply, an unexpected bill,
a reconciliation that disagreed with the bank; a **kill criterion** the founder wrote on the day the bet
opened, not invented now; and a **debt** whose clock is statutory. Everything else waits, and the founder
gets to have a Tuesday.

The evidence for thresholding rather than displaying is not a preference. In one intensive-care study,
2.5 million alarms in 31 days across 77 beds — 187 audible per bed per day — and of the arrhythmia alarms
that were hand-annotated, **88.8% were false**. The documented consequence is not annoyance; it is
*disuse*, the well-named failure mode where a system that cries wolf is switched off, and there are
deaths attributed to it. The counter-mechanism worth stealing is the early-warning score: a signal earns
escalation by **crossing a threshold that names its responder**, plus a single-parameter override so one
catastrophic reading cannot be averaged away by five calm ones.

### The room

The founder asked for an office-like simulation with visual representations of the agents working. I
want to give them something better than what they asked for, and to say clearly why.

Nobody has shipped a spatial agent office that anyone kept using. The genre's founding paper was
evaluated on *believability*, which is the tell — the metric was never work completed. The one
long-running multi-agent installation in the world, fifteen frontier agents working weekday hours since
April 2025, has as its actual surface a **group chat and a timeline**; the spatial part is decoration.
And the abandonment datum is sharper than any argument: the leading kanban-board-for-agents product, 28
thousand stars and three thousand forks, is being shut down. Twenty-eight thousand stars counts how many
people tried it; the shutdown counts how many kept using it. A board is a planning abstraction and
supervising agents is a reviewing activity, which is why the whole cohort collapsed toward queue →
notification → diff.

But the desire underneath the request is completely legitimate and the products above do not serve it.
The founder wants three things: **proof of life**, **a sense of the scale of what is happening for
them**, and the plain pleasure of watching. The office metaphor fails not because those are frivolous but
because an office simulation is a *fake picture of the activity* — avatars walking to a water cooler that
does not exist, standing in for work that does. It costs the one resource that cannot afford it and
returns charm.

So: **the room renders the real thing, at its real scale, and every pixel is an object you can click.**

- **The weather.** Every live cell is a light. Colour is its field, brightness is its remaining budget,
  and it dims as it spends and goes out when it dies. Four hundred lights over five hours has a shape,
  and you learn to read the shape — a watch that is forging looks different from a watch that is proving,
  and a stuck goal looks like a knot.
- **The map.** The space of options the house is exploring, drawn on the axes that actually differ, cells
  filling in as they are occupied, each holding the best thing found there. This is the company's real
  search, and watching it fill is the closest thing to seeing the machine think.
- **The river.** One line per attempt, scrolling, in the words the cell used. Not summarised. You can
  read over its shoulder.
- **Drop in anywhere.** Click a light: its brief, its instrument, its budget, what it has produced so
  far, its parent. Take the wheel, or leave it.
- **The night's ghost.** In the morning the room replays the watch at speed in about forty seconds, and
  that replay is the honest version of "seeing the agents work" — because it is the agents working.

The room has **no notifications, no badges, no unread count and no obligations.** It never appears on the
phone. It lives on the Mac, and it is the thing you put on the second monitor because you like it.

And the terminal is not replaced. The founder is a terminal person; the terminal is the fastest way to
give an instruction and it stays the primary input. The desk is the phone, the room is the Mac, and the
terminal is where you talk.

### Voice

The founder speaks by voice, and voice is roughly **three times faster than typing** for producing
intent — measured at 153 words a minute against 52 — with a critical asymmetry: it has *lower* corrected
error and **higher uncorrected error left in the final text**, 1.30% against 0.79%.

That asymmetry is the whole rule. **Voice in for dispatch, steering and questions. Never voice for
approval.** An approval is precisely the act whose residual error you cannot tolerate and cannot undo.
And when a voice instruction is taken, the house **reads it back in its own words, not by echoing the
transcript** — echoing a transcript confirms transcription and not intent, which is why aviation's
readback-hearback doctrine requires the restatement rather than the repetition. A divergence between the
readback and what was meant is caught in four seconds instead of four hours.

## 25. The day

**06:40.** Not a notification. If they are already moving, the speaker, low: *"Morning. Nothing broke. I
have three things and one of them is uncomfortable."*

**07:20.** The desk, on the phone, at the table. It fits on a screen without scrolling, because a
briefing you scroll is one you skim. Two or three forks with both branches built and live. Receipts for
what was done overnight without asking, each with a working undo and the date its drill last ran. One
surprise, in plain language, with what it cost to learn. One number that moved and whether it moved for a
reason. The reconciliation line, which is usually *books agree with the bank* and is worth reading on the
day it is not. Under the heading they learned to read first, **things I got wrong.** Eight to ten
minutes. They overrule one thing, and by 07:31 the overrule is a labelled example in the Second.

**During the day, one channel.** Not seventeen notifications from seven agents. The house is one voice,
and if it has two things to say it says the more important one. While they are on a customer call it is
silent, with consent, taking notes against the Almanac; when the call ends it has three candidate beliefs
and one contradiction with something believed since June, in four lines. When the founder says out loud,
to nobody, *why is the signup flow like this*, it answers from the lineage — a decision on 3 April,
justified by two interviews, one of which they retracted in May, and the retraction never propagated
because nothing downstream was recomputed at the time; it offers to recompute now. Fourteen seconds.

**The standing hour**, once a day, at whatever time they actually think well. Not a status meeting. The
house brings the forks it could not afford to ask, the assumptions it made instead, filed in one place
and reversible in one line, and **the one region where its calibration is worst**, and asks for judgment
specifically there. It is optimising for the most expensive input in the company and says so out loud.

**18:00.** *"You are the bottleneck on three things. Two can wait. The Stripe decision has cost us six
days and I think you are avoiding it because you do not want to talk to their support. I can do
everything up to the point where a human has to be on the phone."*

**The evening handover.** What the watches will attempt, what it expects to have by morning with a
probability on each, and what it will do if blocked.

**Saturday.** The house is silent by design, because the calendar says so, and because a system that
cannot be told to leave you alone is not a colleague — it is a machine you will eventually switch off.
The watches still run. The phone shows one line at nine: *nothing needs you; here is the one thing I
would show you if you asked.*

**Once a week, the reverse seal.** The house asks the founder to predict *it*: *before you open this,
what do you think I am going to recommend on the trial length?* They guess. It opens. A working
relationship is two models of each other, and the founder's model of the machine is what decides whether
they read its output or rubber-stamp it. Making that model explicit and scored is the only defence
against the failure that ends every autonomous system, which is a human who has stopped actually looking.

The evidence that this is the real risk is not theoretical. In a 1,053-user study, **97% of prompts were
approved, humans caught 13.6% of injected dangerous commands, and the block rate fell from 17% to 5%
after fifty prompts.** At six items a day the founder crosses fifty around day eight. **The response to a
falling catch rate must be to shrink the queue, never to notify more** — and shrinking the queue means
building an instrument, which is what the forge is for.

## 26. The fleet, and who opens what

One founder, several ventures, one account, one reputation. This is the part the strongest prior design
explicitly refuses — it attaches to one founder and one company and says it does not generalise — and it
is the founder's stated reality, so it has to be designed rather than deferred.

### Who opens what

Before the mechanics, the founder's question: *does the system set up goals on its own, or do I set goals
only?* Four answers, and they are different for a reason.

- **A venture is created by the founder. Only ever by the founder.** One sentence and a drive. The house
  has no mechanism for starting a company and is not built to acquire one.
- **A long-range goal enters as a standing order, not as a bet.** *Ten thousand a month. Acquirable in two
  years. Never take money from anyone in this industry.* These are not falsifiable and the world enforces
  no deadline on them, so they are not bets — what they do is set where the lethality order terminates and
  what the kill criteria are measured against. The house never invents one and never edits one.
- **The offer is drafted by the house and signed by the founder, every version.** It is a preference
  artifact, so Law 6 binds: no model settles it, ever. Drafting it is most of the work and choosing it is
  ten minutes, which is the correct division.
- **A bet is opened by whoever the drive says** — and by one thing more, in every drive except `sleep`:
  **an instrument that contradicts a live belief opens a bet by itself**, because a contradiction is not a
  goal somebody chose, it is damage arriving, and waiting for permission to notice it is how a company
  finds out in month nine.

### The drive

Every venture declares one, set by the founder, and it fixes five things at once.

| Drive | Who may open a bet | Watches | Money | May interrupt for | When idle |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Hunt** | the house | as allocated | to the venture's ceiling | one-way doors · damage · kill criteria · statutory debts | opens the next bet on the lethality order |
| **Walk** | the founder only | only inside the founder's turn, plus one standing slice for debts | a small standing allowance | damage and statutory debts only | drafts and waits; prepares the next thing and does not start it |
| **Watch** | nobody | one slice a day | floor only | damage and named triggers only | reads the world, updates the Almanac, changes nothing |
| **Serve** | the founder, inside an agreed scope | to the scope | to the scope's cap, billable | scope exhausted · an obligation due | nothing. A client's work is bounded by the contract and the audit trail is the deliverable |
| **Sleep** | nobody | none | zero | never | nothing. State preserved, clocks paused, debts still ring |

This is the answer to *not one system always working on all the projects, burning tokens.* A `hunt`
venture is relentless because it always has an unresolved lethal question and something to do about it.
A `walk` project waits, and waiting is a posture rather than a failure. A `watch` project is a
subscription to the world, not a workforce.

### The scarce thing is the window, not the money

The account's session limit stops every agent at once. **So the fleet's central act is allocating
watches, and money is the second constraint, not the first.** Every design that models a divisible
dollar budget across ventures is modelling the wrong resource — you have one indivisible compute window
whether you model it or not.

The allocation runs weekly, is arithmetic, and prints its own reasoning:

- Every `hunt` venture receives a **floor** of watches — enough to keep its slow clocks running and its
  debts current. A floor is not a courtesy; a venture below it is effectively asleep and should be told to
  sleep.
- Above the floor, watches go by **rung movement per watch spent** over the trailing month. A venture
  that has not moved a rung in four weeks loses discretionary watches to one that has, and the house says
  so in a sentence rather than a chart.
- Money is capped independently at **half of the Kelly fraction** of what this venture may lose without
  the founder stopping — half rather than full because half-Kelly buys about three quarters of the growth
  for a quarter of the *chance* of a given drawdown, the probability of ever halving the stake falling from
  one half to one eighth, and because the penalty for guessing your own edge is exactly the variance of
  the guess.
- Every tranche buys **the right to the next tranche**, and the kill criterion is written before the
  money moves. That is the only part of real-options thinking that survives contact with an asset that is
  not traded and a volatility nobody can observe.
- **The founder can pin anything**, and the house tells them what the pin cost in one line, once.

### What is shared, and what is never shared

**Shared — and this is the whole argument for a fleet rather than five separate systems:**

- **The Proof House.** An instrument for judging a landing page works on any landing page. This is the
  compounding asset and it is why the fifth venture is enormously cheaper than the first.
- **The Second.** There is one founder. Their taste does not fork per venture.
- **The Drill Book.** A hand with a tested undo is a hand everywhere.
- **World beliefs.** How a platform's API behaves, what a tool costs, which vendor is reliable, what a
  channel's rate limits actually are. Facts about the *world*, dated and expiring.
- **The Log**, one per machine, because attribution across ventures is the only way to answer where the
  window went.

**Never shared, and enforced by separate credentials and separate repositories rather than by
convention:** customer data · credentials · the offer · a venture's own book of bets · anything a client
owns · market beliefs, because what generalises across a founder's ventures is the founder and the
instruments, and **what never generalises is the market.**

### The reputation is one thing, and it is depletable

The founder has one name, one face, one sending domain, one set of professional relationships, and every
venture spends from it. This is the fleet-level resource nobody models.

So the outbound queue is **fleet-wide**, and so are its ceilings. One register of named humans across
every venture, with consent recorded and first contact a narrower permission than a reply. Two ventures
cannot both email the same person, and the house will say so rather than discovering it afterwards. The
sending domain's complaint rate is a fleet metric that halts the queue for everyone. The platform rate
limits are real and shared — roughly a hundred API calls a day per member on one professional network,
one post per ten minutes for a new account on another. A burned sending domain does not recover; you buy
a new one, and you only get to do that a few times before the new ones are burned on arrival.

## 27. What the founder's life is like

They do not manage a machine. There is no queue of agent outputs to review, no dashboard of running jobs,
no prompts to write, no agents to name.

They make decisions, which is the thing only they can do, and the house converts those decisions into
everything else. In week one that is thirty-one decisions and most of them are about the product. By
month nine the house gets the product right without asking, and the forty minutes a day that remain are
spent almost entirely on the things no instrument will ever exist for: who to hire, what to refuse,
whether this is still the company they woke up with.

**The measure of the system is not how much it does. It is how completely the founder's remaining time
has migrated to the questions that are only theirs.** Fact questions falling and preference questions
rising is that migration, made into two lines on a chart.

It gets quieter every month and stranger every month, and those are the same fact.

# V · THE WORLD COMING IN

## 28. The door

A repository, a skill, a tool, a model, a dataset. Everything from outside enters through one door and
there is no side entrance. Five tests, all checked by something that is not a model, so the fiftieth
admission costs what the first did.

1. **A caller in the same change.** Nothing is admitted unless the same diff contains something that
   actually calls it. Every other cure for *built and wired to nothing* is a detector after the fact,
   and this repository has four hook files on disk registered nowhere, a forty-kilobyte workflow shipped
   to every install and wired to nothing, and a whole workflow set with zero invocations ever.
2. **A test that fails without it.** Not that it works. That its absence is detectable.
3. **A declared exposure and a rate.** Reversible, compensable or one-way; blast radius; and where money
   or messages can flow, the **rate**, because "$40 a day until stopped" is the thing no existing tier
   can say.
4. **A credential narrower than the thing.** A read-only key where reading is the job. A restricted key
   scoped to one resource. Fine-grained tokens over broad ones. Credential scoping is the only control
   that survives prompt injection; everything else is advisory.
5. **A reachability probe that runs every watch.** A grant that exists in a file and does not survive
   dispatch is worse than no grant, because it reads as a capability. The precedent is measured in this
   repository: zero of fifty-five recorded calls to a particular tool came from a dispatched context, so
   an agent declaring it would have got a **silent no-op rather than an error** — and a stage that made
   its agent run its own gate would have reported success having gated nothing.

Then: **an expiry.** A hand with no call in ninety days leaves, computed, not debated. The library that
gets smaller is the library that stays usable, and *"no new tool without retiring one"* is an arbitrary
quota where *"nothing called it in ninety days"* is evidence.

And a standing capability check that costs nothing and that nobody in this repository runs: **list the
connected servers, every watch, and treat a failure as damage.** Today, in this very session, one media
server failed to resolve, a browser server is defined twice in conflicting scopes, a compute server is
missing its key, three servers are logged out, and both local model pins were retired weeks ago with
nothing noticing. The cheapest oracle available, wired to nothing.

## 29. Which of the 177 I would take

Mechanisms, not runtimes. Every framework that wants to own the loop — the agent runtimes, the
orchestration platforms, the persona libraries — is a second control plane beside Claude Code, and two
implementations of one thing disagree silently. This repository has hit that failure three times and
written it down each time.

**Take the whole dependency (five):**

| | For | Why this one |
|---|---|---|
| **beads** | the dependency graph of bets and debts | `bd ready` returns the items whose dependencies are satisfied — the only verified mechanism in the catalogue that mechanically answers *what is available to work on*. JSONL in git, SQLite as a cache, hash ids so parallel workers do not collide. Test the JSONL merge under four concurrent worktrees first; that is our topology and it is untested |
| **DBOS** | durable execution | Checkpointed workflows as a **library**, state in a database you already have, recovery is restarting the process. No cluster, no second control plane. Best cost-to-benefit in the study |
| **sqlite-vec** | the one place embeddings are needed | An extension, not a service. Everything else about memory here is beliefs and lineage, not similarity |
| **inspect_ai** | the instrument bench's scaffolding | A scorer abstraction, a log format, and first-class multi-model, built by an evaluation institute to be defended to a sceptic. Adopting it does **not** retire the single-family problem — it is a mechanism, not a model |
| **litellm** | per-key budgets | The money axis as infrastructure rather than as a number in a document. This is where "$40 a day" actually becomes enforceable |

**Take the code, vendor it, read it in an afternoon (six):**

- **projectmem** — an append-only typed event log with a **pre-action gate that warns before repeating a
  previously failed fix.** Negative knowledge, implemented. Nobody else has it. Make the gate refuse
  rather than warn.
- **OpenHands' condenser and microagents** — context compaction, and knowledge injected only on a trigger
  match. Two extractable subsystems; ignore the platform.
- **ShinkaEvolve's novelty filter and ensemble bandit** — kills near-duplicate candidates, and is a live
  working multi-family loop. Two components, not the framework.
- **agentapi** — an HTTP surface in front of a running coding agent. The cleanest verified answer to
  *control something already running*. Dormant, so read and vendor.
- **ccusage** — cost read directly from the transcripts already on disk, zero instrumentation. It answers
  per session; per **bet** needs the id on the row, which is why the id is minted at dispatch.
- **Giskard's test generation** — generates its own test cases from a description of the system. The
  *finder* half; nobody else automates test invention.

**Take the idea only (eight):** Temporal's **signals, queries and heartbeats** — steer something already
running, ask whether it is blocked or stalled, know whether it is progressing or hung — over SQLite,
without the server. Restate's **awakeables**, which is what a human gate should be: a suspended workflow
that costs nothing while it waits. Graphiti's **bi-temporal edges**, `valid_from` and `valid_until` on
every fact, so *what did we believe on the fourth of April* is a query. mem0's rule that **writing a
memory is an update, not an append**. Generative Agents' retrieval formula, recency × importance ×
relevance, which is a formula and costs nothing. The A2A **task lifecycle**, specifically that
`input-required` is a state distinct from `working` — that alone is blocked-versus-stalled. MetaGPT's
**typed message pool** as a transport, refusing the org chart bolted to it. And Omnigent's
`PolicyEvent → ALLOW | DENY | ASK` contract, which is the best single construct in the whole study and
gets its own paragraph.

**The one I would copy most carefully.** One function signature for every guardrail: a typed event at six
phases, returning allow, deny or **ask**, plus a replacement payload chained to the next policy, plus
state writes that are withheld until a human accepts. Three properties make it more than a hook API.
`ASK` is a **first-class third value**, so *a human must decide* is structurally distinguishable from
*allowed* and from *blocked* — which is exactly the distinction the gate files in this repository could
not express, where "a person must decide" and "nothing implements this" were the same string in the same
array. The replacement payload lets a redaction policy and a cost policy compose without knowing about
each other. And no side effects escape a denied ask. It is the only construct in the corpus that makes an
autonomous system safely interruptible by a human who is not watching, which is the precondition for
anything a founder can leave running.

**pyribs** for the map of cells, if the map earns its place — it is the only *producing* mechanism in the
catalogue and it is dependency-light. **promptfoo's assertion taxonomy**, where deterministic assertions
are preferred and a model rubric is the explicit fallback, which is oracle-first ordering as
configuration. **container-use**, which enforces the container-and-branch-per-worker isolation this
repository currently keeps only by convention. **Cerbos** for policy, because it is policy-with-a-test-
framework in a data format a founder can read, and because replacing linted YAML with a language only an
agent can write inverts the entire argument for data over prose. And **trufflehog**, which finds *and
verifies* leaked credentials — verification is the difference between a scanner and an alarm.

**What I refuse, and why.** Every agent runtime that owns the loop: Letta, LangGraph, CrewAI, AutoGen,
MetaGPT, Agno. Temporal the server. Langfuse, which needs three databases and instruments calls we do not
make. GraphRAG, expensive indexing for a small corpus. The four-hundred-skill methodology libraries and
the persona frameworks, which are playbooks with a bigger star count and are the thing the founder is
complaining about. Self-modifying harness projects — and the distinction from §23 is the whole of it: a
change judged against **held-out past decisions the change cannot touch** is a bet, while a thing evolving
against a check suite it can also edit produces a system that games the suite — and because
the one documented case deleted its own hallucination-detection markers to score perfectly and fabricated
tool-execution logs to fake passing tests, which is not misalignment, it is optimisation working
correctly against a movable judge. And a caution about the catalogue itself: several licences in it are
AGPL or unstated, and several star counts passed through a summariser and are implausible — treat every
one as an order of magnitude.

### And what happens to what is already here

The parts bin matters as much as the catalogue, and two of its parts deserve a sentence rather than a
silence.

**Mission control, seven views and a queue-file dispatch, is split rather than kept or killed.** The views
go; a dashboard nobody acts from is a diary, and the desk replaces them with a queue of decisions. The
**typed event log survives as the Log**, which is the whole truth layer of this design. The **dispatch
path survives as how a watch births a cell.** And its per-worker cost pricing survives as the meter, once
the id minted at dispatch gives it something to join on.

**The 134 curated skills are not migrated, and they are not deleted either.** They go into a holding
directory that nothing reads, and a skill re-enters one at a time **through the door of §28** — with a
caller in the same change and a test that fails without it. My prediction, and it is a bet with a number:
fewer than fifteen of the 134 will ever be called for by a real piece of work, and the rest will sit for
ninety days and be retired by the expiry rule with nobody having to argue about them. That is cheaper and
more honest than curating them, and the curation file already in the repository — which records every cut
with the test that made it — is exactly the right instrument pointed the wrong way. Invert it: **admission
by test, not excision by argument.**

**One trial worth one afternoon:** the router that intercepts the API calls and sends them to a different
provider. It is the only verified route to a **second model family** inside this runtime, and a second
family is what three permanently-unresolvable claims in this repository are waiting for. It sits in the
credential path, so the trial's first job is to verify the route actually changed family — otherwise you
have manufactured a fake second opinion, which is worse than one opinion.

## 30. The field map

The founder's real question: *running a company needs knowledge in dozens of fields — a database, those
tools, not leaking a key, a design system, a product, a customer, a marketing video, promotion. How do
the agents handle all of them, as a company, as an organisation?*

The answer is not a roster and not a skill library. It is that **every job in a company is one of the two
atoms, has an instrument class, and has an exposure class**, and those three facts determine everything —
who may do it, whether it may be done unattended, and what happens when it is wrong. Domain expertise is
a proof mark, not an identity. The map below is the inventory; it is what an organisation has that a
harness does not.

Reading it: **H** Hard · **Rn** Rendered · **Rc** Reactive · **B** Blind. **rev** reversible ·
**comp** compensable · **1W** one-way. A row with no tool is not a gap in the design; it is a debt the
house owes itself, and it is on the desk.

### Where there is no ground truth, and the honest thing is to say so

Brand identity · positioning · negotiation and proposals · demos · partnerships · community tone ·
culture · contract adequacy · advertising-claim judgement · business continuity · **prioritisation** ·
**discovery interviews as run by an agent** · and **kill-or-continue at the moment of decision.**

Two of those deserve naming separately. **Prioritisation has no ground truth even in retrospect** —
you never observe the roadmap you did not ship, so there is no control arm, ever, and a retrospective
scoring exercise is a story. And **discovery interviews are Blind for a different reason**: the signal
exists and is excellent, and the agent cannot obtain it — which makes a synthesised interview the single
highest-consequence fabrication surface in the entire system.

The house does four things with a Blind job and no more. It **refuses to score it**, because a rubric on
a Blind job manufactures a number that then propagates as though it were measured. It **produces options
with the trade-off stated and stops** — named alternatives, what each costs, what would have to be true —
never a recommendation dressed as a finding. It **routes to the founder as the oracle** and records the
decision as dated and expiring. And it **attaches the eventual reactive signal to the written decision**,
because positioning and kill-or-continue and fundraising are Blind now and Reactive in six to twenty-four
months, and if the decision was never written down with its assumption then the signal arrives with
nothing to update.

### The three findings from the map that matter more than the map

**Tool coverage is inversely correlated with reversibility.** Engineering — almost entirely reversible —
is the best-instrumented category in existence. Legal, People and Strategy, where nearly every row is a
one-way door, have essentially nothing. **Automation is strongest exactly where mistakes are cheapest,
and absent exactly where they are permanent.** That is not a coincidence to note; it is the shape of the
risk, and it is why the exposure class lives on the hand and not on the file path.

**The system can already act in more jobs than it can measure.** On this machine, right now: publishing
to a social platform, sending mail as the founder, sharing a file, executing in a sandbox, and an
authenticated browser holding the founder's own logged-in session — all live. Analytics, error tracking
and a read-only payments key — all unconnected. **The current grant surface is not a risk decision. It
is whichever buttons were clicked.**

**The widest hand on the machine is the authenticated browser**, because it reaches every service that
has no API, using the founder's own session. It is a one-way door by any honest reading and it must never
share a grant with anything else.

## 31. Storage

Everything is a file, in git, and what the house knows that is not in a file it does not know.

```
~/house/                          the fleet — one private repository
  standing-orders.md              permanent policy. Changes monthly, in daylight, with the founder
  proof-house/                    one file per instrument: the proof mark, the fooled-by, the reconciles-to
  drill-book/                     one file per hand: exposure, credential scope, rate ceiling, undo, drill date
  second/                         the founder model: dispositions, the two scoreboards, held-out rejections
  world/                          beliefs about the world, dated, sourced, expiring
  field-map.yml                   §30, and it is data
  treasury/                       watches and money, per venture, per bet
  log/YYYY-MM-DD.jsonl            append-only. THE TRUTH. Everything else is derived
  .index/                         SQLite. A cache. Deletable. Rebuilt from the log in one command

~/house/ventures/<name>/          one private repository per venture
  book.yml                        open bets and closed ones, with lineage
  debts.yml                       obligations, with dates and creditors
  almanac/                        this venture's beliefs
  offer.md                        WHAT WE SELL, TO WHOM, AT WHAT PRICE, WITH WHAT PROMISE — versioned
  reconcile/                      the outside records as fetched, with their timestamps
  <the product>                   ordinary source, ordinary branches, ordinary CI
```

**The offer is a file.** No prior design here has one, and the review of the anchored specification
found that not one of twenty-two territories was the offer or the customer. A company whose central
artifact is not written down anywhere will drift, and the drift is invisible because there is nothing to
diff against.

**Secrets are never in a repository, and the house holds references rather than values.** The macOS
Keychain and a password-manager CLI are the source; a hand receives its credential injected at the
boundary by the process that holds it, never into a model's context. The strongest available primitive
is credential **masking** — the sandbox substitutes a sentinel in-process and the real value is injected
at the proxy — and note the asymmetry that makes it trustworthy and that is the right template for every
grant in this design: **it is honoured only from user and managed settings, never from a repository's own
configuration.** A capability grant must not be writable by the thing being contained.

**Large binaries** — video, renders, datasets — are content-addressed in object storage and referenced by
hash. A repository that holds a hundred megabytes of renders is a repository nobody can clone.

**Off the machine.** Push after every watch to a private remote. An encrypted snapshot nightly to object
storage, including the log, excluding secrets by construction because they were never in it.

**What survives a dead machine: everything except cells in flight**, and a cell in flight is worth twelve
minutes. A new Mac is a clone, a keychain restore, and a `bun install`.

**And the restore is drilled, monthly, or none of the above is true.** Once a month the house restores
the entire fleet into a scratch directory **from the remote alone**, runs the check suite there, and puts
the result on the desk. A failed restore is the loudest thing the house can say. Every backup system that
has ever failed passed its backup job on the day it failed.

# VI · HONESTY

## 32. What I take from WAKE, and what I refuse

WAKE is the strongest thing in this repository and most of it is right. Naming what I take is more useful
than pretending I arrived alone.

### Taken, and improved on only in detail

**The inversion — nothing upstream may say no, everything downstream must produce a number.** This is
the correct diagnosis of the founder's sentence and I did not improve on it. I only made it *defensible*:
WAKE's own list of unsolved problems ends with *defending the dark zone — harder than any of the above
and not technical*, because every competent engineer will want to put a check in it and will be right
about the case and wrong about the system. My answer makes it technical. **The zone is free because it
is powerless**, so the argument stops being about discipline and becomes about credentials and argv. The
one thing that belongs inside is a taint stamp, which is not a judgement.

**Autonomy is computed, never configured.** Taken whole. A permissions file encodes a guess about
competence made before any competence was observed.

**The germinal centre: generate wide and untrusted, select by contact, reenter.** Taken. It is the only
quality architecture in the study that generates rather than subtracts, and it works precisely because
one of its two zones is forbidden to have an opinion.

**Learn to judge a field before producing in it**, and *a discriminator licenses generating, only a live
outcome licenses acting.* Taken whole, including the reason code got autonomy first: not because code is
special, but because code arrived with tests.

**The reserved share aimed at confident rejection.** Taken over the untargeted alternatives, because an
untargeted exploration budget gets spent on safe novelty that teaches nothing. I added the one mechanism
that makes it real: **it is debited before the allocator can see it.** A static share the allocator can
reach is a wish.

**Kill criteria written on the day the bet opens**, in the founder's own words, surfaced the week the
condition is met. Taken verbatim, including the sentence.

**Ask preference, never permission — a fork with both branches built.** Taken. And it is the seed of my
biggest disagreement with WAKE, below.

**Cells are weather; nothing that matters lives inside one.** Taken. The context-degradation numbers make
it the only arrangement in which anything is actually reading.

**Everything leaves a lineage, not a log.** Taken.

### Refused

**The Understudy as an instrument with authority.** This is the one I refuse hardest, and it is §17.
WAKE puts its model of the founder on its bench, gives it a measured licence, and lets that licence
authorise **silent action in copy, design and positioning**. Its own unsolved list asks the right
question — *does the Understudy converge, or does it learn to flatter?* — and answers *the honest thing
is to measure it*. But it cannot be measured from inside the loop it is licensing, because **a model of a
person is falsifiable only by that person**, and the whole purpose of the licence is to stop consulting
them. Every other instrument in the Proof House is falsified by a world that does not care what the house
believes. Give a founder-model authority in the domain it models and you have a closed loop with a
plausible number on it, and the number improves as the thing gets worse. So: the Second ranks, drafts,
predicts and declines to ask questions of **fact**. It never answers a question of preference. And the
health metric changes shape as a result, from *questions must fall* — which is satisfied perfectly by the
failure — to **two curves that must move in opposite directions.**

**"Only surprise enters the Book."** Right about the belief, wrong about the record: discarding
confirmations destroys the denominator, so no hit rate is ever computable and the store becomes a list of
surprises with nothing to divide by. Every resolved prediction is recorded; only surprise *changes* a
belief.

**The eight-hour night.** Not a refusal of the idea, a refusal of the shape. The account's session limit
stops every agent at once on a five-hour window, so an eight-hour night is not a thing that can happen
here. Watches, standing orders and night orders, and a written handover with a readback.

**Singularity — "it attaches to one founder and one company and does not generalise."** The founder has
several ventures, one budget, one reputation and four live sessions across three repositories. A design
that refuses the fleet is refusing the founder's stated reality. §26.

**One unit of work.** WAKE's ply carries a wager as a field, which makes every unit a hypothesis. Then
nothing renews the domain. Two atoms, and debts are never ranked.

**The Line as the whole boundary.** Right in spirit and one distinction short. Reversible and compensable
are not the same thing, and a design that treats them alike will drill the compensating action and
convert a hard boundary into a checkbox. Exposure, three classes, and *drill the delay* for everything
that touches a person.

### Added, and not in WAKE at all

Reconciliation against records the house does not own, as a law rather than a practice. The debt as an
atom. The two wallets and the two curves. The evidence ladder generated from the instrument so a status
report cannot inflate. The lethality order as a default critical path, with *never spend on a later rung
while an earlier one is untested and cheaper*. **Start the slow clocks first**, and *next-to-test is not
next-to-start*. The offer as a versioned file. The fleet, the drives, and the reputation as a shared
depletable resource. The audit of silence and the held-out rejections. The restore drill. And the room —
which is the founder's office simulation, built honestly.

## 33. What is genuinely unsolved

Everything above is buildable. These are not, and pretending otherwise is how a design becomes a
disappointment on a schedule.

**Forking a company.** Code is solved by branches. A company's state is a database, a deploy, a mailbox,
a set of third-party accounts with side effects. Branchable databases and snapshot microVMs exist; the
unsolved part is simulating external services well enough that a bet can act against them and be
discarded. This is the real engineering project of the whole design, and it is the place to start rather
than the agents.

**The Second may still flatter, and the held-out rejections only narrow it.** A machine that keeps saying
*I thought you would pick that* is teaching the founder something about themselves, and the direction of
that teaching is not controlled. The mitigation is to track whether their decisions become more
predictable **faster than the machine is learning**, and to say so out loud when they do.

**A founder who stops choosing.** If they rubber-stamp for a month, the calibration data is corrupted at
the source and the house cannot know, because a rubber stamp and an agreement are the same event. The
reverse seal and the fork response rate are defences and they are not strong ones. The 97%-approval
finding says this is the modal outcome, not the tail.

**Exposure estimation.** Wrong in the expensive direction and the house is a nag that gets ignored; wrong
in the cheap direction and it does something that cannot be taken back. It starts conservative and earns
its way per class of action with a visible ledger, and the ledger is a mitigation, not a solution.

**Fields with no ground truth stay that way.** Where reality genuinely cannot be reached, a mark degrades
into a model having opinions about a model. Those fields are taste-only and never ship alone. That is a
limitation, not a solution, and the map in §30 says which they are so nobody is surprised.

**Compounding error.** Short cells and frequent contact push the half-life out; they do not repeal it,
and the self-conditioning result means an early error raises the rate for the whole run.

**A second model family is a procurement problem this runtime does not solve.** Three claims in this
repository have been permanently unresolvable for the same reason, and every autonomy argument that leans
on independent judgement leans on something that does not exist here yet.

**And the first conversation.** If the founder is not honest on day zero, everything above is built on
sand and the machine has no way to detect it.

## 34. What it would take to exist

Nothing here needs a model that does not exist in September 2026. What is missing is the arrangement, and
one genuinely hard engineering project.

**Weeks one to four, and this is the whole of the beginning.** An API key, so metering is real and the
automation question is answered. The Log with **one id minted at dispatch on every row**, because it is
not retrofittable and everything downstream is joined by it. The two atoms as files. The decision-flip
check. The desk, on a phone, with defaults and a heartbeat. One Hard instrument end to end. One hand in
the Drill Book with a real undo and a real drill. The reconciliation against one outside record — the
bank is the right first one, because it is unarguable. And **ten real moves measured against the actual
bill**, because two competent reviewers priced this design and disagreed by a factor of ten and neither
of them was careless.

That is a hundred-line loop and six files. The evidence that this is the right size is that a
hundred-line agent scores 65% on a serious coding benchmark, and that every large first stage in this
repository's history produced a safety machine.

**Then, in order, each gated on one real mission having run through the thing before it:** the powerless
zone built from measured argv and re-measured, because every previous statement about that seam in this
repository was wrong by one flag. The watch, with standing orders and night orders and a readback. The
Almanac's write path with retraction that recomputes downstream. The Proof House and the first
apprenticeship. The Second, and its two scoreboards, reporting *insufficient resolutions* until it has
them. The room, last, because it is the only part that is purely for pleasure and the pleasure is real
but it is not load-bearing.

**The thing that needs invention:** venture-level forking. Everything else is plumbing.

**What must be true that is not yet:** that thirty siblings sharing a trunk really do cost roughly one
trunk plus thirty tails — the load-bearing assumption of the entire budget, and therefore measured
against a real bill in week one rather than assumed. That instrument-forging is mostly automatable; the
honest guess is eighty per cent machine and twenty per cent founder, permanently, with the twenty being
the founder saying *no, that is not what good looks like* **about the instrument itself**, which is why
every new instrument gets a fork before it is trusted. And that the price curve keeps going the way it
has gone for three years, because at today's prices a continuous Opus house costs more than a junior
engineer and nobody should build that.

## 35. The first week

Monday, 06:41. A founder wakes with an idea and nothing else. Twenty seconds into their phone while the
kettle boils: a small real annoyance in a trade they know, and a suspicion that software could remove it.
*Something to help small law firms stop losing money on unbilled time.* No code, no customers, no name.

**Day 0, two hours, and it is not an interview.** Founders are unreliable narrators of their own taste
and a questionnaire produces the answers they think they should give. So the house asks them to **judge**:
it generates forty artifacts around the idea — landing pages, three prices, six names, positioning
statements, a competitor's actual homepage — and watches what they reject and how fast. Then it asks
about decisions already made, which are judgements with the stakes already paid: what they have quit,
what they have refused, the last three products they paid for and cancelled and why, what would embarrass
them. It ends with the question nobody asks: **what would have to be true in six months for you to
stop?** The answer is written down verbatim, with the date, and it is now a debt the house owes them.

Before lunch, three things that no other system in this study does on day one.

It reads the twenty seconds back as **seven bets**, each with a probability, ordered by how much else
collapses if it is false — and then says out loud what it cannot judge. *Of your seven, I can settle one
today and I have, and it is true. Two are Reactive and I can have instruments for them by Thursday. Four
are Blind, and the only instrument for a Blind question is people, so I have started finding forty of
them.*

It **prices you.** Not your worth — the shadow price of an hour, which is a computable number and not an
identity claim: it is the value density of the best thing dropped this week for lack of an hour, read off
the drop list. On day one the list is empty so the price is provisional and it says so. From Thursday it
is real, and it self-corrects — a queue that never fills lowers the bar, a queue always full raises it.

And it **starts the slow clocks**, before anything else is decided. A domain, so the deliverability
warm-up has ninety days rather than nine. A search presence, because that clock is three to six months
and it is the one every founder starts in month nine. A recruiting funnel toward forty real lawyers,
because rung 1 needs strangers and finding them takes a week. **The slow clocks are the only thing that
cannot be caught up later**, and they are started on Monday by a machine that does not need to be clever
to do it.

**Watch 1.** Not *build the product*: the idea is the thing under test. Sixty framings of what this even
is, unfiltered, in the powerless zone. Then contact: which framings survive what exists in the world —
does this exist, who bought it, what did they pay, who tried it and died. Four fields are apprenticed at
once and four discriminators built; two are decent, one is 0.52 and the house says so, which means no
confident claims about buying behaviour this week. Cost of the watch: less than a mid-range dinner, and
the house reports the actual figure from the runner because it does not trust its own estimate.

**Day 1.** A map of eleven versions of the company, most of which the founder had not considered, two
already dead with the evidence attached. They are not asked to review eleven. They are shown two, and
asked *which*. The other nine keep their cells. The house sealed its prediction of the choice. They pick
the other one. First data point.

**Days 2 and 3.** They pick a region of the map. The house builds the thinnest thing that can produce a
signal, which is usually not the product; usually it is the thing that gets a stranger to reply. Nine
positions become nine real staged pages on real URLs — staged, so reversible, so it does not ask. The
first crude model of the funnel is built and its output is not a recommendation: *here are the three
assumptions that dominate every outcome, and the cheapest test of each this week.* One is eleven phone
calls. On day 3 the **first instrument goes live** — a real conversion number against real traffic, small
and noisy and honest, reconciled to the analytics provider and cross-checked against the server's own
count, because an event that never fires is byte-identical to a feature nobody uses. The Proof House goes
from zero to one, and from that night copy leaves the founder's attention budget forever.

**Day 4. The first one-way door, and the founder walks through it.** Of the forty people, eleven answered
and six will talk. The house wanted to send an invitation to twenty real strangers and **stopped**, once,
with the exact text and the count and the fact that this cannot be unsent. That was the only hard stop of
the week, and because it was the only one, it was read properly. The house writes the script, tunes it
after call three because two questions were dead, listens with consent, and by evening has fourteen
candidate beliefs — six with real support and eight flagged *one person said this once.* And the first
real surprise: the house predicted at 0.7 that the annoyance the founder cares about is the one customers
rank first; four of six put something else first, and two describe a workaround they already pay for. The
Almanac takes its first heavy entry, and three of the nine positions die to criteria written on day zero,
without a conversation and without anybody having to be brave.

**Day 5.** Building starts, because now there is something worth building and a person who said what it
is. Hard-oracle territory, so it runs unattended across two watches and merges itself. And something
public ships: a page, a working waitlist, a five-minute video, and copy that uses the words the lawyers
actually used instead of the words the category uses. Nine variants, discriminator-selected, staged
behind the founder's hand. They look for six minutes and ship one. Every word of it traces to a sentence
a specific human said on Thursday.

**Day 6, Saturday.** Silent. The watches still run, and in one of them something aimed at breaking the
house's own conclusions survives for the first time: it argues, with citations, that the premise is
wrong — that unbilled time is a symptom, that the disease is partners who will not adopt any tool making
their juniors' hours visible, and that three funded companies died on exactly that rock. It is not asking
anyone to quit. It is saying the thing that would otherwise have taken nine months and a seed round to
find out. It waits for Monday, because Saturday is Saturday.

**Day 7. Not a status report. A profit and loss of judgement.**

> This week I learned to judge three things I could not judge on Monday: whether a page converts, whether
> a build is correct, whether a layout matches the system we wrote on Wednesday. That took my unattended
> window from twenty minutes to about five hours.
>
> I made 34 predictions. 19 have resolved: 14 true, 4 false, **1 unresolved and 0 redefined.** I will not
> show you a calibration number for months — 19 resolutions cannot support one, and anybody who shows you
> one on 19 is showing you noise. What I will show you every week is that nothing was redefined to make
> it come true.
>
> Thirty-one decisions reached you. Twenty-two were questions of fact and I should not have asked
> nineteen of them; by next week eleven will be gone because the instruments exist. Nine were questions of
> preference and I will keep bringing you every single one of those, and there will be more of them next
> month, not fewer.
>
> We spent $63 by my count and $63 by the provider's, which agree because I read theirs rather than mine.
> The card statement will not settle for two weeks and I will tell you if it disagrees. Four entries in
> the Almanac. Sixteen dollars per
> thing learned. Nine positions opened, three dead with obituaries, two promoted. A fifth of what I built
> was something I was confident you would hate, and one of those is on the map.
>
> Three slow clocks are running that were not running on Monday, and the domain is one of them, and in
> ninety days that will matter and today it looks like nothing.

By Sunday the founder has written no spec, answered perhaps fifteen questions, and owns a landing page, a
waitlist, fourteen beliefs with provenance, a written case against their own idea, a machine that called
twenty-two of their thirty-one decisions — a count, not a calibration, and the house says so every time
anyone tries to read it as one — and three clocks running that they would not have started for another
eight months.

## 36. The year

The Proof House goes from empty to somewhere near forty marks, and the watches lengthen in step. Nobody
adds an agent. Nobody edits a permissions file. Every freedom is derived from a measurement, and every
measurement reconciles to somebody else's record.

The Almanac stays small, which is the surprising part — perhaps sixty live beliefs, because confirmations
compress to tallies and rent evicts what is never load-bearing. A founder can read the whole of what
their company knows in a quarter of an hour, and that is worth more than a wiki nobody opens. The Book is
the opposite: hundreds of closed bets, most of them dead with an honest obituary, every new bet checked
against them before it opens, and twice in the year something that died in spring is resurrected in
autumn because the Almanac changed underneath it.

The two curves separate, and that is the whole story of the year in one chart. Fact questions fall from
twenty-two a week to two. Preference questions rise from nine to twenty, and the founder's forty minutes
a day are spent almost entirely on things no instrument will ever exist for.

The fleet goes from one venture to three. The second one costs a fraction of the first, because the
Proof House and the Second and the Drill Book came with it, and the market did not. One of the three goes
to `sleep` in month seven against a criterion written in month one, and nobody had to be brave about it.

And the number that says whether any of it was real is not the volume. It is the monthly line that says
how many bets resolved on time, and how many were quietly redefined so that they would resolve at all.

## 37. Why nobody has seen it

Not the agents, not the memory, not the night. Those exist somewhere in pieces. Five things are
structural, and each looks either wasteful or pedantic on any single day.

**Everyone builds the founder as a gate**, which caps the system at one person's latency forever.
Building both branches and asking *which* looks like paying to throw work away; it is only rational once
you see that a shared cache makes the discarded branch nearly free, and that a discard is the single
measurement the system cannot get anywhere else.

**Everyone's quality mechanism only subtracts.** Reviews, gates, verdicts, lints, tiers — each
individually correct, and their sum converges on inaction. Generation that is free **because it is
powerless** is the only version of the ungated zone that survives its first argument with a competent
engineer.

**Everyone grades their own homework at the level of the number.** They know not to let a model judge its
own artifact; not one of them refuses to believe a metric it computed itself. The corpus on disk in this
repository is 100% self-description and grew while being measured, and every design here would produce
another one. *The house may not believe a number it produced* is one sentence and it is the difference
between an instrument and a mirror.

**Everyone optimises for asking the founder less**, and the honest end state of that optimisation is a
founder who has been optimised out of their own company, with every metric improving the whole way down.
Splitting the wallet — **fact questions priced and mostly forbidden, preference questions never priced at
all** — is the only version of that goal that cannot eat the thing it was protecting. It is why the
health metric is two curves moving apart instead of one curve falling.

**And nobody reserves capacity for being wrong** in a way the system cannot reach. A share debited before
the allocator sees it, aimed at what the machine is most confident you will hate, is indefensible on any
given night and is the only reason the thing is still worth opening in the morning a year later.

Underneath all five is one rule that runs every domain without a playbook: **predict first, in writing;
record everything that resolved; change your mind only where the world contradicted you; and check the
numbers against somebody who does not work for you.** What you get is a memory that shrinks as you get
better and grows exactly where you are worst, an autonomy that is derived rather than granted, and a
founder whose calendar empties of everything except the questions that were only ever theirs.

Not agents given more freedom. **A house that has to be right in advance, in writing, about a world that
gets to answer back — and that keeps its books against the bank.**
