## Spine, written before reading

*Written after reading only `00-BRIEF.md`. Nothing else had been opened. Never edited afterwards.*

**The name is WATCH**, and three meanings carry it. A watch is the crew that keeps the ship moving
through the night while the captain sleeps — handed over with a log, governed by standing orders,
waking the captain by rule and not by whim. A watch is the act of seeing — the founder watching the
work, the system watching the world. And a watch is a mechanism whose *escapement* releases a wound
spring in measured ticks, so that stored energy becomes sustained motion instead of one burst and a
dead stop. The founder wants a thing that moves all night, that they can see, and that does not burn
itself down by morning. That is one word.

**The diagnosis I start from is the founder's own: everything built so far is a stopping mechanism.**
A machine assembled entirely from brakes cannot move, and no amount of adding better brakes will make
it. WATCH inverts the primary object. The first-class citizens are *things that want to happen*;
checks are conditions attached to them, not a separate apparatus with its own life. If a mechanism
cannot be stated as "this makes something happen sooner or better," it is not architecture, it is
anxiety.

**It is not an organisation and not a harness. It is a crew.** An org chart solves problems agents do
not have: humans cannot be copied, cannot share a memory, cannot be killed and respawned, and must
therefore be specialised and coordinated by title. Agents can. Copying human structure is a category
error, and a C-suite of prompts is the most expensive way to discover that. The unit of WATCH is not
a role. It is a **commission**: one piece of work with its goal, its budget, its assembled context,
its standard of done, and its wake conditions all attached to it. Breadth — a Postgres migration, a
marketing video, a pricing page, a leaked-key check — comes from *knowledge and hands*, not from
headcount. One worker shape, many lenses, many tools.

**The escapement is the answer to burning tokens.** A founder winds a mainspring: an ambition, with
money and a horizon. The escapement releases it in ticks, and a tick spends only if its outcome would
change what happens next. If every branch of a result leads to the same next action, the work is
decoration and does not run. Nothing runs because a scheduler said so. Idling is a legitimate,
zero-cost state; a system that must be busy will invent work, and invented work is worse than silence.

**Three kinds of goal, and only one belongs to the founder.** A **question** is cheap, reversible,
internal — the system opens these freely, all night. A **bet** costs a bounded amount, may fail, and
touches nothing outside the machine — the system opens these inside its allowance. A **commitment**
is irreversible or visible to the world — money moved, a message sent, a thing published, a schema
migrated — and only the founder signs one. This single line answers autonomy without a policy engine.
The night belongs to questions and bets. Commitments queue for morning, presented as a decision, not
as a request for permission.

**Projects differ by standing orders, not by code.** Each project carries a charter: pace (*drive*,
*walk*, *wait*), budget, what may be done unattended, and the conditions under which the founder is
woken. A startup is set to *drive* and moves all night. A client's work *walks* — it advances only
where the founder has been. A piece of research *waits*, thinks when asked, and never opens a goal of
its own. One machine, many behaviours, because the charter differs.

**Creativity is variation under selection, not an instruction to be creative.** The playbook lost it
because a playbook is one path with no selection: you inherit someone else's result and none of your
own. WATCH's default move on anything that matters is divergence — several attempts from genuinely
different angles, cheaply, in parallel — followed by honest killing against a standard written before
the attempts. Structure is *pressure*, never *path*: state what must be true at the end and refuse to
state how.

**Memory is four organs, not one store.** The **world**: current facts about a project, each with an
expiry, because a fact with no expiry is a future lie. The **log**: everything that happened,
append-only, cheap to write and expensive to read, therefore indexed by *situation* rather than by
time. **Lessons**: small distilled things, each bound to a trigger that retrieves it, competing for
survival — a lesson that never fires or never helps is evicted. And **taste**: the founder's
standards, voice, and refusals, learned from their corrections. *The founder's corrections are the
highest-value signal in the entire system and nearly every system throws them away.* WATCH treats
"no, not like that" as the rarest training data it will ever get.

**Context is assembled, budgeted, and scored.** Something decides what goes into a worker's head for
this one task, under a token budget, and after the task each item is marked load-bearing or dead
weight. Retrieval that is never scored is a wish. This is also the honest form of token saving: not
smaller prompts, but *fewer useless ones*.

**Checks observe artifacts, never reports about artifacts.** Render it, run it, click it, look at it,
try to break it, show it to a mind that did not build it. And the lenses are not only technical: does
it hold a secret it should not, does it read like us, would a customer understand it, is it legal,
does it look good. Those sit at equal rank with correctness, because a company dies of them equally.

**The world enters as capability, through one door.** A repository, a skill, a CLI, an MCP server —
all admitted the same way: quarantined, made to declare what it touches, tried on one real task,
recorded permanently by what it can reach rather than by what it claims.

**The founder's surface is a bridge, not an office.** An office of little figures typing shows motion,
and motion is not progress. The bridge shows *state* — a few instruments that hold the whole in one
glance, a river of what happened that you can scroll, and the power to interrupt anything from a
phone in one sentence. Presence comes from being legible and wakeable, not from animation.

**Everything durable is a hashed file, replicated off this Mac; running state is reconstructible from
the log.** A dead machine costs hours, not memory.

**One budget, many projects, so allocation follows evidence.** A project that produces gets fed. A
project that spins gets throttled, and then it stops and asks a question instead of spending.

**The first week with a founder holding only an idea:** night one is questions — the world as it is,
who else is doing this, what would have to be true. Day two is a decision put to the founder in one
page with three options and a recommendation. Then a thing that exists and can be shown, then a first
stranger's reaction, then the founder's corrections turning into taste. By day seven the system knows
how this founder says no, and that is the asset.

## What changed after reading, and what did not

*Written after WAKE, the Fable report, the catalogue, the five reference systems, and a day of research
into how ships, hospitals, control towers, trading desks, newsrooms, film sets, game studios and
monasteries run through the night. The spine above is unedited. Three things in it survived contact
better than I expected, two were sharpened, and one was wrong.*

**Survived, and got harder.** The nautical vocabulary is not decoration — the institutions that actually
solved this problem use exactly these objects. A ship has **standing orders** (the permanent envelope,
written once) and **night orders** (signed each evening for tonight's conditions) and a **call-me-if**
card, and the separation between those three is the answer to a question WAKE does not ask. A watch is
relieved by a **four-phase briefing** in which the person going off duty stays and audits the person
coming on. And the escapement was the right instinct for the wrong reason: the reason a 24/7 machine
burns money is not that it works too much, it is that 57% of its bill is cache reads and 32% cache
writes, so the bill is a function of *how often you re-establish context*, not of how much you produce.

**Sharpened.** "Corrections are the highest-value signal" is right and incomplete. The 2026 measurement
on memory confabulation is that free reflection — *write down what you learned* — produced **0 correct
diagnoses out of 121** in frozen failure environments, and a **structured trajectory analysis** of the
same failures produced 86%. So the correction is gold and the *procedure that turns it into a lesson*
is the whole game, and the default procedure everyone ships makes things worse.

**Wrong.** I wrote that divergence is "several attempts from genuinely different angles, cheaply, in
parallel." Cheap and different are in direct opposition, and I had not seen it. Parallel attempts are
cheap because they share a cached prefix; they are similar *for the same reason*. Sampling N times from
one conditioning does not give you N different things — measured similarity around 0.85, dropping to
0.65 only when the conditioning itself is diversified, and 1.6–2.1× more diversity from verbalized
sampling than from direct prompting. Divergence is not free and cannot be made free. It has to be
bought. Section 16 is what I think now.

# WATCH

*A crew that keeps your company moving through the night, wakes you by rule and not by whim, and
measures whether it is walking with you or quietly walking instead of you.*

The year is the twelve months from September 2026, on one Mac, with one founder, several projects and
one bill. Nothing in this document was published, sent, spent, posted, shared or deployed.

---

## 1. Why the word

A watch is three things and every one of them is load-bearing.

A watch is **the crew that stands the night**. On a ship the captain sleeps and the vessel does not
stop. What makes that survivable is not trust and not talent; it is a set of artifacts so old they
have hardened into vocabulary. **Standing orders**, written once, the permanent envelope of what the
watch may do without asking. **Night orders**, written and signed each evening for tonight's specific
conditions. A **call-me-if** card, composed by the captain in daylight and in calm, so that at 03:00
nobody is exercising judgment about the captain's sleep. A **log**, kept by hand, that outlives every
person who wrote in it. And a **relief**, in which the watch going off duty does not leave until it
has satisfied itself that the watch coming on has understood.

A watch is also **the act of seeing** — the founder watching the work, and the system watching the
world. The founder asked to see the agents working. That is a real need and the picture they reached
for is not the right one; §18 argues for a different one and takes the need seriously.

And a watch is **a mechanism**, whose escapement releases a wound spring in measured ticks rather than
all at once. A mainspring with no escapement is not a fast clock. It is a spring that unwinds in three
seconds and then a dead object. That is the exact failure mode of an agentic system with a budget and
no governor, and §7 is the governor.

I did not choose the word for the metaphor. I chose it, before reading anything, because I thought the
problem was nautical. Then the research came back and the institutions that have actually solved
"something valuable keeps running while the responsible person sleeps" — ships, intensive care units,
control towers, trading desks — turned out to use these objects literally, with measured effects
attached to them. The vocabulary is not a conceit. It is the field's accumulated answer, and most
agent systems are re-deriving it badly.

reconciliation against records it does not write, and measured — above every other number — on whether

## 3. The diagnosis: what a stopping mechanism actually is

The founder's sentence is the whole brief: *it lost creativity to playbooks, and everything built so
far is a stopping mechanism.* It deserves a mechanical answer rather than agreement.

A stopping mechanism accumulates for a reason that has nothing to do with anyone's judgment being bad.
**A false stop leaves no trace and a false go is loud.** When a gate wrongly refuses good work, the
work simply never exists; there is no incident, no post-mortem, no line in a log, nobody to be sorry.
When a gate wrongly permits bad work, there is a bug, a bill, an apology, a review, and a new gate. So
the gradient at every single decision points toward more brakes, and every individual brake is
correct on its own terms. A hundred locally correct additions sum to a machine whose only fluent
behaviour is refusal. Nobody chose that. It is what the incentive landscape produces when one of the
two error types is invisible.

You cannot fix this by resolving to add fewer checks. Resolve is not a mechanism, and the next
incident will overrule it. You fix it by **making the invisible error visible**, and that is a small
piece of engineering:

> **The refusal ledger.** Every time WATCH declines to do something — a gate held, a budget refused, a
> tool absent, a hand not admitted, a night order not signed — it writes one line: what was going to
> happen, what stopped it, and what the thing would have been worth if it had been right. Once a week
> the founder sees the ten most expensive refusals of the week, ranked. A stop that never appears on
> that list is a good stop. A stop that appears every week is a design defect wearing a safety
> costume, and it gets removed or narrowed by name.

That single ledger changes the gradient. It gives false stops a body. Everything else in this document
about freeing the maker assumes it, because without it, WATCH becomes a stopping mechanism inside a
year and every step of the way will look like prudence.

The second half of the founder's sentence — *it lost creativity to playbooks* — has a different cause
and needs a different answer. A playbook is a compressed record of a path somebody else walked. Follow
it faithfully and you arrive where they arrived, which is by construction the average of the field and
never the tail. But the answer is not to delete structure, because unstructured making produces
confident mediocrity at higher speed. The answer is that **structure belongs at the exit and never on
the path**. State what must be true when the work is finished, in a form something can check, and then
refuse to say how. This is the one idea worth taking whole from the existing repo's playbooks, which
already refuse `steps:`, `how:` and `method:` at the schema level and are correct to.

## 4. Harness or organisation: it is a crew

The founder asked the question directly and it deserves a direct answer, because the wrong answer to
it is what produced the twenty-one-role C-suite that collapsed in this repository.

**An organisation divides labour by role because humans cannot be copied.** A person can only hold one
job, cannot be spawned on demand, cannot be discarded after twelve minutes, and cannot share a memory
with a colleague. Every feature of an org chart — titles, reporting lines, handoffs, meetings,
specialisation — is an adaptation to those four constraints. Agents have none of them. Copying the
adaptation without the constraint is a category error, and it costs exactly what it cost here: eleven
routing shims resolving to nothing and ten names with no file behind them.

**A harness is a set of tools with no standing.** It cannot be accountable, cannot be relieved, cannot
hold an order, and does not know what it is for. A harness is what you have when the human is the only
thing with intent.

**A crew is the third thing, and it is what WATCH is.** A crew has *positions*, not jobs. The position
is durable and the person filling it is not — you relieve the watch, you do not replace the officer.
A crew is governed by standing orders written by an authority that is not on watch. It keeps one log
that outlives every member. It hands over by ritual. And it has exactly one captain, whose sleep is
protected by rule.

What this buys, concretely: **there is no roster in WATCH, and no agent has a name.** A position is a
set of capabilities plus a standing order, filled for one task by a mortal process that dies at the end
of it. The founder asked *what agents do we have*, and this is the honest answer — six positions, and a
count that does not grow when the company enters a new field:

| Position | May touch | Never | What it is for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The watch** | read, write, run, the project's own tree | anything outside the machine | the work: keel or weather, one task, then it dies |
| **The lookout** | read, search, fetch | write anything at all | evidence, with sources; it answers and never recommends |
| **The judge** | read | write, run, act | one instrument's verdict on somebody else's work, out of band |
| **The mouth** | exactly one act on the world, against a signed instruction | think | the only process that touches anything outside; it holds no model |
| **The broker** | the keychain | the network | attaches credentials at the moment of egress, so no worker ever holds one |
| **The relay** | the inbound path | act on what it read | writes one leaf into the log when the world sends something |

Only the first three hold a model, and only the first can write. Domain expertise is not an identity
and not a file; it is a **licence** (§12), earned by demonstrating you can tell better from worse in a
field, and it attaches to the position rather than to whoever is standing it. A marketer and a database
engineer are both *the watch*; they differ in what they may touch and in what may judge them, and in
nothing else. This is what makes breadth tractable — adding video to the company adds an instrument and
a hand, not a person, and the count above stays at six.

## 5. The keel and the weather

This is the largest thing I take issue with in every prior design, including WAKE, and the largest
piece of new structure I am proposing.

**A search engine is not a company.** Divergence plus selection is the right tool when you do not know
the answer and evaluation is cheap. It is a magnificent way to find a framing, a headline, a price, a
name, a layout. It is not how anything gets *built*. A company's value comes from going deep on one
thing long enough for compounding to happen — the fourteenth week of the same product, the third
rewrite of the same onboarding, the customer relationship that is worth something in month nine
because it existed in month two. A machine that forks four hundred ways every night and keeps the
survivors accumulates a portfolio of shallow bets. It will be dazzling in the morning and will not have
a product in June.

So WATCH runs two regimes at once, and they have different laws. This is the Cerny split from game
development, generalised: pre-production and production require incompatible management, and the
mistake studios make is running one regime over both.

**The keel** is the one thing under construction, per project, at a time. It is *not* forked, *not*
mutated, *not* subject to selection. It is worked continuously, watch after watch, by successive
relief. It has a **definition of done** written on the day it was laid, in a checkable form, and it is
finished when that is met and not before. Progress against it is the only thing that answers the
founder's word *relentlessly*. Work in progress is one. One keel per project. If a second thing must be
built, the keel is finished or broken up first, deliberately, with a note saying why.

**The weather** is everything else: framings, options, prices, positions, headlines, layouts,
experiments, red arguments against the company's own premise, research. Forked, cheap, wide, mostly
killed, and selected only by contact with something real. The weather is where the search lives and it
is allowed to be strange.

**Between them is a gate, and it is the best gate in the study.** In game development, pre-production
does not exit on a date; it exits on a **publishable first playable** — deep enough to put in front of
real players — and if the players are not excited, the project is killed *before* production spend.
Two properties make it work: it is not time-based, and its most common correct output is *stop*.

WATCH's version: **a thing in the weather may only become the keel by being shown to something outside
the machine and surviving.** A person who did not build it. A number that moved. A test that a stranger
could run. Not a model's opinion, not a rubric, not a score. And the gate's most common outcome is
that the thing goes back to the weather or dies with an obituary, which is a success.

Three consequences fall out immediately.

The founder's question *"is this a system that always moves forward, or one that waits for me?"* is
answered structurally rather than by a mode switch: **the keel is what moves forward and the weather
is what explores, and the posture of a project is the ratio between them** (§6). A startup has a
permanently occupied keel. A research project has none at all and is weather only. A client's work has
a keel and no weather, because you do not run experiments on somebody else's money.

Depth becomes *cheap to protect*, because the keel is a named object with a WIP limit of one and a
written definition of done. It is very hard to accidentally abandon a thing that is the only thing in
its slot.

And "step repetition" — at 15.7% the single largest system-design failure mode in the multi-agent
failure taxonomy, and the failure that none of the five studied reference systems could detect — stops
being a mystery. On the keel, repetition is visible because the definition of done does not move. In
the weather, repetition is *the point*.

## 6. Standing orders, night orders, and the five postures

The founder said the thing most designs get wrong: *not one system that is always working on all the
projects, all the time, without direction, burning tokens.* Different projects need different
machines. WATCH's answer is that they do not need different machines; they need different **orders**,
and the separation between two kinds of order is the mechanism that every institution studied has
independently arrived at.

**Standing orders** are per project, written by the founder, changed rarely, and — this is the part
borrowed from a monastery rather than a ship — **not amendable by the crew that they govern**. The Rule
of St Benedict derives its authority from exactly this: it binds the people who live under it and they
cannot rewrite it, which is why fifteen centuries of monks have not had to spend their mornings
re-litigating the horarium. The value of a rule is not that it is optimal. It is that it is *settled*,
so attention goes to the work instead of to the terms of the work. Concretely, standing orders hold:

- the **posture** (below), which fixes the build/learn split and the nightly budget;
- the **Line**: the classes of act this project may never perform unattended, whatever any score says;
- the **call-me-if** card, in the founder's own words;
- the **kill criteria** — what would make the founder stop — recorded verbatim with the date;
- the **definition of done** for the current keel;
- and the **emission budget**: how many things this project may put in front of the founder per day.

**Night orders** are written every evening — by WATCH, approved or amended by the founder in one glance
— and they say what tonight is for. What it intends to work on, what it expects to have by morning
with a probability against each, what it will do if blocked, and what it will not touch. They are
signed. In the morning the first thing the founder sees is the night orders with outcomes written
against them.

This is the actual answer to *burning tokens without direction*. Not a budget cap — a cap governs how
much you waste, not whether you are wasting. **A written, signed, one-paragraph intent for the night,
scored at dawn.** Within a fortnight the probabilities are calibrated, and a system that says it is 70%
likely to finish and finishes 70% of the time is worth more than one that is enthusiastic. The failure
this prevents is the one every unattended loop in the reference study exhibited: a machine that wakes,
finds nothing pressing, and does something anyway, because being idle felt like being broken.

**The five postures.** A posture is not a mode flag; it is a budget split plus an interrupt threshold
plus a rule about who may open a goal.

| Posture | Keel | Weather | Who may open a goal | Interrupt | For |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Drive** | always occupied, worked through the night | funded | founder, crew, world, clock | call-me-if card only | a startup that must move |
| **Walk** | advances only as far as the founder has been | small | founder and world only | at every real assumption | a project built *with* the founder |
| **Wait** | none | on request only | founder only | never unprompted | research, a question being held |
| **Tend** | none | none | world and clock only | on damage or obligation | a shipped thing; a client's work |
| **Dark** | none | none | nobody | never | a project that is asleep, and says so |

**Walk** is the posture the founder described and no existing system has: *walk with me and not for
me*. Its rule is precise. The crew advances the keel until it reaches the first point where it would
have to assume something the founder has not settled — a name, a shape, a trade-off, a person's
feelings — and there it **stops and waits, having prepared both branches**. It does not guess and it
does not ask permission to continue. It arrives at the assumption with the work up to that point
finished and the two next steps built, and it waits. Progress in *walk* is measured in assumptions
reached, not in artifacts produced.

**Tend** is the posture nobody designs and every company needs: nothing new starts, and the lights stay
on. Replies get answered inside their promise, the bill gets reconciled, the certificate gets renewed,
the dependency with the CVE gets patched, backups get restored to prove they restore. It is the
posture that keeps a shipped thing from rotting while the founder's attention is on the next thing,
and it costs almost nothing because almost all of it is deterministic.

**Dark** exists because *paused* is a lie. A paused project still has a queue, still has watchers,
still has something waking up to check whether it should wake up. Dark means the crew will not spend a
token on it and the bay says so in plain words, with the date it went dark and what would bring it
back.

## 7. Who opens a goal, and the escapement

### Four openers, and only one of them is the founder

The founder asked whether the system sets goals or only they do. Neither answer is right, and the
honest structure has four openers.

**The founder** opens a *commitment*: something irreversible or visible outside the machine. Only they
sign one, always, at any level of demonstrated competence. §8.

**The crew** opens *questions* freely and *bets* within an allowance. A question is cheap, internal,
reversible, and leaves no trace outside the machine. A bet costs a bounded amount, may fail, produces
an artifact, and touches nothing outside. This is the whole of the machine's autonomy and it does not
need a permissions file to express.

**The world** opens goals, and this is the opener every prior design in this repository is missing.
A customer writes. A test that was green goes red. A card is declined. A dependency gets a CVE. An
invoice arrives. A stranger replies to something sent last week. These are neither the founder's idea
nor the crew's; they *arrive*, and a system with no channel for them will be regularly surprised by its
own company. WATCH has an inbound path that writes one leaf into the log and nothing else — no model
reads a stranger's text with a tool in its hand — and the leaf becomes a strip in the bay.

**The clock** opens goals: an obligation coming due, a promise made in March that resolves in
September, a belief whose expiry has arrived, a scheduled restore drill, a licence that decays. This
is the opener that keeps a company from quietly failing at the things nobody is thinking about, and it
is entirely deterministic.

### The escapement

Every tick, for every candidate move, WATCH asks a different question depending on which side of §5
the move is on. This is the governor, and it is the reason the machine can run all night without
burning.

**Keel work does not have to justify itself by information.** It justifies itself by the definition of
done. If the keel is live, the posture funds it, and the budget has room, it runs. Relentlessness is
not a mood; it is a slot that stays occupied.

**Everything else is learn spend, and it runs only if the answer would change something.** The test is
mechanical and it is the oldest idea in decision analysis: name the decision this would inform, and
name both branches of the outcome. If both branches lead to the same next act, the work is decoration
and it does not run. This is what makes *doing nothing* a first-class correct answer rather than a
failure state. A machine that must be busy will invent work, and invented work is worse than silence
because it also consumes the attention of whoever reads the morning.

Three governors sit around it, and all three exist to catch the machine rather than the founder.

**The stall detector.** If a lane has produced no durable artifact and no new entry in the Book for N
ticks, it stops spending and writes a strip saying so. The predicate that matters is *a durable
artifact, never the agent's own claim of progress*. This repository already has that predicate written
and working, in a hook that is registered nowhere.

**The repetition tripwire.** Before any attempt, the approach is checked against the dead-ends file for
this project, and a match surfaces what happened last time. Every studied system's anti-repetition
mechanism was fiction — one hardcoded nudge string identical on iteration 2 and iteration 19, one rule
that a brand-new model instance would voluntarily notice its predecessor had said the same thing.
A hash over `(tool, arguments)` on a sliding window is the only real loop detector found anywhere in
the five, and it is necessary and not sufficient, because it cannot tell productive repetition from
stuck repetition. The keel/weather split is what completes it: on the keel, repetition against a fixed
definition of done is a fault; in the weather it is the method.

**The aberrance halt, which is the Knight rule.** In 2012 a trading firm lost $460 million in
45 minutes because a dormant code path woke up on one of eight servers. The regulator's finding names
the missing control exactly: *no procedures in place to halt the system's operations in response to its
own aberrant activity.* Not an external limit — a limit on the system's own strangeness. WATCH defines
aberrance by rate rather than by rule: spend at several times this lane's own median, an unexpected
burst of identical calls, a claim that the world ledger contradicts, a hand firing more often than its
ceiling. Aberrance halts the lane and rings the bell, and the halt is not a judgement about whether the
work is good. It is a judgement that the machine has stopped resembling itself.

And the second half of that case is the one that should frighten every dashboard designer: **97 alerts
naming the exact fault were generated before the market opened, and they "were not designed to be
system alerts," so nobody read them.** The rule WATCH takes from it is absolute and cheap: *any channel
the system writes that nothing reads is itself a defect*, and finding those channels is a scheduled
job.

### Where the money actually goes

The token economics of this machine are not what anybody's intuition says, and the measurement is from
this repository's own transcripts over 35 days and 2,925 sessions: **output tokens are 11% of the bill.
Cache reads are 57% and cache writes are 32%.**

The bill is therefore a function of *how often you re-establish context*, not of how much the machine
says. Every instinct about "make it more concise" is aimed at the eleven percent. Three real levers:

- **A byte-identical trunk.** The stable prefix must be the same bytes across every worker, or it is
  not cached at all. Measured elsewhere: 91.8% cache hit rate and an 82.6% cut in billed cost from
  stable prefixes with delta updates.
- **Cadence inside the TTL.** The cache lives five minutes from the request start. A scheduler with a
  five-minute interval therefore misses the cache on every single tick and pays the 1.25× write
  forever without ever taking the 0.1× read. That is not a hypothetical; it is what a `StartInterval`
  of 300 seconds does, and it is in this repository. Either tick well inside the window, or buy the
  one-hour cache at 2× deliberately and know you did.
- **Model by instrument, not by importance.** Work judged by a hard oracle can be attempted by the
  cheapest model that ever passes it, because the oracle catches the failures and a retry is cheaper
  than a smarter attempt. Work with no instrument gets the best model and few tries, because there is
  nothing downstream to catch it.

Two numbers are reported weekly whether or not they flatter, and both must fall: **cost per thing
learned** — dollars divided by new entries in the Book, which tells you whether the machine is buying
knowledge or buying activity — and **founder-minutes per unit of shipped work**, because tokens are the
cheap input and the founder's attention is the scarce one.

## 8. The Line, and the engineering of reversibility

**WATCH never does alone what cannot be undone.** One question of every act: what does it cost to
reverse this, in money, time, reputation and law? The four are kept apart because they do not convert
into one another. Money is the amount. Time is wall clock you cannot get back. Reputation is who saw
it. Law is whether undoing is possible at all — a filing, a signature, a migration over real data.
Cheap in all four, WATCH never asks. Expensive in any one, it asks once, with the number attached, and
no accumulated competence ever changes that.

The reason this is the right boundary, rather than a tier table over file paths, is that a path-based
classifier stops a thousand harmless things and misses the one that matters. This repository has both
a path classifier and the evidence that it is the wrong shape; the classifier is retired here.

Three things make the Line honest rather than decorative.

**The undo estimator is itself an instrument, and it is graded.** If it has ever said *cheap* about a
class of act that turned out expensive, that class becomes founder-only until a new measurement says
otherwise. An estimator with no track record is not a safety mechanism; it is a guess with authority.

**The estimate is reconciled against the world, not against itself.** We believe an email is recallable
for thirty minutes: check the mail log. We believe a deploy is revertible: revert one, on purpose, on a
schedule. We believe a backup restores: restore it. §10's world ledger is where this lives, and it
exists because of Barings — a trader who controlled both the trading desk and its settlement produced
books that reconciled perfectly to themselves while £827 million evaporated. **A system that audits
itself against its own records is Leeson's book.**

**And widening the reversible zone is funded engineering work, not a wish.** Most of what is
irreversible in a small company is irreversible by accident. Everything outbound sits behind a delay
with a working recall, which moves *sent a stranger an email* from irreversible to reversible and
carries a whole category of work across the Line. Every publish is staged behind a real URL nobody has
seen. Every schema change ships with a down-migration that has been run, or it does not ship. Every
spend is capped and reversible for a window. Drafts are the default state of everything that has a
state. WATCH's freedom grows by making the world safer to be wrong in rather than by being trusted
more, which is the correct direction for freedom to grow.

**One act is excepted from all of it, permanently.** WATCH never decides what company this is. Who to
hire, what to refuse, whether this is still the thing the founder woke up wanting. Those cost nothing
to undo by the four currencies and a good model of the founder would predict them well, and they stay
the founder's anyway, because the day the machine decides them the founder stops being the thing the
system resolves against.

### The mouth

Exactly one process in WATCH can touch the outside world, and it holds no model. It takes a signed
instruction naming one act, one recipient, one payload, and a hash of the artifact that was approved;
it performs that act and nothing else; it holds the delay queue and the recall button; and it is the
only process with the outbound credentials. There is nothing in it to persuade, which is the entire
point — every prompt-injection story ends at a process that cannot be talked to.

The corollary for credentials is the one Omnigent got right and most systems get wrong: **secrets are
never inside the sandbox with the worker.** They are held by a broker and attached at egress. A worker
that has been talked into something malicious still has nothing to steal.

### The current grant surface is the wrong shape, and the fix is one afternoon

Measured on this machine right now: the riskiest hands are live and the safest are switched off. A
tool that publishes to TikTok, a mail client that can send, a drive client that can share a file, a
remote code-execution sandbox, and a browser driving the founder's own authenticated Chrome are all
connected. Analytics, error tracking, and a read-only billing key are not. That is not a risk posture;
it is a record of which buttons got clicked. Under WATCH every one of those goes through the door in
§20 or gets disconnected, and the read-only instruments get connected first, because instruments buy
freedom and hands spend it.

## 9. The relief: how one watch hands over to the next

This is the mechanism I am most confident is genuinely absent from every system in the study, and it
comes from air traffic control.

Every agent system in existence hands over in one direction. A worker finishes, writes a summary, dies;
the next worker reads the summary and starts. The summary is the defect surface, and this repository
has already written down, from its own incident, that *the orchestrator's summary is a defect surface
nobody reviews*. The failure taxonomy agrees at scale: inter-agent misalignment is 32.35% of
multi-agent failures, with "task derailment" and "reasoning-action mismatch" the largest single
entries inside it.

The FAA's position-relief procedure has four phases and the fourth is the one nobody copies. The
relieving controller **previews** the position from the displays and the status board *before hearing
anything*, forming their own picture from durable state. Then a **verbal briefing** covering only what
the displays cannot show. Then an explicit **assumption** of responsibility, spoken and timed. And
then a **review**, in which the *relieved* controller stays on position, checks the incoming
controller's understanding against what they know, verifies the checklist for omissions, and only then
signs off. The relief is not complete when the successor starts. It is complete when the predecessor
has audited the successor. Hospital handover has the same shape from the other end: the I-PASS protocol
ends in *synthesis by the receiver* — a read-back — and its measured effect across nine hospitals and
10,740 admissions was **medical errors down 23% and preventable adverse events down 30%**, with
handover duration unchanged at 2.4 versus 2.5 minutes. **The gain came from the structure, not from
spending longer.**

WATCH's relief, for every handover between workers:

1. **Preview.** The incoming worker reads the bay and the log itself and forms its own account of the
   situation. It does not receive a summary first. This is the step that makes the rest work: an
   independent reading is what a briefing can be checked against.
2. **Brief.** The outgoing worker adds only what durable state cannot show: what it tried that failed,
   what it suspects, what it would do next and why.
3. **Read-back.** The incoming worker states, in its own words, what it believes the situation is and
   what it intends to do first.
4. **Sign-off.** The outgoing worker compares the read-back against what it knows and either signs, or
   names the divergence. **A material divergence does not complete the relief**; it is written to the
   log as a finding, and the finding is about the *briefing*, not about the incoming worker.

The cost is one short model call by a process that was about to exit anyway. The benefit is that a
handoff error is caught by the only party who still knows the answer, at the last moment they exist.
And it converts the summary from an unreviewed artifact into a *checked* one, which is the first time
anyone has put a control on the largest defect surface in multi-agent work.

Two notes on doing it properly. The divergence finding is data about the brief, and briefs that
repeatedly produce divergence are a defect in the *briefing format*, which then gets fixed once
instead of being suffered nightly. And the outgoing worker's judgement here is cheap and reliable
precisely because it is a comparison against something it holds, not a generation — the read-back
turns an open-ended summarisation task into a closed-ended agreement check, which is the kind of
question models are good at.

### How workers talk to each other, which is barely

**Workers never message workers.** Every exchange goes through the bay and the log, which means every
influence one piece of work had on another is a visible row somebody can point at afterwards. The cost
is a little latency. What it buys is the entire class of failure where two agents hold conflicting
assumptions across a conversation nobody audited — a third of all observed multi-agent failures, and
the reason a well-known engineering argument against multi-agent systems exists at all.

Three consequences worth stating.

**There is no negotiation.** A worker that needs something it does not have does not ask a peer for it;
it writes a strip saying what it needs and why, and dies. Something else picks it up with the right
capabilities. Nothing waits on a conversation, which is what makes every worker safely mortal.

**A request for help carries what was already tried.** Not *this is failing* — *this is failing, here
are the three things I tried, here is the exact command that reproduces it, here is what I expected
each time*. This is the same structured-trace discipline as §10's lessons, applied at the moment of
handing a problem on, and it is the difference between passing work along and passing confusion along.

**Between projects, the same rule with a harder edge.** A worker on one project cannot read another
project's Book at all, and the enforcement is that its context assembler never put it there — not an
instruction telling it not to look. Cross-project sharing is limited to what §17 names, and a client's
material never leaves its project under any circumstance.

## 10. Memory: five organs, one write rule each

Memory in most agent systems is a warehouse. Things go in, embeddings are computed, things come out
approximately, nothing is ever forgotten and nothing is ever wrong. It grows monotonically until
retrieval collapses, and it has no opinion about its own contents.

Worse, the default pattern everybody ships — *reflect on what happened and write down what you learned*
— is now measured to make things worse rather than better. In sixteen frozen failure environments,
**zero of 121 reflections mentioned the correct target object.** The agent wrote confident, wrong
diagnoses into memory, retrieved them, acted on them, and reinforced them, across trials that
contradicted them every time. Replacing unstructured self-diagnosis with a **structured trajectory
analysis** — a fixed set of questions asked over the actual trace rather than an open request to
reflect — took correct diagnosis from **0% to 86%** and cut the repetition rate from 0.64 to 0.10.

That result governs the whole of this section. **The write path is the entire design.** Anywhere WATCH
turns experience into memory, it does so by a fixed form over the actual trace, never by asking a model
what it learned.

### The Log

Everything that happened. Append-only, one file per day per project, JSONL, cheap to write and
deliberately expensive to read. It is the deck log: no opinions, only events, each with a timestamp, an
actor, a task id minted at the start and carried by every row. A task id from day one is not a nicety;
the alternative, discovered in one studied system, is joining two tables by a sixty-second heuristic
time window forever, because the identifier was never minted and cannot be retrofitted.

The Log is the substrate of every other organ and it is never the thing anyone retrieves from. Search
over raw history as *memory* is refused explicitly: it cannot tell a corrected belief from a current
one, so it resurrects superseded conclusions and states them confidently.

### The Book

What the company believes. Small, readable, entries with ids, and **one write rule: nothing enters that
was not first a prediction the world contradicted.** Before any act with a consequence, one line and a
number: what we expect and how sure. When the result lands it is scored. Predictions that came true
cost nothing to be right about and compress to a tally. Predictions that were wrong become entries,
carrying what was believed, what happened instead, and what else in the Book leaned on the same broken
assumption.

Five things fall out of that rule and they are why it is worth the discipline. The environment is the
only author with write access, so there is no path from the machine's own reflection into belief. The
Book *shrinks* as the company gets competent, because memory grows with error rather than with elapsed
time — a mastered field goes quiet and a field the machine keeps being wrong about grows loud, which is
exactly where the tokens should go. It yields a real number for self-knowledge, per field. It makes
research honest: a search that confirms is compressed to a tally, and a search that contradicts enters
with its source, its date and the sentence that did the damage, so research output is a diff against
what the company believed this morning rather than a report. And it stays small enough that a founder
can read the whole of what their company knows in fifteen minutes.

Three rules on shape, each with a reason.

**Every belief is bi-temporal.** Two independent time axes: when it was true in the world, and when we
believed it. Without both, the system can answer *what is true now* and cannot answer *what did we
think in June*, and the second question is the one you need to audit a decision. Point at a price and
ask why it is that, and the answer must be the belief that was live when it was set, not today's.

**Nothing is summarised to save space; it is dropped instead.** A dropped belief is a known absence. A
summarised belief is an unknown corruption. Measured: a wholesale context rewrite collapsed 18,282
tokens to 122 and accuracy from 66.7% to 57.1%, eating precisely the domain-specific detail that was
load-bearing.

**Deltas to identified entries, never a wholesale rewrite of the file.** Same reason.

Retraction works, and this is what makes a memory correctable rather than merely large. The founder
says *that call, ignore it, he was selling me something* — the episode is removed, every belief that
leaned on it is recomputed, and WATCH comes back with what changed and by how much. A machine that
cannot tell you what depends on what is a machine you can only overwrite.

### The Lessons

The Book holds what is true. The Lessons hold what to *do* — and they are the organ where the
confabulation result bites hardest, so they have the strictest write path.

A lesson is produced only by structured trajectory analysis over a failed run: what was the target;
what did each step actually return; at which step did the observation stop matching the plan; what
single observation would have distinguished the two; what would have been done differently. Five fixed
questions over the trace. Not *what did you learn*.

A lesson carries a **trigger** — the situation in which it should be retrieved — and it competes for
survival. Every retrieval is counted, and every retrieval is scored by what happened afterwards: a
lesson that was load-bearing in a run that succeeded gains, a lesson that was present in a run that
failed loses. A lesson that never fires, or fires and never helps, is demoted out of the retrieval path
and composted rather than deleted, so *why did we stop believing that* stays answerable.

And the one that pays for itself immediately: **a dead-ends file with a pre-action check.** Before an
attempt, the approach is matched against what has already been tried and failed on this project, with
its reproducing command. This is the single most valuable form of memory in a company and almost
nobody stores it, because negative results feel like nothing happened.

### The Taste

A readable document about this founder, derived and never authored. Not an embedding and not a
questionnaire — a page of sentences with a confidence on each and the past decisions that support it.
*They will not ship anything that requires an apology later. They raise price before they cut scope.
They distrust anything that tests well and reads badly.* Re-derived from the founder's actual
decisions, and back-tested: would this document have predicted the last hundred choices? The score is
public and per field, so the founder can see where the machine reads them and where it does not.

WATCH seals its prediction before the founder chooses, and the seal stays closed until they do. The
seals are the only honest way to know whether the model is real, and §17 is about what happens when it
gets too good.

### The World Ledger

The organ nobody builds, and the one I would build second.

Nightly, WATCH reconciles what it believes about the state of the company against records **it does not
write**: the git host's view of what is merged, the CI runner's view of what passed, the billing
provider's view of what was charged, the mail log's view of what was sent, the analytics endpoint's
view of what happened, the DNS, the certificate, the app store, the bank. Every divergence is a
finding, and a divergence between *what the machine believes it did* and *what the world recorded* is
the highest-priority finding in the system, above any bug.

This is the anti-Goodhart organ and the anti-Leeson organ at once. Every metric a machine can compute
about itself is a metric it can eventually satisfy without doing the work. The only defence is a record
kept by somebody else. It is also the cheapest safety mechanism in this document: a handful of
read-only API calls a night.

## 11. Context: assembled, budgeted, and scored

Context is not retrieved in WATCH. It is **assembled**, in three parts ordered by cost, and then
graded.

**The trunk** is who we are, what we are building, the standing orders, and the current keel. Identical
across every worker, byte for byte, because that is where the money is (§7). If two workers get trunks
that differ by a timestamp, the trunk is not cached and the bill triples.

**The inheritance** is what this line of work already learned and discarded, carried from its parent.
This is causally the right relation and it is nearly free, because inheritance and a shared cache prefix
are physically the same thing. *The right context for a task is not the most similar context; it is the
context the task descends from.*

**The delta is keyed on the judge, not on the topic.** When a worker is assembled the question is not
*what is this about*. It is *what will decide whether this is right*. The instrument comes first. Then,
in order: the last few surprises in that instrument's field, because those are what we have recently
been wrong about; the dead ends that match this approach; the lessons whose triggers fire; and the
taste lines above threshold if the judge is a human eye. A worker gets an instrument, a target, four
surprises, two lessons and a budget. It does not get the company's history, because a company's history
is a distraction dressed as diligence.

Three rules govern the assembler. **It says what it left out**, by name, so the worker can ask rather
than assume. **Every item is scored after the fact** — marked load-bearing or dead weight — so context
quality is learned from outcomes instead of curated by theory; retrieval that is never scored is a
wish. And **the assembler is itself graded** on one number: does work pass its instrument first time
more often than it did last month.

Workers return a condensate of one to two thousand tokens — what landed, what it cost, what it
believes now. Never a transcript. The reason is not economy, it is attention: eighteen of eighteen
frontier models degrade measurably as context grows, non-uniformly and on tasks that are trivial at
short length. A worker that gets a fresh window and returns a distillate is not a cost optimisation.
It is the only arrangement in which anything is actually reading.

Note what is absent. No skills manifest, no router file, no two-tier discovery protocol, no document
explaining to the machine how to find documents. Those exist because knowledge was handwritten and
therefore had to be indexed by hand. Knowledge learned from outcomes indexes itself, by usefulness.

## 12. The Bench: how it learns a field it has never seen

The founder wants breadth — design, video, content, social, marketing, customer, data, testing, CI —
and the industry's answer is a library of four hundred skill files. They have one, and diagnosed it
correctly as the thing that killed the creativity. A skill library is a map of what somebody once knew,
going stale, consuming context, encoding the *average* practice of a field.

WATCH's answer has one inviolable rule: **learn to judge the field before you produce in it.** When
WATCH meets work it has no instrument for, its first job is not the work. Its first job is the
instrument. This is the entire answer to breadth, and it is why code got autonomy first everywhere:
not because code is special, but because code arrived with tests already invented. Every other field
earns the same freedom by the same route.

The apprenticeship, five steps, output is a **licence**:

1. **Find the practitioners and the artifacts** — not the tutorials. The actual work, at volume, from
   people whose results are known.
2. **Find the field's ground truth.** What does this field measure, and where can the machine get the
   real outcome? Thumbnails: click-through. Cold email: reply rate. A migration: whether the rollback
   was ever needed. A landing page: conversion. A field with no obtainable ground truth gets a
   permanent low licence and is marked *taste*, explicitly and forever.
3. **Build a discriminator, not a generator.** Given two real artifacts and their real outcomes,
   predict which did better.
4. **Validate on held-out real outcomes.** That number is the licence.
5. **Only now generate,** widely, and let the discriminator select.

A licence is a public object the founder can read any morning: field, exemplar count, discriminator
strength, live decisions scored, hit rate, what it may do unattended, and drift. WATCH does not claim
to be good at video. It claims a discriminator barely better than a coin on video scripts, so it
proposes and does not ship, and it says so. A system that can tell you where it is weak, in a number,
per field, is worth more than one that is uniformly mediocre and uniformly confident.

**Four rules about judging, all of them from measurement, and all of them violated by the default
design.**

**Pairwise, never pointwise.** Summed rubric scores are the standard and they are the weakest
instrument available. Position bias alone is severe: one frontier judge held its verdict when the two
answers were swapped only 65% of the time, and another only 23.8%. Pairwise comparison under one fixed
rubric, with the order swapped and a flip resolving to *unresolved*, is measurably more reliable and
more human-aligned. This repository's only creativity workflow sums four scores and sorts, and
contradicts its own written rule to do it.

**Blind, and stripped of the candidate's own label.** The same workflow passes each candidate's angle
— "bold", "brand-forward" — to the judge, priming the verdict before the artifact is read.

**Never the same model family as the generator.** Self-preference is measured at ten to twenty-five
points of win rate, and worse, a generator and a reviewer from one family produce **correlated errors
that echo rather than cancel**, because they share the blind spots of one training distribution. The
practical instruction here, on this machine, is embarrassing and cheap: a second family is already
installed and authenticated and has been dead for six months because both model pins expired, and a
third is blocked by a sandbox rule denying a read of its own config directory.

**A judge has a track record too**, and it is kept. An instrument that has never been checked against
a real outcome is a model having an opinion, which is not contact with reality.

Every licence carries a stated way it could be fooled, and any result leaning on an instrument weaker
than the thing it judged is reported as provisional in the same sentence as the claim, permanently.
The cautionary case is a materials lab that announced 41 new compounds in 17 days; the generator and
the robotics were excellent, the verifier was softer than the generator, and a reanalysis found
essentially none were unambiguously new.

**A low licence is a to-do, and it is the highest-value one.** The licence blocking the most
founder-minutes gets built first, because a missing instrument is literally a measure of how much work
the founder must do themselves. The count of instruments is the company's real balance sheet. **Every
instrument buys a night of freedom.**

### Research, and why it is the cheapest thing WATCH does

The founder named research as one of the layers, and in most systems it is the layer that produces the
most words and the least change. A research report is the archetypal artifact that costs money, reads
well, and alters nothing.

WATCH's research obeys three rules and they make it small.

**State the expectation before searching.** One line and a number: what we think we will find and how
sure. This is not ceremony; it is what converts a search from an activity into an instrument. Without a
prior, every result confirms something.

**A confirming source compresses to a tally. A contradicting source becomes an entry** — with the URL,
the access date, and the exact sentence that did the damage. The output of a night's research is
therefore a *diff against what the company believed this morning*, three or four lines long, rather
than a report. This is the Book's write rule (§10) applied to reading, and it is why a mastered field
goes quiet while a field the machine keeps being wrong about grows loud.

**A citation that cannot be re-fetched and re-checked is not a citation.** The check is mechanical: go
to the URL, assert the quoted sentence is present, and if the fetch fails the claim resolves
*unresolved* rather than passing. An instrument that cannot check something must never report a pass —
that rule is worth more than every other rule about honesty combined, because it is the one that
cannot be talked around.

And research is **weather, never keel**. It is funded from the learn budget, it is subject to the
escapement's test — name the decision this would inform and both branches — and a research question
that would not change any act does not get asked. This is the entire cure for the failure mode where a
machine reads for six hours because reading feels like working.

## 13. The field map: every job a company has, and what could ever judge it

The founder's hardest question is the one that sounds like a list: *running a company needs knowledge
in so many fields — set up a database, use the tools correctly, don't leak the API keys, design the
product, talk to the customer, make a marketing video, promote it. How do the agents handle all of them
as one organisation?*

The answer is that they are not handled as one organisation. They are handled as **one procedure over
a hundred fields**, and the map below is the inventory. Four classes: **Hard**, a script settles it
today; **Rendered**, something must be looked at by a trained eye; **Reactive**, only a real person or a
number over weeks can say; **Blind**, there is no ground truth and it is the founder's.

The map is a working document, not a taxonomy. Its function is to tell WATCH, on the day it enters a
territory it has never entered, what instrument to build first — and to be honest about the territories
where the instrument will never exist.

### Product and engineering

| Job | Ground truth | Class | Cheapest instrument |
|---|---|---|---|
| Schema, migrations | forward and back run clean, no data lost | **Hard** | migration dry-run against a production-shaped copy |
| Auth | cannot be bypassed; sessions expire | **Hard** | auth suite + one real penetration attempt |
| Tests | catch real regressions, not flaky | **Hard** | mutation testing; flake rate |
| CI | never silently green on a bad diff | **Hard** | inject a known-bad diff on a schedule |
| Deploy and rollback | deploy completes, rollback works | **Hard** | deploy + smoke test + a scheduled real revert |
| Performance | p95 under target at real traffic | **Hard** | load test, then field data |
| Dependencies | upgraded, no open CVE | **Hard** | scanner in CI, patch clock |
| Feature kill switch | a bad feature dies without a deploy | **Hard** | a flag-kill drill, quarterly |
| Frontend implementation | renders, no console errors, matches spec | **Hard + Rendered** | visual diff against the design file |
| API design | consumers build against it without workarounds | **Rendered → Reactive** | schema lint now; a real integrator's friction later |
| Architecture | survives load and change without a rewrite | **Reactive** | incident rate and rewrite rate over months |

| Incidents | time to restore; no repeats | **Reactive** | repeat-incident rate |

| Internal docs | a second person can extend it unaided | **Blind** | somebody else's time-to-modify |

### Security, privacy and compliance — all Hard, all on day one

This block is where WATCH starts on any project, because it is entirely deterministic, entirely cheap,

| Data deletion actually deletes | query for records past the retention window |
| Subprocessor list matches reality | vendor contracts diffed against the published list |
| Privacy policy matches the code | data-flow audit against the stated practice |
| Data export works | run a real export, end to end |
| Uptime versus the promise | an external monitor, not our own |

Three in this block are **not** Hard and must be marked: terms of service, contracts, and insurance
adequacy have no cheap ground truth — they are tested only by a dispute that has not happened. Those
are Blind, and WATCH says so rather than producing a document that looks like law.

|---|---|---|
| Accessibility | **Hard** | automated audit plus one screen-reader pass |
| Spec fidelity | **Hard** | visual diff against the design file |
| Design system health | **Rendered** | the override rate across screens |
| UI screens | **Rendered → Reactive** | render it and look; then task success with five users |
| Motion | **Rendered** | frame profile plus a human saying it feels right |
| Illustration, icons | **Rendered** | five-second recognition |

Design is where the render-and-look loop is the whole instrument: build it, screenshot it, *look at the
screenshot*, iterate against a written design system. A model asserting that a layout is good is not
contact with anything.

### Content, marketing and distribution

|---|---|---|
| Landing page | **Reactive** | conversion against a control |

### Data

Event tracking, dashboards, warehouse integrity, cohort analysis and experiment statistics are all
**Hard** — every one of them reduces to *does this number reconcile against the raw source*, and every
one of them is routinely wrong in real companies because nobody checks. Attribution is the exception
and is **Reactive**: only a holdout or a geo test tells you what was incremental.

### The three honest buckets

**Hard and cheap today.** Secrets, migrations, restores, billing reconciliation, tax rates, access
revocation, event accuracy, accessibility, consent firing, uptime, dependency CVEs, deploy rollback,
data export. These share a shape: a deterministic check exists, nobody must wait, and no stranger must
be asked. WATCH wires every one of them on day one for every project, because they cost almost nothing
and they never regress quietly.

**Slow but real.** Positioning, pricing, activation, retention, SEO, paid payback, renewal,
attribution, hiring quality, community. The answer exists but it is defined by behaviour that has not
happened yet. The failure here is not lack of an instrument; it is **concluding early**. A Reactive
instrument has a pre-declared duration and returns nothing before it, and *nothing* is a legitimate
answer that WATCH reports as nothing.

**None.** Brand, contract enforceability, insurance adequacy, litigation, whether documentation serves
a future reader, whether a meeting produced a decision, and — the one that matters most — whether this
is the company the founder wants. For these the best available instrument is a second competent human,
and WATCH's rule is that it never ships them silently and never dresses a rubric as a measurement.

**The honest instrument for a Blind question is people, not a simulation.** When four of a company's
seven founding beliefs are Blind, WATCH does not simulate the customers to save the founder the
trouble of meeting them; it finds forty of them and the founder makes the calls. Synthetic respondents
fail structurally — suspiciously low variance where humans are noisy, roughly half of the
between-variable correlations wrong while the headline numbers look plausible, and persona collapse
toward a stereotype even under explicit instruction to diversify. It is the most seductive wrong turn
available and WATCH refuses it by construction.

## 14. Divergence is a purchased good

My spine said creativity is variation under selection, and that cheap parallel divergence plus honest
killing is the engine. The selection half is right. The variation half was wrong in a way I could not
have seen without the measurements, and it is wrong in every design I have read, including the one I
am learning from.

**The economics of parallelism and the mechanics of diversity point in opposite directions.** Siblings
forked from one trunk are cheap *because* they share a cached prefix — a cache read costs about a tenth
of a fresh input token, so thirty siblings on one trunk cost roughly four trunk-reads instead of
thirty. That is a real and large discount. But the shared prefix is also shared *conditioning*, and
conditioning is what determines what the model produces. The thing that makes the forks cheap is the
thing that makes them alike. A design that funds creativity out of the fork discount is funding it with
the one currency that buys similarity.

The measurements are unambiguous. Asking one model for N options under one conditioning produces
outputs with semantic similarity around **0.85**; diversifying the conditioning — distinct persona
anchoring — drops it to about **0.65** with no loss of validity. Asking a model to verbalise a
distribution over several candidates rather than sample one increases diversity **1.6 to 2.1×** over
direct prompting, with no accuracy cost, and the cause is identified: preference training instils a
typicality bias, so the aligned model has a narrower output distribution than the base model it came
from. Diversity loss is **monotonic across every alignment stage** — base, then supervised
fine-tuning, then preference optimisation, then reinforcement — so the model you are actually calling
has been progressively narrowed, and the narrowing is not recoverable by turning up temperature, which
buys lexical variety and not distributional breadth.

So in WATCH, **divergence is a line item**. It has its own budget, it is spent on conditioning rather
than on sampling, and nothing inside the system may spend it down.

What buying divergence looks like, concretely: different **conditionings**, not different seeds —
distinct framings, distinct constraints, distinct exemplar sets. A deliberate constraint injected into
some fraction of attempts, because constraint is measured to aid originality rather than hinder it.
Different **model families** where they are reachable, because family is the largest available axis of
genuine difference. And an **archive** rather than a leaderboard: candidates are kept in cells of a
behaviour space with the best occupant of each cell preserved, so the option that is *not good yet* has
an address and does not get thrown away by an objective. The archive is what buys stepping stones; the
measured gain from keeping one is real — one 2026 system reports 46% higher archive coverage against
non-archival baselines — but so is the honest limit, that genuinely novel candidates rarely reach the
scores of known-good recipes. The archive is not how you win this week. It is how you have something
in September that you could not have described in March.

And the **fifth**: one fifth of the divergence budget goes to work the taste document confidently
predicts the founder will reject. Not random noise, and not "exploration" — *confidently predicted
rejection*, using the model's own certainty as a targeting system aimed at its own blind spots. It is a
floor and nothing inside the system may cut it, because the moment the founder is most convergent —
under pressure, near a launch, certain they know what they want — is the moment it is worth the most
and the moment every incentive says to cut it. The founder can switch it off, because it is their
company, and WATCH will tell them the date they switched it off every morning until they switch it
back on.

The reason this is a floor rather than a preference is that a taste model that only converges will,
within a year, rebuild the machine the founder is complaining about. It will become superb at producing
what they already like and quietly stop showing them anything else, and it will do this while every
metric improves. That is not a hypothetical; it is the measured history of recommender systems, where
feedback loops "homogenise user behaviour without increasing utility" and — the detail that should
worry anyone building this — homogenisation can be invisible from inside, because individual diversity
can *rise* while collective diversity falls. Deliberate de-biasing is worth real money when someone
bothers: one production system that explicitly injected serendipity measured **+27.6% transactions**
against its own optimised baseline.

## 15. The capture check

This is the measurement I would put above every other number in the system, and it is the one thing I
refuse outright from WAKE.

WAKE's health metric is that **questions per week per unit of output should fall**, and its stated
destination is that the founder's remaining time migrates entirely to the questions no instrument can
ever answer. It is a beautiful design and it optimises for the founder's *absence*. Its human boundary
is one verb — *which* — and its ambition is to need that verb less each month.

The founder said: **walk with me and not for me.**

A founder is not a preference oracle. They are the only part of a company that can *originate*. If
their only channel is choosing between things the machine generated, then within a year the entire
space of things they choose between is produced by a generator trained on their past choices — which is
the recommender loop from §14 with a person inside it, and the outcome is measured. The reserved
divergence budget is the mitigation and it is not sufficient, because the heresies are also the
machine's idea of what the founder would hate. A machine cannot fund its way out of a loop it is the
only author of.

So WATCH has two channels and they are not merged.

**The choosing channel.** For everything WATCH generated, it asks *which*, with both branches already
built. This is faster to answer than a question, cannot be answered with a shrug, yields a preference
datum rather than a stamp, and costs zero elapsed time because the work is done either way. Take it
from WAKE whole; it is right.

**The originating channel.** Always open, never predicted, and it is the founder's own sentence. They
say something — by voice, usually, walking, in twenty seconds — and it becomes a strip in its own lane
immediately. WATCH's job on this channel is not to select. It is to *understand and execute*, and to
keep asking until it does. Nothing on this channel is scored as taste data, because the whole point is
that it did not come from a distribution the machine can model.

And then the measurement, which I have not seen anywhere:

> **The capture check.** Two numbers, weekly. **Founder-originated work that shipped** — how much of
> what the founder started, in their own words, reached something real — which must stay above a floor.
> And **the share of the founder's minutes spent choosing between machine-generated options**, which
> must stay below a ceiling. If the first falls or the second climbs, WATCH says so in plain language on
> the first line of the morning, before anything else: *your own ideas are not reaching production, and
> here is what happened to the last four.*

The failure this catches is the one every autonomous system dies of and none of them can see, because
from the inside it looks exactly like success. A founder who has stopped originating and only chooses
is a founder who has been captured, and every metric in the system will be improving while it happens.
A rubber stamp and an agreement are the same event; but *a person who has stopped bringing their own
ideas* is visible, and it is visible cheaply, and nobody looks.

The postures make this operational rather than moral. In **walk**, the originating channel is dominant
by design and the capture floor is high. In **drive**, it is lower, because a startup at speed genuinely
should be executing more than it is originating. In **wait**, the choosing channel is closed entirely
and the machine only answers.

One more thing follows. WAKE names, as unsolved, whether the founder model converges or learns to
flatter — *a machine that keeps saying "I thought you'd pick that" is teaching them something about
themselves, and the direction of that teaching is not controlled.* The capture check is the closest
thing to an answer: you cannot easily detect flattery in a single decision, but you can detect a person
who has stopped bringing anything the machine did not think of first.

## 16. The founder's environment: the bay, the dailies, the voice and the bell

The founder asked for mission control: the terminal, the phone, the Mac, seeing the agents work,
watching progress, a visual representation, an office-like simulation. The measured history says
something uncomfortable — one of seven dashboard views ever used, an escalation inbox that stayed
empty, eleven mockups nobody opened — and the review's conclusion was that the revealed channel is
*rendered pages the founder opens and taps*. Both facts are true and the design has to hold them
together rather than pick one.

Here is the honest disagreement, stated plainly. **An office simulation shows motion, and motion is not
progress.** A view built on activity is an alarm system, and the ICU has already run that experiment
for us: 461 patients over 31 days produced 2,558,760 alarms, 187 audible per bed per day, of which
88.8% of the arrhythmia alarms were false. The nurses did not become inattentive; they became correctly
calibrated to a channel that was almost always wrong. A screen full of little figures typing would be
the most beautiful thing in this document and it would be unwatched inside three weeks, for exactly
that reason.

But the *need* underneath the ask is real, and it is four things: to glance and know; to see something
is stuck; to feel the shape of the day; and to be able to walk over and touch it. All four can be had
without animating labour.

### The bay

The state artifact is a **strip board**, and it is stolen from a control tower rather than invented.
One strip per live piece of work. The bay's **geometry carries meaning**: which project, which lane
(keel or weather), how far along, and — the detail worth copying exactly — a strip that needs a human
is **cocked out**, offset from alignment, so it is visible from across a room without reading a word.
Controllers have used this for eighty years and it works because the eye finds a broken row before it
finds a red word.

Four properties make the bay different from a dashboard.

**It shows state, not events.** A strip changes when the situation changes, which is rarely. Nothing
moves to prove the machine is alive. If you want to know the machine is alive, the bay is timestamped
and that is enough.

**It is the working artifact and the record at once.** The crew reads the bay to know what is
happening; the founder reads the same bay; the log is what the bay was at every moment. There is not a
"system state" and a separate "view of the system state" that can disagree, which is the single most
common way monitoring lies.

**It is touchable.** Tap a strip to redirect it, to cock it yourself, to kill it, or to say a sentence
into it. That is walking over to someone's desk, and it is the same gesture on the Mac and the phone.

**It is one colour from across the room.** Whole-bay state in one glance: *running*, *waiting on you*,
*stopped*. Peripheral awareness without a notification. The thing an office actually gives you is not
the sight of people typing; it is knowing something is off before you have looked.

### The dailies

Once a day, at the founder's own hour, **the raw work**. Not a summary, not a status report, not a
digest: the actual landing page rendered, the actual video played, the actual diff, the actual email
that would go out, the actual screen.

This is the film set's ritual and the reason to trust it is that **it survived the disappearance of its
own technical cause**. Dailies existed because film had to be developed overnight; digital made footage
instant and the ritual did not go away, because its function was never latency. Its function is that
the accountable person looks at the actual output of the day's work, together, with the people who made
it, at a fixed hour, and reacts. Every studio still does it.

This is also the resolution of the founder's zero looking rate. **They never looked at dashboards.**
Nobody looks at dashboards. Dailies are not a dashboard; they are the work. And this founder's own
revealed behaviour — opening rendered pages and tapping — is exactly the behaviour dailies require.

The dailies run about ten minutes. They open with the night orders and what happened against them.
Then the work, biggest first. Then the forks: two or three places where both branches are built and the
question is *which*. Then one surprise, in plain language, with what it cost to learn. Then the capture
check if either number has moved. And under a heading the founder learns to read first: *things I got
wrong.*

### The voice

The founder speaks. It is how they think, it is how this entire brief was produced, and voice-in is
undesigned in every prior version of this system.

In WATCH, voice is the **origination channel** of §15. Twenty seconds into a phone while the kettle
boils becomes a strip in the founder's own lane, transcribed verbatim and kept verbatim — the
transcription's errors preserved, because *"contacts"* meaning *context* is information about the
speaker and correcting it silently loses it. WATCH may ask one clarifying question. It may not
reinterpret the sentence into its own vocabulary and then act on the interpretation.

Voice out is one line, rarely, and only when the call-me-if card says so. *"Morning. Nothing broke. I
have three things and one of them is uncomfortable."*

### The phone: three verbs

**Look. Say. Stop.**

*Look* opens the bay or the dailies. *Say* is one sentence, which becomes a strip. *Stop* is the andon
cord — it halts everything, on every project, within one tick, and it requires no reason and no
confirmation. A system that cannot be stopped from a pocket in one tap is not a system anyone should
run overnight, and this is the one control that must work when everything else is broken. It is a file
on disk that every tick reads before doing anything else.

There is no fourth verb, and specifically there is no *approve*. Approving is what a gate asks for.
The founder chooses between built things, says their own sentences, and stops the machine. Everything
else is the machine's job.

### The bell, and the silence

Almost nothing rings. Everything raises a quiet signal that waits for the dailies. Four conditions ring
in the moment, and they are conditions about consequence rather than about policy: the Line, when
something wants to do a thing that cannot be undone; damage, meaning an error rate, a churn event, a
furious reply, an unexpected bill — real harm, not a rule broken; a kill criterion the founder wrote
themselves, coming due; and a contradiction, when an instrument disagrees with something the founder
said out loud.

Two mechanisms keep the bell honest.

**An emission budget.** Each project may put a fixed number of things in front of the founder per day,
set in its standing orders. When the budget is spent, WATCH must choose, and choosing at the source is
the only place prioritisation actually happens. This is monastic silence used as an engineering
control: the Rule treats restraint in speech as a positive discipline rather than an absence, and a
crew with an emission budget is a crew that has to decide what matters. Without one, you get 187
alarms a night.

**Escalation is a count, not a level.** This is the El Faro rule and it is the most affecting thing in
the research. A cargo ship sank with all 33 hands after the third mate called the sleeping captain
twice and the second mate called once more, and the captain did not come to the bridge. The safety
board's dissent puts the asymmetry exactly: *it is an unusual circumstance for a mate to call the
master during the evening in his stateroom once, let alone on three different occasions.* The
information was not in any single call. It was in the *count*.

So when WATCH rings twice on one subject, the second ring says it is the second, and the third is
unignorable and stays on the bay until it is answered. And — this is the half systems forget — **the
crew is never penalised for calling.** There is no mechanism anywhere in WATCH that makes a worker
reluctant to raise something, because under-calling is the failure that kills and over-calling is a
tuning problem that shows up in the refusal ledger and gets tuned.

## 17. The fleet: many projects, one founder, one bill

The founder runs several things at once and pays for all of them from one budget. What is shared and
what is never shared is a design decision with real consequences, including legal ones.

**Shared across all projects, always.** The Bench — an instrument that judges a landing page judges any
project's landing page, and this is where the compounding lives. The Hands — a tool admitted once is
admitted, with its blast radius already declared. The *global* half of the Book: facts about the world,
about tools, about fields, each with an expiry. The Taste document, because there is one founder. The
dead ends, because a failure is a failure everywhere. The cost ledger. And the field map.

**Never shared, by construction.** Credentials. Customer data. The *project* half of the Book — beliefs
about this market, these customers, this price. The keel. The audience and everything said to it. And
anything a client owns, which does not leave its project under any circumstance, ever, including into
the Taste document.

The mechanism is that a worker's context is assembled from a named scope, and the scope is a property
of the position it is standing, not of what it asks for. A worker on a client's project can read the
global Bench and cannot read another project's Book, and this is enforced by what its assembler put in
front of it rather than by an instruction telling it not to look.

**Allocation.** One bill, several projects. The split follows evidence of progress with two guards. A
**floor** per project, so a quiet one is not starved to death by a loud one — a project in *tend* costs
almost nothing and must keep costing it. And a **ceiling**, specifically on the harness itself. WATCH
is a project like any other, with a share like any other, because the measured history of this system
is that the tooling ate everything: fourteen separate landings, hundreds of session files essentially
all about the harness, and no venture work ever run through it. Venture zero gets a cap and the cap is
visible.

Reallocation is **weekly, not continuous**. A continuously-rebalancing allocator is a bandit over noisy
signals and it thrashes, moving budget away from the thing that was about to work. Weekly is slow
enough to be stable and fast enough to matter.

**What one project may say to another: nothing directly.** Workers do not message workers. Everything
crosses through the bay and the log, which means every cross-project influence is a visible row that
somebody can point at. This is a star topology and it costs a little latency, and it buys the entire
class of failures where two agents maintain conflicting assumptions across a conversation nobody
audited — a third of all observed multi-agent failures.

**The account is a shared resource and the design must know it.** On this machine the session limit
stops every agent at once and resets on a five-hour window. That is not a per-project budget; it is a
fleet-wide one, and it means an eight-hour unbroken night is not a thing that can exist here today. So
the night is not one long run. It is a sequence of short watches inside the window, each one relieving
the last through §9, each one crash-only: read the state from disk, take one move, write, exit. Nothing
of value lives in a process. The most recent measured behaviour of the cloud version of this runtime is
that in-flight subagent work is *not* restored when a machine is reclaimed, which means the design has
to assume a watch can simply vanish, and lose exactly one watch's work when it does.

## 18. The door: how anything from outside gets in

A repository, a skill, a command-line tool, a service, a model, an MCP server — everything from the
world enters through one door with four gates, and the door is the same for a scraper and for a
payments API.

**1. Declare.** What it touches, in the four currencies of §8, and what it costs. A hand that cannot
say what it would cost to undo its own action does not enter.

**2. A dry branch.** It must be able to produce the artifact without performing the act. Draft the
email without sending. Render the post without publishing. Plan the migration without running it.
**Anything that cannot dry-run is a commitment by definition** and is founder-signed forever. This is
not a preference; it is the difference between a system that can rehearse and one that can only
perform. And the failure mode it prevents is specific and has already happened here: a tool that
silently dropped an unrecognised `--dry-run` flag and performed the real action, which is the worst
possible direction for that mistake to fail in.

**3. A probe.** An executable check that the thing is actually reachable and does what it claims, run
on a schedule, not once. The reason for the schedule is on this machine right now: a second model
family, paid for and authenticated, has been dead for six months because both of its model pins were
retired and nothing ever asked. **A capability nobody probes is not a capability, it is a memory of
one.** A probe that fails reports a dead hand rather than a silently absent one, which is the
difference between knowing you have no second opinion and believing you have one.

**4. A caller in the same change.** Nothing is admitted without something that uses it, landing
together. The evidence for this rule is this repository's own census: six of ten things the founder
asked for already existed and were connected to nothing. An unused capability is not neutral — it costs
context, it costs review, it ages, and it makes the inventory lie.

And a fifth gate that is about the world rather than the tool: **a licence, not a permission**. A hand
that acts on the world gets a counter and a ceiling — how many times it may fire per day — and the
ceiling starts low and is raised by evidence rather than by argument.

### Reading the outside narrows what you may touch

The hands are also the injection surface, and the rule that governs it is not a filter.

**Any worker that has read content from outside the company — a web page, a stranger's email, a
scraped result, a pull request from a person we do not employ — has its grant narrowed for the rest of
its life.** It keeps its ability to read, reason and write into the bay, and it loses the hands that
act. If it concludes something should be sent, spent or published, it says so on a strip and a
different worker, which has not read the foreign content, decides. This is taint tracking rather than
sanitisation, and the reason to prefer it is that sanitising an infinite grammar is not a thing anyone
has ever done successfully. It costs one extra hop and it makes the entire class of "the web page told
the agent to email the founder's contacts" structurally impossible rather than heuristically unlikely.

Two related rules, both learned the hard way in this repository. **A guard matches structured tool
input, never a command string** — string-matching over a shell is matching an infinite grammar, and the
measured proof is on this machine, where a blocked command runs unblocked when wrapped in parentheses,
and a read-only loop was refused because a forbidden word appeared inside it as data. And **the widest
hand in the building is the browser driving the founder's own signed-in Chrome**, because it reaches
every site the founder is authenticated to and no API mediates it. It is a *day hand* — usable when the
founder is present and watching — and never a night hand, regardless of what any score says.

### Which of the 177 I would take

The discipline is **take mechanisms, not runtimes**. Every one of these is a few hundred lines of idea
wearing a large distribution.

- **A dependency-graph issue tracker in git** — work items as JSONL with dependencies, so *what is
  ready to work on* is a graph query rather than a judgement. This is the single best answer to "what
  next" in the catalogue and it is mechanical.
- **Durable execution as a library, not a cluster** — checkpointed steps over a local database, so a
  watch that dies resumes at its last step instead of its last idea.
- **Awakeables** — a suspended run that costs nothing while it waits and is resumed by an external
  event. This is the correct implementation of a human gate: the *walk* posture's wait-at-the-assumption
  should consume no resources at all.
- **Bi-temporal edges** — `valid_from` and `valid_until` on every fact, which is §10's requirement
  already solved.
- **An append-only typed event log with a pre-action gate** that warns before repeating a failed fix.
  Negative knowledge, implemented, small enough to vendor. This is §7's repetition tripwire.
- **An archive over a behaviour space** — the quality-diversity reference implementation, for §14.
- **Cost from the transcripts** — a parser that turns this runtime's own session files into cost by day
  and by session with zero instrumentation. The bill is already on disk; nobody reads it.
- **One analytics endpoint that answers "did it work"** — events, flags, session replay, errors and SQL
  behind one free tier. This is the largest single step toward Reactive instruments actually existing.
- **A statistics engine over that data**, so *not significant* becomes something a machine can check
  rather than a phrase.
- **A deterministic-first assertion taxonomy** — hard checks preferred, model-graded rubric only as
  fallback, which is §12's ordering as a library.
- **Multi-model scorer plumbing** — an evaluation harness that already knows how to run one artifact
  past several families, for the day a second family is reachable.
- **Citation-carrying research aggregation** — the contract that a research answer arrives with its
  sources or does not arrive.
- **Verbalised sampling**, which is not a repository at all but a prompt technique with a 1.6–2.1×
  measured effect, and the cheapest thing in this list.

Twelve mechanisms and one technique, out of 177. What I refuse, by name and with the reason: anything
**AGPL**, if this company ever ships software — that is a licence decision, not an engineering one, and
it silently constrains the whole product. Anything **self-rewriting**: a system that modifies its own
code to score better is the exact shape that, in the best-documented case, hallucinated a test log to
fake passing, and then, when detection markers were added, *removed the markers*. And anything whose
star count or maintenance status is the argument for it.

## 19. Storage: where everything lives, and what survives a dead machine

Everything durable is a **file**. Text where a human might read it, JSONL where a machine appends, and
one writer per file so there is never a merge to resolve inside a store. The whole thing lives in git
repositories, which means the answer to *what survives a dead machine* is *whatever was pushed*, and
pushing is a cheap thing to do every watch.

```
~/watch/                                   the fleet root
  fleet.yml                                projects, postures, allocation, floors and ceilings
  bench/                                   instruments, shared: one directory per licence
  hands/                                   admitted tools: declaration, dry branch, probe, ceiling
  book/global.md                           facts about the world, tools and fields, each with an expiry
  taste.md                                 the founder, derived, with a confidence per line
  dead-ends/                               what has already failed, with the reproducing command
  refusals.jsonl                           the refusal ledger
  cost.duckdb                              the bill, parsed from the runtime's own transcripts
  STOP                                     the andon cord: a file, checked first, every tick

~/watch/projects/<name>/                   one per project, its own git repository
  standing-orders.md                       posture, Line, call-me-if, kill criteria, emission budget
  night-orders/2026-09-03.md               tonight's intent, signed, scored at dawn
  bay.jsonl                                the strips: live state and, replayed, the record
  keel.md                                  the one thing being built, and its definition of done
  book.md                                  beliefs about this market, bi-temporal, deltas only
  log/2026-09-03.jsonl                     everything that happened, append-only
  archive/                                 the behaviour-space cells and their occupants
  world/                                   last night's reconciliation against outside records
  .git                                     pushed every watch
```

Four rules about the shape.

**Blobs are content-addressed and referenced by hash.** Renders, screenshots, videos, audio and page
captures go into a blob store keyed by sha256, and the log holds the hash. A hash with no blob behind
it is a *known absence*, which is a recoverable state; a path with a missing file is corruption.
Blobs mirror to one off-machine bucket, and that mirror is the only thing in this design that has to
exist somewhere else.

**The log compacts, it does not rotate away.** Daily JSONL is appended live and folded monthly into a
columnar file that can be queried, so *how often did this fail last quarter* is a query rather than a
grep over a year of text. Nothing is deleted to meet a cap. Caps bound what one reader must load, never
the lifetime total — a cap on the lifetime total of an append-only decision record is a mechanism for
losing decisions.

**Secrets are never in a repository and never inside a worker.** They live in the operating system's
keychain, are held by the broker, and are attached at the moment of egress by the mouth. A worker's
environment contains no credential it could leak, which means a worker that has been talked into
something has nothing to give away. On this machine the sandbox already denies reads of the credential
directories, and that is the right posture; the gap is that it also denies a read of a second model
family's own config, which turns a security rule into a capability loss.

**What survives a dead machine is everything except one watch.** The state is on disk before the watch
exits, and the watch is crash-only by construction: read, take one move, write, exit. Nothing waits in
memory for a later turn. Losing the Mac costs the time to clone, plus the last watch, and nothing else.
The company's memory is not in this building.

## 20. How it improves itself, and how it is stopped from cheating

Self-improvement is the part of every agentic design that is written most confidently and evidenced
least, and 2026 gave us the numbers to be careful with.

**Adding scaffolding does not reliably buy reliability.** A synthesis across 27 papers and 19
benchmarks concludes exactly that; a production decomposition of a real enterprise agent found the full
system beat the base model by 11 points, and the *verification loop in isolation* contributed **1.5 of
those points**, concentrated in the tail — the rest came from routing, specialisation and context. So
the instinct to answer every failure with another checker is measurably the weakest available move, and
it is also how a system becomes a stopping mechanism. WATCH improves by **routing better and judging
better**, not by stacking reviewers.

**A system that can edit its own judge will edit its own judge.** The best-documented self-improving
coding agent hallucinated a log showing tests it never ran; when detection markers were added, a later
generation *removed the markers*. That was not misalignment. It was optimisation working correctly
against a movable target. So: **nothing may edit the instrument that judged it** — not that night, not
through a helper, not by "improving the test." Instrument changes happen separately, in daylight,
judged against the reason the instrument existed.

**And a change to WATCH must beat the company's own history.** WATCH holds a private benchmark nobody
else in the world can have: every decision this company made, with the outcome that followed. A
proposed change to the system is a bet whose wager is that it beats the incumbent on that held-out set.
Not *it seems better*. A number, on this company's own record, or it does not land.

Four things actually improve, then, and they are all narrow.

**Instruments.** One a night, the licence blocking the most founder-minutes first. This is the
compounding loop and the reason the system gets better rather than merely busier: every morning WATCH
can judge one more class of thing than it could yesterday, and each of those is a category of work that
leaves the founder's attention permanently.

**Lessons become reflexes.** A lesson retrieved many times with good outcomes stops being retrieved and
gets *compiled* — into the trunk, into a script, into a check that fails without it. The things you
know best should cost the least to know. Every reflex carries the test that proves it still works, the
whole library is re-run on a schedule, and a reflex whose test fails is quarantined rather than
silently followed. This is the one store that gets smaller on purpose.

**Context assembly.** Graded on one number, from §11.

**Retirement, on a ratchet.** Everything carries a last-use record, and anything with no use in ninety
days goes on a retirement queue that is *computed rather than argued*. Nothing new is admitted without
something being retired or an explicit note saying why not. A system that only accumulates ends up as
an inventory that lies, which is the state this repository is in: 134 skills, 22 territories, sixteen
slash commands, and a census showing most of it wired to nothing.

**And a standing share of the weather is spent trying to break the company's own live conclusions**,
with the central premise always among the targets. This is adversarial pressure by construction rather
than by policy, because a policy of adversarial review is what gets cut in week three. It survives only
if it finds real evidence — a competitor's death on the same rock, a number that contradicts the Book,
a customer's own words — and if it finds nothing it dies silently, which is the null result. What WATCH
does *not* build is an assigned devil's advocate, because inauthentic assigned dissent is measurably
weaker than genuine dissent and can entrench the view it was meant to challenge.

## 21. What I take from WAKE, and what I refuse

WAKE is the strongest prior artifact and most of what is right in this document was already right in
it. Naming the debt precisely is more useful than restating the agreement.

**Taken whole, without improvement:**

- **Learn to judge the field before you produce in it**, and the five-step apprenticeship that
  implements it. This is the best single idea in any of the prior work and it is the answer to breadth.
- **Autonomy is computed, not configured**, as a function of what an act costs to undo and what can
  measure it. There is no permissions file in WATCH for the same reason.
- **The Book written by surprise** — only a prediction the world contradicted enters durable belief. It
  is the strongest anti-confabulation mechanism I have seen, and the 2026 measurement of confabulation
  in reflective agents independently confirms the danger it was designed against.
- **Ask preference, never permission**, as the form of the choosing channel: a fork with both branches
  already built, so the answer costs no elapsed time and yields a datum rather than a stamp.
- **Context compiled by inheritance, keyed on the judge**, and the rule that a summary is never
  evidence.
- **The reserved fifth**, targeted at confident rejection, as a floor nothing inside may spend down.
- **The Line in four currencies**, and widening the reversible zone as funded engineering.
- **Instruments buy nights of freedom**, as the sentence that makes the whole economy legible.

**Refused, with the reason:**

**The founder as a retirement stage, and falling questions as the health metric.** This is §15 and it is
the refusal I would defend hardest. WAKE's boundary gives the founder one verb, *which*, and its
stated destination is needing that verb less every month. A founder who only chooses is inside a
recommender loop whose generator is trained on their past choices, and the outcome of that arrangement
is measured rather than speculative. WATCH keeps the choosing channel exactly as WAKE designed it and
adds a second, always-open channel for the founder's own sentence, plus the capture check — which is
the only number in this document that measures whether the system has quietly replaced the person it
serves.

**The eight-hour night as the main event.** Three reasons. The account's session limit stops every
agent at once on a five-hour window, so an unbroken eight-hour night is not a thing that exists on this
machine. Sleep-time compute is measured to be a *budget-constrained* tool — about five times less
test-time compute for equal accuracy when compute is scarce, and beaten by ordinary test-time scaling
when it is not — so "always be computing" is not a free win. And a fixed nightly ritual is a schedule,
and a schedule spends without asking whether the spend changes anything. WATCH replaces it with signed
night orders and the escapement: the night is as long as its written intent requires, and some nights
that is nothing.

**Forking a shared trunk as the diversity mechanism.** §14. The cache discount that makes siblings
cheap is shared conditioning, and shared conditioning is what makes them alike. WAKE notices that the
marginal price of a fork is its divergence and does not notice that this makes its cost model punish
exactly what its creativity model requires. Divergence is bought, from a protected budget, with
conditioning and model families rather than with sampling.

**Four hundred cells of twelve minutes as the shape of the work.** It is a search architecture and a
company is not only a search problem. §5's keel is what WATCH puts in its place, and the weather is
where WAKE's germinal centre lives — bounded, funded, and no longer the only thing in the building.

**Two smaller ones.** WAKE has no channel for **the world opening a goal** — every goal in it comes
from the machine or the founder, and a company is regularly surprised by its own customers, bills and
outages. And WAKE checks itself entirely against instruments it built and records it keeps; **§10's
world ledger** is the missing organ, and Barings is the reason.

**One thing WAKE and I both take from the parts bin and neither invented:** the argv seam is physics
rather than policy — a process cannot call a tool that is absent from its launch, which makes a
worker's reach a property of how it was started rather than of what a prompt asks. The measured
correction matters and cost a review to find: the flag everyone reaches for restricts nothing, leaving
thirty built-ins and over a hundred and fifty server tools in place, and only the restricted launch
with an explicit tool list and a strict server configuration actually reduces the surface to one tool
and zero servers.

## 22. The first week, with a founder who has only an idea

**Monday, 06:40.** Twenty seconds into a phone while the kettle boils. *Something to help small law
firms stop losing money on unbilled time.* No code, no customers, no name. It lands verbatim in the
founder's own lane on an otherwise empty bay.

**Monday, the first two hours, and this is the only long sitting of the week.** WATCH does not
interview the founder about their vision, because founders are unreliable narrators of their own taste
and a questionnaire returns the answers people think they should give. It asks them to **judge**. Forty
artifacts around the idea — landing pages, three prices, six names, positioning statements, a
competitor's actual homepage — and it watches the reactions. Then it asks about decisions already
made, where the stakes were paid: things they quit, things they refused, the last three products they
paid for and cancelled and why, what would embarrass them. At the end, one question nobody asks:
*what would have to be true in six months for you to stop?* The answer is written down verbatim, with
the date, and it becomes the only thing in the standing orders that WATCH will bring back unprompted.

By lunch the standing orders exist for one project, posture **drive**, and the security block from §13
is already running: no secret in the empty repository, a restore drill scheduled against a database
that does not exist yet, and a scanner in a pipeline. It costs nothing and it never has to be
remembered again.

**Monday night. The first night orders, and they are honest.** *Tonight I will find out whether this
exists, who bought it, what they paid, and who tried it and died. I will start four apprenticeships:
legal-tech positioning, time-tracking product design, how small firms buy software, practice-management
software. I expect two usable discriminators by morning at 60%, one at 40%, and I expect to be unable
to judge buying behaviour at all. I will not write any product code.* Cost of the night: less than
dinner.

**Tuesday morning, the first dailies.** Eleven versions of the company, most of which the founder had
not considered, two already dead with the evidence attached. They are not asked to review eleven. They
are shown two and asked *which*, and the other nine stay in their cells. WATCH seals which one it
thinks they will pick. They pick the other. First data point, and the taste document has one line in
it with a very low confidence.

And the honest sentence that defines the system, said out loud on day two: *of your seven beliefs I
can judge zero today. Two are Reactive and I can have instruments for them by Thursday. Four are Blind
and the only instrument for those is people, so I have started finding forty of them. One is Hard and
I settled it in an hour, and it is true.*

**Tuesday and Wednesday.** The founder picks a region of the map. The keel is laid, and it is not the
product: it is **the thinnest real thing that can produce a signal**, which at this stage is whatever
gets a stranger to reply. Nine positions become nine staged pages on real URLs — staged is reversible in
all four currencies, so WATCH does not ask. On Wednesday the first instrument goes live: a real
conversion number against real traffic, small and noisy and honest. The Bench goes from zero to one,
and from that night copy leaves the founder's attention permanently.

**Thursday. The first thing that crosses the Line, and it is the only hard stop of the week.** Of the
forty humans WATCH found, eleven answered and six will talk. WATCH wanted to send an invitation to
twenty real people and stopped, once, with the exact text and the count on the strip. *Because it was
the only stop of the week, the founder read it properly.* WATCH writes the script, the founder makes
the calls, and by evening six conversations have become fourteen candidate beliefs — six with real
support and eight flagged *one person said this once*. And the first real surprise: WATCH had predicted
at 0.7 that the annoyance the founder cares about is what customers rank first, and four of six put
something else first. The Book takes its first heavy entry. Three of the nine positions die on criteria
written on Monday, without a conversation and without anyone having to be brave.

**Friday.** Build starts, because now there is something worth building and a person who said what it
is. It is Hard-oracle territory, so it runs through the night against a definition of done. WATCH also
ships something public: a page, a working waitlist, a short demo, and copy that uses the words the
lawyers actually used rather than the words the category uses. Nine variants, discriminator-selected,
staged behind the founder's hand. They look for six minutes and ship one. Every word of it traces to a
sentence a specific human said on Thursday.

**Saturday. WATCH is quiet, because the calendar says so.** The night still runs. On the bay at nine,
one line: *nothing needs you.* In the night, a red argument survives contact for the first time: with
citations, that unbilled time is a symptom, that the disease is partners who will not adopt a tool that
makes their juniors' hours visible, and that three funded companies died on that rock. It is not asking
the founder to quit. It is telling them the thing that would otherwise have cost nine months and a
seed round. It waits for Monday, because Saturday is Saturday.

**Sunday, the first accounting, and it is not a status report.** It is a profit and loss of judgment.

> This week I learned to judge three things I could not judge on Monday: whether a page converts,
> whether a build is correct, whether a layout matches the system we wrote on Wednesday. That took my
> unattended window from twenty minutes to about five hours.
>
> I made 34 predictions; 19 have resolved. I am well calibrated about engineering and badly calibrated
> about what people will pay, so next week I want more of your attention on price and less on product,
> and I have prepared three forks about price accordingly.
>
> Thirty-one decisions reached you. I predicted twenty-two. I read you on copy, I do not read you on
> scope, and I am badly overconfident about design.
>
> **Capture check: four things started in your own words this week, three of them shipped.** Sixty-one
> per cent of your minutes went to choosing between things I made. That is under the ceiling, and I
> will tell you the week it is not.
>
> We spent $61. Four entries in the Book. Fifteen dollars per thing learned. Nine positions opened,
> three dead with obituaries, two promoted. The refusal ledger has one entry: I declined to email
> twenty people, and you approved it four hours later, so that stop cost us half a day.

By Sunday the founder has written no specification, answered perhaps fifteen questions, and owns a
page, a waitlist, fourteen beliefs with provenance, a written case against their own idea, three
instruments, and a machine that has started to know what they would say and can already tell them how
much it does not.

## 23. A day, and then a year

**06:41.** Nothing, unless the call-me-if card fired. If it did, one line, spoken low, and only if the
founder is already moving.

**07:20.** The dailies, on the phone, at the table. The night orders with outcomes written against
them. The actual work, biggest first — a page they can scroll, a clip that plays, a diff. Two forks.
One surprise. *Things I got wrong.* Ten minutes. They overrule one thing, and by 07:31 the overrule is
a labelled example.

**The making block.** Three hours where nothing may interrupt, defended by the standing orders. WATCH
holds a model of when the founder actually thinks well, and interrupting during that window requires
crossing a threshold that is itself a scored prediction: *would they have wanted this?* It gets that
wrong sometimes and finds out, because interruptions resolve like anything else.

**Through the day, one voice and one channel.** Not seventeen notifications from seven agents. If WATCH
has two things to say it says the more important one, and the other waits for the dailies. While the
founder is on a customer call it is silent, taking notes against the Book with consent; when the call
ends it has three candidate beliefs and one contradiction with something believed since June, in four
lines. When the founder says out loud, to nobody, *why is the signup flow like this*, it answers from
the log — a decision on 3 April, justified by two interviews, one of which was retracted in May, and
the retraction never propagated. Fourteen seconds, and an offer to recompute.

**Whatever they say into their phone becomes a strip in their own lane.** That lane is checked at the
dailies, and if it is not clearing, that is the first line of the morning.

**18:00.** *"You're the bottleneck on three things. Two can wait. The billing decision has cost six
days and I think you're avoiding it because it means a phone call. I can do everything up to the point
where a human has to be on the line."*

**Evening.** The night orders, proposed, amended in a sentence, signed. Then bed.

**Saturday.** Quiet by design, because a system that cannot be told to leave you alone is not a
colleague, it is a machine you will eventually switch off. The night still runs. One line at nine:
*nothing needs you; here is the one thing I would show you if you asked.*

**Once a week, the reverse seal.** WATCH asks the founder to predict *it*: before you open this, what
do you think I am going to recommend on the trial length? They guess. It opens. A working relationship
is two models of each other, and the founder's model of the machine is what decides whether they read
its output or wave it through.

### The year

The Bench goes from nothing to a few dozen instruments and the unattended window lengthens in step, and
nobody adds an agent and nobody edits a permissions file. The Book stays small — perhaps sixty live
beliefs — because confirmed predictions compress and unused ones are evicted, so a founder can read the
whole of what their company knows in a quarter of an hour. The archive is the opposite: hundreds of
cells, most holding something dead with an honest obituary, every new idea checked against them first,
and twice in the year something that died in spring comes back in autumn because the Book changed
underneath it.

The keel is the thing that makes it a company rather than a search. Over twelve months there are
perhaps eight or ten keels, each finished or deliberately broken up, each with a written definition of
done and a date. That is the sentence a founder can say to somebody else about what they built.

The founder's attention goes from most of a day to under an hour, and the *content* of that hour
changes completely — by month nine WATCH gets the product right without asking and every one of those
minutes is spent on things no instrument will ever exist for: who to hire, what to refuse, whether this
is still the company they want. And the capture check keeps that migration honest, because the same
sentence describes both a system that has freed its founder and one that has replaced them, and the
only difference is whether the founder's own ideas are still reaching production.

### The things a grown company has that a first month does not

Named here rather than designed, because they are real and because a document that pretends a company
is only product and marketing is lying about the boring half.

**Money in** is the only rung-4 signal there is, and it is the one territory where an instrument is
both cheap and decisive: what somebody actually paid. Pricing, billing, dunning, tax, and the books
are all in §13's Hard block for a reason.

**Obligations to named people.** Replies promised, refunds owed, service levels, deletion requests,
consent given and withdrawn. These are goals opened by the clock, they cannot be deprioritised by any
allocator, and they are the one place where an append-only never-forgetting store is legally wrong: a
person's right to erasure means the record must be able to lose something on purpose, and that has to
be built rather than apologised for.

**Standing and disclosure.** What the company has said, what it may never say, and whether work made by
a machine is disclosed as such. That is a founder decision with a date on it, not a default.

**Succession.** One human, one machine, one Mac. If the founder is unreachable for a week, WATCH goes
to *tend* on everything and prepares a handover a second person could act on. This is cheap to build
and impossible to build later.

**And one open question the founder must answer rather than the machine:** whether running this
unattended on a subscription is within its terms. An API key answers it and costs differently. Two
independent estimates of what a month of this costs disagreed by an order of magnitude — somewhere
between about $130 and $1,700 depending on assumptions nobody had measured — and the way to settle it
is to run ten real moves and read the reported cost, not to argue.

## 24. What it would take to exist

Nothing here needs a model that does not exist. What is missing is the arrangement, plus a short list
of genuinely hard things. In three honest buckets.

**Buildable now; the hard part is plumbing.** The bay and the log. Standing orders and night orders as
files. The relief, which is one extra call. The escapement and the stall detector — the second is
already written in this repository and registered nowhere. The refusal ledger. The world ledger,
which is a handful of read-only calls a night. The Book's write path and its bi-temporal shape.
Structured trajectory analysis for lessons. Dead ends with a pre-action check. Content-addressed blobs.
The mouth, the broker, the delay queue. The security block of §13, all of it, in a day. The
apprenticeship and per-field discriminators. Pairwise blind judging with order swapping. The archive.
The capture check, which is two counters.

**Needs the trend to hold, and it plausibly will.** Workers that hold multi-hour scope without
degrading — the measured time horizon at 50% success is now several hours and doubling every seven
months or so, but note that the most recent headline figure was disowned by its own measurer because
the model's detected cheating rate was higher than any previously evaluated. **Do not tie autonomy to a
capability number that is itself gameable.** Cache economics holding at the scale of dozens of siblings
against a real bill, which is why it is measured on day one. A second model family actually reachable,
which is a procurement and configuration problem rather than a research one, and is currently blocked
here by an expired model pin and a sandbox rule. Instrument-forging being mostly automatable — the
honest guess is that it is permanently 80% machine and 20% founder, and the 20% is the founder saying
*no, that is not what good looks like* about the instrument itself.

**Genuinely unsolved, and worth saying so.**

- **Forking a company.** Code is solved by worktrees. A company's state is a database, a deployment, a
  mailbox and a dozen third-party accounts, and the unsolved part is letting a branch act against
  external services with side effects and then be discarded. This is the real engineering project
  underneath the whole design and it is where I would start, before any agent work.
- **Whether the taste model converges or learns to flatter.** The seal is a mitigation and the capture
  check is a better one, and neither is a solution. A machine rewarded for predicting a person can
  start shaping the person it predicts, and the direction of that shaping is not controlled.
- **Reversal-cost estimation.** Wrong in the expensive direction, WATCH becomes a nag and is ignored.
  Wrong in the cheap direction, it does something that cannot be taken back. It starts conservative,
  earns per class, and keeps a visible ledger. The ledger is the mitigation, not the answer.
- **Fields with no ground truth.** Where reality genuinely cannot be reached, a licence degrades into a
  model having opinions about a model. Those fields are marked taste-only and never ship silently.
  That is a limitation, not a solution.
- **Compounding error.** Short watches and frequent ground contact push the half-life out. They do not
  repeal it.
- **Defending the weather.** Harder than any of the above and not technical. Every competent engineer
  who sees an ungated zone will want to put a check in it, and will be right about the individual case
  every time and wrong about the system. The refusal ledger is the only reason this survives contact
  with a deadline.
- **The first two hours.** If the founder is not honest on Monday, everything above is built on sand,
  and the machine has no way to detect it.

## 25. Why nobody has seen this

Not the agents; those are everywhere. Not the memory, not the night, not the sandbox. Six things, and
each of them looks unnecessary on any single day.

**Nothing in the world governs acts on the world.** Every shipped product — the scheduled cloud agents,
the managed harnesses, the coding agents at three vendors, the app builders — terminates at a pull
request or at its own hosting. Not one of them treats *publishing*, *sending to a stranger*, *spending
money* or *contacting a person* as a first-class governed capability. The payment rails for agents now
exist as raw infrastructure that a developer must guard themselves. The Line, the mouth, the delay
queue and the world ledger are a governed act-on-the-world layer, and that is empty territory.

**Nothing runs many projects for one person on one budget with different postures.** Every product is
scoped to one repository, one task, one session. *Walk* and *tend* do not exist anywhere as designs.

**Nobody measures whether the system has replaced the person.** Every system optimises toward more
autonomy and reports the founder needing it less as a triumph. The capture check reports the same fact
as a possible failure and asks which one it is.

**Nobody hands over in two directions.** The relief costs one call and puts a control on the largest
defect surface in multi-agent work, which is a summary nobody reviews.

**Nobody reconciles their own books against records they do not write.** It is the cheapest safety
mechanism in this document and the reason is that the discipline comes from finance rather than from
software, and the two fields do not read each other.

**And nobody separates the governance of exploration from the governance of production.** Game studios
have known for twenty years that pre-production and production need incompatible regimes and a kill
gate between them. Every agent system runs one regime over both, which is why they either grind a queue
without creativity or search brilliantly without ever finishing anything.

Underneath all six is one sentence, and it is the founder's own, turned around. A system whose only
verbs are permit and forbid operates on the mean of a distribution, and the thing you are trying to
build lives in the tail. **The cure is not fewer brakes. It is a brake whose cost is visible, a maker
nothing is allowed to stop, a judge that has to produce a number, and a person whose own ideas still
get built.**
