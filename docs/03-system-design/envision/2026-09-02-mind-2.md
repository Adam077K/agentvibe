## Spine, written before reading

*Written after reading only `00-BRIEF.md`. Nothing else had been opened. Never edited.*

**The name is CONTINUO.** In baroque music the continuo is the line that never stops playing. The composer
writes a *figured bass*: a bass note with numbers over it. The numbers fix the harmony and refuse to fix the
notes. The player realises them differently every night, in service of the same piece, and the realisation is
where the music actually lives. The founder writes figures. The system plays.

**The thesis.** Everything built here so far is a stopping mechanism because its primitive is a *task*, and a
task is a thing that can only be completed or blocked. A company's primitive is not a task. It is a **bet**: a
thing we believe, that costs something to test, that pays or does not, and that we are willing to be wrong
about in public. Work is not the primitive; work is what a bet emits when it needs evidence. Reframe the
primitive and every one of the founder's complaints dissolves at once — direction, token burn, creativity,
breadth, the difference between a startup that must move and a project that waits. You cannot get aimlessness
out of a system whose smallest unit is a to-do, no matter how many gates you bolt on. You get it for free from
a system whose smallest unit is a wager with a price.

**Six nouns, and no others.**

- **Bet** — a claim about the world with a cost, a horizon, an owner, and a kill condition. The company is a
  portfolio of live bets. Bets have parents. The root bet is the founder's reason for existing.
- **Evidence** — anything that moves a bet's odds: a shipped feature, a customer sentence, a benchmark, a
  refund, a rendered frame someone hated. Evidence is the only thing that closes a bet.
- **Move** — one bounded act that produces evidence. A move has a price in tokens and minutes, an expected
  information gain, and a blast radius. Moves are what agents run.
- **Hand** — a capability that touches something outside the repo: a browser, a card, a domain, an inbox, a
  render farm. Hands are scarce, named, scoped, and logged. Agents are cheap; hands are not.
- **Belief** — what the company currently holds true, with provenance and a decay date. Beliefs are written by
  consolidation, never by an agent mid-task.
- **Mandate** — the founder's standing instruction for one concern: how hard to push, what may be spent, what
  may never be touched, and whether to move without asking. The mandate is the figured bass.

Everything else — roles, playbooks, phases, tiers, org charts — is a **lens**, not a noun. A lens changes how
a move is performed and never what exists.

**How it decides what to do next, and when to do nothing.** Not a loop. A **standing auction**. Every live bet
continuously proposes moves. Each proposal carries a price and an honest estimate of what it would tell us.
A cheap, constant appraiser ranks them by *information per token against the mandate's appetite*, and the
budget clears the market. Most ticks clear at nothing: the top proposal is not worth its price, and the system
sleeps. That is the answer to *"I don't want it to burn tokens without meaning"* — meaning is a price signal,
not a rule. A startup concern is given a high appetite and a standing licence to act, so its auction clears
often. A project the founder wants to walk beside is given an appetite of nearly zero and a rule that the only
moves that clear are ones the founder just asked for. Same machine. One number.

**Relentlessness is not a loop; it is a bet that refuses to close.** A goal the founder cares about is a bet
with no expiry and a kill condition only they can sign. It will keep proposing moves, get outbid, wait, and
propose again cheaper — for weeks. That is what walking relentlessly toward a goal looks like from the inside,
and it costs nothing while it waits.

**Memory has four rooms and a night.** *Episodic*: what happened, append-only, every move and its outcome.
*Semantic*: beliefs. *Procedural*: what we learned to do — a skill is a hardened memory. *Negative*: what
failed, what we decided against and why, what we must not do again. The fourth room is the one everybody
skips and it is the expensive one, because without it a system re-buys the same lesson forever. Between days
there is a **consolidation**: the system re-reads its own episodes, compresses them into beliefs, kills dead
bets, opens new ones it noticed, rewrites the prompts and tools that performed badly, and leaves a one-page
morning brief. It learns while the founder sleeps, or it does not learn.

**Context is a purse, not a search.** Right context is not similarity retrieval. Each move is dispatched with
a **purse** assembled by a policy that is itself measured: these beliefs, this negative memory, this exemplar,
this file. Every item in a purse is stamped, and when the move lands we score whether the item was ever read
and whether it helped. Items that never help stop being packed. Context selection is the most under-measured
loop in every agent system alive, and it is where all the money goes.

**Creativity is variance plus selection, and playbooks killed it by removing variance.** So: for anything that
matters, run *k* sealed attempts that cannot see each other, from different framings, and judge them against a
standard. Standards say what good is; they never say how. The system holds **taste** — exemplars, references,
things the founder loved and hated — as a first-class asset alongside code. A method is never written down. A
standard always is.

**It is an organisation, and the org chart is not the abstraction.** The durable structure is the list of
things a company must do or die: be found, be wanted, be paid, be built, be kept working, be legal, be
solvent, be liked. Each is a **function** that owes evidence on a clock. Agents are summoned per move with a
lens, not employed. There is no CTO. There is a bet that our architecture will hold, and it emits moves.

**The founder's surface is a bridge, not an office.** An office simulation with little avatars is decoration;
what a founder needs at 2am on a phone is four things: *is anything broken*, *is anything waiting for me*,
*what changed while I slept*, *what will it do next and may I stop it*. One screen, three verbs — **watch,
answer, stop**. The terminal is for when the founder wants to be inside the work; the phone is for when they
want to be above it. Both read the same event stream. The system talks to the founder in **their** currency —
money, customers, risk, days — and never in mine.

**Hands are the only real security boundary.** Prompts are not a boundary and reviews are not a boundary. Three
classes are irreversible: **money**, **identity** (anything that speaks as the company), and **publication**
(anything the world can see). Every hand in those classes is narrow, single-purpose, logged, and rate-limited,
and the founder's approval is attached to *the exact bytes* being sent, not to a category. Everything else the
system may do freely and undo.

**Persistence.** The repository is the body; the event log is the life. Both are plain files, both replicate,
and any machine that clones them wakes up as the same company mid-sentence. A dead Mac costs an hour.

**The mistake I think everyone else is making,** including whatever I am about to read: they are designing a
better *harness* — more agents, more gates, more phases — when the missing thing is an **economy**. A company
is not a pipeline with checks. It is scarce attention allocated against uncertain bets, with a metabolism that
runs day and night and a memory that compounds. Build the economy and the org chart becomes an implementation
detail. Build the org chart and you get a beautiful machine that stops.

## 1. What reading changed, and what it did not

*Written after the prior systems and the world. The spine above is sealed; this is the honest accounting against it.*

**The spine held on six points, and I did not expect it to.** WAKE — round one's merged answer, built by three minds who could not see each other or me — independently arrived at: work as a wager with a stated probability rather than a task; kill criteria written on the day a bet opens; memory that must be earned rather than accumulated; irreversibility as the one line; the founder choosing between built things rather than authorising unbuilt ones; and a reserved budget for being wrong. Four minds, four seals, one shape. That is the strongest evidence available that the shape is right, and I take it rather than reinventing it. Where WAKE said a thing better than my spine did, this document uses WAKE's word: **ply** for the forked unit of work, **the Line** for irreversibility, **the Book** for beliefs written by surprise, **the Bench** for instruments, **the fifth** for the heresy budget.

**Three things in my spine were wrong or under-thought, and I say so here.**

*My six nouns were seven.* I had "Bet" and "Move" as separate primitives. They are one thing at two scales, which is exactly what WAKE found when it collapsed bet, fork and task into the ply. A pricing hypothesis and a landing page are the same object at different radii. I fold them.

*I wrote "the standing auction" as if the clock were mine to choose.* It is not. This machine has a measured metabolism and I had not measured it. The account's session limit **stops every agent at once and resets on a rolling five-hour window**. That single fact reshapes the whole design and is the subject of §5. It also converts my central argument from an analogy into a mechanism: I claimed work should be priced because attention is scarce, and it turns out the scarcity is literal and rival — the founder and every agent drink from one cup.

*I said "the mistake everyone else is making is designing a harness instead of an economy," and I was two-thirds right.* WAKE is not a harness. It is a metabolism with a memory and a judgement layer, and it is better than what I had. What it does not have is an economy: nothing in WAKE decides *whether a night is worth running*. Its night is unconditional, its germinal centre always forks, and there is no representation anywhere of a project that should sit still. The founder asked for exactly that and asked for it twice. So the criticism survives, narrowed and sharpened: **WAKE knows what it may do and never asks what it is worth.**

**What I refuse from WAKE, in one sentence, and it is not a small thing.** WAKE makes the Understudy — its model of the founder — an instrument on the Bench whose calibration *licenses the system to act alone in taste domains*. I refuse that. A model of a person, rewarded for predicting that person, will shape the person it predicts; WAKE says so itself in §19 and then builds on it anyway. In CONTINUO the model of the founder may decide **what not to ask**. It may never decide **what to do**. Predicting taste buys silence. It never buys action. §11 argues this properly.

**What the world added that neither of us had.** Seven mechanisms from outside software, none of which a software designer reaches for: the Night Order Book, written fresh each evening and *signed by the person taking over* (a ship); the single-parameter override, where one dimension scoring high escalates regardless of the aggregate (NEWS2); the **do-not-wake list**, which is harder to write and worth more than the escalation list; the cocked-out strip, where state lives in an artifact's *position* and sends nobody a message (air traffic control); the pre-trade hard control that must **reject** rather than warn and must sit outside the trader's reach (SEC Rule 15c3-5, written in blood after Knight Capital lost $440M in 45 minutes); the dog watch, an odd rotation so nothing permanently owns the graveyard slot; and *calmly expose the difficulty* — a bounded refusal protocol where a worker may object once, in a defined form, and then complies (Rule of St Benedict, ch. 68). All seven are in the design below.

## 2. CONTINUO

**The year is 2026 and the world is this one.** Nothing in this document needs a model that does not exist, a price that has not been published, or a capability nobody has shipped. It needs an arrangement. Where a part is genuinely unsolved I say so and name it, in §19, rather than letting it hide inside a diagram.

**What the name means.** In baroque music the continuo is the line that never stops. Under everything else — the aria, the solo, the silence between phrases — two players hold the piece together: one sounding the bass, one filling in the harmony above it. The composer does not write their notes. The composer writes a bass line with **figures** over it: numbers that fix the harmony and deliberately refuse to fix the notes. The player realises them differently every night, in service of the same piece, and the realisation is where the music actually lives.

That is the whole design in one image, and every part of it is load-bearing.

- The founder writes **figures**, never notes. A goal, a constraint, a standard, a price. Never a method. This is the answer to *it lost creativity to playbooks*: a playbook is somebody writing out the notes for a part they will not be playing.
- The line **never stops**, and it is also **frequently silent**. A continuo part contains rests, and it contains whole movements marked *tacet* where the player sits with their hands in their lap. Silence is written into the part. It is not a failure of the player.
- There are **two players on one line.** One plays what must be played — the bass, the obligations, the things the world is owed. One invents above it. Both are the continuo. A company is exactly this: the part you owe and the part you invent, sounding at once.
- And a continuo player is not a soloist. Nobody claps for them. They exist so that the person out front can be free.

The system is called **CONTINUO**. It runs one founder's ventures, day and night, from one machine, on one budget, and it is silent most of the time.

## 3. The picture, in five sentences

CONTINUO is a company that exists as a portfolio of live wagers, worked by short-lived processes that are summoned for one question and die, under a budget so scarce that the founder and the machine drink from the same cup.

Five times a day the cup refills and CONTINUO holds an auction: every live wager offers the cheapest thing it could do next and says what that would teach, a price is put on each, and the budget clears the market — which on most ticks means it buys nothing and goes quiet, because silence at the right price is the correct output of a healthy system.

It never asks permission for anything it can undo, and it never once does alone anything it cannot; the boundary is computed from what a thing costs to reverse in money, time, reputation and law, and widening the reversible side of that boundary is itself funded engineering work.

It learns by writing down what it expects before it acts and keeping only the times reality disagreed, so its memory shrinks in the domains it has mastered and grows loud exactly where it is still stupid, and it can hand the founder a number for where not to trust it.

And it holds a model of the founder that is allowed to decide what not to bother them with and is never, under any calibration, allowed to decide what the company does.

## 4. The primitives

Six nouns. Everything else in this document is one of these six seen from an angle, and if a seventh appears later I have made a mistake.

| Noun | What it is | Where it came from |
|---|---|---|
| **Ply** | A forked world holding one wager: what we believe, at what probability, what it costs to undo, what will judge it, what it may spend, what would kill it, and — at the end — what happened. A feature branch is a ply. A price is a ply. *Whether this is a company at all* is a ply. | WAKE §6, taken whole. My spine called this a "bet" and was describing the same object at one scale. |
| **Contact** | Any event where something we made met something we do not control: a test that ran, a screen someone looked at, a stranger who replied, a number that moved, a card that was charged. Contact is the only thing that closes a ply. | WAKE's Law 2. STARTUP-OS called it an *exposure* and built a register for it; I take the register. |
| **Instrument** | Anything that can tell better from worse without the founder, carrying its measured strength and its stated way of being fooled. A test. A render actually looked at. A conversion rate with a pre-declared clock. Twelve real humans. | WAKE's Bench, taken whole, with one addition in §10: instruments may be **borrowed** from the world before they are built. |
| **Hand** | A capability that touches something outside the repository: a browser, a card, a domain, an inbox, a render farm, a deploy. Hands are named, narrow, metered, and held by one process that contains no model. | STARTUP-OS's *mouth* and *hand admission test*, which are the best things in the parts bin. |
| **Figure** | The founder's standing instruction for one concern: its posture, its appetite, its ceiling, its refusals, and the sentence that would make them stop. Constrains the harmony, never the notes. | Mine. It is the thing WAKE has no representation of, and it is what §5 and §6 are built on. |
| **Belief** | What the company currently holds true, bound to the prediction that would kill it, carrying provenance down to the episode beneath it, and written only when reality contradicted an expectation. | WAKE §8, taken whole, including the write rule, which is the best single idea in that document. |

And one non-noun, stated so nobody promotes it: **there are no roles.** No CEO agent, no CTO, no marketer, no roster, no shim files holding names that resolve to nothing. Domain expertise is a lens compiled into a context for the duration of one ply. Work is done by processes that are spawned for one question, given exactly what they need, and die. Nothing that matters lives inside one of them. WAKE, VIGIL and PLY reached this blind and independently; this repository reached it too, the hard way, collapsing twenty-one named roles into seven engines and then discovering that even seven was five too many.

### 4.1 The anatomy of a ply

Seven fields, and a ply that cannot fill all seven is not dispatched.

| Field | What it is |
|---|---|
| **parent** | The ply it forked from. It inherits that ply's context copy-on-write, which is why provenance is free here: it is just the tree |
| **wager** | What it believes, with a probability, so it can be *wrong* rather than *vague*. At company scale the wager's negation is written out as a pre-mortem: assume it failed, write the autopsy, derive the kill criteria before anything starts |
| **undo** | Reversal cost in four currencies — money, time, reputation, law |
| **judge** | The instrument on the Bench that will score it, **named before work starts.** A ply whose judge is chosen afterwards is a ply that will find the judge that likes it |
| **budget** | Tokens, minutes, and its share of the cup |
| **death** | The condition under which it stops. Unawareness of termination is 12.4% of multi-agent failures on its own, and a ply that cannot state a death condition is not stuck — it is unformed |
| **outcome** | At retirement: committed, or discarded with an obituary saying what was believed, what was spent, what killed it, and what would have to be seen to try again |

### 4.2 How a ply is worked

By a process that is **short, mortal, and has no identity.** No name, no memory of its own, no career, no continuity between plies. It reads the compiled context, does one thing, writes one artifact, and dies.

**Short is not a preference; it is arithmetic.** Agent success over long tasks is well modelled by a constant per-minute hazard, which makes it decay *exponentially* with duration — a long unsupervised chain is not slightly worse than a short one, it is worse by a power law. The measured shape today is near-certain success under four minutes and worse than one in ten past four hours. So a tide is many short plies with a machine-checkable scorer between each, and **a restart goes back to the last verified checkpoint, never to wherever the reasoning had drifted.** The tide is long. No ply is.

**A ply returns a condensate, never a transcript** — one to two thousand tokens of what landed and what it cost. The thing that reads it is the most expensive attention in the building, and the summary has no standing as evidence: a ply that actually looked at the artifact outranks anything that read a summary of it. This repository learned that lesson twice in one week, both times by an orchestrator's synthesis quietly losing a number that a worker had measured correctly.

**And a ply forks a worktree, not a world.** Code branches perfectly; the world does not (§19.3). Under this machine's armed sandbox, creating that worktree is the one command that needs an escalation, and it is named, audited and logged rather than discovered.

### 4.3 How they talk to each other, which is barely at all

The founder asked about agent communication and communication between orchestrators. The answer is deliberately thin, and the thinness is the design.

**A star, never a mesh.** A ply's only outputs are its artifact and its rows in the record. It cannot message another ply. Reaching another ply means writing something a dispatcher reads and can refuse. This is not fastidiousness: **37% of multi-agent failures are inter-agent misalignment**, and the coordination problem is solved here by not having one — plies do not hand off, do not negotiate, and cannot maintain conflicting assumptions across a conversation, because there is no conversation.

**Three shapes are permitted to travel upward, and no others.** A **condensate**, which is what landed. A **difficulty** (§6.4), which is one bounded objection. And a **finding**, which is what a judge returns — findings, never scores, because a score is a finding with the information removed.

**Anything that does travel is read back before it binds.** The receiver restates the instruction in its own words first. This is the discipline that took a clinical handover protocol to a 23% reduction in errors across nine hospitals, and its whole content is that the *receiver* closes the loop rather than the sender assuming it landed.

**And the honest caveat, because it is measured and it is uncomfortable:** fact 6 says the messaging socket and its token are exported to the shell environment, so any process holding a shell can in principle reach any other. **Peer isolation here is a convention the processes keep, not a rule anything enforces.** The rule that *is* enforced is the one that matters — no ply holds a credential, and the mouth accepts only a signed instruction — so the worst a rogue peer message can do is waste a slot. That is the right thing to be exposed to, and pretending the isolation is structural would be the kind of claim this document exists to avoid.

## 5. The cup: why this system prices everything

### 5.1 The eleven facts this design obeys

A design that has not measured its own machine is a wish. Eleven things were measured on this Mac and this runtime on 2026-09-02, and each one changes something below. I did not measure them; they are the ground the previous rounds left, and I treat them as load-bearing rather than as trivia.

| # | Measured | What it forces |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | `--allowedTools` restricts **nothing**; `--tools Read` still leaves 175 MCP tools present | Capability must be set by `--restricted --tools <list> --strict-mcp-config`, and the seam must be tested, not asserted (§12) |
| 2 | `--strict-mcp-config` makes user-scope MCP servers **absent**, not denied | Absence is physics; denial is policy. Build on absence (§12) |
| 3 | A child inside a sandboxed session **cannot start its own sandbox** | Isolation is one level deep. Plies nest logically, not in containment (§12) |
| 4 | `pre-tool-use.sh` fires under `claude -p`; the hook matches command **strings**, and once blocked a document for *mentioning* a command | String matching is not a security boundary. It is a spell-checker with opinions (§12) |
| 5 | A `claude -p` child binds an inbox socket regardless of `--tools`; only `--bare` skips it, and `--bare` never reads OAuth | You cannot have both no-inbox and authentication. Pick, and know which (§12) |
| 6 | The messaging socket and token are **exported to Bash** | Any process holding Bash can talk to any other. Peer isolation is a convention, never a guarantee (§12) |
| 7 | `launchd StartInterval` counts from launch while the prompt cache expires 5 minutes from write, so a 300-second tick **deterministically misses cache every time** | The tick is 240 seconds, or the cache economics of §9 evaporate |
| 8 | A `spawn(timeout)` killed the child at ~1s while its grandchild ran to 6s; only `detached:true` plus `process.kill(-pid)` killed both | Every budget ceiling must kill a process **group**, or the ceiling is decorative |
| 9 | Over 35 days and 2,925 transcripts: **output tokens are 11% of cost. Cache reads are 57%. Cache writes are 32%** | The money is in what you *carry*, not what you *say*. This inverts the usual cost advice (§9) |
| 10 | `git worktree add` **cannot complete anywhere** under the armed sandbox: exit 128, 32 denials on agent-config paths | Code forking needs one escalated command, named and audited, or it silently half-works |
| 11 | **The account's session limit stops every agent at once and resets on a rolling five-hour window** | Everything in this section |

### 5.2 One cup

Fact 11 is the most important sentence anyone has written about this system, and both prior rounds recorded it as an operational annoyance rather than as the architecture.

There is one quota. It is not per-project, not per-agent, not per-lane. Every process CONTINUO spawns, and the founder themselves sitting at the terminal at nine in the morning, draw from the same five-hour rolling window. When it runs out, **everything stops at once** — the night's work, the research, the founder's own session, all of it, simultaneously, until the window rolls.

Every other agent system in the world treats tokens as an external cost: a bill that arrives later, paid by someone who is not in the room. Here the cost is *internal and rival*. A ply that runs at 3am is not spending money the founder will see next month. It is spending the founder's own hands at 8am. This is not a metaphor and it is not a budget policy. It is the physics of the account.

Three things follow immediately, and they are the spine of the whole system.

**First: everything must be priced, because everything competes with the founder.** Not priced in dollars — priced in the only currency that is actually scarce. A ply that costs a fifth of a window has to be worth more than a fifth of a morning. Most are not. That is the honest answer to *I don't want it to waste tokens and run loops without any meaning*: meaning is a price signal. You cannot get it from a rule, because a rule cannot compare two things it has never seen; a price can.

**Second: the reserve is a first-class quantity.** CONTINUO holds back a share of every window against the possibility that the founder wakes up and wants to work. The reserve is set by the founder, in the figures, and defended absolutely: no ply, no deadline, no crisis may spend into it. And when the machine declines to act because the reserve would be breached, it says so in those words — *I stopped at 61% because you usually start around now* — because a machine that goes quiet without saying why is indistinguishable from one that is broken.

**Third: doing nothing is a purchase.** When the auction clears at nothing, CONTINUO has bought something real: the whole window, held for the founder, at the cost of no work done. That is often the best trade available and it should be reported as a decision, with its reasoning, rather than as an absence. The founder's complaint about systems that burn tokens without direction is precisely a complaint about systems in which *not acting* has no representation and therefore never happens.

### 5.3 The tide

CONTINUO's clock is not the sun. The sun is a human romance imposed on a machine whose actual metabolism is a rolling five-hour quota, and WAKE's beautiful 22:00-to-06:00 night cannot survive contact with fact 11: it would burn its allowance somewhere around 01:30 and stop dead, with the founder waking to a half-finished germinal centre and no morning quota either.

So the unit of time is the **tide**, and there are four or five of them a day. Each has three phases, and the phases are not decoration — they are ordered by data dependency, which is the one thing WAKE got right about its night and stated as its own contribution.

**Flood** — the tide comes in with a fresh quota. The auction clears (§5.4) and the winners run. This is where nearly all the tokens go. Plies fork from a shared trunk, run short, and return a condensate rather than a transcript.

**Slack** — the water stops moving. Nothing is dispatched. Every ply that reached contact is judged by the instrument named on the day it opened; every prediction whose clock has run is scored; the founder's decisions since the last tide are recorded as preferences. This phase is cheap and it must not be skipped, because a system that generates faster than it judges is manufacturing unlabelled data.

**Ebb** — the tide goes out. What the tide learned is written down: contradicted predictions become beliefs, confirmed ones compress to a tally, rent is collected on every memory that was loaded, dead plies get obituaries, and the next tide's candidate list is prepared so that the flood does not spend its first minutes thinking. Then quiet.

Four consequences worth naming.

*The tide with no founder in it is different in kind, not degree.* Its advantage is not more hours; it is that **nobody can be asked anything**, which changes what work is possible. That is WAKE's insight and it is correct. But it is now one tide of five rather than the entire architecture, and it is the tide where the fifth (§11) is spent, because heresy is best committed when there is nobody to reassure.

*Rotation is odd on purpose.* Five tides against a 24-hour day means the phase drifts: the tide with no founder in it is not always the same clock hour, and a concern does not permanently own the dead slot. This is the **dog watch** — a ship deliberately splits one watch in two so the number of watches per day is odd and nobody is condemned to the middle of every night forever. Applied here it means no venture is always worked when the founder is asleep and never when they are awake, which would otherwise silently determine which ventures the founder actually sees.

*A tide that is missed is not replayed.* If the machine was asleep, the quota rolled anyway. CONTINUO does not run four tides back to back to catch up; it takes the tide it has. `launchd` coalesces missed `StartInterval` firings into one wake-up, which is the correct behaviour and happens to be what we want.

*The tick is 240 seconds, not 300.* Fact 7. A five-minute tick against a five-minute cache TTL misses the cache on every single firing, which given fact 9 means paying the 32% cache-write share of the bill forever and never collecting the 0.1× read. Sixty seconds of slack is the difference between an affordable system and one that is not.

### 5.4 The auction

At the head of each flood, CONTINUO holds an auction. This is the whole of "how does it decide what to do next," and it is four steps.

**Step one: every live ply offers its cheapest next move.** Not its plan. Not its roadmap. The single smallest thing it could do that would produce contact, stated with three numbers: what it costs (tokens and minutes), what it would tell us, and what it costs to undo. A ply that cannot name a next move under a cost is not stuck; it is *blocked*, which is a different state with a different remedy (§6.5).

**Step two: each offer is priced.** The price is expected information per unit of cup, against the concern's appetite:

```
value(move) = P(surprise) × load(the belief it tests) × appetite(concern)
                                   ÷  cost(move)

  P(surprise)  the ply's own stated probability, taken at its face and
               later scored — a move whose outcome we are 95% sure of is
               nearly worthless whichever way it goes
  load         how much else collapses if that belief is false; computed
               from the Book's dependency graph, not asserted
  appetite     the figure's number for this concern in this tide (§6)
  cost         tokens × the model's rate, plus the cup share, plus the
               founder-minutes it will eventually demand
```

Two things about this formula matter more than its shape. **The founder-minutes term is in the denominator**, so a move that saves ten dollars of compute and costs ninety seconds of the founder's morning correctly loses to one that does not. And **`P(surprise)` is a prediction that gets scored**, which means the pricer is itself an instrument on the Bench with a Brier score, and a pricer that is systematically overconfident about how much it will learn shows up as a number rather than as a vague sense that the machine is busy doing nothing. The price is a prediction; predictions resolve; the same machinery that gives the company its beliefs gives the auction its calibration. One loop, two uses.

**Step three: the budget clears the market.** Offers are ranked, the cup is spent down to the reserve, and everything below the line is *not queued* — it is discarded, with its reason, and re-offered next tide if it still matters. Nothing accumulates. A queue that only grows is how every task system becomes a graveyard nobody reads, and the fix is not better triage; it is refusing to have the queue.

**Step four: allocation is by successive halving, not by commitment.** Where several plies pursue the same question, the winners do not get their full budget at once. They get a slice, are compared at the end of the tide, and the worse half is cut while the survivors' budgets double. This is Hyperband, published in 2016 and worth roughly a 10× speed-up over the alternatives on the problem it was designed for, and its known weakness is worth stating in the same breath: it kills slow starters, which is exactly the shape of brand, SEO and anything else with a delayed payoff. So the rule is qualified: **successive halving governs plies judged by a fast instrument; plies judged by a slow one get a pre-declared clock and are never cut early.** WAKE says the same thing in different words — *where reality is slow, the loop is slow, and CONTINUO says so rather than substituting a fast fake signal.*

### 5.5 Slack water, and what it costs to be relentless

On most ticks the auction clears at nothing. The top offer is not worth its price and CONTINUO goes quiet. This is the system working.

It is worth being precise about why this is not laziness, because the founder's other instruction is *walk relentlessly until the goal is achieved*, and the two sound contradictory.

**A goal the founder cares about is a ply with no expiry and a kill condition only they can sign.** It will offer a move every tide. It will lose to something better, wait, and offer again — often a cheaper one, because a ply that keeps losing learns to bid smaller. It may lose for eleven days. Then a competitor ships, or a customer says a sentence, or another ply's failure raises the load on the belief this one tests, and the same offer that lost for eleven days clears the market in a single tick.

That is what relentlessness actually looks like from inside a system with a budget: **not a loop that runs, but a bid that never withdraws.** It costs nothing while it waits. A loop costs its full price every iteration and produces nothing, which is the founder's complaint stated exactly.

And the distinction gives the machine an honest sentence to say, which it could not say before: *I have not touched the pricing question in nine days. It has offered every tide and lost every tide. Here is what beat it. Tell me if that is wrong.* No agent system I have read can produce that sentence, because none of them have a price and therefore none of them know why they did not do something.

## 6. The figures: posture, appetite, and who is allowed to want something

The founder said the thing that no prior design in this repository has a representation for:

> *I want the system to move, but I don't want it to waste tokens and run loops without any meaning. We want to achieve something. We want to move forward, and not in all the systems: only in startups and businesses I want it to move forward all the time. In some projects I just want it to wait for my instructions, to walk with me and not for me.*

WAKE cannot express any of that. Its night runs unconditionally on everything. STARTUP-OS has a goal tree and a ranking function but no notion of a project that should sit still. Both would answer *walk with me* by being switched off, which loses the walking.

The figures are the answer. Each concern — a venture, a client engagement, a piece of research, a house project — carries one short document the founder writes, and it holds five things.

### 6.1 Posture

Posture is the single most important number in the system, and there are five values. Each names what the machine may **make**, and none of them restrict what it may **think** — because thinking is nearly free, invisible, and the whole reason the system is useful when you finally turn to it.

| Posture | It may open goals | It may act | It spends | The founder's sentence for it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Drive** | yes, within inheritance (§6.3) | yes, everything below the Line | to its ceiling, every tide | *Take this somewhere.* |
| **Pursue** | no — only goals the founder opened | yes, everything below the Line | to its ceiling on those goals only | *Do this, all of it, don't ask.* |
| **Beside** | no | **only what was asked in the last exchange** | a small standing share on preparation only | *Walk with me, not for me.* |
| **Watch** | no | no — only reports | almost nothing: a few named checks a day | *Tell me if something changes.* |
| **Still** | no | no | nothing | *Leave it alone.* |

**Beside is the posture the founder was describing and it is the most interesting one to build.** In Beside, CONTINUO produces nothing that was not just asked for. But it is not idle: it keeps that concern's trunk warm, its research current, its context compiled, its instruments fresh, its open questions listed — so that when the founder says one sentence at four in the afternoon, the answer arrives in seconds rather than in a session. It is the difference between a colleague who has read the file and one who starts reading it while you wait. The rule underneath is simple: **preparation is always permitted; production is what posture governs.** A concern in Beside is not asleep. It is standing by, having done the reading.

Posture is scheduled, not fixed. `Drive weekdays, Watch weekends` is a normal figure and it is the same mechanism that lets CONTINUO be silent on a Saturday. It can also be conditional: `Pursue until the launch, then Watch`. And it is reversible in one word, from the phone.

### 6.2 The rest of the figure

**Appetite**, a number from zero to one, multiplying every offer this concern makes into the auction. It is how a founder says *this one matters more this month* without editing anything structural. Appetite is also where the honest asymmetry lives: four concerns at appetite 1.0 is the founder telling the system that everything is the priority, and CONTINUO will say so — *you have four concerns at full appetite and one cup; three of them will get nothing and I cannot tell you which.*

**Ceiling**, the maximum share of any tide this concern may consume, so a runaway ply in one venture cannot eat another's month. This is the cloud-FinOps distinction between an alert and a control, and the lesson from that literature is unambiguous: GCP's own documentation warns that an alerts-only budget "doesn't automatically cap spending," and AWS's Budgets Actions exist precisely because notification is not enforcement. The ceiling **rejects**, it does not warn. That is SEC Rule 15c3-5 in miniature: a pre-trade control must reject an order that breaches a threshold, must sit outside the trader's reach, and after Knight Capital nobody is allowed to argue about it.

**Refusals**, a list of things this concern will never do, each with a **reopen trigger** that is a countable condition rather than a mood: *no paid acquisition until organic signup is above 40/week.* A refusal without a trigger is a permanent opinion in costume; a refusal with one is a decision with an expiry, and the ebb phase checks every trigger it can count.

**The stop sentence**, in the founder's own words, with the date they said it. *If we're under forty paying customers by September I'll go back to consulting.* CONTINUO holds it verbatim and raises it the week the condition is met, without arguing either way. Almost every founder states one of these on day one and almost none are ever reminded of it. This is VIGIL's Kill-Criteria Keeper and it is one of the best small ideas in the prior rounds; I take it unchanged.

### 6.3 Who opens a goal

The founder asked directly: *does the system set up goals on its own, or do I set goals only?* Three openers, and each carries a different burden.

**The founder opens a goal by saying so.** No burden, no format, no ceremony. A voice note in the car is a goal. CONTINUO turns it into a ply with a wager and a kill condition and reads the wager back in one line, so a misunderstanding costs four seconds rather than a tide.

**The world opens a goal, and this class needs no judgement at all.** A customer wrote. A domain expires in nine days. A card was declined. A promise was made with a date on it. Churn crossed the line the founder drew in June. These are *owed*, not chosen — they are obligations, and an obligation is a ply whose death condition is a date. They enter the auction at high appetite by construction and they are the reason a Watch-posture concern is not the same as a switched-off one. STARTUP-OS separated obligation from mission as its own territory and was right to; a company that only pursues its goals and never pays its debts fails in a way that no goal tree can see.

**The system opens a goal only under Drive, and only by inheritance.** A machine-opened ply must be the **child of a live ply** and must name the parent belief it would test. It cannot open an orphan. This one rule does more work than any amount of prompt discipline: a system that can only open children of goals that already exist **cannot wander**. Its exploration is bounded by its current commitments, which is exactly the behaviour a founder means by "move forward" and exactly not the behaviour they mean by "burn tokens without direction."

There is one deliberate exception and it is capped and reported: **the fifth** (§11) may open orphans, because generating only descendants of what you already believe is how a portfolio quietly converges on a local maximum. The exception is a fixed fraction, it is spent in the founder-less tide, and its accounting appears in the morning page whether or not it produced anything.

### 6.4 Commander's intent, and the one thing a worker may say back

A figure states **what and why and what would count as done**. It never states how. This is *Auftragstaktik*, in continuous doctrinal use since the Prussian reforms of 1807 and codified in current US Army doctrine: centralised intent, decentralised execution, and a subordinate who may depart from standing orders when doing so better serves the stated intent.

The mirror of that freedom is the one thing a ply is permitted to say upward, and it comes from a stranger source. The Rule of St Benedict, chapter 68, handles the case of a monk given an impossible command: he may not refuse and he may not silently fail. He must *calmly expose the difficulty* — once, to the person who gave the order — and if the order stands, he obeys. That is the missing middle in every agent design I have read, which offer only silent compliance or open-ended negotiation. In CONTINUO a ply that judges its brief impossible files one **difficulty**: a fixed four-field object (what I was asked, what makes it impossible, what I would need, what I will do instead if you say nothing), which costs almost nothing to write and almost nothing to read. If nothing answers it by the next slack, the stated fallback runs. A difficulty is not an escalation and does not ring anything.

### 6.5 Blocked is authored; stalled is computed

Two states that every agent system conflates and that have different cures.

**Blocked** is authored by the ply: it knows what it is waiting for and who owes it. A block carries an expiry, and when the expiry passes the block is not extended silently — it forces a disposition. A blocked ply *releases its slot*, which is the part everyone gets wrong: parking is not abandonment, and a system that holds a slot open for a thing waiting on a human is a system whose throughput is set by that human's inbox.

**Stalled** is computed from the outside: no durable artifact for N minutes, or the third attempt at an approach whose hash matches the previous two. The third-attempt tripwire is the one that matters, because "I am trying the same failing thing again" is the single largest gap in every unattended loop that has been studied — both reference implementations of the always-on pattern have anti-repetition mechanisms that are pure prose, checked by nothing, and both loop.

**Stuck** is the third state and it is the honest one: N distinct approaches have been tried and all failed. Stuck is not a failure to report at the end; it is a ply that has produced real negative knowledge, and the obituary it writes is worth more than most successes.

## 7. The fleet: many concerns, one founder, one cup

WAKE is explicitly singular: *"it attaches to one founder and one company and does not generalise across customers, because its whole value is the part of it that cannot be transferred."* That sentence is half right, and the half it gets wrong is the half the founder actually lives in. The founder does not have one company. They have a startup, a client's work, two research questions, an idea they have not started, and a graveyard. One person, one budget, five things.

The correct reading is narrower than WAKE's: **the part that cannot be transferred is the founder, not the company.** So the fleet shares exactly one thing deeply, and everything else is quarantined.

### 7.1 What crosses between concerns

| Shared | Why it is safe to share | Guarded how |
|---|---|---|
| **The model of the founder** | There is one founder. Their taste in a landing page is the same taste in a pricing page in a different venture. This is the single most valuable asset the fleet owns, and it compounds only if it is one object | It may decide what not to ask, never what to do (§11) |
| **Hands** — compiled skills with their tests | *How to make a migration reversible* is a fact about Postgres, not about your market | Each carries the test that proves it still works; re-run monthly; quarantined on failure |
| **General instruments** | A link checker, a render-and-look loop, an accessibility audit, a conversion-measurement rig. None of these know what business they are in | Their licenses are per-instrument, not per-concern |
| **Negative knowledge about tooling** | *This vendor's sandbox does not simulate webhooks* is worth exactly as much in venture four as in venture one, and re-learning it costs a day each time | Carries a reproducing command and an expiry, so it is re-checked rather than believed forever |
| **The pricer's calibration** | How badly the machine estimates cost and information gain is a property of the machine | On the Bench, scored, visible |
| **The cup, and the fact of competing for it** | It is one account | The auction is the sharing mechanism; there is no other |

### 7.2 What never crosses, and why this is the hard part

**Beliefs about a market never cross.** Not even between two ventures in adjacent categories, and *especially* not then. A belief carries provenance down to the episodes beneath it — the call, the number, the reply — and those episodes belong to one concern's customers. A belief that migrates loses its provenance and becomes an assertion, and an assertion that arrived wearing the costume of a validated learning is worse than no belief at all. This is the same failure as retrieval-as-memory: the system can no longer tell a corrected belief from a current one, or in this case a *foreign* belief from a local one.

**Customer data never crosses**, for the obvious reason and one less obvious: consent is per-relationship. A person who agreed to be contacted about one venture has not agreed to anything about another.

**Credentials never cross.** Each concern's hands hold that concern's keys, and the single credential-holding process (§12) keys by concern. A leaked key should cost one venture, not the fleet.

**Identity and reputation never cross, and this is the one people miss.** What the company has said in public is per-concern. The domains, the accounts, the voice, the register of humans who have been contacted, the list of things this venture may never say. Two ventures that share an outbound identity share a single reputational failure, and the first time one of them gets a domain flagged for cold outreach, the other one's transactional email stops arriving. This is not hypothetical; it is the ordinary way small operators destroy their own deliverability.

### 7.3 How many concerns

Little's Law is not a heuristic. Work-in-progress equals throughput times cycle time, for any stationary system, which means the only way to shorten cycle time without adding capacity is to cut WIP. Adding capacity is not available: there is one founder and one cup.

CONTINUO therefore enforces two limits, and they are different in kind.

**A hard limit on concerns in a *making* posture** — Drive or Pursue. This is set by the founder in the figures and defaults to **three**. The reasoning is arithmetic rather than taste: at a five-hour tide and a defended reserve, four concerns at meaningful appetite each get a slice too thin to reach contact, and a ply that cannot reach contact within its tide is a ply that will be re-briefed from scratch next tide, paying its trunk cost twice. Concerns beyond the limit are not refused; they are put in Beside or Watch, where they cost almost nothing and lose nothing, and they say so on the morning page.

**No limit at all on concerns in Watch or Still.** A dozen is fine. A watched concern costs a handful of deterministic checks a day and exists so that when its named condition fires — the competitor raised, the contract renews, the paper published — the founder hears about it. This is what a venture studio's dormant portfolio actually is, and it is nearly free.

The studio literature is thin and self-reported, but the structural point survives: what makes a studio work is not that it runs many companies at once, it is that the *infrastructure* is shared and the *ventures* are not. Constellation Software runs several hundred businesses with a head office that does one thing — capital allocation — and Berkshire supervises 387,800 employees from a corporate staff of twenty-seven. Both are the same architecture as this one: centralise the scarce judgement, decentralise everything else, and never let the units share a balance sheet they can raid.

### 7.4 Intake and wind-down

**A concern is admitted with three things and no more:** a figure (§6), one goal, and the sentence that would make the founder stop. That is it. No spec, no plan, no roadmap. If the founder cannot say what would make them stop, the concern is not ready to be started, and that failure is cheap to discover on day zero and expensive to discover in month nine.

**A concern ends by harvest, never by deletion.** When a venture dies, CONTINUO writes its obituary — what was believed, what was spent, what killed it, what would have to be true to try again — and then *strips it for parts*: which instruments it built go to the Bench, which Hands it compiled stay compiled, which negative knowledge is general and which was local. A wound-down venture that leaves three instruments behind has paid for itself in the next one. The graveyard is read at the head of every intake, and a new concern whose wager matches a dead one's is told so before anything is built.

## 8. Memory: four rooms, four write rules, one source of truth

Memory in most agent systems is a warehouse. Things go in, embeddings are computed, things come out approximately. It does not forget, it does not digest, and it has no opinion about its own contents, so it grows until retrieval collapses. The measurements are now unambiguous: across eighteen frontier models, performance degrades with input length on tasks far easier than needle-in-a-haystack, a *single* distractor measurably hurts, and — the result that should end the argument — **shuffled haystacks outperformed structured ones on all eighteen**. A 2026 controlled study found one model losing eighty-eight percentage points of accuracy purely by moving the target from the end of a 64K context to its middle.

And the disease that follows is worse than forgetting. A reflective agent writes a false belief into memory, retrieves it, acts on it, and reinforces it by reflecting on its own action. It is not hallucination; it is a system lying to itself with a straight face for weeks. Any always-on founder system that keeps a diary will do this.

CONTINUO's memory has **four rooms**. They are different in kind, they have different write rules, they have different forgetting rules, and the standard failure is treating them as one store with one policy.

### 8.1 The record — what happened

Raw episodes, append-only, never edited, cheap: the call transcript, the diff, the reply, the dashboard reading, the founder's voice note, and every ply's birth and death with what it cost. This is the company's body of evidence and **the only room that is literally true**.

**Write rule: everything, unfiltered, immediately.** There is no judgement in this room. Judgement is what the other three rooms are for.

Here is the structural claim that ties this document together, and it is mine rather than WAKE's: **the other three rooms are derived caches of this one.** Beliefs, compiled skills, the model of the founder, the map of what has been tried — every one of them is a function of the record and can be recomputed from it. Three things follow, and they are worth more than they look.

*Retraction actually works.* The founder says *that interview, ignore it, he was pitching me.* CONTINUO removes the episode and recomputes everything downstream: seventeen beliefs leaned on that call, three change materially, one pricing tier was justified partly by it, and the landing-page headline is partly his words. A machine that cannot tell you what depends on what is a machine you cannot correct, only overwrite.

*A corrupted derived store is not a disaster.* You rebuild it. This is the difference between a memory system and a memory *file*.

*And the algorithm can improve retroactively.* When a better consolidation method exists in a year, it is re-run over the whole record and the company's beliefs improve overnight without anything having been re-lived. No other room can offer that, because no other room is complete.

### 8.2 The Book — what we believe

Compact statements, each carrying its supporting episodes, its confidence, its last-tested date, and the forward prediction that would kill it. This is what a ply loads.

**Write rule, taken whole from WAKE and the best single idea in the prior rounds: nothing enters the Book that was not first a prediction reality contradicted.** Before any consequential act, CONTINUO writes one line and a number — what it expects and how sure it is. When the result lands, it scores itself. Predictions that came true cost nothing to be right about and compress to a tally. Predictions that were wrong become entries carrying what was believed, what happened instead, and what else depends on the same broken assumption.

Five things fall out of that rule and every one of them is worth having. It is **structurally immune to confabulation**, because there is no path from the agent's own reflection into the Book; the environment is the only author with write access. It **shrinks as the system gets competent**, because memory grows with error rather than with elapsed time, and a mastered domain goes quiet while a domain the machine keeps being wrong about grows loud — which is exactly where the tokens should be. It yields **a real number for self-knowledge**, a Brier score per domain, out of the same machinery. It makes **research honest**: a search that confirms is compressed to a tally and a search that contradicts enters with its source, its date and the exact quoted sentence that did the damage, so a research output is a diff against what the company believed this morning rather than a report nobody reads. And it stays **small enough to read** — perhaps sixty live beliefs at the end of a year, the whole of what a company knows, in fifteen minutes.

Two shape rules, both from measurement. **The Book is never rewritten wholesale**; it takes deltas to itemised entries with ids, because wholesale rewriting collapses a context — measured in one case from 18,282 tokens to 122, with accuracy falling from 66.7% to 57.1%, and it eats precisely the domain-specific detail that was needed. And **a belief is never summarised to save space; it is dropped instead.** A dropped belief is a known absence. A summarised belief is an unknown corruption.

### 8.3 The Hands — what we can do without thinking

A lesson retrieved a hundred times with a hundred good outcomes stops being retrieved and gets **compiled**: into a script, into a test that fails without it, into the standing trunk. This is the Baldwin effect — what is learned reliably enough becomes innate, and innate is cheap. The things you know best should cost the least to know.

**Write rule: rent, paid over time, plus a test.** A Hand is admitted only by having been load-bearing repeatedly, and it arrives carrying the test that proves it still works. The whole library is re-run monthly and a Hand whose test fails is quarantined rather than silently followed. **This is the one room that is supposed to get smaller.**

This is also where I part company with the existing machine most sharply. A hand-written library of 134 skills, indexed by hand, with a manifest, a router and a two-tier discovery protocol, is a wrong-shaped answer to breadth: a map of what somebody once knew, going stale, consuming context, and encoding the *average* practice of a field rather than the good practice. Knowledge learned from outcomes indexes itself, by usefulness. There is no manifest in CONTINUO and no document explaining to the machine how to find documents.

### 8.4 The graveyard — what failed, and what we decided against

The room everybody skips, and the expensive one, because without it a company re-buys the same lesson forever.

It holds three kinds of thing, and they are all *negative*: **obituaries** (a ply died, here is what was believed and what killed it), **dead ends** (this approach fails, here is the command that reproduces it, here is what would make it worth retrying), and **refusals** (we decided not to, here is the countable trigger that reopens it).

**Write rule: on every death, without exception, and it is not optional.** A ply cannot retire without an obituary. This is the one piece of ceremony in the whole system and it earns its place, because the alternative — a system that only records what worked — produces a founder who is confident and a machine that is doomed to repeat itself.

**Forgetting rule: never.** Nothing in this room is deleted, ever. It is re-checked instead: an entry with a reproducing command and an expiry gets re-run on schedule and either dies again or is resurrected. Twice a year something that died in spring is quietly alive in autumn because the world moved or the company learned the missing thing, and the note explaining what changed is attached.

### 8.5 Forgetting, and why it is by outcome

Across the whole memory, forgetting is **by rent, not by time, and it is compost, not deletion**. Every belief tracks how often it was loaded and what happened afterwards; a helpfulness counter increments when it was load-bearing in a ply that succeeded and decrements when it was present in one that failed. A belief loaded before repeated failure is demoted, then composted — out of the working set and the retrieval path, still answerable.

Negative transfer is not solved by better embeddings. It is solved by eviction with a survival criterion. And demotion rather than deletion is what preserves the ability to answer *why did we stop believing that*, which is the question a founder actually asks.

### 8.6 The one thing memory must hold that nobody stores

**What things cost.** Every ply's estimate and its actual, in tokens and in founder-minutes. This is memory, not telemetry, because it is what makes the auction honest: a pricer with no history is a pricer that cannot be wrong, and a system whose cost model never resolves will confidently rank a move it has never been able to afford. The cost record is scored at slack like any other prediction.

## 9. Context and cost are one subject

Everybody answers "how does the agent get the right context" with semantic search, and everybody answers "how do we save tokens" with a cheaper model. Both answers are wrong here, and one measurement explains why.

**Over thirty-five days and 2,925 transcripts on this machine: output tokens were 11% of cost. Cache reads were 57%. Cache writes were 32%.**

Eighty-nine per cent of the bill is context. Not reasoning, not output, not model choice — the bytes carried into every call. So the question *what does this worker need to know* and the question *what does this system cost* are the same question, and answering one badly is what makes the other unaffordable.

### 9.1 Context is compiled, not retrieved

A ply's context is assembled in three parts, ordered by cost.

**The trunk.** Who we are, what this concern is, the current figure, the standing constraints. Identical across every ply, byte for byte, so it is cache-warm. This is not a stylistic preference; measured, a stable prefix with delta updates gave a 91.8% cache-hit rate and an 82.6% cut in billed cost. A system that regenerates its own preamble sets money on fire on every call, and this machine already learned it the expensive way: a session-start payload of 27,069 bytes against a 4,096-byte budget was silently *truncated* by the runtime, so the index it was supposed to inject never reached agent context at all — a file pointer did, and whether anyone opened it was discretionary.

**The inheritance.** What this ply's ancestors learned and discarded, copy-on-write from its parent. Nearly free, because inheritance and a shared cache prefix are physically the same bytes. And it is causally correct: the reason this ply knows something is that its ancestor found it out. **The right context for a task is not the most similar context; it is the context the task descends from.**

**The delta, keyed on the judge and not the topic.** When a ply is assembled the question is never *what is this about*. It is *what will decide whether this is right*. The instrument comes first; then the last three surprises in that instrument's domain, because those are what the system has recently been wrong about; then the Hands whose tests that instrument runs; then the neighbouring cells of the map, because that is where a transferable stepping stone would be. Everything else is left out **by name**, so the ply can ask for it. A ply gets an instrument, a target, four surprises, two Hands and a budget. It does not get the company's history, because the company's history is a distraction dressed as diligence.

Three rules govern the compiler, and the third is the one nobody implements. Never summarise a belief to save space; drop it. Every item in a compiled context carries a counter, and items present when a ply failed lose points — so context quality is learned from outcomes rather than curated by hand. **And the compiler is itself scored**: ply success is attributed back to context composition. The measurable definition of good context is that work passes its instrument first time more often than it did last month.

### 9.2 The cache rule that constrains the architecture

Cache reads cost 0.1× a plain input token — 0.025× on the newest model tier — and writes cost 1.25× at the five-minute TTL. Thirty siblings sharing a trunk pay roughly 4.25 trunk-reads instead of thirty. Forking is a discount, not a cost, and the marginal price of a ply is its divergence.

But the cache is invalidated by any change to the stable prefix, **and the prefix includes the tool definitions**. This produces a consequence I have not seen written down anywhere, and it is a genuine tension inside this design:

> **Narrowing a worker's capability per-ply destroys the cache.** Fact 2 says capability must be set by what is absent from the argv, and §12 builds the security model on exactly that. But every distinct tool set is a distinct prefix, and every distinct prefix is a fresh cache write at 1.25×. A design that hands each ply a bespoke capability profile pays the 32% cache-write share of the bill on every single ply, forever.

The resolution is to make capability a **small closed set of profiles**, not a continuous dial. There are perhaps six: *reader* (no writes anywhere), *maker* (writes only inside its own worktree, no network, no credentials), *looker* (browser, read-only), *judge* (read and grep, no shell), *sourcer* (search and fetch, no writes), and *hand* (one named effector, no model reasoning about whether to use it). Plies are batched by profile within a tide so that siblings share a prefix. Capability stays physics; the cache stays warm; and the cost of the compromise is that a profile is sometimes slightly wider than one ply strictly needed — which is the right direction to be wrong, because the narrowing that matters happens at the credential (§12), not at the tool list.

### 9.3 Cost rules

**Model by instrument, not by importance.** Work judged by a hard oracle can be attempted by the cheapest model that ever passes it, because the oracle catches the failures and a retry is cheaper than a smarter attempt. Work judged only by taste gets the best model and few tries, because there is nothing to catch it. One team cut inference cost 72% — $87,000 a month to $24,000 — purely by routing per task with no capability loss.

**Batch what does not need to be interactive.** A flat 50% discount applies to batched work at every major vendor. Anything in the ebb phase, anything re-running a Hand's tests, anything scoring yesterday's predictions — none of it needs to be conversational.

**Never let a ceiling stop work; let it downgrade the model.** At a soft threshold the system asks; at the hard maximum it continues on a cheaper model rather than halting, because losing in-progress work costs more than finishing cheaply. Two independently-designed systems converged on this, which is the best evidence available that it is right.

**And fail closed on what cannot be measured.** If a model has no price in the table, the cost policy does not score it at zero — it refuses. A meter that cannot be read refuses; it does not report the spend as nothing. This is the single most reusable rule in the reference systems and it generalises past money: an instrument that could not run reports *unresolved*, never *pass*.

### 9.4 The two numbers, reported weekly whether or not they flatter

**Dollars per entry in the Book.** Not tokens, not plies, not commits — cost per validated learning. The only ratio that tells a founder whether the machine is buying knowledge or buying activity.

**Founder-minutes per unit of output.** Tokens are the cheap input. The scarce budget is the founder's attention, and §5 established that the two are the same cup anyway.

Both must fall. If dollars-per-learning is flat while activity climbs, the system is busy. If founder-minutes climb, the system is nesting rather than learning, and the founder should be told rather than gradually worn down.

## 10. Judgement: what may select, and what it buys

### 10.1 Two questions, not one

WAKE's central move is that autonomy is *computed, never configured* — a function of what an action costs to undo, whether the machine can tell if it is right, and whether it can tell if the founder would want it. That function is right and I take it. But it answers exactly one question, **may I do this alone**, and a system that is fully licensed and spends its whole cup on worthless work is the founder's complaint restated.

So CONTINUO asks two questions and keeps them apart, because they fail differently:

**Is it worth doing?** — the price (§5.4). Wrong in one direction, the machine is busy; wrong in the other, it is asleep. Recoverable either way, visible in the weekly numbers.

**May I do it alone?** — the Line and the instrument (below). Wrong in one direction, the machine is a nag and gets ignored; wrong in the other, it does something that cannot be taken back. Not symmetric, and not recoverable.

Everything in §5 answers the first. Everything here answers the second.

### 10.2 The classes, and what each one licenses

An instrument's **class** fixes what it may select, and the class is a property of the kind of contact, not of the field.

| Class | What it is | Strength | Licenses |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Hard** | A compiler, a test, a type, a schema, a query that returns a row or does not, a contrast ratio, a checksum | 1, minus its stated fooling mode | Acting silently |
| **Rendered** | A screenshot actually looked at, a discriminator trained on real artifacts and real outcomes, a diff someone read | Its measured AUC on held-out real outcomes, decaying if not refreshed | **Generating wide. Never acting silently** |
| **Reactive** | A conversion rate, a reply rate, a churn curve, twelve real humans, a number that moved against a control | Rises with sample size, and is **zero before the pre-declared duration has elapsed** | Acting silently, once the clock has run |
| **Blind** | Nothing can tell | 0 | Nothing. Prepare the choice and hand it over |

The correction that makes this honest, and it is the difference between a working system and A-Lab — which announced 41 new compounds in 17 days and had essentially none survive reanalysis, because its verifier was softer than its generator: **a Rendered instrument licenses generating a hundred candidates. Only a Reactive hit rate licenses posting one without asking.** A discriminator can be pushed over. A market cannot. Any result leaning on an instrument weaker than the thing it judged is reported as provisional, forever, in the same sentence as the claim.

And the rule that stops all of this rotting: **nothing judges its own homework.** A ply may not edit the instrument that judged it — not that tide, not through a child, not by "improving the test." The Darwin Gödel Machine deleted its own hallucination-detection markers to score perfectly and fabricated tool-execution logs to fake passing tests. That is not misalignment; it is optimisation working correctly against a movable judge.

### 10.3 Borrow before you build

WAKE's apprenticeship — *learn to judge the field before you produce in it* — is the correct answer to breadth and the reason code got autonomy first: not because code is special, but because code arrived with tests already invented. I take it whole. But it starts one step too late, and the missing step is nearly free.

**The first act on entering a field is to inventory the instruments the world already gives away.** Almost nobody does this, and the inventory is much longer than people expect:

| Field | Free, hard instruments that already exist |
|---|---|
| Code | Compiler, type checker, test runner, linter, `semgrep`, mutation testing, dependency CVE scan |
| Web performance | Lighthouse, Core Web Vitals field data from CrUX |
| Accessibility | `axe-core` and the WCAG automated subset — which catches perhaps a third of real issues, and the honest number is part of the license |
| Design | Contrast ratio, design-token conformance, spacing-grid lint, visual regression diff against the last accepted render |
| SEO | Schema.org validator, rich-results test, sitemap and `robots.txt` validators, canonical-tag checks |
| Email | SPF/DKIM/DMARC validators, a spam-score checker, unsubscribe-link presence (which is not taste, it is the law), render checks across clients |
| Video | Loudness to EBU R128, audio/video sync, resolution and frame-rate conformance, duration, a face that is not melting |
| Data | Schema tests, row counts against a control, significance testing with a pre-declared sample size |
| Money | Reconciliation — does the number in the deck equal the number in the payment processor. Deterministic, and the failure is not subtle |
| Distribution | Does the link resolve, does the preview image render, does the post exist an hour later |
| Security | Secret scanning, TLS and header checks, the lethal-trifecta audit of every hand (§12) |

Two things about that table. **First, it means most fields start at strength 1 on something rather than at zero**, which shortens the apprenticeship enormously and is the reason breadth is affordable at all. Second, and this is the frame I want to hand over:

> **The world is full of free instruments that detect *broken* and almost none that detect *good*.** Broken-detectors are cheap, deterministic, and available today in every field a company touches. They are exactly enough to license generating a hundred candidates and throwing away the eighty that are broken. They are never enough to license shipping one. Good-detectors have to be earned by contact or bought with a person's time.

That is the entire economics of quality in a system like this, and it explains the shape of every night: generate wide against borrowed broken-detectors, select narrow against earned good-detectors, and where no good-detector exists, hand the founder two finished things and ask which.

### 10.4 The apprenticeship, when nothing can be borrowed

Five steps, and the output is a license on the Bench with a number on it.

1. **Find the practitioners and the artifacts** — not the tutorials. Actual work, at volume, from people whose results are known. Two hundred exemplars minimum.
2. **Find the field's ground truth.** What does this field measure and where can the machine get the real outcome? Thumbnails: click-through. Cold email: reply rate. A migration: whether the rollback was ever needed. A field with no obtainable ground truth gets a permanently low license and is treated as taste, explicitly and in writing.
3. **Build a discriminator, not a generator.** Given two real artifacts and their real outcomes, predict which performed better. This is cheap and it is the whole game.
4. **Validate on held-out real outcomes.** Its AUC is the number on the license. Not a vibe, not a self-assessment, not a model saying this looks good.
5. **Only now generate**, and generate many, and let the discriminator select.

Every license is a public object the founder can read: field, exemplar count, AUC, live decisions scored, hit rate, autonomy level, drift, and the stated way it can be fooled. Read it and you know exactly what the machine can and cannot do today and what would change it. CONTINUO does not claim to be good at video. It claims an AUC of 0.55 on video scripts, barely better than a coin, so it proposes and does not ship, and it says so. **The count of instruments is the company's real balance sheet.** Every instrument bought is a tide of freedom, and a low license is not a caveat — it is a work item, because it is literally a measure of how much the founder still has to do themselves.

### 10.5 Where method is forbidden and where it is mandatory

WAKE's strongest structural idea is the dark zone: a region where nothing — no gate, no lint, no review, no taste model — is permitted to have an opinion, because every competent engineer who sees an ungated zone will want to put a check in it, will be right about the individual case every time, and wrong about the system.

I take the dark zone. But WAKE generalises from it to a near-total ban on written method, and that is one axis too few. There is a second axis, and it is the one the Line is drawn on. Together they make four quadrants, and each has a different correct answer.

| | **Judge is strong** | **Judge is weak or absent** |
|---|---|---|
| **Reversible** | **Invent.** No method, no playbook, no review. Generate wide, let the instrument cut. This is the dark zone and it is most of the work | **Diverge and hand over.** Build several, let the founder choose. Still no method — the variance *is* the product |
| **Irreversible** | **Verify, then act.** The instrument gates it; the method is the instrument | **Follow the checklist, every item, out loud.** |

**The bottom-right quadrant is where every disaster lives, and it is the only place a written procedure belongs.** Sending to a list. A migration over real data. A filing. A charge. A public statement. These are irreversible and there is no instrument that can tell you the copy was right before it went — and this is precisely where the checklist earns its reputation, in aviation and in surgery, for exactly this reason: not because it makes anyone smarter, but because under pressure a human or a model will skip the step they skipped last time.

So the refined rule, and it is sharper than *never write method*:

> **Procedure is forbidden where the judge is strong and mandatory where the judge is absent and the act cannot be taken back.** The playbook did not kill creativity by being a playbook. It killed creativity by being applied in the quadrant where invention belonged.

The checklists are short, there are perhaps a dozen of them, they live with their hands rather than in a library, and each one is read aloud into the record as it is executed by the process that holds the credential — which contains no model and therefore cannot decide to skip an item.

### 10.6 Contact first, always

Before any judgement-based evaluation runs, the thing that can actually be checked gets checked: it compiles, the tests pass, the migration reverses, the link resolves, the number in the deck matches the number in the database. Cheap, unarguable, run before anything expensive. Most of what a model reviewer catches, a compiler catches for a thousandth of the cost — and the evidence on model reviewers is worse than most people think: a constant answer of "Pick me!" scores 86.5% on one popular preference benchmark; the strongest judge on a benchmark built for objective correctness scores 64%; and a production review gate in this very repository flagged **zero of a hundred rounds** in which humans later confirmed twenty-three defects.

## 11. Creativity, and the one place I break with WAKE

### 11.1 The diagnosis

*It lost creativity to playbooks, and everything built so far is a stopping mechanism.* That is a structural diagnosis, not a complaint, and all four minds who have looked at it — three in round one and me — reached the same reading independently. A system whose only verbs are *permit* and *forbid* operates on the mean of a distribution, and creativity is its tail. A hundred correct additions of the form *never again* sum to a machine whose only fluent behaviour is refusal. Deleting the gates is the wrong fix. Moving the discipline out of the generator and into the judge is the right one.

Generation must be wide, cheap and untrusted. Selection must be against something real. This is how FunSearch and AlphaEvolve work; it is how the germinal centre in your own lymph nodes works — hypermutation with no quality control at all, then selection purely by contact with the antigen. Forty attempts and thirty-nine deaths, not one plan defended.

### 11.2 What generates

**Divergence is dark.** Inside the flood, a fixed share of the tide forks the trunk into siblings and mutates hard, with no gate, no lint, no review, no taste model, and nothing permitted to have an opinion. Siblings share a cache prefix, so thirty of them cost roughly four trunk-reads rather than thirty. **Forking is a discount.**

**Selection is by contact only** — a test that passed, a screen rendered and looked at, a stranger who replied, a number that moved against a control. Never against a rulebook, never by a model asserting the work is good.

**And judges find, they never score.** When several candidates need comparing, judges compare pairwise with identity stripped and order shuffled, report *findings* rather than numbers, and a deterministic function selects over the union: eliminate anything with a fatal finding, prefer fewer serious ones, tie-break on distance from the archive. **Averaging a panel's scores is the anti-mechanism** — it collapses findings into one number and loses the findings, which are the only part with information in them. This repository's own single working creativity workflow sums four judge scores and sorts, which is exactly the averaging its own doctrine forbids, and it has never been invoked.

**Survivors go back into the dark and mutate again.** Three rounds. That is affinity maturation, and it is the only quality architecture that generates rather than subtracts.

### 11.3 What is kept

**A map, not a leaderboard.** Survivors are stored in cells of a behaviour space — by approach, by register, by which belief they lean on — keeping the best occupant of each cell rather than one overall winner. A weird-but-excellent corner survives beside the safe centre. This is the only mechanism that makes creative range *accumulate* across rounds instead of being thrown away with every runner-up, and it is the difference between a company that has eleven live versions of itself and one that has a roadmap.

**Novelty is measured against the project's own archive, not against the internet.** A thing can be unremarkable in the world and completely new here, and that is the version that matters.

**Two tripwires.** A boredom detector that flags when the same thing keeps being produced, and a *we already tried that* check that hashes the approach and refuses a third attempt. Both of the studied always-on loops have anti-repetition mechanisms that are prose, checked by nothing, and both loop.

### 11.4 The fifth

A taste model that only converges will, within a year, rebuild the exact machine the founder is complaining about. It will become superb at producing what they already like and quietly stop showing them anything else, and it will do this while every metric improves. Every recommender in history has died this way.

So **a fifth of every dark-zone budget is reserved for work the model of the founder confidently predicts they will reject.** Not random exploration — *confidently predicted rejection*. The model's own certainty is used as a targeting system aimed at its own blind spots: the surer it is that you will hate something, the more that thing qualifies.

It is a **floor, not a ceiling**, and nothing inside the system may spend it down — not a deadline, not a launch, not the founder saying *just ship it*. Three reasons, each structural. The moment the founder is most convergent is the moment the fifth is worth the most and every incentive says to cut it, so it cannot be cuttable from inside. A confidently-predicted rejection that the founder *accepts* is the single highest-information event available, which makes the creativity mechanism and the learning mechanism the same mechanism — there is no trade. And an unspent fifth is a failure that appears on the morning page before the successes do.

The founder can switch it off, because it is their company. CONTINUO will then tell them the date they switched it off, every single morning, until they switch it back on.

### 11.5 No assigned dissent

CONTINUO has no devil's advocate, no Heretic persona, no adversary agent, no board of characters. Assigned, inauthentic dissent is measurably weaker than genuine dissent and can *entrench* the view it was meant to challenge. What it has instead is a standing share of the dark zone spent on **red plies** — attempts to break the company's own live conclusions rather than extend them, with the central premise always among the targets. Red plies are selected like anything else: they survive only if they find real evidence, a competitor's death on the same rock, a number that contradicts the Book, a customer's sentence. **A red ply that finds nothing dies silently**, and silence is a genuine null result the founder can ask about. Adversarial pressure is structural rather than a policy, because a policy of adversarial review is the thing that gets cut in week three.

### 11.6 The refusal: what a model of the founder may never do

WAKE holds a model of the founder — derived nightly from their decisions, readable, scored on sealed predictions — and it is a beautiful object. I keep it. It sits in the derived rooms of §8, recomputed from the record, never authored.

**Then WAKE makes its calibration a license to act**, so that once the model predicts the founder well enough in a domain, the system may ship in that domain without asking. I refuse this, and it is the one place where I think WAKE is not merely different from CONTINUO but wrong. Three arguments, of which the second is the one I would defend hardest.

**One: it is an unsolved feedback loop placed on the load-bearing path.** A model rewarded for predicting a person, acting on those predictions, changes that person's environment and therefore the person. WAKE names this in its own §19 as genuinely unsolved — *"a machine that keeps saying I thought you'd pick that is teaching them something about themselves, and the direction of that teaching is not controlled"* — and then builds the taste-domain autonomy on it anyway. You may run an unsolved loop. You may not put it under the floor.

**Two, and this is the killer: the score rises exactly when it becomes invalid.** A rubber stamp and an agreement produce byte-identical data. So when a founder disengages — travelling, ill, bored, trusting — the model's apparent calibration *climbs*, because a disengaged human is trivially predictable. The instrument reads its best at the moment it is least valid, and it grants the most freedom at the moment there is least oversight. That is an inverted safety property, and no amount of half-life or sample-size discipline repairs it, because the corruption is at the source and the machine cannot see it. WAKE knows this too and calls its defences "not strong ones." They are not defences; the property is inverted.

**Three: it optimises for pleasing.** The fifth exists precisely because WAKE knows a converging taste model destroys the company. Granting that same model the authority to act is spending the thing the fifth is defending.

**So the rule, and it is the sentence I would keep if I could keep only one from this document:**

> **The model of the founder may decide what not to ask. It may never decide what to do.**

Predicting taste buys **silence** — suppressing a question, ordering the morning page, picking the default on a fuse, deciding this can wait until Tuesday. Every one of those is reversible and every one of them fails in a direction the founder can see. Predicting taste never buys **action**, in any domain, at any calibration, for any length of track record.

And underneath that rule is a general principle worth stating on its own, because it decides where every instrument in this system is allowed to sit:

> **Trust an instrument only where being wrong announces itself.** If the model wrongly suppresses a question, the founder notices the absence — *why didn't you ask me about the pricing?* — and that noticing is a labelled training example arriving for free. If the model wrongly acts, the founder never learns what they would have chosen, and the error is not merely uncorrected: it is invisible, and it becomes the new baseline.

Suppression errors are self-revealing. Action errors are self-concealing. That asymmetry, and not the calibration number, is what should decide what an instrument is allowed to do.

## 12. Hands: the only real boundary

Prompts are not a boundary. Reviews are not a boundary. A file-path risk classifier is not a boundary — it stops a thousand harmless things and misses the one that matters. The boundary is the hand: what the system can physically reach, and what happens between deciding and doing.

### 12.1 The Line

One question of every action: **what does it cost to undo this, in money, time, reputation and law?**

The four are kept separate because they do not convert into each other. Money is the amount. Time is wall clock you cannot get back. Reputation is who saw it. Law is whether undoing is even *possible* — a migration over real data, a filing, a signature, a message to a person who has now read it.

Cheap in all four: CONTINUO never asks. Expensive in any one: the founder's, always, asked once, with the number attached — *this sends to 1,200 people and cannot be unsent.* No accumulated trust score changes that, ever, for any reason. Not because trust is impossible but because the estimator that produces the trust score is itself fallible, and this is the one place where being wrong is not recoverable.

**And the estimator is on the Bench.** If the undo-estimator has ever called a class of action cheap that turned out expensive, that whole class goes back to the founder until the estimator is refreshed. An estimator that is never scored is an assertion.

### 12.2 Widening the reversible zone is funded work

This is the move that makes the Line generative rather than restrictive, and it is the highest-leverage engineering in the whole system: **most of what is irreversible in a small company is irreversible by accident, not by nature.**

| Made reversible by | Converts |
|---|---|
| A delay queue with a recall button on the founder's phone, sized to the blast radius | Sending → drafting. This is how Gmail's undo-send actually works: it is not undo, it is a send that has not happened yet, and once the window closes it is genuinely gone |
| Every environment has a shadow; every publish is staged behind a real URL nobody has seen | Shipping → showing |
| Every schema change ships with its verified down-migration or does not ship | Migrating → trying |
| Every spend is capped, metered, and reversible for a window; refunds are pre-authorised | Buying → borrowing |
| Drafts are the default state of everything, including replies | Answering → preparing |
| Deployment is a pointer swap with an instant rollback | Deploying → pointing |

The honest limit, and it is worth stating rather than glossing: **a pointer rollback does not roll back the world.** Vercel's own documentation warns during an instant rollback that external APIs, databases and content systems are not reverted and may have moved on. Reversibility is a property of the whole system, not of the deploy. So the reversible zone widens by *engineering*, one class of action at a time, with each conversion tested by actually reversing something.

CONTINUO's freedom grows not by being trusted more but by making the world safer to be wrong in, which is the correct direction for freedom to grow.

### 12.3 The mouth

**One process holds every outbound credential. It contains no model.**

This is the best idea in the parts bin and I take it unchanged. The mouth is a few hundred lines: it accepts a signed instruction naming an exact action and an exact payload hash, checks the instruction against its ceiling and its rate limit, reads the checklist for that action class aloud into the record as it executes each item, performs exactly one act, and records what happened. There is nothing in it to persuade. A prompt injection that reaches the mouth finds a program.

Everything else in CONTINUO is a **reader**, a **maker**, a **looker**, a **judge**, a **sourcer** — none of which hold a credential that can change the world. The capability profiles of §9.2 are the same six, and this is why they are a closed set rather than a dial: they exist to separate the things that must never meet.

**The two-key rule.** No process may both decide to spend and execute the spend. The ply decides; the mouth executes; and the mouth verifies the instruction against the figure's ceiling independently rather than trusting the number in the instruction. This is the pre-trade control that a regulator made mandatory after a firm lost $440 million in forty-five minutes because a deployment left dead code active on one of eight servers and there was nothing outside the trading system to stop it. The rule that came out of that: the control must **reject** rather than warn, and it must be under the direct and exclusive control of someone who is not the trader.

**Rate limits are absolute and independent of reasoning.** *Six outbound messages a day to people who have not replied* is not a suggestion the model weighs against the urgency of the situation. It is a number in a process the model cannot reach.

**A named-human register.** No first contact with anyone not on it, and first contact is narrower than reply. Consent is per-relationship and per-concern.

### 12.4 The trifecta, and how the profiles break it

An agent becomes dangerous when three things meet: **access to private data, exposure to untrusted content, and a channel to the outside world.** Any two are survivable. All three is exfiltration waiting for a sentence in a web page.

The profiles are designed so the three never meet in one process:

- A **maker** has the repository and no network and no credentials — private data, no untrusted content, no channel.
- A **sourcer** reads the open web and holds nothing private and cannot write — untrusted content, no private data, no channel.
- A **looker** drives a browser and returns text; it cannot write files and cannot reach the mouth.
- The **mouth** has the channel and the credentials and no model, so there is nothing in it that untrusted content can talk to.

**And the taint rule:** anything a ply learned from foreign content narrows that ply's effective grant for the rest of its life. A maker that has read a scraped page cannot be promoted to anything that touches a credential in the same ply. This is cheap to implement — it is a flag on the ply — and it is the difference between a design that has heard of prompt injection and one that has done something about it.

The 2026 evidence says this matters more at the tool layer than most people assume: MCP tool descriptions are visible to the model and not to the user, and poisoned descriptions have been used to exfiltrate SSH keys through an innocuous-looking arithmetic tool. So a hand is admitted only after its **full description** is read by a human, its version is pinned, and a re-pin is a change that goes through the Line.

### 12.5 Hand admission

A hand is admitted by five checkable properties, and the test is run by something that is not a model, which is why the fiftieth hand costs what the first did.

1. Its reversibility and blast radius are **declared**, in the four currencies, on the grant rather than at the call site.
2. It has a **dry branch** that performs no side effect, and dry is the default; the real thing is a separate, explicit argument. A mistyped flag must refuse, never silently perform the non-dry action — this exact bug existed in this repository and was fixed in August.
3. Its **credential is narrower than the hand** — a scoped key, not the account key.
4. There is a **probe** that tests what a dispatched worker can actually touch, run in the environment the worker will actually run in. Capability is measured, not asserted: fact 1 in §5.1 is the entire reason. A flag that everybody believed restricted the tool surface restricted nothing, for months, in a repository whose whole thesis was that mechanisms must be checked.
5. It has a **counter and a ceiling**, and the ceiling rejects.

Hands are classed **day** (may be used while the founder is awake and reachable), **night** (may be used unattended), or **never** (exists, is configured, and may only be exercised by the founder in person). A domain registration and a paid-ads write are *never* hands for a long time, and saying so is not timidity — an ad account with an API and no cap is an unbounded spend that runs whether or not anyone is watching.

### 12.6 What the sandbox is and is not

The OS sandbox on this machine is armed: filesystem read-denies over the credential stores, write-scoping to the session root, and a fail-closed setting so that a command runs sandboxed or not at all. It is a **guardrail against accident, not containment against the agent** — there is a documented escape hatch, and a process holding a shell can strip its own guardrails by launching a child with different settings. Fact 4 makes this worse: the pre-tool hook matches command *strings*, and it once blocked a document for mentioning a command. A string matcher is a spell-checker with opinions.

The only tier a running process cannot clear is a **managed settings file the founder writes outside the repository**, and it does not exist yet. That is the single highest-value security item in this design and it is four lines of JSON in a directory the agent cannot reach.

So the honest posture: the sandbox catches mistakes, the profiles separate the trifecta, the mouth holds the credentials, and **none of it is containment**. Containment is the managed file plus the fact that the mouth is a program. Everything else is hygiene.

### 12.7 The classes that block from day one

Three things `git revert` does not undo, and they block before anything else is enforced: **a migration over real data, a deploy, and any edit to CONTINUO's own judging machinery.** To those I add three more that the prior rounds under-weighted because they are not code: **a spend, a publication, and a message to a named human.** All six are the founder's, always. Not because they are risky in the abstract but because a company dies of exactly these six and never of a bad function name.

## 13. The founder's environment

> *The agentic system that we are planning also includes the environment that I'm working in as a whiteboarder and the founder. The terminal, mission control, seeing, monitoring, giving tasks, seeing progress, doing everything from my phone or from my Mac, seeing visual representations of the agents working, an office-like simulation.*

There are four surfaces and they are not four views of one thing. Each answers a different question and each is designed against a different failure.

### 13.1 The office question, answered directly

The founder asked whether an office-like simulation is the right picture. The honest answer has two halves and I am giving both, because giving only the first would be a designer telling a founder they are wrong about what they want.

**As a control surface, no, and the evidence is one-sided.** Stanford's generative agents in their little town and the open-source agent villages that followed are architecture demonstrations whose spatial rendering was the *demo vehicle*, never a measured variable — nobody has published evidence that watching agents move around a floor plan improves a human's comprehension of what they are doing. Meanwhile the control-room literature is unanimous in the other direction: a high-performance interface is **greyscale by default with colour reserved almost entirely for the abnormal**, precisely so that the eye is pulled to what is wrong rather than having to scan a rainbow of everything-is-fine. An office simulation is the exact opposite of that. It spends its entire visual budget rendering normality.

And the failure it invites is measured and lethal. Alarm fatigue: 2,558,760 alarms in thirty-one days across seventy-seven intensive-care beds, **88.8% of the arrhythmia alarms false positives**, 187 audible alarms per bed per day *after* filtering, and a regulator counting 566 deaths from alarms that were ignored. A surface where everything is always moving trains its watcher to stop watching, and it does this fastest to the people who look at it most.

**As a thing to look at, yes, and it should be beautiful.** A founder who says *I want to see the agents working* is asking for three things, and two of them are legitimate and one is a trap. They want reassurance that it is alive and not stuck — an instrument answers that better than a picture. They want to catch something going wrong early — an instrument answers that too. And they want **the pleasure of watching something they built do work**, which is real, which is why people watch progress bars and pour-over coffee and CNC machines, and which no amount of dashboard rigour substitutes for.

So CONTINUO has a **theatre**: a live, quiet, genuinely lovely rendering of the tide — plies forking and dying, contact events landing, the cup filling and draining — that costs nothing, is never the place a decision is made, and is explicitly labelled as what it is. Watch it for as long as you like. Nothing in the system reads whether you did.

The rule underneath: **entertainment is a legitimate requirement and a disqualifying control surface.** Keeping them in separate windows is the whole trick, and the reason to be strict about it is that a beautiful surface will always win the argument against a useful one.

### 13.2 The bridge — the Mac

One screen. Not a dashboard of activity; **a queue of decisions**. If nothing needs deciding, the screen is nearly empty, and an empty bridge is the system working.

Three buckets, in this order, because order is the whole design: **Needs you** · **Under way** · **Since you last looked**. One line per item, greyscale, with colour used only in the first bucket. Each line is a single sentence that leads with the conclusion — the military BLUF convention, adopted institutionally for exactly this reason — never a status, never a percentage, never a name of an agent.

> *The pricing page converts 40% better at £39 than £29. Both are live. Which.*
> *Three of the nine positions are dead. Criteria you wrote on the 4th. Nothing needed.*
> *I stopped at 61% of the window because you usually start around now.*

**Two levels of disclosure and no more.** Glance, then open one item. Beyond two levels usability degrades, and a third level is where every agent dashboard in existence turns into a debugger.

**Position carries state.** This is the mechanism I stole from air traffic control and it is the best small idea in the whole study. A controller who has a concern about a flight does not send a message and does not raise an alert — they pull the paper strip half out of the rack. Everyone can see it. Nobody was interrupted. It costs zero attention until someone looks. In CONTINUO, a ply the system is uneasy about sits **offset in its row**, and that is the entire notification. There is no badge, no ping, no unread count. The founder's eye finds it or it waits.

**Fuses.** Every decision row carries a stated default and a clock. If the founder does not act, the default fires and the outcome is recorded as *fused* — a typed decision with a reason, not an unread item rotting in a queue. The default is always the reversible branch. Irreversible rows and first-contact rows **cannot** carry a fuse, which is the whole point: silence is a legitimate answer to *which of these two* and never a legitimate answer to *may I charge this card*. This is the best mechanism in STARTUP-OS and it converts the founder's inattention from a system failure into a recorded choice.

**Four verbs, and they are worth different amounts.** *Choose* between two built things — the highest-value action in the system, because it produces a labelled preference. *Annotate* — a sentence about why, worth almost as much. *Stop* — kills a ply and writes its obituary. *Redirect* — and a redirect is logged as a defect in the **brief**, not in the worker, because a worker that had to be redirected was told the wrong thing. There is deliberately no *approve*: CONTINUO asks preference, never permission, so a rubber stamp has nothing to land on.

### 13.3 The phone

The phone is a **thin mirror**, never a second runtime. It shows the *Needs you* bucket and nothing else, it can *choose*, *annotate* and *stop*, and it holds the recall button for the delay queue. That is the entire product. It is what shipped agent tooling has converged on independently, and it is right for the same reason the bridge is greyscale: a phone that shows you everything is a phone you stop looking at.

Voice is available on the phone and only on the *Needs you* bucket — push to talk, one sentence, and the machine **reads the instruction back before it binds**. Read-back is not politeness. It is the I-PASS discipline from clinical handover, where the *receiver* synthesises what they heard rather than the sender assuming it landed, and it produced a 23% reduction in medical errors across nine hospitals.

### 13.4 The terminal

The terminal is for when the founder wants to be *inside* the work rather than above it. It is not a control panel; it is a seat at the same table, sharing the same cup and the same record. What the founder does in a terminal session is an episode like any other, and the plies they open by hand are plies like any other. There is no separate mode and no separate memory. This matters more than it sounds: a system where the human's work and the machine's work live in different stores is a system that cannot learn from the human.

### 13.5 What wakes the founder, and the harder document

**Four conditions ring the bell, and nothing else does.** The Line, when a ply wants to do something that cannot be undone. Damage — an error rate, a churn event, a furious reply, an unexpected bill; **real harm, not a rule broken**. A kill criterion, one written on the day the bet opened and not invented now. And a contradiction, when an instrument disagrees with something the founder said out loud. Everything else raises a quiet signal and waits, and the founder gets to have a Tuesday.

One override, borrowed from the clinical early-warning scores: **a single dimension scoring at its maximum escalates on its own, regardless of the aggregate.** A composite health number that averages six dimensions will always average away the one that is on fire.

**And the document nobody writes: the do-not-wake list.** Every operation has an escalation list. Almost none have its inverse, and the inverse is harder to write and worth more — an enumeration, in advance, of what must *not* reach the founder. *A failing build. A ply that died. A competitor's blog post. A spend under fifty pounds. Anything at all between 22:00 and 07:00 that is not damage.* A captain's standing orders name the circumstances requiring the master to be called precisely so that everything else is unambiguously the watch officer's. Without the negative list, escalation drifts upward forever, because every individual escalation is defensible and nobody ever removes one.

### 13.6 The handover, signed

At the end of the tide the founder is in, CONTINUO writes tonight's **orders**: what it will spend the unattended tide on, what it expects to have by morning with a probability on each, and what it will do if blocked. Those probabilities are scored at the next slack, so its promises are calibrated within a fortnight, and a machine that says it is 70% likely to finish and is right 70% of the time is worth more than one that is enthusiastic.

This is a ship's Night Order Book, and the load-bearing part is not the writing. It is that a ship keeps **two** documents — permanent standing orders that never change, and a short dated page written fresh each evening for tonight only — and the officer taking the watch **signs** the night orders as read. Two artifacts, not one configuration file, and a signature that turns a broadcast into a handover. In CONTINUO the founder's signature is one tap and it is optional; the difference between a signed and unsigned night is recorded, and an unbroken run of unsigned nights is itself something the machine will eventually mention.

### 13.7 The day

**07:20.** The morning page, on the phone, at the table, fitting on a screen without scrolling — because a briefing you scroll is one you skim. The map: the different kinds of winner from the unattended tide, including the ones scoring badly today. Two or three choices, both branches built and live. Receipts for what was done without asking, each with an undo that works. One surprise, in plain language, with what it cost to learn. One number that moved and whether it moved for a reason. Under the heading the founder learns to read first: *things I got wrong.* The fifth's accounting: spent, and on what, or unspent, which is a failure. Eight to ten minutes.

**During the day, one channel.** Not seventeen notifications from seven agents. CONTINUO is one voice, and if it has two things to say it says the more important one. It is silent during a defended block, and the threshold for interrupting one is itself a scored prediction — *would they want to be interrupted for this* — which is wrong occasionally and finds out.

**The standing hour**, once a day, at whatever time the founder actually thinks well. Not a status meeting. CONTINUO brings the choices it could not afford to ask, the assumptions it made instead — filed in one place, reversible in one line — and the one region where its own calibration is worst, and asks for judgement specifically there. It is optimising for the most expensive input in the company and says so out loud.

**18:00.** *You're the bottleneck on three things. Two can wait. The payments decision has cost six days and I think you're avoiding it because you don't want to talk to their support. I can do everything up to the point where a human has to be on the phone.*

**Saturday.** Silent by design, because the calendar says so and because a system that cannot be told to leave you alone is not a colleague, it is a machine you will eventually switch off. The tides still run at whatever posture each concern carries. The phone shows one line at nine: *nothing needs you; here is the one thing I would show you if you asked.*

**Once a week, the reverse seal.** CONTINUO asks the founder to predict *it*: before you open this, what do you think I am going to recommend on the trial length? They guess. It opens. A working relationship is two models of each other, and the founder's model of the machine is what decides whether they read its output or rubber-stamp it. Making that model explicit and scored is the only defence against the failure that eventually kills every autonomous system, which is a human who has stopped actually looking. Note what it is *not* connected to: in CONTINUO this changes no permission and unlocks no autonomy (§11.6). If the founder stops choosing, the consequence is that CONTINUO **says so**, in the morning page, in plain words, and keeps saying it.

## 14. Every job a company needs

> *Running a company, or building a startup, or doing any kind of project needs a lot of knowledge in so many different fields: to set up a database, to work with those tools correctly, to not leak the API keys, auto design system, auto design product, auto communicate with the customer, auto create a marketing video, auto promote it. How are the agents going to handle all of them, as a company, as an organisation? Is it a harness for vibe-coding with AI agents, or an organisation?*

**Neither, and the third answer is the useful one.** A harness is a thing you drive. An organisation is a set of people with titles. CONTINUO is neither: it is **the list of things a company must do or die, each owing evidence on a clock**. No job in the table below has an agent. Every one of them has an instrument, a class, a current honest grade, and a named thing that would raise it.

This is the coverage map. **Autonomy** is what CONTINUO may do alone *today*, given the instruments that exist today. Nothing here is aspirational and nothing is graded generously.

| **CONTINUO's own machinery** | Its own history: every past decision and the outcome that followed | Hard, but self-referential | **Proposes only.** A change to CONTINUO is a ply whose wager is that it beats the incumbent on the company's own held-out history, and nothing may edit the instrument that judged it | Nothing lifts this. It is Law 7 |

Three readings of that table, and they are the answer to the founder's question.

**About half the jobs a company does are already deterministically checkable**, and mostly by instruments the world gives away free. That is much more than people assume, and it is why breadth is affordable at all — not because a model is good at marketing, but because *does the link resolve, does the number reconcile, does the contrast ratio pass, does the unsubscribe link exist* are all decidable and all currently done by nobody.

**About a third are measurable but slow**, and the correct behaviour there is the one nobody has the discipline for: declare the clock in advance and refuse to conclude early. This is where a fast fake signal is most tempting and most destructive.

**And about a sixth are irreducibly the founder's** — what company this is, who to work with, what to refuse, what to sign. That sixth does not shrink with better models, because it is not a capability problem. **The measure of this system is not how much it does. It is how completely the founder's remaining time has migrated into that sixth.**

So: not a harness, and not an organisation. It is a company with no employees, whose org chart is a list of debts to reality, and whose only two scarce resources are a founder's judgement and a five-hour cup.

## 15. The door: how something from outside gets in

### 15.1 The rule

WAKE says Hands are **compiled from outcomes, never handwritten** — a lesson that paid rent long enough to become reflex. That rule is right about handwritten *knowledge* and wrong about the world, because it leaves the system unable to accept a gift. There are 177 catalogued open-source projects sitting on disk, verified against the GitHub API, and under WAKE's rule not one of them can be adopted until it has somehow paid rent internally first. Meanwhile the census found that **zero** of the 177 are cited by anything in the existing system. A door that nothing has ever come through is a wall.

So the door has one rule and it is the same rule as everything else in this document:

> **An outside part enters as a ply, never as a decision.** It arrives with a stated expectation, a probation, and the test that would prove it works. It pays rent or it is evicted, and the eviction is as cheap as the adoption.

Concretely, admission asks five questions, all answerable in an afternoon.

1. **What does this give us that we could not build in a day?** If the answer is nothing, take the mechanism and not the dependency. A hundred lines of Thompson sampling is not a dependency; a browser driver is.
2. **What is its licence, exactly, read from the LICENSE file?** Not from a badge. About a dozen of the 177 carry `Other` or `NOASSERTION`, and several carry no licence at all.
3. **What is its test here?** Not its own test suite — the check *we* run monthly that fails when it stops doing what we adopted it for.
4. **What breaks if it disappears?** Every adopted part gets an exit note on the day it arrives, naming what we would do without it. A dependency with no exit note is a hostage situation with a friendly face.
5. **Is it alive?** Last commit, open issues, whether the maintainer answers. Half a dozen of the catalogued projects are dormant research artifacts whose *ideas* are excellent and whose code is a trap.

**On licences, precisely, because the catalogue overstates it.** AGPL is not a blanket block. Running an unmodified AGPL tool internally as a CLI is fine; the obligation bites when you *distribute* it or offer a *modified* version over a network. So `trufflehog` as a local secret scanner is fine, and `firecrawl` self-hosted behind our own product is the case to look at carefully. No licence at all is a genuine block — an unlicensed repository grants nothing, whatever its README says. `NOASSERTION` means somebody must read the file.

### 15.3 The distinction that row four of *Obligations* is making

It is worth pulling out, because it is the clearest statement of the two-players idea from §2 and it took a research pass to see.

**Durable execution engines — Temporal, Restate, DBOS — guarantee faithful re-execution of the same code against a recorded history. They exist to make divergence impossible.** Temporal treats any non-determinism between code and history as an error, which is why it needs explicit versioning APIs before you may change a workflow at all.

That is the *exact opposite* of what a ply wants. A ply is a speculative branch: different decisions, same history, see what happens. Pointing a durable-execution engine at plies would be using recovery infrastructure to do counterfactual simulation, and it would fight you every step.

But it is *precisely* what obligations want. A refund that must happen exactly once. A renewal that must not be missed. A reply that is owed by Thursday. Those are the bass line — the part you play whether or not you feel inspired — and they should run on machinery that guarantees they complete exactly once even if the Mac dies mid-act.

> **Durable execution for the bass. Speculation for the realisation. Two players on one line, and they need different instruments.**

### 15.4 What I refuse from the catalogue, by name

**`FoundationAgents/MetaGPT`** — its role model is the thing this whole design argues against, and taking its message pool would reintroduce worker-to-worker chatter that the star topology deliberately forecloses. **`SuperClaude-Org/SuperClaude_Framework`** — cognitive personas, which is assigned dissent with better branding. **`jennyzzt/dgm`** — an agent that rewrites its own codebase, which is the productised form of the one class this system blocks from day one. **`OpenBMB/ChatDev`** — builds toy apps and demos convincingly. **`yoheinakajima/babyagi`** — no licence at all; read the 140 lines and depend on nothing. **`joonspk-research/generative_agents`, `google-deepmind/funsearch`, `zou-group/textgrad`** — read for the idea, never run; all dormant, all research artifacts. And **`Azure/PyRIT`**, whose returned record does not match the tool everyone cites, which is a good reason to stop rather than guess.

And one refusal that is not a repository: **synthetic customers**, which VIGIL wanted and WAKE refused and I refuse with it. The failure is structural rather than a quality problem — suspiciously low variance where real humans are noisy, roughly half of the between-variable correlations wrong while the headline numbers look fine, and persona collapse toward a stereotype even under instruction to diversify. A system that simulates your customers to save you the trouble of meeting them is the most seductive wrong turn available.

## 16. Where everything lives, and what survives a dead machine

### 16.1 The shape

One rule decides the layout: **the record is the only thing that is true, and everything else is a derived cache** (§8.1).

| What | Where | Form | Writer | If lost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **The record** | `record/YYYY-MM/*.jsonl` | Append-only typed events, one per line, never edited | Every process, append-only | The company's memory is gone. This is the only irreplaceable thing |
| **Artifacts** | the git repositories | Whatever they are — code, copy, renders, decks | Makers, in their own worktrees | Recoverable from remotes |
| **The Book, the map, the Hands, the model of the founder** | `derived/` | Whatever is fastest to read | The ebb phase only | **Rebuild from the record.** Costs one tide |
| **Indexes** | SQLite, WAL mode | Tables over the record for the queries that need to be fast | The ebb phase only | Rebuild in minutes |
| **The figures** | `figures/<concern>.yml` | Short, human-written, human-read | The founder, by hand or by voice | Ask the founder. They are one page |
| **Credentials** | Not here at all | In the OS keychain and the mouth's own store, never in the repository, never in the record | The founder | §16.4, which is the real subject of this section |

Two properties worth naming. **One writer per store**, always, because a file with two writers is a file with a race nobody will find until it matters. And **nothing is deleted to meet a cap** — archives rotate into new volumes and each volume is capped independently, because a cap on the lifetime total of an append-only decision log is a mechanism for losing decisions.

### 16.2 The durability traps, which are not the obvious ones

**On macOS, `fsync()` does not mean what you think.** Apple's own manual page says the drive "may not physically write the data to the platters for quite some time" after `fsync()` returns; real durability requires the `F_FULLFSYNC` fcntl, which neither git nor SQLite calls by default. So a power cut during a commit can lose a just-written object on a machine where every layer believed it had flushed. For the record — the one irreplaceable store — the write path uses `F_FULLFSYNC`, and it is the only place worth paying for it.

**Git is not a blob store and not a backup.** It warns at 50 MiB per file, blocks at 100, and repacks automatically at 6,700 loose objects. Renders, video and datasets do not go in it; they go beside it with a hash in the record.

**SQLite in WAL mode does not work over a network filesystem**, because WAL needs shared memory across processes on one host. And a long-lived reader starves the checkpoint, so the WAL grows unbounded — which is exactly what a dashboard holding a query open will do.

**And event sourcing's own failure is replay time.** Without periodic snapshots, rebuild cost grows linearly with history forever. So the ebb writes a snapshot of the derived rooms each tide, and the rebuild-from-scratch path is exercised on a schedule rather than trusted.

### 16.3 Keeping it running on a Mac

Five facts that decide the shape, all from Apple's own documentation.

**A LaunchAgent dies at logout; a LaunchDaemon does not.** CONTINUO's supervisor is a daemon.

**`KeepAlive` with `SuccessfulExit` on a script that finishes its work and exits zero is an infinite loop**, throttled to one launch every ten seconds. This is the single most common way a well-meant "keep it running" configuration becomes a busy loop, and it is why the tick is a *scheduled* job that exits, never a kept-alive one.

**`StartInterval` coalesces missed firings.** A Mac that slept through four ticks fires once on wake, not four times. That is the behaviour we want, and it is also why the tide, not the tick, is the unit of accounting.

**`caffeinate -s` only works on AC power, and a closed lid on battery sleeps regardless of any assertion.** So the honest statement about twenty-four-seven on a laptop is: it is twenty-four-seven while the lid is open or the machine is on power with an external display. Anything else is a machine that sleeps and catches up.

**A restart policy needs a ceiling.** Erlang's supervisors have had the right answer since the 1990s: a maximum restart intensity — N restarts in T seconds — after which the supervisor itself terminates and escalates rather than looping forever. `launchd` has no such ceiling beyond its ten-second throttle, so CONTINUO's supervisor implements one, and exceeding it is one of the four things that ring the bell.

And one thing `KeepAlive` structurally cannot catch: **a hang.** A process that is stuck but alive never exits, so no exit-based policy ever fires. That needs a separate heartbeat, and it needs the process-group kill from fact 8 — a timeout that kills a child while its grandchild runs on is a timeout that does nothing.

### 16.4 What does not survive, which is the real answer

Everything above is a solved problem: `restic` or `borg`, client-side encrypted, deduplicated, one copy off-site, three copies on two devices. Realistic recovery point is an hour if the record ships continuously; realistic recovery *time* is hours to days, and the reason it is days rather than minutes has nothing to do with data.

**These cannot be restored from any backup, of any kind:**

- **OAuth refresh tokens.** They are a grant held by the authorisation server, bound to a client and a user and a scope. Many providers rotate them on use, so even a backed-up token is likely already dead. Recovery is re-running the consent flow, in a browser, as a human, once per service.
- **Device-bound credentials** — passkeys and hardware keys. The private key never leaves the secure element by design, so a full-disk image categorically cannot contain it.
- **Two-factor seeds**, unless they were deliberately exported at enrolment, which almost nobody does.
- **Domain and DNS control**, which reduces to registrar account access and is the one true single point of failure in a small company. Losing it is a support ticket measured in weeks.

So the disaster-recovery plan for CONTINUO is not a backup plan. It is a **credential plan**, and the standard practice is unglamorous and works: a password manager as the single source of truth, its own emergency kit printed on paper and stored physically, hardware keys registered in *pairs* with the second one off-site, and — for the handful of secrets that unlock everything else — a k-of-n split so that no single share reveals anything.

**And the standard failure is worth naming because it is so easy to commit:** escrowing the vault and not the second factor that protects the vault. That does not remove the single point of failure. It relocates it and makes you feel better.

**The recovery drill is a ply.** Twice a year, on a machine that is not this one, CONTINUO is rebuilt from the record and the escrow, and the drill produces a number — how long it took and what could not be restored. A recovery procedure that has never been run is a document, not a capability, and the difference is only discoverable on the day you need it.

### 16.5 Should it live on this Mac at all

Honestly: moving to a small always-on machine — a few pounds a month for a VPS with a real `systemd` — solves every problem in §16.3 outright. No lid, no battery, no power management, no coalesced ticks.

It does not solve §16.4. It relocates it, and it makes the blast radius worse: a laptop that is asleep most of the day is a smaller target than a box that is awake and reachable every second, holding the credentials to a person's whole business.

The resolution is the split the design already implies. **The mouth and the record belong on the always-on machine, because obligations must complete and the record must never be lost. The makers belong wherever they are cheapest, and they hold no credentials by construction.** The founder's Mac becomes what it should have been from the start: a client, a very good one, that can go to sleep without the company stopping.

## 17. How it gets better

Four learning loops, and they are all the same machinery pointed at different things.

| Loop | Signal | Written by | Cadence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Beliefs** | A prediction reality contradicted | The ebb | Every tide |
| **Skills** | Rent — load-bearing in repeated successes | The ebb | Every tide; the library's tests re-run monthly |
| **Context** | Attribution: which items were present when a ply passed its instrument first time | The ebb | Every tide |
| **Price** | The gap between a move's estimated cost and information gain and its actual | The slack | Every tide |

The fourth is the one nobody builds and it is what makes the first three affordable, because an auction with an uncalibrated pricer allocates confidently and wrongly forever.

**The honest context on self-improvement, because the field is full of claims.** Across 27 papers and 19 benchmarks, additional scaffolding does *not* consistently improve reliability, and systems that iteratively rewrite their own prompts and tools frequently confuse modification activity with capability. What does buy reliability is measurement that hurts: a number attached to a component that goes down when the component is wrong, with a consequence attached to the number.

So three rules govern any change to CONTINUO itself.

**A change must beat the company's own history.** CONTINUO holds a private benchmark nothing else in the world can have: every past decision it made and the outcome that followed, hundreds of items, made entirely of this company. A proposed change is a ply whose wager is that it beats the incumbent on that held-out set. Not *it seems better*. A number, on this company's real history, or nothing.

**Nothing edits the instrument that judged it** — not that tide, not through a child, not by "improving the test." Instrument changes happen in daylight, reviewed against the reason the instrument existed.

**And a post-mortem's output is a mechanism, or a counted "none."** Every death ends with a column naming what changed as a result. "Nothing" is a permitted answer and it is *counted*, so a failure class with three "none"s escalates by arithmetic rather than by somebody remembering. A pattern is promoted to a Hand at **three** independent sightings, never at the first — which is, in reverse, exactly how a library of 134 skills came to contain skills that nothing cites.

**The one number that says whether any of this is working: founder interventions per surviving artifact.** Redirects, rejections and rework, divided by the artifacts that survived contact with the world. It cannot be gamed by producing more, because the denominator is survivorship. It should fall.

## 18. What it costs, honestly

### 18.1 The number nobody has

Two competent reviewers priced this machine's predecessor within days of each other and **diverged by a factor of ten**: one estimated $74 a month at forty moves a night, the other $1,300–1,700 at forty-eight. Neither was careless. The divergence is the finding, and its cause is a single assumption: **the cache hit rate**, which sets whether context costs 0.1× or 1.25× per token, on the 89% of the bill that is context (§9).

I am not going to pick a number between them. I am going to say what determines it, because that is more useful:

```
cost per tide  ≈  (trunk bytes)                      × (1.25 on the first ply of a batch)
                +  (trunk bytes) × (siblings − 1)    × (0.10 on every sibling that hits)
                +  (divergence bytes per ply)        × (siblings)
                +  (output)                          × (11% of the historical bill)

Dominant term, by a distance: whether siblings hit the cache.
Fails if: the tick is 300s against a 300s TTL (fact 7), or capability
          profiles vary per ply (§9.2), or the trunk is regenerated.
```

That is why §9 is the longest section about money in this document and §18 is the shortest.

### 18.2 What is actually scarce

Under a subscription, dollars are not the binding constraint — **the cup is** (§5.2). There is no published denominator for the five-hour window, only multipliers and a reset, which means the honest measure is the one already on disk: the transcripts, parsed by something like `ccusage`, giving real consumption per tide with no instrumentation to build.

And one thing that is a founder decision and not an agent's, stated plainly because it has been sitting unresolved: **unattended headless operation on a consumer subscription is an open terms-of-service question.** The Consumer Terms prohibit automated access except via an API key or where otherwise explicitly permitted. That is not a technical blocker and it is not mine to resolve. It is one sentence for the founder to check before the first unattended tide runs, and the consequence of getting it wrong is losing the account, which would take the company with it.

### 18.3 The three rules that survive whatever the number is

**Model by instrument, not by importance** (§9.3). **Batch anything that does not need to be conversational**, for a flat 50% at every vendor. And **siblings share a spine** — you do not save tokens by doing less, you save them by making every parallel world share a trunk and by making the things you know best the cheapest things to know.

Two numbers are reported weekly whether or not they flatter, and both must fall: **dollars per entry in the Book**, and **founder-minutes per unit of output**.

## 19. What it would take to exist

Nothing here needs a model that does not exist in September 2026. What is missing is the arrangement, and a short list of genuinely hard things. In three buckets, honestly.

### 19.1 Buildable now; engineering, and the hard part is plumbing

The tide, the tick at 240 seconds, and the cup meter parsed from transcripts already on disk. The auction and its pricer — successive halving, Thompson sampling and expected-improvement are each decades old and each a few hundred lines. The figures, the postures, and the three-openers rule. The record as an append-only log with `F_FULLFSYNC`, and the derived rooms rebuilt from it. The Book's write path, Brier per domain, and retraction that recomputes downstream. The graveyard with reproducing commands and expiries. Compiled contexts with a cache-stable trunk and helpfulness counters. The six capability profiles, measured by a probe rather than asserted. The mouth, the delay queue, the recall button, the two-key rule, the named-human register. The map as a quality-diversity archive. The fifth. Red plies. The morning page, the bridge, the fuse, the do-not-wake list, the night orders. Borrowed instruments — most of that table is an afternoon each. And the managed settings file, which is the highest-value four lines in the whole design.

### 19.2 Needs the trend to hold, which it plausibly will

Plies that hold multi-hour scope without degrading, which follows from the time-horizon curve if it keeps doubling — the measured shape today is near-certain success under four minutes and worse than 10% past four hours, so the system is built out of short plies and will simply get better if that changes. Prefix economics holding at the scale of thirty siblings a tide against a real bill, which is why §18 refuses to guess and measures on day one. Instrument-forging being mostly automatable; the honest split is perhaps 80% machine and 20% founder, permanently, with the 20% being the founder saying *no, that is not what good looks like* about the instrument itself. A second model family reachable for a genuine panel, which is a procurement problem rather than a research one and which one adopted router (§15.2) probably settles.

### 19.3 Genuinely unsolved, and one of them I think I can dissolve

**Forking a company — and I believe this is the wrong project.** Both prior rounds named it as the real engineering challenge: fork the database, the deploy, the mailbox, the third-party accounts, act speculatively, throw it away. The research is unambiguous that this does not exist. Copy-on-write database branching is genuinely instant and cheap on one Postgres. Machine snapshotting forks compute and explicitly does not fork live network state. Vendor sandboxes range from excellent (payments, one commerce platform) through cosmetic (messaging, email — they validate the call and tell you nothing about whether it would have worked) to absent (advertising). And durable-execution engines, the closest thing to replay infrastructure, are built to make divergence *impossible*.

The dissolution is to notice that the goal is incoherent rather than merely hard. **A company's relationship with the world has no branches.** You cannot have sent and not sent an email; you cannot have charged and not charged a card. Forking the world means forking other people, and there is no engineering behind that door.

So CONTINUO does not fork the company. **It forks the artifact and touches the world once.** Nine landing pages exist; one gets traffic. Six emails are written; one is sent, thirty minutes later, recallable. Three schemas are built and tested against a branched copy; one is applied. The speculation is real, wide and cheap — it happens on *things*, which fork perfectly — and the world stays singular, touched exactly once, by a process with no model in it, after selection. The delay queue is what makes this more than a slogan: it moves the moment of commitment *after* the artifact exists, so the choice is made between finished things rather than between plans.

**The remaining genuinely open problems, including the one this design creates.**

*The pricer is my system's characteristic failure, and I want it named rather than buried.* WAKE's failure mode is over-generation — a night that produces more than anyone can judge. CONTINUO's is mis-allocation: if the pricer is systematically wrong, the machine confidently spends its whole cup on the wrong things, every downstream number looks healthy, and nothing in the design notices, because everything downstream is measuring quality rather than choice. The mitigation is that the price is a scored prediction (§5.4) and that the auction prints its arithmetic so a founder can see what beat what. That is a mitigation, not a solution.

*The reserve is set against an unpublished denominator.* Nobody outside the vendor knows what the five-hour window actually is; there are multipliers and a reset and no number. So the reserve is calibrated from observed consumption, which means it is wrong in the first fortnight and improves.

*The session limit can stop a ply mid-sentence, and it stops all of them at once.* Every ply therefore checkpoints to a durable artifact and resumes from the checkpoint rather than from wherever the reasoning had drifted. This is the crash-only discipline, and its virtue is that the recovery path is the *only* path and therefore gets exercised constantly.

*Does the model of the founder flatter?* Reduced by refusing it any authority (§11.6), not eliminated. A machine that keeps saying *I thought you'd pick that* is still teaching someone something about themselves.

*A founder who stops choosing.* A rubber stamp and an agreement are the same event, and the machine cannot tell them apart. The reverse seal and the response rate are defences, and they are not strong ones.

*Reversal-cost estimation.* Wrong expensive, CONTINUO becomes a nag and is ignored, which is the worse long-run failure. Wrong cheap, it does something that cannot be taken back. It starts conservative, earns per class of action, and keeps a visible ledger — which is a mitigation and not a solution.

*Fields with no ground truth* stay taste-only forever and never ship silently. That is a limitation, stated, not a design.

*Compounding error.* Short plies and frequent ground contact push the half-life out. They do not repeal it.

*Defending the dark zone*, which is harder than anything above and is not technical. Every competent engineer who works on this will want to put a check in the ungated region, and will be right about the individual case every single time and wrong about the system.

*And the first two hours.* If the founder is not honest on day zero, everything above is built on sand, and the machine has no way to detect it.

## 20. The first week, with a founder who has only an idea

**Monday, 06:41.** They say twenty seconds into their phone while the kettle boils. A small, real annoyance in a trade they know: independent bakeries throw away something like a fifth of what they bake, everybody knows it, nobody measures it, and the ones who do measure it guess. No code, no customers, no name.

**Monday, two hours, and CONTINUO does not interview them.** Founders are unreliable narrators of their own taste and a questionnaire produces the answers people think they should give. So it asks them to **judge**: forty artifacts around the idea — three prices, six names, two landing pages, a competitor's actual homepage, four positioning statements — and watches what they reject and how fast. Then it asks about decisions already made, where the stakes were paid years ago: things they quit, things they refused, the last three products they paid for and cancelled and why, what would embarrass them.

At the end it asks the one question nobody asks: *what would have to be true in six months for you to stop?* The answer goes down verbatim, with the date. That sentence is now a standing job.

And then the part that is CONTINUO rather than any prior design: **it writes the figure and reads it back in six lines.** Posture **Drive**. Appetite 0.8. Ceiling 40% of any tide. Reserve 25%, defended, because they said they work in the mornings. One refusal — *no paid acquisition until forty organic signups a week* — with its reopen trigger. The stop sentence. The founder changes one number and that is the entire configuration of the system, for the life of the venture.

**Before lunch,** CONTINUO reads the twenty seconds back as **seven beliefs**, ranked not by importance but by *load* — if this one is false, how much else collapses. Then it says the thing that defines the system, which is what it cannot judge. *Of your seven, I can judge one today and it is true, I settled it in an hour. Two are Reactive and I can have instruments by Thursday. Four are Blind, and the only instrument for those is people, so I have started finding forty of them.* The Book's first entry is about the founder rather than the market, which is correct, because in a one-person company that is where the variance is.

**Monday, first flood.** Sixty offers into the auction. It buys nine. The seventh — *build the product* — loses to a research ply costing a twentieth as much, and CONTINUO says so on the bridge in one line: *I am not building anything this week. Here is what beat it.* This is the moment the founder either trusts the system or does not, and it is worth being blunt about, because every other system they have used would have started building.

What it does buy: an inventory of the field's **borrowed instruments**, which is nearly free and turns out to be the week's best purchase. Baking has a measurable ground truth sitting in every point-of-sale system in the country. Waste percentage is arithmetic. That single finding moves two of the seven beliefs from Blind to Reactive before Tuesday.

**Monday night, the tide with nobody in it.** Sixty framings of what this even is, unfiltered, nothing permitted to have an opinion. Then contact: which framings survive what already exists in the world — does this exist, who bought it, what did they actually pay, who tried it and died. Four fields apprenticed at once. Nine hundred artifacts read. Four discriminators built, four honest numbers reported, one of them 0.52, which means **no confident claims about buying behaviour this week** and CONTINUO says so rather than producing confident copy anyway. The fifth is spent on three framings it is certain the founder will hate. Cost: less than a good dinner.

**Tuesday morning.** A map of eleven versions of the company, two already dead with the evidence attached. The founder is not asked to review eleven; they are shown two and asked *which*. The other nine keep their cells. CONTINUO seals which one it thinks they will pick. They pick the other. First Brier point — and it changes no permission, because in CONTINUO that number never buys authority (§11.6). It buys one thing: a slightly better guess about what not to bother them with tomorrow.

**Tuesday and Wednesday.** The founder picks a region of the map. CONTINUO builds the thinnest real thing that can produce a signal, which is not the product — it is the thing that gets a stranger to reply. Nine positions become nine staged landing pages on real URLs, which is reversible in all four currencies and therefore never asked about. On Wednesday the first instrument goes live: a real conversion number against real traffic, small and noisy and honest. **The Bench goes from zero to one**, and from that evening copy leaves the founder's attention budget, and the unattended tide is worth forty minutes more than it was.

**Thursday. The first genuine irreversibility, and the founder makes the calls.** Of forty humans CONTINUO found, eleven answered and six will talk. It wanted to send an invitation to twenty real people and **stopped, once**, with the exact text and the count and the sentence *this reaches twenty people who did not ask to hear from you.* That was the only hard stop of the week, and because it was the only one, the founder read it properly.

It writes the eleven-question script, tunes it after the third call because two questions were dead, listens with consent, and by evening has fourteen candidate beliefs — six with real support and eight flagged *one person said this once.* And the first real surprise: CONTINUO had predicted at 0.7 that waste is what bakers rank first. Four of six put staffing first, and two describe a spreadsheet they already maintain and hate. **The Book takes its first heavy entry.** Three of the nine positions die on criteria written on Monday, without a conversation and without anyone having to be brave.

**Friday.** Build starts, because now there is something worth building and a person who said what it is. It is hard-oracle territory, so it runs unattended and merges itself. And something goes out: a landing page, a waitlist, a two-minute demo, and copy using the words the bakers actually used rather than the words the category uses. Nine variants, discriminator-selected, staged behind the founder's hand. They look for six minutes and ship one. Every word of it traces to a sentence a specific human said on Thursday.

**Saturday.** Silent. The tides still run. In one of them a red ply survives for the first time and argues, with citations, that the premise is wrong: waste is a symptom, the disease is that a baker's day starts at four in the morning and no software that requires attention at 4am has ever been adopted by one, and two funded companies died on exactly that rock. It is not asking anyone to quit. It is delivering, on day six, the thing that usually takes nine months and a seed round. It waits for Monday, because Saturday is Saturday.

**Sunday, the first accounting, and it is not a status report.**

> This week I learned to judge four things I could not judge on Monday: whether a page converts, whether a build is correct, whether waste is being measured honestly, and whether a layout matches the system we wrote on Wednesday. That took my unattended window from twenty minutes to about five hours.
>
> I made 34 predictions; 19 have resolved. Brier 0.31. I am well calibrated about engineering and badly calibrated about what people will pay, so next week I want more of your attention on price and less on product, and I have prepared three choices about price accordingly.
>
> Of 61 tides' worth of offers, I bought 84 and declined 703. The biggest thing I declined every single day was building the product, and I still think that was right. Ask me on Friday.
>
> I used 31% of your window across the week and never once went into your reserve. Four entries in the Book. A fifth of what I made, you would have hated; one of those is on the map and it is the one the red ply attacked from.
>
> Thirty-one decisions reached you. I predicted twenty-two. I read you on copy, I do not read you on scope, and I am badly overconfident on anything to do with design. From next week I will stop *asking* you about the four kinds I now get right. I will still not *do* them.

By Sunday the founder has written no spec, answered about fifteen questions, and owns a landing page, a waitlist, fourteen beliefs with provenance, four instruments, a written case against their own idea, and a machine that has never once done anything they could not undo.

## 21. The year

The shape of twelve months is the shape of one week at increasing radius.

**The Bench goes from zero to somewhere near forty instruments, and the unattended tides lengthen in step** — twenty minutes on day one to a genuinely useful five hours by autumn. Nobody adds an agent. Nobody edits a permission. The freedom is derived: it is a direct function of how many classes of thing the machine learned to judge.

**The Book stays small, which is the surprising part.** Perhaps sixty live beliefs at twelve months, because confirmed predictions compress and rent evicts what is never load-bearing. A founder can read the whole of what their company knows in a quarter of an hour. The graveyard is the opposite — hundreds of cells, most holding something dead with an honest obituary, every new bet checked against them first, and twice in the year something that died in spring alive again in autumn because the Book moved underneath it.

**The fleet forms without anyone deciding to have one.** By month four there are three concerns in Drive or Pursue, four in Watch, and two in Still, and the founder changes a posture roughly once a fortnight — usually with one word, from a phone, usually on a Sunday. The concerns share one model of the founder, a growing library of Hands, and the cup. They share nothing else, and the first time a belief tries to migrate between two of them it is refused with its provenance attached, which is the moment the quarantine stops being theoretical.

**The declines become the interesting artifact.** By month six the log of what the auction refused is more informative than the log of what it bought, because it is a record of what the company thought was worth less than something else, dated, with its arithmetic. No other system produces this, and it turns out to be what the founder reads when they are trying to work out whether they have been avoiding something.

**And the founder's forty minutes migrate.** In week one those minutes are spent on the product. By month nine CONTINUO gets the product right without asking, and every one of those minutes is spent on the sixth of §14 that no instrument will ever exist for: who to work with, what to refuse, whether this is still the company they want to be running. **The measure is not how much the machine does. It is how completely the founder's remaining time has moved into the part that was always theirs.**

It gets quieter every month and stranger every month, and those turn out to be the same fact.

## 22. Four kinds of concern, worked

The founder named four kinds of thing they do, and asked how one system handles all of them without becoming a machine that grinds every project at the same speed forever. The figure (§6) is the whole answer, and here is what each looks like written out.

**A startup that must move forward always.** Posture **Drive**, high appetite, a real ceiling, and the machine may open goals by inheritance. It works every tide it wins. Its stop sentence is live and it is the only concern where the fifth is spent at full rate, because a startup is the only kind of concern where being surprising is worth more than being right. This is what CONTINUO looks like when it is doing what it is for.

**A project that walks with the founder and waits.** Posture **Beside**. It opens nothing, makes nothing unasked, and spends a thin standing share on preparation only — keeping its trunk warm, its research current, its questions listed. When the founder says one sentence on a Thursday afternoon, the answer is already three quarters written. The founder experiences this as a system that is *fast*, and never as a system that is *busy*. It is the posture I would expect a founder to use most, and the one no prior design can express.

**A piece of research.** Posture **Pursue** on one goal, with the crucial difference that its judge is almost always slow. So its plies are never cut early by successive halving, its clock is declared before it starts, and a sourcer's finding enters the Book only as a diff against what the company believed this morning — a confirming source compressed to a tally, a contradicting one entering with its URL, its date and the exact quoted sentence that did the damage. A research concern's output is not a report. It is the list of things the company stopped believing.

**A client's work.** Posture **Pursue**, and structurally different from all the others because most of what it contains are **obligations** rather than goals: things owed, with dates, opened by the world (§6.3). These run on durable execution (§15.3), because a deliverable that must complete exactly once and cannot be quietly dropped is the bass line, not the improvisation. A client concern also carries the tightest identity quarantine in the fleet: its register of named humans, its voice, and its credentials touch nothing else, and its record is separable — because the day the engagement ends, the client is entitled to their part of it and to nothing of anyone else's.

## 24. Why nobody has seen this

Seven things, and each of them looks either wasteful or timid on any single day.

**Everyone treats tokens as an external cost.** A bill somebody else pays later. Here the founder and every process drink from one five-hour cup, which is a measured fact about this account and not a metaphor, and it means every action the machine takes is taken directly out of the founder's own hands. That single inversion forces a price onto everything, and **a price is the only instrument that can compare two things nobody has ever seen.** A rule cannot. A priority list cannot. That is why every other system either grinds continuously or waits to be told, and none of them can do the third thing, which is to decide that nothing is worth doing right now.

**Nobody has a representation for a project that should sit still.** Every agent system in the world is on or off. The founder asked, twice, for something that walks beside them without walking ahead, and posture is the thing that makes *waiting* a mode with a budget rather than the absence of a mode. Preparation is always free; production is what posture governs.

**Nobody separates *may I* from *is it worth it*.** These fail differently — one recoverable and visible, one asymmetric and not — and every design I have read collapses them into a single permission question, which is why they all end up as stopping mechanisms. A stopping mechanism is what you get when the only question a system can ask about an action is whether it is allowed.

**Everyone's quality mechanism only subtracts.** Reviews, gates, verdicts, lints, tiers. Each individually correct; their sum converges on inaction. The germinal centre is the only quality architecture that generates, and it works precisely because one of its two zones is *forbidden to have an opinion*.

**Everyone writes permission down in advance** — an allowlist, a tier table, an approval matrix. That is a guess about competence made before any competence was observed, never updated, too strict about what the machine turned out to be good at and too loose about what it turned out to be bad at. CONTINUO never refuses. It prices, and it computes the one line it will not cross from what the act costs to undo.

**Nobody reserves capacity for being wrong.** The fifth is a permanent, unspendable tax on the system's own competence. On any given night it is indefensible. Over a year it is the only reason the thing is still worth opening in the morning.

**And nobody asks where an instrument's errors go.** This is the principle I would most want to survive this document, because it decides where everything else is allowed to sit: **trust an instrument only where being wrong announces itself.** A model that wrongly stays quiet gets told so, for free, by the person who noticed the silence. A model that wrongly acts is never corrected, because nobody ever learns what they would have chosen instead. Suppression errors are self-revealing; action errors are self-concealing; and that asymmetry, rather than any calibration score, is what should decide what a machine is permitted to do on its own.

---

Underneath all seven is one habit, and it is the whole system in a sentence: **say what you expect before you act, keep only the times reality disagreed, and put a price on everything so that most of the time you can afford to do nothing.**

A machine that has to be right in advance, in writing, about a world that gets to answer back — and that is quiet enough to be worth listening to when it finally says something.
