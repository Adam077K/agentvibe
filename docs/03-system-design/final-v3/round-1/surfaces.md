## Headline

The surfaces should render the OWNER'S EXPOSURE and the work's NEGATIVE SPACE, not activity — every shipped precedent I found (alarm systems, NOC dashboards, agent-observability UIs, tmux grids, kanban-of-agents) renders throughput, and the two best-documented catastrophes in this shape (Knight Capital, Milford Haven) both had a surface that was showing everything and telling nobody anything.

**Fields covered:** 21, 22, 12, 46, 24, 42

## Claims

### 1. [reasoned]

FIELD 21 — THE FIRST 30 SECONDS. The morning page leads with negative space and exposure, and activity is below the fold. Top block, in this order: (1) EXPOSURE OUTSTANDING — irreversible acts taken in your name since you last looked, money that left, words that reached strangers, other people's data touched; this is a STOCK, not a flow, and every dashboard I found renders flow. (2) WHAT IT DID NOT DO — skipped, stale, assumed, could-not-determine, chose-not-to-tell-you-and-why. (3) THE STRANGER CLOCK — 'N days since anything we made reached a real stranger', rendered larger than any other number on the surface, because the vision's own success measure is attempts reaching strangers and every other metric on a dashboard rewards interesting work drifting away from a customer. This is technically free here: `scripts/lib/usage.js` already implements `sinceLastArtifact()` and `windowUsage()` over the runtime's own transcript corpus, and `scripts/lib/events.js` is an append-only JSONL event log — so a surface needs no new instrumentation to start, only a different choice of what to put at the top.

**Basis:** Read /Users/adamks/VibeCoding/agentvibe/.worktrees/ceo-1-1788609834/scripts/lib/usage.js and scripts/lib/events.js (sinceLastArtifact, windowUsage, append-only logEvent); contrast with https://sre.google/sre-book/monitoring-distributed-systems/ (alert on symptoms, keep causes on dashboards) — reasoned inversion

### 2. [reasoned]

FIELD 21 — HOW A SURFACE STOPS CLAIMING MORE CERTAINTY THAN EXISTS: make the TYPOGRAPHY carry the epistemic claim, and make UNKNOWN a first-class rendered state. Three visual registers, never mixed: MEASURED (a command ran, here is its exit code and argv) / RECORDED (this is in the log, here is the as-of timestamp) / NARRATED (a model wrote this sentence). A model-written sentence may never sit in the same face, weight or colour as a number produced by a command. Three liveness states, visually distinct and decaying: LIVE (socket open, heartbeat <5s), RECENT (derived from the log, stamped 'as of HH:MM'), RECONSTRUCTED (regenerated narrative). A tile that cannot prove its freshness GREYS OUT rather than showing its last value — a stale number shown confidently is the whole failure. And the governing rule, which is the resolver discipline applied to pixels: A WIDGET NEVER RENDERS A PASS IT COULD NOT CHECK. 'I don't know what effort B is doing' must be a cell state the UI can draw, because its absence is what makes a green dashboard a lie.

**Basis:** reasoned; grounded in Google SRE's symptom/cause separation (https://sre.google/sre-book/monitoring-distributed-systems/) and in the observation from https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system that 'users would report agents not finding obvious information, but we couldn't see why'

### 3. [sourced]

FIELDS 21/12 — AN ALERT IS NOT A CONTROL, AND THE STOP MUST BE REHEARSED ON THE SURFACE. The SEC's Knight Capital order is the cleanest case in existence: 'An internal Knight Capital system generated 97 automated emails that went to a group of personnel. The emails referenced the router and identified an error before the markets opened on August 1.' Nobody acted. 'During the first 45 minutes after the market opened on August 1, Knight Capital's router rapidly sent more than 4 million orders into the market when attempting to fill just 212 customer orders.' Loss: 'more than $460 million'; penalty $12 million. DESIGN CONSEQUENCE: the primary control on every surface — terminal, web, phone, ambient — is a single always-present STOP, and the surface displays THE LAST TIME THE STOP WAS EXERCISED AND HOW LONG IT TOOK, like a fire-drill placard. A stop that has never been pulled is an untested claim, and this system's own Rule 10 logic applies: never pass what you could not check. A surface that emits 97 notifications and owns no tested abort has built a record of its own innocence, not a control.

**Basis:** https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2013-222 — quotes verified by fetch 2026-09-11

### 4. [sourced]

FIELD 46/22 — THERE IS A MEASURED CEILING ON HUMAN ALERT THROUGHPUT AND THE DECISION QUEUE MUST HONOUR IT BY DECLINING WORK, NOT BY QUEUEING. Process industry has already done this arithmetic: EEMUA 191 puts steady-state at roughly one alarm per ten minutes (~6/hour) with a response budget under 10 minutes each, and caps emergency peak at ~10 alarms per 10 minutes; the standard exists because at Texaco Milford Haven (24 July 1994) hundreds of alarms arrived in the final minutes and the HSE case record lists 'excessive number of alarms in emergency situation reduced effectiveness of operator response' as a control-room design failure. Judgements are more expensive than alarms, not less. So: the owner's decision queue has a HARD CAPACITY, and when it is full the system STOPS WORK rather than growing the queue — and the surface renders that honestly as 'capacity 6/6 · three efforts paused, waiting on you', so the cost of your own attention appears as halted production rather than as a longer list. A backlog of pending approvals is the visual signature of a rubber stamp being manufactured.

**Basis:** EEMUA 191 rates via https://humanfactors101.com/topics/alarms/ and https://industrydigits.com/resources/alarm-load-scorer/; Milford Haven control-room finding at https://www.hse.gov.uk/comah/sragtech/casetexaco94.htm (fetched; the page carries the qualitative finding, not the alarm count)

### 5. [sourced]

FIELD 21/24 — THE BOTTLENECK PRACTITIONERS ACTUALLY HIT IS REVIEW, IN THEIR OWN WORDS, AND IT IS ATTENTION-SHAPED NOT TOOL-SHAPED. Simon Willison: 'AI-generated code needs to be reviewed, which means the natural bottleneck on all of this is how fast I can review the results' and 'I can only focus on reviewing and landing one significant change at a time, but I'm finding an increasing number of tasks that can still be fired off in parallel.' Armin Ronacher, who reduced his parallelism: 'The thing is: it's only so much my mind can review!' Peter Steinberger: 'I run between 3-8 in parallel in a 3x3 terminal grid.' The tooling market has churned around this without solving it — Vibe Kanban (kanban board, one git worktree per task) shut down April 2026; Conductor and Crystal took the one-workspace-per-agent route; Anthropic shipped Claude Code on web and iOS with parallel cloud sessions and monitoring from the phone. DESIGN CONSEQUENCE: attention is the scarce resource, so it is what the owner ALLOCATES. In the portfolio view the throttle is not per-task approval — it is an ATTENTION BUDGET split across efforts, and lowering an effort's attention share automatically narrows how much that effort may do unsupervised. That is the only knob that is honest about what is actually scarce.

**Basis:** https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/new-trend-programming-by-kicking (Willison, Ronacher quotes, fetched); https://simonwillison.net/tags/parallel-agents/ (Steinberger, grid, worktrees); https://munderdiffl.in/blog/best-claude-code-multi-agent-tools/ and https://agentsroom.dev/blog/best-multi-agent-coding-tools (Vibe Kanban shutdown 2026-04-10, Conductor, Crystal); https://anthropic.com/news/claude-code-on-the-web

### 6. [reasoned]

FIELD 21 — THE VIBE CODING SURFACE IS NEITHER A TERMINAL GRID NOR A KANBAN BOARD. Build THE BENCH. The terminal grid is wrong not because it is text but because it is serial and modal: it makes the human the scheduler and the router, which Bainbridge's 1983 result says is the one job the human is worst at once the machine does the rest. The kanban-of-agents is wrong the other way: everything becomes a card, the stream disappears, and review batches at the end. THE BENCH has exactly two zones. Zone 1: ONE FOCUS — a single artifact at full fidelity, live diff, editable, and the system enforces that it is one. Zone 2: THE READY LINE — an ordered queue of things wanting you, order defended by the system (you may re-order, and your re-ordering is recorded as a signal), each row carrying one line of reason, an exposure tag, and minutes-to-decide. EVERYTHING ELSE IS A COUNT, NOT A LIST: '7 running · 2 blocked · 1 waiting on a stranger'. You cannot see the list without asking. NO AGENT IS VISIBLE WHILE IT IS MERELY WORKING — work-in-progress is not information, it is theatre, and the tmux grid's real function is anxiety management for which the honest cure is a trustworthy abort, not a window. Two thefts: from RTS, invert the idle-worker key into an IDLE-OWNER key — one keystroke jumps to the single highest-value thing wanting you. From ATC paper flight strips, give every work item a MARGIN THE OWNER WRITES IN BY HAND, freeform, which travels with the item and is shown to the worker — the strip literature's finding is that the physical act of marking is how the controller holds the picture, and that electronic replacements failed largely by taking away the controller's freedom to arrange and annotate.

**Basis:** Bainbridge via https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ironies_of_Automation and https://humanfactors101.com/2020/05/24/the-ironies-of-automation/; ATC strips via https://commons.erau.edu/ntas/2020/presentations/67/ and https://nats.aero/blog/2026/01/atc-explained-flight-progress-strips/ (Mackay's TOCHI 2000 'Is Paper Safer?' PDF 404'd on both known mirrors — see unknowns); Factorio alert taxonomy via https://wiki.factorio.com/Alerts; single-threaded-writes finding via https://cognition.com/blog/multi-agents-working

### 7. [reasoned]

FIELD 46 — FIVE MECHANISMS AGAINST THE RUBBER STAMP, RANKED BY HOW CHEAP THEY ARE. (1) BLIND-FIRST: show the artifact BEFORE the system's verdict. Anchoring is the mechanism that manufactures assent, and no shipped agent-approval UI I found does this — LangChain's Agent Inbox presents the proposed action and its args, verdict-first, with allow_accept/allow_edit/allow_respond/allow_ignore. (2) NO APPROVE BUTTON: replace assent with a CHOICE BETWEEN DRAFTED FUTURES — two or three real options plus 'neither, and tell me what you are not showing me'. Assent is free; choosing costs the thing you want it to cost. (3) COST-OF-BEING-WRONG SHOWN IN THE OWNER'S UNITS before the control is reachable: days of runway, strangers reached, reversible y/n, whose data. (4) DISAGREEMENT RATE AS A DISPLAYED VITAL: the surface tracks your modify/reject rate over the last N decisions and shows it to you; a rate near zero is reported as a fault in the loop, not as a compliment. Baseline from the largest study of human review at scale: at Google, 'more than 75% of code reviews have just one reviewer' and 'over 80% of all changes involve at most one iteration of resolving comments' — that is what an industrial-grade review process looks like, and it is already rubber-stamp-shaped. (5) SEEDED DEFECTS: at a low, disclosed rate, inject a known-bad draft into the queue and measure whether it is caught; the assay is the only way to know the reviewer is still reviewing. Mechanisms 1, 2 and 3 are near-free and I would ship them first.

**Basis:** https://github.com/langchain-ai/agent-inbox/blob/main/README.md (fetched: accept/edit/response/ignore, allow_* config) ; Google study figures via https://sback.it/publications/icse2018seip.pdf and https://graphite.com/blog/how-google-does-code-review ; seeded-defect assay is reasoned by analogy (precedent recalled but NOT verified — see unknowns)

### 8. [reasoned]

FIELD 46/22 — HANDING WORK BACK NEEDS AN INSTRUMENT, AND THE INSTRUMENT IS A MODEL OF THE OWNER'S OWN STALENESS. The vision states correctly that there is no signal for owner drift from inside, because the owner is the instrument that drifted. Build the signal. (a) OWNER FRESHNESS CLOCKS with half-lives, one per competence: days since you read a customer's raw unsummarised words; days since you wrote something that shipped; days since you spoke to a stranger who could say no; days since you read a full run transcript rather than its summary. (b) When a clock expires, THE SYSTEM REFUSES to do work in that domain and routes it to you — a refusal, not a nudge, because the vision is explicit that this needs a mechanism rather than good intentions, and a nudge is an intention with a UI. (c) CALIBRATION SCORING: before revealing its own answer or the real outcome, the surface periodically asks the owner to predict it, and keeps a running calibration score per domain. A falling score in a domain is the drift alarm that otherwise does not exist, and it is measurable, cheap, and scores the human rather than the machine — which is the correct direction for this system and the opposite of every eval harness I found. Bainbridge's finding is the justification and it is 43 years old: operators removed from hands-on work lose the skill precisely for the rare intervention they are retained for, and therefore need MORE training, not less.

**Basis:** Bainbridge 1983 via https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ironies_of_Automation and https://humanfactors101.com/2020/05/24/the-ironies-of-automation/ ; calibration-scoring construction is reasoned

### 9. [sourced]

FIELD 21/12 — CATCHING UP AFTER A WEEK IS NOT A REPLAY. It is three objects and a read-back. (1) THE BELIEF DIFF: what the company believed when you left versus now, one row per changed belief, each with its cause and its evidence. Most of a week's events change no belief; the ones that do are the entire briefing, and an event feed buries them under the ones that don't. (2) THE WOULD-HAVE-ASKED LIST: decisions taken under standing policy that would have come to you had you been reachable, sorted by how uncomfortable the system was, each one reopenable cheaply and the reopening recorded. (3) A DECLARED READING BUDGET: you say 'I have 12 minutes', the briefing is generated to that budget and must state what it dropped to fit — never silently. (4) THE READ-BACK, which is the part software never copies from medicine: the catch-up ENDS with the owner stating the situation in one line and the system telling them where that diverges from the record. This is the 'S' in I-PASS — synthesis by receiver — and the I-PASS bundle across nine training sites was associated with a 23% relative reduction in overall medical errors and a 30% relative reduction in preventable adverse events, 'without negative impact on their workflow'. Note the honest caveat in the same study: error rates did not change significantly at three of the nine sites even though handoff process improved everywhere — so the structure is necessary and not sufficient, and the surface should not claim otherwise.

**Basis:** https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMsa1405556 and https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25629753/ ; belief-diff and reading-budget constructions are reasoned

### 10. [sourced]

FIELD 21/24 — MISSION CONTROL: THE PIXEL COMPANY SHOULD BE A FACTORY, NOT AN OFFICE, AND THE TIMELINE SHOULD MOSTLY REFUSE TO BE DRAGGED. The pixel-art agent village already exists and has been studied: Stanford/Google's Smallville put 25 generative agents in a sprite sandbox where they 'wake up, cook breakfast, and head to work'. It is compelling and it is a SIMULATION VIEWER, not an instrument — motion reads as progress, and an office view animating little people typing is a lie generator. Keep the medium, change the depicted noun: rooms are STAGES, belts are HANDOVERS, and BUFFERS ARE VISIBLE, so you watch work-in-progress physically pile up in front of the bottleneck — which will always be you, and which is the most honest possible thing for this system to draw. Pixel art earns its place precisely on SATURATION: a bar chart cannot show forty things at once and a factory floor can. ONE HARD RULE MAKES IT AN INSTRUMENT: a sprite may only move when a measured event moved it; no idle animation, ever. Steal Factorio's global alert bar literally — a small fixed taxonomy of alert types as icons on one strip, click to jump to the location, hover to expand, per-type mute where the mute is itself visible and expires. On the TIMELINE: refuse draggable future dates that have no consequence on arrival — that is the Gantt lie, a promise the owner makes to themself with no mechanism behind it. Allow dragging to change ORDER, and to set a KILL DATE (a date after which the effort auto-kills or auto-escalates), because a date that does something is worth dragging. Replace the plan timeline with a real HISTORY timeline plus a FORECAST BAND drawn from measured throughput and rendered as animated samples rather than a line — Hullman's hypothetical outcome plots beat error bars and violin plots for untrained observers judging distributional information, and a single projected line is the exact visual form of false certainty.

**Basis:** https://dl.acm.org/doi/fullHtml/10.1145/3586183.3606763 and https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-023-02818-9 (Smallville); https://wiki.factorio.com/Alerts (fetched: 15 configurable alert types, icon strip, click-to-locate, mute states, alerts-global vs warnings-on-entity); https://users.eecs.northwestern.edu/~jhullman/hops_jobs_pfs.pdf (HOPs)

### 11. [sourced]

FIELD 42 — SEEING INSIDE A RUN: SHIP THE DECISION SPINE, NOT THE TRACE. Every observability product I looked at renders SPANS, which are CALLS. A 400k-token run has thousands of calls and maybe a dozen moments where a worker chose between alternatives it could name. The primary artifact of a run should be those twelve rows — choice, alternatives considered, what decided it, what would have changed it — each expandable to the raw transcript, which is always one level below and never the default view. Anthropic's own write-up names the gap exactly: 'Adding full production tracing let us diagnose why agents failed' but also 'Agents make dynamic decisions and are non-deterministic between runs, even with identical prompts. This makes debugging harder,' and 'minor changes cascade into large behavioral changes.' Non-determinism is why a call-level trace does not compose into an explanation. THREE STRUCTURAL DEMANDS: (1) every worker return must carry DECLINED / UNREACHABLE / WITHHELD as required fields, so the negative space is in the data rather than reconstructed later by a model; (2) the unit that the surface follows is the INTENTION, not the request — a work item carries an id across every worker and step, and the view is item-first, not run-first; (3) state the retention split out loud: full transcripts for N days, decision spines forever, and show the boundary on the surface so nobody discovers it during an incident. All of this is already technically available here without new instrumentation: the runtime writes a transcript corpus with `isSidechain` per turn and per-turn `usage`, which `scripts/probe-workflow-reach.mjs` and `scripts/lib/usage.js` already read.

**Basis:** https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system (quotes verified by fetch); read of /Users/adamks/VibeCoding/agentvibe/.worktrees/ceo-1-1788609834/scripts/probe-workflow-reach.mjs and scripts/lib/usage.js for what the corpus already carries

### 12. [speculative]

THE SURFACES THAT DO NOT EXIST YET, IN ORDER OF HOW SURE I AM. (1) THE ABSENCE PROTOCOL — absence should automatically SHRINK permission, and on return the system's first act is to report what it did with your silence and require re-authorisation of the standing policies before it resumes full scope. Every agent system I found does the opposite: scope granted once persists forever, and an unreachable owner is treated as a tacit yes. This is the single most under-built surface and it directly answers field 22's 'what happens when they are unreachable'. (2) SEPARATE THE READING SURFACE FROM THE ACTING SURFACE ON PURPOSE. The morning account should be a read-only generated page with no buttons and no links — because the ability to act turns reading into triage, and triage is how you read a whole briefing and retain none of it. (3) VOICE FOR INPUT, NOT OUTPUT. Not an assistant that reads you a report; a ten-minute walking conversation in which the system ASKS YOU QUESTIONS about your own business. Voice is a poor channel for dense data and an excellent one for interrogation, and if you cannot answer, that is the staleness measurement from claim 8 arriving for free. (4) PHONE IS BOUNDED BY REVERSIBILITY, NOT BY CONVENIENCE. From a phone, standing up: the exposure block, the stranger clock, the stop, and decisions whose worst case is undoable-by-refund. An irreversible decision must NOT be approvable from a phone — not because the screen is small but because the context that makes the judgement real cannot fit on it, and a surface that lets you say yes to anything anywhere is a rubber-stamp factory with push notifications. (5) ONE AMBIENT OBJECT — a single physical lamp whose colour is your outstanding exposure, not system health. Green means nothing irreversible is outstanding in your name. This is the lava-lamp build light applied to liability rather than to CI, it is the only surface that is always on, and it is cheap enough to be worth trying even though I found no evidence that ambient devices change behaviour durably.

## Refusals — what this lane says NOT to do

- NO APPROVE BUTTON AND NEVER A BULK APPROVE. Assent is the cheapest possible human act and a queue with an Approve-all control will be cleared, not read. Replace with a choice between drafted futures plus an explicit 'neither — tell me what you are not showing me'.
- NO AGENT-CENTRIC EDITABLE CANVAS AS THE PRIMARY OBJECT. The founder names 'a canvas to add/remove/edit agents'; I would refuse it as the home surface. It makes the owner manage an org chart — the exact structure this company exists not to have — and it is the highest-leverage way to break everything while feeling productive. The roster should be DERIVED from what the company is trying to do, rendered read-only, with proposed deltas shown for approval. Editing an agent is a change to the rules, not a change to the work, and belongs behind the same gate as any irreversible act.
- NO ANIMATION THAT IS NOT BACKED BY A MEASURED EVENT. No idle loops, no ambient bustle, no sprite typing because a session is open. Motion reads as progress at a level below conscious inspection; an office full of animated workers is the most efficient possible way to build false confidence.
- NO DRAGGABLE FUTURE DATES WITHOUT A CONSEQUENCE ON ARRIVAL. Dragging a ticket to Thursday, where nothing happens on Thursday, is a promise the owner makes to themself with no mechanism behind it. Order is draggable. Kill dates and escalation dates are draggable. Aspirational dates are not.
- NO MODEL-GENERATED CONFIDENCE PERCENTAGES, AND NO NARRATED TEXT IN THE SAME VISUAL REGISTER AS A MEASUREMENT. A number a model produced about its own reliability is the single most efficient way to import unearned certainty into a surface. Express uncertainty as WHAT WOULD CHANGE THIS and as animated samples of outcomes; never as 87%.
- NO USER-CONFIGURABLE SUPPRESSION OF THE NEGATIVE-SPACE PANEL. Arrangement yes — the flight-strip literature is clear that personal arrangement is how an operator holds the picture, so let the owner order and annotate freely. Suppression no: a dashboard you can tune shows you what you want to see, and the whole value of this account is the part you do not want to see.
- NO FULL-CONTROL PHONE APP. The phone gets the exposure block, the stranger clock, the stop, and reversible decisions. Irreversible approval from a phone is refused at the surface level, not warned about.
- I DID NOT APPEND ANYTHING TO THE CLAIM LEDGER, despite holding the one tool that can. The parent scoped this lane to a single file and said commit nothing; a surprise record in docs/03-system-design/SOURCED-CLAIMS.md plus a rebuilt .claude/ledger/index.json is scope it did not grant, in a repo where the ledger is a blocking CI step. The Knight Capital and I-PASS facts are the two that would have justified it, and both are quoted verbatim above with fetched URLs if someone wants to register them deliberately.
