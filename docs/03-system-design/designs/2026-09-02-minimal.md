# The Minimal Company — a whole-system design from the smallest thing that still deserves the name

**Design 1 of 4, written 2026-09-02, angle: THE MINIMAL COMPANY.** Written blind to the other three.
Shape fixed by [`00-BRIEF.md`](00-BRIEF.md). The fifteen board decisions of
[`2026-09-01-startup-os-r01.md`](../../08-agents_work/board-meetings/2026-09-01-startup-os-r01.md) bind,
except one argued against in §17 with evidence.

**Every mechanism cites where it came from** — a catalogue id (`C2`, `P7`, `W1`, `R3`, `X1`), a repo path, a
studied system, or `INVENTED`. **Every rule names what enforces it or is labelled `WISH`.** Sections that
build nothing say so and name the mechanical condition that reopens them — a trigger, never a date.

---

## 0 · THESIS

**The centre of this system is one artifact a stranger has seen, and one honest record of whether it did
anything. Everything else is inventory until something reads it.**

The company is not the harness. The company is the sequence of artifacts leaving the machine and the
readings that come back. Nine phases produced one venture task; beeond got roughly eight product files
against roughly 582 harness files. That is not a wiring defect and it is not a discipline defect. It is what
happens when the thing being built and the thing being measured are the same object, so the machine can
improve forever without anything leaving it.

So this design inverts what gets the benefit of the doubt. A mechanism does not earn its place by being
correct, cheap, or reversible. **It earns its place by being read by something, on a schedule, in the same
commit that creates it.** If a store grows and returns nothing until something reads it, it is refused now
and reopened by a trigger. This is `X1`'s birth certificate narrowed from *has a caller* to *has a reader*,
which is stricter and which answers `D8`'s own admission that callability is satisfiable by a trivial caller
written to satisfy the check.

What serves the centre, and nothing else does:

```
        the founder's taste ──┐
                              ▼
   a goal tree ──▶ one move ──▶ an artifact ──▶ the founder's eye ──▶ the world ──▶ a reading
        ▲            (WIP 1)     (staged)        (one tap)          (published    (rung 0-4,
        └──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────  by hand)      fail-closed)
                          the reading changes the tree
```

Five state files, six mechanisms, one worker at a time, no outbound hands. **The founder publishes; the
machine makes.** That is not a limitation waiting to be removed. It is the design, and it dissolves five of
the safety subsystems the board sequenced ahead of the loop — because a machine that cannot send does not
need a wrapper, a rate ceiling, a contact register, a dry-run default, or an answer to
*could_not_resolve #6*.

**Think big is relocated, per `D12`, into the depth of the goal tree and the ambition of the artifact — never
into the count of mechanisms.** The system may pursue a goal thirty nodes deep. It may not hold a
thirty-first governed file.

## 1 · Missions & drive

### What it IS

**`ventures/<slug>/GOALS.yml`** — a tree, not a queue.

```yaml
venture: fakeco
constraint: "nothing has ever left this machine and been seen"   # P2, one pointer, expires
missions:
  - id: m1
    state: in_flight              # exactly one in_flight, ever — P5
    intent:                       # P7 — four fields, no method
      task:      "a landing page for FakeCo that a stranger can read"
      purpose:   "prove the machine can make a thing and stage it without me"
      end_state: "a preview URL renders at 1280x800 and 390x844, and one stranger
                  can say what FakeCo does after five seconds"
      constraints: [ "pack: web-feature", "no outbound", "3 attempts then escalate" ]
    falsifier: "if I cannot write the one-sentence promise, the mission is wrong"   # C36
    done:
      rung: 1                     # W2 — declared, and it caps what may be claimed
      resolver: human             # D7 — never the producing model
      test:  "the founder, or one stranger, states FakeCo's promise after 5s"
      approved_by: founder
      approved_on: 2026-09-15
    goals:
      - id: g1
        state: open
        end_state: "index.html renders with no console error"
        done: { rung: 0, resolver: command, test: "node scripts/see.mjs --url … --shot" }
        goals: []                 # a leaf
      - id: g2
        state: blocked            # B1 — authored, never computed
        block:
          because:      "no copy: TASTE.md has no adjectives"
          clearable_by: founder   # must resolve to a known clearer or the write is refused
          until:        2026-09-18   # B3 — no `until:`, no block
```

**Done is a rung, and the rung caps the sentence.** `W2`'s ladder, fixed and ordered: **(0) it renders ·
(1) a stranger understands it in five seconds · (2) someone clicked · (3) someone came back · (4) someone
paid.** Every done-test names its rung, the claim's `assert` string is *generated from* the rung, and rungs
never aggregate — a rung-1 result cannot be phrased as a rung-2 claim, which is the exact mechanism by which
a machine spends two years learning to lie to itself.

**Priority is not a function over declared fields. It is the tree.** `scripts/next.mjs` returns **the
leftmost open leaf of the single `in_flight` mission**. That is the whole algorithm. It is pure, it is one
line to test for determinism, and it has **no field a model benefits from filling favourably** — which is
`P1`'s named failure mode and the reason `P1` is refused here along with `P2`'s bottleneck pointer as a
dispatch rule, `P3`'s WSJF, and `P4`'s forecast tournament.

**Why a tree beats a ranking, concretely:** ranking needs `deadline`, `blocks`, `reversibility`,
`cost_estimate`, `evidence_of_demand` and `unlocks` — six fields per goal, authored by the thing that
benefits from them, at a volume of two to four rows a day. With `P5`'s WIP limit of one there is nothing to
rank *across*. Ordering *within* a mission is the founder's, expressed once, as the order they wrote the
children in. Taste enters once, at the top, exactly as §7 of the spec requires.

**`constraint:` survives as one pointer with an expiry** (`P2`), because naming the bottleneck is worth
having and is cheap. It does **not** gate dispatch. A pointer that gates dispatch needs an override path,
and an override path is a fourth way to spend a night doing nothing.

**Blocked is authored; stalled is computed** (`B1`). A worker writes `state: blocked` with a `because`, a
`clearable_by` that must resolve to a known clearer, and an `until`. A worker **cannot** author stalled —
that is `budget-guard.js`'s stall ceiling, counted in output tokens since the last durable artifact on disk
and **never the agent's own claim to be done**, which is the property that makes it unforgeable. `B2`'s
third state (*stuck*) is **refused for now**: it needs attempt hashes from `C24` and the two-state version
already routes correctly, because both stuck and stalled end at the same place — three attempts, then
escalate.

**Blocked frees the slot** (`B5`). A blocked mission is not `in_flight`, so `P5`'s cap is liveable. Parked
missions are themselves capped at three, and the cap is visible on the balcony.

**Abandonment** is `B3` running its course: a block whose `until:` passes forces exactly one disposition —
cleared, escalated, or waived with a new deadline — and three waivers on one block is surfaced as a decision
being avoided. **`P6`'s sunk-cost kill rule is refused**: it needs per-goal budget consumption, which needs
the task id to have been carried for months, and its re-justification brief is a new artifact class.
Reopen when: a mission has carried a waived block for 60 days.

**The cycle.** One move per cycle. The move is one leaf. The cycle is: read the kill file → read `BOARD.md`
→ `next.mjs` → dispatch one worker with fresh context → the worker runs ORIENT · PROPOSE · MAKE · ATTACK ·
SEE → done-test → write rows → rewrite `BOARD.md` → exit. **The process exits.** It does not sleep and it
does not hold state, so launchd restarting it is indistinguishable from it finishing, which is what makes
`S4`'s outer layer trivial.

### Built from

`P5` (WIP limit of one) · `P7` (commander's intent) · `B1` `B3` `B5` · `W2` (evidence ladder) · `C36`
(cheap falsification) · `P2` (constraint pointer, demoted) · the tree-as-priority is `INVENTED`.

### Board decisions that bind

`D7` (no self-scored done-test, no summed scores — honoured absolutely: there are no scores anywhere in this
design) · `D12` (no cap on the goal tree, cap on everything else) · `D1` (every state change is an event row
with a task id).

### Enforced by

`schema-lint.js` already refuses a playbook stage carrying `steps:`, `how:`, `method:` or `implementation:`
— **point the identical predicate at `intent:`** and commander's intent is enforced by code that is already
written. `GOALS.yml`'s state machine is an enumerated transition table; a transition not in the table is
refused. A block with no `until:` is refused at write time by reusing the ledger schema's existing refusal
of a `waive` with no `until`. A second `state: in_flight` is refused by the schema.
`scripts/next.mjs` carries a determinism test: same file in, same leaf out.

## 2 · Workers & roster

### What it IS

**One worker at a time. One pack. No new agent files.**

The seven engines survive as files because deleting them is harness work with no artifact (§17's rule
applied to myself), but the loop dispatches exactly one shape: a `builder`-equivalent running the universal
worker loop of `STARTUP-OS.md` §5. `reviewer` is reachable for the deterministic oracle. The other five are
not dispatched by the loop in year one.

**A pack is the argv, not an agent file.** This is the design's answer to `D12`'s sharpest measurement — that
frontmatter is a closed 15-key allowlist and the `Agent` dispatch tool has no `tools` parameter, so packs as
agent files means one generated file per engine × pack, which is roster growth by multiplication and the
exact pressure that produced 26 agents.

```yaml
# packs/web-feature.yml — a GRANT and a STOP. No method, ever.
tools:   [Read, Write, Edit, Glob, Grep, Bash, WebSearch, WebFetch]
mcp:     [playwright]
reach:   internal            # R2 — declared on the GRANT, per D3. Every value is `internal` today.
reversible: yes
blast_radius: self
done:    <proposed by the worker after ORIENT, approved once by the founder, then binding>
stop_after: 3 failed attempts
```

`bin/loop` reads this file and constructs `claude -p --allowedTools "Read,Write,…" --model …`. **The grant
is a property of the dispatch path** — which `D14` established by measurement and which the board meeting
demonstrated against itself when five ungoverned personas held `tiktok_publish` and Gmail `send_message`.
Putting the pack on the dispatch path is therefore putting it where the grant actually lives, rather than in
a file the runtime may ignore.

> **This is the design's one load-bearing unexercised assumption, and it is named rather than assumed.**
> `could_not_resolve #6` records that `claude -p --disallowedTools` "exists on the binary and has not been
> exercised." Until it is, the pack is a policy and not a physics. **§15 day 3 exercises it, before anything
> is built on it.** If `--allowedTools` does not narrow a `claude -p` process, packs-as-argv is dead and the
> fallback is one generated agent file for the single pack — one file, not a matrix, because there is one
> pack.

**Four pack families, one built.** Founder decision 6 names `web/feature` · `design/brand` ·
`content/video` · `customer/market` as the families that come first. This design reads that as *which four
are eligible*, not *four must exist before the first mission*, and builds **one**: `web-feature`, because
founder decision 7 makes the first mission a landing page. The other three are named, unbuilt, and reopen on
a mechanical trigger: **a mission whose approved done-test cannot be met by the granted tools** — which the
worker discovers at PROPOSE, writes as a `blocked` with `clearable_by: founder`, and which therefore arrives
as a balcony row rather than as a plan.

**How many at once: one.** Not two. Parallelism inside a mission is where the catalogue says it is cheap,
and it is where this design says it is unaffordable — because two workers on one tree need leases (`CO4`),
collision detection, and a merge story, and the founder watches two to four rows a day. Reopen when: a
single cycle's wall clock exceeds four hours **and** the stall counter is clean, which is the only reading
that distinguishes *slow because there is a lot to do* from *slow because it is circling*.

**Fresh context per unit of work** is free here and is not a mechanism: the loop's process exits after one
move, so the next move starts at zero by construction. This is the one place where the minimal design gets a
paid-for property for nothing.

**How a worker learns a field it lacks: ORIENT, at the moment of use.** No injected skill, no field cache,
no curriculum. The worker asks *do I know this field?* and if not, *who does this best, what does good look
like here* — with `WebSearch` and `WebFetch` on the grant. `STARTUP-OS.md` §4 already makes the argument and
it is right: a model researching a field today beats a procedure written today. **`K1`'s bounded learning
protocol with a checkable exit is refused** as premature: the exit is the done-test, which already exists.

### Built from

`STARTUP-OS.md` §4 (grant and stop) and §5 (the worker loop) · `H1` (the grant is the security boundary) ·
`D10` (`claude -p` with a narrow roster) · packs-as-argv is `INVENTED` on `D14`'s measurement.

### Board decisions that bind

`D10` (loop body is `claude -p`, narrow `allowedTools`, kill switch in the supervisor) · `D11` (worker trust
is dissolved, not deferred — and it is doubly dissolved here, because there is one worker and it starts at
zero every move) · `D12` (the ceiling) · `D14` (the grant is a property of the dispatch path).

### Enforced by

`schema-lint.js`'s existing rule that fails a declared `mcpServers` no configuration backs — pointed at
`packs/*.yml` so a pack granting an unbacked server fails the lint. A pack with no `reach:` /
`reversible:` / `blast_radius:` is invalid (`R2`). `bin/loop` refuses to dispatch with a pack it could not
parse — fail-closed, per Rule 10. The day-3 probe (`H2`) records what a dispatched process could actually
touch and is re-run whenever a pack changes.

## 3 · Hands

### What it IS

**Eight tools and one MCP server. No outbound hands exist in year one.**

Granted: `Read` `Write` `Edit` `Glob` `Grep` `Bash` `WebSearch` `WebFetch`, plus `playwright` for the SEE
phase. That is the whole roster.

**Refused, and these are the ones already connected and already dangerous:** higgsfield (84 tools spanning
`generate_image` through `tiktok_publish` and `sandbox_exec`), Gmail `send_message`, Google Calendar, Google
Drive, Notion, Figma, Stitch, Refero, Pencil, Miro, n8n, mem0, RunPod. Every one is reachable from this
machine today and every one is off the pack.

**The five servers to build: none.** Zero new MCP servers in the first 30 days and none planned. The nearest
candidate — a transcript server over the 2,936 files — is refused twice: `A4` refuses transcripts as memory
because retrieval cannot tell a corrected belief from a current one, and the instrumentation use (`M1`'s
~50-line regex correction classifier) is a script, not a server.

**What this refusal buys, and it is the design's largest single saving.** With no outbound hand:

| Board item | Status under this design |
|---|---|
| `D2` — no outbound money before an enforced rate ceiling | **Honoured by construction.** Nothing spends. Founder action item 2 (supply the dollars-per-day number) is **not needed yet**, which removes the only board item blocked on a number only the founder holds. |
| `R5` — absolute rate limits at the wrapper | Unnecessary. There is no wrapper because there is no send. |
| `R6` — the named-human register | Unnecessary. Nothing addresses a human. |
| `R4` — dry-run by default | Unnecessary. **Staging is not the default; it is the only state.** |
| `could_not_resolve #6` — is the outbound wrapper buildable at all? | **Does not need answering in year one.** The board could not settle whether a wrapper is a control given the hook's two verbs; this design does not depend on the answer. |

**Reach is still declared and still resolved in the one classifier** (`D3`), even though every value is
`internal`. This is the one piece of outbound safety built before there is outbound, and the reason is
`D3`'s own: the irreversible mistake is a *second* classifier written under pressure, and pressure is
exactly the condition under which the first outbound tool gets connected. One field on the grant, one `max()`
in `classifyFile`, all values `internal` — and the axis is ready.

**The capability oracle is one line and it has a live finding today.** `claude mcp list` as a daily check
(`hands.md` §6 item 5). It is not inventory, because it has a reader on day one: the pack grants
`playwright`, and `playwright` is measured **failing** — `CONNECTION_CLOSED`, with the tool warning that it
is **defined in two scopes with different endpoints** and that OAuth tokens do not carry between them. The
SEE phase depends on it. So the first thing the oracle reports is a break in the one hand this design grants.

**The reopen condition for every refused hand, and it is the same one:** *the founder performs the same
outbound act by hand three times.* Three is `SI2`'s rule of three, applied to capability rather than to
patterns, and it is mechanically countable from `SHIPPED.yml` because every publish is a founder tap that
writes a row. A capability requested by a plan is a guess; a capability the founder has already performed
three times is measured demand from the only customer in the room.

### Built from

`H1` (grant as boundary) · `H2` (the reachability probe) · `R2` (reversibility and blast radius on the
grant) · `hands.md` §0.1 (the measured roster and its health) · `hands.md` §8 sentence 2 (*"the riskiest
capabilities are already granted and the cheapest safe ones are not"*) · `A4` (transcripts refused as
memory).

### Board decisions that bind

`D2` (honoured by construction) · `D3` (reach on the grant, one classifier, extended never paralleled) ·
`D14` (the census, §15 day 2).

### Enforced by

The pack's `tools:` and `mcp:` lists become the `claude -p` argv, so a tool absent from the pack is absent
from the process. `schema-lint.js` fails a pack declaring a server `.mcp.json` does not back.
`scripts/lib/classifier.js` gains one axis and `.claude/qa-tier-floor.yml` gains rows —
**`A5` forbids a second implementation and this design does not write one.** The daily `claude mcp list`
writes an event row per unhealthy server the current pack grants; an unhealthy granted server is a balcony
row, not a silent degradation.

## 4 · Knowledge

### What it IS

**Nothing is injected. ORIENT is the knowledge layer.**

The measured state: 134 curated skills, 28 mental models with stop rules, **0 of 18 agents cite one**. The
minimal reading of that number is not *wire them up*. It is that a skill library is a store which grows and
returns nothing until something reads it, and the thing that would read it is a worker who, at the moment of
use, can read the live web instead.

**So the skills library is neither kept nor deleted. It is put on the meter.** `X2`'s last-use telemetry,
keyed on the task id that lands on day 1, records every `SKILL.md` a worker actually opens. In 90 days the
reading is a fact rather than an argument, and `CURATION.yml` — which already records 55 cuts *with the test
that justified each*, and more than ten reversals — is the right instrument to act on it. **This is the only
way to settle a 134-item library without either a retention argument or a deletion argument, both of which
this repo has already had.**

**`FIELDS/` is not created.** Founder decision 9 ("global facts, project taste") is honoured, but its global
half has no content on day 1 and no reader until a second mission touches a field a first mission already
learned. Creating an empty directory with an expiry policy is `A6`'s failure in the storage layer: a
mechanism that reads as knowledge accumulation while nothing accumulates.
**Reopen when:** two missions ORIENT the same field. Detectable, because ORIENT writes an event row naming
the field, and the trigger is a `GROUP BY` over rows that carry the task id.

**Exemplars over rules (`C16`) is accepted in place and costs nothing:** `TASTE.md` holds references — URLs
and adjectives — not rules. A reference is an exemplar. The founder writes three to five of them once, at
intake, capped at fifteen lines.

**Taste**: `ventures/<slug>/TASTE.md`, founder-authored, project-scoped, changed only by the founder, ≤15
lines. References · adjectives · no-gos. Injected into the MAKE prompt whole, because at fifteen lines it
fits under `CT1`'s byte ratchet without tiering.

**How a worker finds what it needs:** it searches the web at ORIENT, and it reads `TASTE.md` and `BOARD.md`.
That is the entire retrieval surface, and it is small enough to name in one sentence — which is the test.

### Built from

`STARTUP-OS.md` §4 (why no method) and §5 (ORIENT) · `C16` (exemplars over rules) · `X2` (last-use
telemetry) · `K3` (global facts, project taste — the split honoured, the global half deferred) · `CT1`
(byte-budget ratchet) · founder decision 9.

### Board decisions that bind

`D8`'s second half — last-use telemetry keyed on the task id — is the mechanism that governs the skills
library here. **`D8`'s first half is the decision this design overturns; see §17.**

### Enforced by

`CT1`'s byte cap on the MAKE prompt, checked in the suite against the emitted bytes — the precedent is
measured twice in this repo (skills discovery ~15,000 → ~1,070 tokens; `session-start.js` 27,069 → 2,941
bytes). A `TASTE.md` over 15 lines fails the intake check. Last-use telemetry is an event row per skill
read, joined on the task id, reported monthly beside `D15`'s harness:venture ratio.

## 5 · Memory

### What it IS

Five stores. One rule each. **Every one names its reader in the row below, and a store with no reader is not
created.**

| Store | Holds | Scope | The one rule | Its reader |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `GOALS.yml` | the tree, done-tests, blocks | project | one `in_flight`, ever | `scripts/next.mjs`, every cycle |
| `BOARD.md` | the baton: last move, next move, blocked | project | **rewritten** every cycle, hard byte cap, never appended | the next cycle's prompt |
| `TASTE.md` | references, adjectives, no-gos | project | founder-only writes, ≤15 lines | the MAKE prompt |
| `SHIPPED.yml` | what left the machine, with a `check_on` | project | a row is written by a **publish tap**, never by a worker | the `world` resolver |
| `events.jsonl` | every event, each with `task` | global | append-only; **no row without a task id** | `warroom rows`, the stall counter, `X2` |

**`DECISIONS.md` and the claim ledger survive unchanged** (founder decision 1, board `D1` of the spec's §2).
They are the one part of this repository that already does the job well, and the eviction tool
(`scripts/evict-memory.mjs`) is the forgetting mechanism — four mechanised rules, a stub left under every
original heading, and a refusal to lose a byte. **Never hand-edit an entry out.**

**Retrieval is not a mechanism here, and that is deliberate.** There is no embedding store, no graph, no
mem0, no RAG. The loop reads `BOARD.md` and `GOALS.yml` in full every cycle, because both are capped small
enough to read in full. **A retrieval system is what you build when your state does not fit; the correct
first move is to make the state fit.** Reopen when: `BOARD.md`'s byte cap is hit twice in one week by
content that cannot be compressed without losing the next move.

**Conflict: newer wins, older superseded in place with its reason** (`MEM2`). This repo's supersession
discipline is unusually good and is already the practice; it becomes a rule for `GOALS.yml` end-states and
`TASTE.md` too.

**Forgetting** is `evict-memory.mjs` for `DECISIONS.md`, and for `BOARD.md` it is the rewrite: the baton
forgets by construction every cycle, which is why it must be durable and capped (`S4`'s outer daemon
restarts the loop *from* `BOARD.md`).

**What transcripts are for: instrumentation, and not yet.** 2,936 files, nothing reads them. `A4` refuses
them as memory. `M1`'s regex correction classifier would make them instrumentation — ~50 lines, an
afternoon — and it is still refused today, because its output (a founder-correction corpus) has no reader
until something conditions on it, and the thing that would condition on it is `C21`'s preference pairs.
**Reopen when:** the founder overrides the same kind of decision twice, which the balcony's redirect rows
make countable.

**`CT3` (compaction declares what it dropped) is a `WISH`** on native compaction, which the runtime performs
and this repo does not control. It is enforceable only for what the system owns — the baton and the MAKE
prompt — and both are capped and rewritten rather than compacted, so there is nothing to declare. Labelled
`WISH` and left.

### Built from

`MEM2` (conflict) · `MEM3` (forgetting is a tool) · `A4` (transcripts refused as memory) · `M1` (deferred) ·
`CT1` (the caps) · `W3` (the shipped register) · founder decision 9 · `evict-memory.mjs` as built.

### Board decisions that bind

`D1` (no row without a task id — this is what makes four of the five stores joinable) · founder decision 1
(keep L1 unchanged).

### Enforced by

`BOARD.md`'s byte cap is a suite check comparing file size to the declared cap, and the cap only ratchets
down (`CT1`). A `SHIPPED.yml` row written by anything other than the publish tap fails a check on its
`written_by` field. `logEvent` itself refuses a row with no `task`. `check-memory-budget.mjs` already governs
`DECISIONS.md`'s two caps.

## 6 · Communication

### What it IS

**Nothing. There is one worker.**

Worker↔worker communication is not deferred, it is **dissolved**: `P5`'s WIP limit of one plus one move per
cycle plus a process that exits means two workers never coexist. `CO3`'s star topology is satisfied by
having no second point. `CO4`'s leases on path prefixes are unnecessary — there is one writer. Collisions
are impossible.

**Orchestrator↔worker is `BOARD.md` plus the dispatch prompt.** `CO1`'s SBAR handoff shape is accepted
because it is free: `BOARD.md`'s four headings *are* Situation · Background · Assessment · Recommendation
under different names — what happened, where we are, what is blocked, the next move. `CO2`'s read-back is
**refused**: it doubles every handoff to catch an error class this design has no instance of yet.

**Help is a two-rung ladder, not three** (`B4`, narrowed). **L1:** the worker tries a different approach, up
to the pack's `stop_after: 3`. **L2:** the worker writes `state: blocked` with `clearable_by: founder` and
the loop moves to the next leaf. There is no L3 and there is no council, because there is nothing between
the worker and the founder. **Auto-promotion is mechanical** — three attempts is a count, not a judgement,
which is `B4`'s own condition for a ladder that is not the empty Inbox again.

**The council and the persona board are refused for year one.** `D13` already forbids the board convening
without an agent file per persona and a cost cap something reads. This design does not build those five
files, so the board does not convene. That is not a loss: the one meeting it held produced fifteen decisions
this design is built on, and a second meeting before an artifact has reached a stranger would be the third
consecutive session of the system reasoning about itself.
**Reopen when:** a mission is abandoned or reversed *after* an artifact reached a stranger — that is a real
fork with real evidence, which is what a board is for.

### Built from

`CO1` (SBAR, free) · `CO3` (star topology, dissolved) · `B4` (escalation ladder, narrowed to two rungs) ·

## 7 · Context & cost

### What it IS

**The task id, and three caps.**

**`task` on every row, minted at dispatch, refused if absent.** `D1` is the only board decision whose
omission cost is unbounded, and the measurement is stark: 3,813 events at `~/.agentvibe/events.jsonl` and
the only id-shaped keys ever written are `id` and `dry_run`. **The mechanism is one guard clause in
`logEvent`** (`scripts/lib/events.js:55`), which today accepts any object:

```js
function logEvent(obj, repoRoot = REPO_ROOT) {   // today: accepts anything
```

It becomes: an object with no `task` string is refused and the refusal is loud. Parent/child ids from the
start (`CT2`'s own note), because a hierarchy added later has the identical retrofit problem as the id
itself. **CAST is the cautionary evidence and it is not theoretical**: it needs a heuristic 60-second
time-window join between two tables because there is no foreign key, and that is a documented audit finding
open two months.

**What is injected into whom.** The MAKE prompt receives: the move's `intent` (four fields), `TASTE.md`
(≤15 lines), `BOARD.md` (capped), and nothing else. No skills, no lenses, no playbook, no manifest, no
`FIELDS/`. **The declared cap is 4,096 bytes and it only ratchets down** (`CT1`) — the same budget
`session-start.js` blew through at 27,069 bytes, at which point the runtime truncated the payload and the
lenses never reached agent context at all.

**Caching:** the system prompt and the pack are stable strings, so they cache. Nothing is randomised.
`orchestrator` specifying a model per brief is preserved because it costs nothing.

**Batch: refused.** There is one move at a time.

**Compaction:** does not arise. The worker's process exits after one move; nothing lives long enough to
compact.

**Cost per mission** is a `SUM` over rows grouped by the mission id in the task id's parent slot. It is
available the day after `D1` lands and is unavailable in **either** direction before it — the Adversary's
sixth open question asks what the 858 commits cost and answers *unanswerable, including mine*.

**`EC2` — cost per *surviving* artifact — is adopted with its honest reading.** The denominator is artifacts
that reached rung ≥ 2. It will be **zero for months**, so the metric is **undefined**, and undefined is
reported as undefined, never as zero and never as excellent. The numerator and the zero are reported
separately so a month of cheap runs producing nothing cannot read as efficiency.

### Built from

`CT2` (the task id) · `CT1` (the byte ratchet) · `EC2` (cost per surviving artifact) · `EC3` refused

## 8 · Quality & truth

### What it IS

**Three layers, and only the first two run in year one.**

**Layer 1 — the deterministic oracle, unchanged.** L1 survives per founder decision 1: the claim ledger with
forced expiry, `scripts/lib/classifier.js`, `scripts/verdict.mjs`'s sha256 binding, and the 48-step check
suite. `qa.js` already runs an oracle before any panel agent is dispatched, and it has blocked its own
author. Nothing here is rebuilt.

**Layer 2 — the done-test, at rung 0 and rung 1 only.** `D7` binds absolutely: **no done-test whose resolver
is the producing model, anywhere; no score summed or averaged from judge outputs, anywhere.** This design
goes further and has **no scores at all** — there is no judge model in any path. Rung 0 (*it renders*) is a
command: a playwright screenshot at two viewports with a non-empty pixel check and zero console errors.
Rung 1 (*a stranger understands it in five seconds*) is a human: the founder, or one actual stranger, at the
same tap where they approve the publish.

**Layer 3 — the world's verdict — exists as a resolver and mostly returns `unresolved`.** `W1`:
`verified_by: world`, whose evidence names an instrument and a threshold, reading `SHIPPED.yml`. **Rule 10
governs it absolutely: an unreachable instrument resolves `unresolved`, never `pass`.** Say in advance what
this will feel like: **it will report `unresolved` far more often than `pass`, and that is the honest
reading, not a failure of the mechanism.**

**The second family: not in year one, and the risk stays accepted.** `CLAUDE.md` carries single-family
review as an accepted risk with an exit condition of 2026-11-17. This design does not discharge it and does
not pretend to. It is also **cheaper here than anywhere else**, because there is no judge to be
single-family about: correctness is deterministic and taste is the founder's. `gemini` is installed at
`~/.npm-global/bin/gemini` and dies sandboxed because `~/.gemini` is in `denyRead`; `ollama`'s two model
pins are six months retired. **Both are one command from working and neither is on this design's path.**
Reopen when: a done-test at rung ≥ 2 exists and needs a judgement no deterministic check can make.

**The council: refused.** See §6.

**Taste vs correctness, stated as a boundary rather than a balance:** correctness is what a command can
decide, and it is the check suite. Taste is what only the founder can decide, and it enters **once per
artifact, at the publish tap**. There is no third category and nothing in between, which is what makes the
absence of a judge affordable.

### The seam the Architect named and nobody engaged with — answered here in one sentence, per their request

`architect:R2:P1` observed that `scripts/verdict.mjs` binds `subject = sha256(git diff)`, so **a published
video, a sent email, a live landing page and a price change have no diff, therefore no subject, therefore no
verdict record** — and that the first non-code mission discovers this by shipping.

**The answer for this design: the first mission's artifact *is* a diff** — a landing page is files in a
repository — so the seam does not bite in the first 30 days. It bites on the first `content/video` or
`customer/market` mission. The one-line generalisation is `subject = sha256(the artifact bytes)`, the same
function with a different input, and it is **stated now and untested**. It is written into §15's day-30
handoff as the first thing the second pack family must settle, so it is a decision rather than a surprise.

### Built from

`W1` (world verifier, fail-closed) · `W2` (evidence ladder) · `W3` (shipped register) · `A1` `A2` `A3`
(refused: averaging, weighted voting, self-scoring) · `qa.js` as built · `W4` (counterfactual holdback)
refused — at one-founder traffic nearly everything is below threshold and the mechanism would mostly say
*cannot tell*.

### Board decisions that bind

`D7` (no self-scored done-test, no summed scores, first mission at rung 0 and 1) · `D6` (`blocking-human` by

### Enforced by

## 9 · Control & safety

### What it IS

**The policy seam is not built. Four controls are, and three of them already exist.**

Omnigent's typed policy seam (`PolicyEvent → PolicyResponse | None`, six phases, three levels, first DENY
short-circuits) is the best single architectural idea in the reference studies and it is **refused for year
one**. The argument for it is that it replaces five planned subsystems with one. **This design does not plan
those five subsystems**, so the seam would replace four small things with one general thing, which is
inventory with a good pedigree. Reopen when: a fourth distinct control is needed at the `tool_call` phase.

**The four controls:**

**1. The kill switch is a file** (`RT3`). `~/.agentvibe/STOP` — the founder creates it with `touch`. It is
the first statement of every dispatch, before anything else, and if the check itself errors, dispatch
refuses. No service, no API, no auth, nothing that can be down. **It stops new dispatch and does not stop a
running worker** — that is `S3`'s job and the two are named as different switches so nobody believes the file
stops the world.

**2. The grant is the argv** (§2, §3). Reach declared on the grant (`R2`), resolved in the one classifier as
`max(path, reach)` (`R1`, `D3`), never paralleled (`A5`).

**3. `blocking-human` by type, and it is already built.** `.claude/gates.yml` declares
**`outbound-approval`, `kind: human`, `decided_by: founder`** — *"A human has seen the staged artifact
before anything reaches a customer, a public URL or an external service. Publishing is not undone by a
revert."* It has **no `run:`, and `scripts/check-gates.mjs` refuses one written with a `run:`.** That is the
structural impossibility `R3` asks for, already implemented, already tested by `scripts/gates.test.mjs`, and
**lacking only a caller.** This design gives it its caller, and the composition the board produced applies at
full force: because `verdict.mjs` binds to a diff, **for three of the four pack families the human gate is
not one control among several, it is the entire enforcement spine.**

**4. The rope** (`EC4`, `D4`). The repaired stall counter, then `budget-guard.js` registered — a rolling
5-hour window ceiling and a stall ceiling counted since the last durable artifact on disk. At the ceiling a
safelist still permits `git commit/push`, `npm run check`, `gh pr create`, ledger writes and session-file
writes, so **landing work is never blocked; only starting new work is.**

**What runs at 3am:** ORIENT · MAKE · ATTACK · SEE · the done-test at rung 0 · `npm run check` · a commit to
a branch · writing `BOARD.md` and event rows.

**What never runs at 3am, by type and not by policy:** anything that sends, publishes, spends, or contacts a
person (there is no such tool on the grant) · `git push` to `main` · any write to `.claude/settings.json`,
`.github/workflows/`, or the loop's own launchd plist. **Harness self-edit is already `enforcement: block`
in `.claude/qa-tier-floor.yml` from day one, because `git revert` does not undo it** — and a loop that can
rewrite its own supervisor has no kill switch.

**The eight residual risks are accepted and named, not closed** (`risk-modeler:R2:P14`, founder action item
6). Three of them apply with full force here: the sandbox is a guardrail against accident and **not
containment** (`dangerouslyDisableSandbox` exists); a verdict record is hash-bound and **not signed**, so
hash-binding stops an inherited verdict and not a forged one; and **the founder is a single point of failure
for every human gate while watching two to four rows a day.** That last one is this design's central bet and
§17 argues against it.

### Built from

`RT3` (kill switch as a file) · `R1` `R2` `R3` · `EC4` · `A5` (one classifier) · `.claude/gates.yml` and
`check-gates.mjs` as built · Omnigent's policy seam, refused with a reopen · `S5` (steering as a policy
handler) conditional on a seam that is not built, therefore not built.

### Board decisions that bind

`D3` · `D4` · `D6` · `D9` (inbound last — and inbound does not exist in this design at all, so it is free to
honour and the concession is not needed) · `D10` (the kill switch lives in the supervisor).

### Enforced by

`bin/loop`'s first statement, with a test asserting dispatch refuses when the file exists and refuses when

## 10 · Surfaces

### What it IS

**One surface: the terminal. `warroom rows`, twenty lines, reading `events.jsonl` grouped by task id.**

Goal-sized rows, two to four a day, as the binding facts require. A row is: task id · mission · the move ·

A redirect **must** carry a task id or it is refused — CAST's lesson, and the reason `D1` comes first.

**No web UI in the first 30 days.** mission-control has seven views of which one acts, and its escalation
Inbox has been empty on every project ever. Building a second dashboard before the first has ever been read
is the design's own refusal applied to itself. **Reopen when:** the balcony passes its own done-test, below.

**Reopen when:** the founder sends the same instruction twice in a form the terminal could not take.

**When it may interrupt: never.** The loop does not notify. When a leaf is gated it writes a row, marks the
mission blocked, and takes the next leaf. If every leaf is gated it writes one `idle` row and exits. The
founder looks in the morning. **This is the honest version of Decision 5** (escalation is balcony-only,
Claude-native): the alternative is a notification path whose reachability from a launchd `claude -p` process
is unmeasured. **Reopen when:** §15 day 3 records whether hooks fire under `claude -p` — if `Notification`
fires, one notification on `blocked` is free and is added.

### The balcony has its own done-test, and this is the design's real risk instrument

**`W5`, pointed at the surface itself: did the founder open the balcony twice, unprompted, more than 48 hours
apart.** It is mechanically observable from event rows and it asks nothing of anybody.

This matters more than any other reading in the design, because **the human gate is the entire outbound
enforcement spine, and a gate nobody visits is a deadlock, not a control.** The escalation Inbox has been
empty on every project ever built here — that is the base rate, and it is the base rate for *this exact
mechanism*.

**If the balcony fails its own done-test, the design's answer is not "build a better balcony."** It is that
the approval tap must move to a surface the founder already opens every day, and the candidate is named:
a `gh pr` comment, or the terminal they are already typing in. That is a measurement deciding a design
question, which is the only way this class of question has ever been decided here.

## 11 · Runtime

### What it IS

**`claude -p` under launchd, one move per invocation, exiting every time.**

```
com.agentvibe.loop.plist
  ProgramArguments:  /Users/adamks/…/bin/loop
  StartInterval:     1800          # one move every 30 minutes, at most
  KeepAlive:         { SuccessfulExit: false }   # restart on crash, not on clean exit
  WatchPaths:        [ ~/.agentvibe/STEER.md ]   # a steer wakes it
```

**`D10` binds and this is its shape.** The loop body runs as `claude -p` with a narrow `--allowedTools` list
under a launchd supervisor, the direct outbound tools are struck from the roster (there are none to strike),
and **the kill switch lives in the supervisor and in the wrapper, never in the prompt.**

**`S4`'s dual-layer supervision, for free.** The outer daemon is launchd's `KeepAlive` — it restarts the
*script* if the script dies. The inner watchdog is a per-invocation timeout in `bin/loop` that SIGTERMs a
hung `claude -p` and records it. Neither supervises the other's failure, which is the point. **Auto-Co's own
`.gitignore` names a circuit breaker that exists nowhere in its code** — so the reference implementation has
the hole this design must not have: `SuccessfulExit: false` plus a crash counter that stops the job after
three restarts in ten minutes.

**Why a process that exits beats a process that sleeps.** `RT2` offers three homes. (a) scheduled, (b)
long-running with a sleep, (c) event-driven. This design takes (a) with an exit, which gives crash-only
semantics for free (`RT1`): every move is idempotent because a re-run recomputes the leftmost open leaf and
redoes at most one move's work. **Non-idempotent moves do not exist in year one, because the non-idempotent
acts are all outbound and there are no outbound hands.** `RT1`'s no-default schema field is therefore not
needed yet, and that is worth naming as a saving rather than an omission.

**Containment is preserved.** `mission-control/test/crosscheck.test.ts` bans a shell call under `server/` at
**zero exceptions** — it closed three RCEs on 2026-08-14. The loop lives beside the server, not in it, and
that blocking test is the mechanism.

**Models per job:** Opus for MAKE and ATTACK. Sonnet for the loop's own bookkeeping cycle (read board, pick
leaf, write rows) — which mostly does not need a model at all, because `next.mjs` is a pure function. Haiku
for nothing yet. `claude-fable-5` is valid and unused.

### Two things that must be measured before they are relied on, and both are in §15

**1. Does a `Workflow` invocation succeed from a launchd `claude -p`?** Founder action item 9. `D10`'s
confidence is `med` **only** because of this, and the Architect labelled its own answer an inference: a
`claude -p` process is not a sidechain, but nobody has run one. **If it fails, the producing workflows are
reachable only while the founder is typing and the 24/7 premise needs re-examining.** Note that this design
does not *depend* on a Workflow — `bin/loop` is a shell wrapper, and `design.js` is refused in year one — so
a negative result costs this design nothing and is worth having anyway.

**2. What runs with the lid shut.** A launchd job on a sleeping Mac does not run. `caffeinate` is installed;
`pmset -g` reports the current settings. **This is unmeasured and it is a precondition of the phrase "runs
24/7."** The measurement is one command and it belongs before the first unattended night, not after it.

### Built from

`D10` · `S4` (dual-layer supervision) · `RT1` (crash-only, satisfied by construction) · `RT2` option (a) ·
`RT3` · Auto-Co's missing circuit breaker as the named anti-pattern · `hands.md` §0.2 (`launchctl`,
`caffeinate` present).

### Board decisions that bind

`D10` (held at `med` confidence, and this design records why and measures it) · founder decision 4 (built for
this Mac).

### Enforced by

`crosscheck.test.ts`'s zero-exception shell ban, already blocking. A test asserting `bin/loop` checks the
kill file first and refuses on an errored check. The crash counter in the wrapper. The two measurements above
are recorded as claims with expiry, so they come due and force a disposition rather than aging into folklore.

## 13 · Economics

### What it IS

**One ceiling, one rope, one line of P&L.**

**The rate ceiling (`D2`) is honoured by having nothing to rate-limit.** No tool on the grant spends money
outside model tokens. Founder action item 2 — *supply the dollars-per-day number* — is **not required in year
one**, which removes the single board item blocked on a number only the founder holds. The refusal is
inaction and costs zero to hold, and the asymmetry is the whole argument: a missed spend costs nothing and a
wrong spend is a bill `git revert` cannot refund.

**The rope (`EC4`, `D4`).** `budget-guard.js` registered **after** the stall-counter repair, never before.
The repair is not polish: measured live 2026-09-02 with the last durable artifact 19.1 hours old,
`sinceLastArtifact().output_tokens` and `windowUsage().output_tokens` returned **the same number, 193,027**.
Past `RETAIN_HOURS = 6` (`scripts/lib/usage.js:40`) the stall counter degenerates into the window counter,
so **the detector is inoperative for exactly the regime it governs** — a 24/7 loop crosses six hours on
night one by construction. Two small repairs, both named by the board: trigger on elapsed time since the
last durable artifact, which `budget-guard.js` already computes and discards into a warning string; **or**
have `sinceLastArtifact` return `unresolved` past the horizon, which is Rule 10 applied to its own meter.
And scope the account-wide numerator to the repo-local denominator, which needs the task id.

> **Registering it before the repair does not give the loop a brake. It gives the founder a reason to
> believe there is one.** That is the sentence that orders these two, and it is the board's.

**Model tiering as spend:** Opus for MAKE, Sonnet for bookkeeping. **`EC1`'s downgrade-instead-of-stop is
refused for year one** — it needs a price table and a policy handler at the `llm_request` phase, and the

**Cost vs worth** is `EC2`, reported with an undefined denominator until a rung-2 reading exists (§7).

```
2026-09  tokens: <sum>   artifacts staged: N   published by hand: M   rung>=2 readings: K

```

**Every figure that cannot be computed is printed as `undefined`, never as zero and never omitted.** A
missing row and a zero row are different facts, and this repo has already paid to learn that a dropped

## 14 · The company itself

### What it IS

**One venture. Intake produces three artifacts and stops.**

**`CY1`, unchanged, because it is already minimal:** a **taste file** (references, adjectives, no-gos) · **one
mission with a falsifier** · **one approved done-test**. Nothing else is authored, and no move dispatches
until all three exist. One sitting. The system proposes drafts the founder edits rather than asking open
questions, which is what stops intake becoming a questionnaire the founder abandons.

**Several at once: refused.** `P5`'s WIP limit of one at the mission level makes multi-venture allocation
meaningless — there is one in-flight mission in the company, not one per venture. `CY2`'s floor-and-ceiling
portfolio allocation is real and well-sourced and it solves a problem this design does not have.
**Reopen when:** a second venture exists **and** the first has a rung ≥ 2 reading. Both halves matter: a
second venture before the first has ever been read by the world is the 92-to-1 ratio arriving in a new
costume.

**A second human: nothing.** Every `kind: human` gate carries `decided_by: founder`, which is already true in
`.claude/gates.yml` for all three. `CY3`'s RACI decision rights are honest ceremony for a one-founder
company; the field's value is making the exception visible when it arrives, and it already exists.
**Reopen when:** a second human holds a credential this system uses.

**Wind-down is `CY4`, four steps, cheap:** stop dispatch · resolve every open claim to a disposition (bulk
`deprecate` with one shared reason is permitted, because resolving each one individually is real work at
exactly the moment nobody will do it) · write a dead-end record for the venture as a whole · archive with a
stub. **The global facts learned stay global** — which is the strongest argument for founder decision 9's
split, and which this design cannot yet honour because `FIELDS/` is not created. Named as a known cost of
the deferral.

### The first mission, and an honest correction to how it is usually described

Founder decision 7 sets the first mission as a synthetic one — a landing page for a fake company. This design
runs it, and states plainly what it can and cannot establish.

**The synthetic mission is an acceptance test of the machine. The demand test is an experiment on the
market. They are different experiments and neither substitutes for the other.** A fake company's page cannot
reach rung 2, because nobody will visit it — which is exactly why `D7` caps the first mission's done-test at
rungs 0 and 1, and the two decisions agree. But it means the synthetic mission's success says *the machine
works* and says **nothing at all** about whether anything it makes is wanted.

**Therefore mission 2 must be real, and its artifact must be published.** If the machine's only ever customer
is a fake company, the system has tested itself against itself, which is the failure this whole design is
organised against. §15 puts mission 2 inside the 30 days for that reason and for no other.

`CY1` (intake) · `CY4` (wind-down) · `CY2` and `CY3` refused with triggers · founder decisions 7 and 9 ·
`P5`.

### Board decisions that bind

## 17 · WHERE THIS DESIGN IS WEAKEST

### My strongest counter, stated fairly

**This is not a company that runs 24/7. It is a machine that drafts overnight and a founder who ships in the
morning — and the founder is the constraint.**

The theory of constraints says the bottleneck in a one-founder company is almost always the founder's
attention. This design routes **every single artifact** through that bottleneck, by design, and calls it a
safety property. A design that granted outbound hands behind an enforced rate ceiling would relieve the
constraint; this one loads it. The residual risk the Risk Modeler named and nobody adopted lands directly
here: *the founder is a single point of failure for every human gate while watching two to four rows a day.*

**My answer, and its breaking point stated honestly.** At two to four rows a day the tap costs perhaps thirty
seconds, and the asymmetry justifies it: a missed send costs nothing and a wrong send cannot be recalled. But
**that answer is a function of volume, and it breaks at a nameable number.** At roughly twenty rows a day the
founder becomes a queue, the taps get batched, batched approval is approval without looking, and the gate
degrades into the thing it was built to prevent — while still reading, on every dashboard, as a control.

So the design carries its own falsifier: **if the machine ever produces more than ten stageable artifacts in
a week, the outbound wrapper becomes forced work and this design's central refusal expires.** That is a
measurable trigger, not a judgement, and it is on the monthly report.

### My second counter

**The inventory rule can refuse a thing that would have compounded.** *Nothing reads it yet* and *nothing
will ever read it* are the same observation on day one, and I have used the first to justify decisions that
only the second would warrant — against the skills library, against `FIELDS/`, against the transcripts, and
against the whole knowledge layer. If field knowledge is the thing that compounds, I have refused the
flywheel and kept the feeders.

**The mitigation is real but partial:** every refusal carries a mechanical trigger, and the triggers are rows
on the monthly report rather than promises in a document. But a trigger is only as good as the thing that
reads it, and the thing that reads it is a monthly report that does not exist yet and that nobody has ever
run.

### The one board decision I would overturn: D8, in its first half

**`D8` says: unify the four existing birth-certificate checks into ONE `hasCaller` predicate, and land it
with last-use telemetry.** I keep the second half and make it load-bearing across §4, §12 and §16. **I refuse
the first half.**

**The evidence:**

1. **`D8`'s own text withdraws its justification.** The decision states that the predicate *"proves
   CALLABILITY, not CALLEDNESS: wiring IS adding a caller, so the check is satisfiable by a trivial caller
   written to satisfy it"*, and the persona who ranked it first **withdrew the word "prevents."** What
   remains after that withdrawal is a de-duplication. The finding lives entirely in the telemetry half.

2. **The four implementations are four working checks.** `build-skill-routers.mjs` for skills,
   `check-suite.test.mjs` for test files, `check-gates.mjs` for gates, `EXCLUDED` for suite steps. Each
   works. Each is tested. **Unifying four working checks produces zero artifacts and changes zero verdicts**,
   and it is precisely the shape of work `D15` was written to count — it would appear in the harness column
   of the very report this design runs monthly.

3. **The stated defect is a fifth instance, and this design does not write one.** `D8`'s argument is that *a
   fifth instance is the disease it treats.* Correct — and the treatment for a threatened fifth instance is
   to not write a fifth instance, which costs nothing. The six mechanisms in §0b add no birth-certificate
   check. The disease is prevented by abstention, and the refactor is a separate purchase.

4. **`strategist:R2:P2` supplies the general form and it applies to `D8` itself.** *"Cheap to reverse is not
   cheap to carry"* — and its converse holds here: a cheap, safe, correct refactor is still a purchase, paid
   in the only currency that is scarce, during the only thirty days in which the first artifact has to reach
   a stranger. **Nine phases and 858 commits produced one venture task, and not one of the 48 check steps
   was hard to reverse.**

**What I would do instead:** land `X2`'s last-use telemetry on day 1 with the task id, since it is the same
field. Leave all four checks alone. Revisit unification when a fifth is genuinely wanted — at which point the
refactor has a caller, which is the standard `D8` itself proposes.
