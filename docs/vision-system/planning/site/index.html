What this is

Deciding to run a business means taking on duties that do not switch off. Someone has to answer a customer who is unhappy nine months after buying. Someone has to notice that a promise made in March was never kept. Someone has to close the thing down properly if it fails — refunds finished, records handed over, the people who are owed something still able to reach a human being.

Those duties survive every tool and every change of mind. This is a plan for something that carries that work for you while keeping you genuinely in charge of it — able to understand what was done, able to disagree with it, and able to stand behind it to a customer or a regulator without guessing.

What it would do. Investigate whether an opportunity is real, using actual observations of actual people. Design, make and offer things. Sell, take money, deliver, support what was sold. Keep the books, meet institutional duties, prepare what a qualified professional then acts on. Change direction when you do, and close down responsibly. And keep a record of what failed, what was skipped and what nobody knows — because an account that only contains successes is not an account.

What it refuses to do. It refuses to let a report about work stand in for the work: a summary of a delivery is not a delivery, and no internal status discharges an outside obligation. It refuses to invent your values, your appetite for loss, or your consent. It refuses to treat a good track record as licence for bigger consequences. It refuses to quietly spend money. And it refuses to fabricate people — a professional is not created by naming an endpoint.

What your day would look like. Not supervision. The design is built against the failure where every uncertainty becomes an approval request and you become a full-time reviewer. You get a bounded set instead: decisions only you can make, contested promises, evidence that contradicts what was believed, and deadlines coming due.

The architecture

The selected architecture is called S1.0. It puts one authority in charge of admitting work and of the obligations that outlive it, lets each business domain keep its own records, tools and meanings for its own work, and protects three things behind separate boundaries that no ordinary producer can reach: what may actually happen in the outside world, what counts as evidence that something happened, and how the company recovers when a machine, a provider or a person is lost.

It decomposes into nine logical components. These are responsibilities, not agents, services or machines — the executive guide states directly that "this does not require nine agents, people, services or computers."

Records and rules

Everything the company knows is held as records. A record here is not a row that gets overwritten. Each business revision is immutable — it is written once and kept — and the record's phase moves separately on top of it. Superseding a record does not erase its history, and a lifecycle change never rewrites the business facts underneath it.

A record's phases are the honest vocabulary of the business. A sale is not "open" or "closed"; it is at interest, proposed, accepted, partially performed, performed, disputed, terminated with residuals, or terminated — and the difference between the last two is the whole argument about whether anything is still owed.

Agents, skills, models

There is no roster

The most consequential thing about the agent model is what it refuses. There is no permanent cast of named agents — no standing "marketing agent", no "finance agent". A decision record, AD-002, settles this deliberately: work and authority are determined first, and a performer is chosen for each piece of work afterwards.

An AgentTemplate is a versioned recipe — which capability contracts it supports, its input and output schemas, its instructions and skills, what runtime it may use, its bounds, its self-checks and its known limits. It is explicitly not an identity with standing. An AgentInstance binds that recipe to exactly one admitted work order, one executor identity, one runtime profile and one lease. Creating one does not add a role to the company.

When one existsOnly for an admitted work order, under an existing mandate and allowance. Temporary by construction.
Producer vs evaluatorA producer's self-check is useful preparation and never becomes acceptance. Acceptance is a separate component's job.
DelegationDefault maximum depth is one child layer. A child can never be handed founder values, professional standing, independent acceptance, or the parent's responsibility.
RetriesOne retry after a changed input, environment or method is identified. Two failures with the same cause open a blocker — they do not prove impossibility. Rework and abandoned output stay in the denominator.
RefusalA worker reports capability_mismatch, missing_prerequisite, no_progress or unresolved_question with evidence — instead of declaring something impossible after an arbitrary number of tries.
TrustScoped eligibility, not a score. New templates start with no outward credentials. Success never expands authority automatically.

Skills

A skill is a unit of know-how, packaged: applicability and non-applicability predicates, typed inputs and outputs, finite steps, its dependencies and their supported versions, its consequence boundaries, its acceptance criteria, examples and counterexamples, provenance and licence, a test suite with known failures, a maintenance owner, a review date, and a rule for how it gets replaced or removed. "A prose package cannot install, contact, spend or execute by being loaded."

Selection is bounded on purpose: at most five candidates inspected and two full skill bodies loaded per selection, charged to the work order. The library is never fed into context whole. A skill retires on dependency and obligation checks — not on how rarely it is used, because an unused emergency skill can still be necessary.

What was decided

Five layers, each answering a different question, with a fixed order: when two disagree, the earlier one wins.

1 · Who may actHow much damage an action can do decides who may perform it — and that is computed from records the actor cannot write, then recomputed at the moment the effect leaves the building. A mismatch stops the work; it never quietly resolves downward.
2 · How it runsA declared procedure decides how work runs and what each step is allowed to see. The step declares what it needs, and the thing that fetches it records what it actually handed over. Nobody is taken at their word about what they read.
3 · When it is dueWork becomes due when a typed standing interest fires. The conditions are mechanical — no model may sit inside one — and nothing in the system can stop an interest firing.
4 · Whether a worker existsA worker is created only against a written justification naming one reason, with a test that can come back false. If the test cannot come back false, it is refused. There is still no permanent cast of named agents.
5 · Who is still there afterwardsFor every duty that outlives the job that created it, exactly one standing party exists — a record with an address and reserved capacity. It never does the work, never releases anything, never judges a matter it is the subject of, and carries no memory of its own.

Four more rules worth knowing in plain terms:

  • Specialized knowledge is no longer a reason to create an agent. It belongs to a written, tested, versioned procedure that an ordinary worker loads. Across four model families, 162 roles and 2,410 questions, giving a model a job title gave no benefit, and picking the best title was no better than picking at random. Being identifiable is required of every worker and is never a reason to create one — a thing everybody must have cannot justify making another one.
  • Whoever will judge the work writes the test first, and cannot be the one doing the work. The criteria are written by the capability's owner, frozen before the work starts, and cannot be amended by the producing side afterwards.
  • Anything that crosses a boundary is typed — including the brief that reaches you. A receiver may act only on what it can restate as a typed value; prose travelling alongside is evidence, never a substitute. This is the fix for your handoff worry, and it is aimed differently than you aimed it: ordinary facts survive a compressed handover about 97% of the time, but rules, deadlines and promises survive about 57% of the time. Typed constraints leaked 0 of 48 where prose leaked 73%. The answer is not to split less; it is to type what crosses.
  • Two rules about running out. If a lane holds reserved capacity, the holder's stated minimum is evidence, not permission — there is no veto — and unused reservation is released on a timer, because under an allowance that resets whether you spend it or not, a reserve is waste rather than thrift. Separately, if the allowance is empty and something is genuinely due, a named non-model way of doing it performs it, a named person is alerted, and the choice between breaking the promise and performing it leaves a record. Fifty percent of an empty bucket is zero.

Distinct tools or permissionsConfirmed — the strongest of the five. The only one enforced outside the model and the only one with a published price: 77% of benchmark tasks solved with provable security, against 84% with no defence at all. It does not come free — in the runtime you would use, a helper inherits every tool by default. It works, and it has to be built.
Isolated contextSplit in two. Isolation to help a model concentrate keeps shrinking with each model generation. Isolation to keep a secret is structural and real — and it is the only sense any of your six test cases exercises, so only it may justify creating a worker. Even then, separating things cut disclosure violations by 20 to 50 points and still left over 75%.
Independent verificationHalf of it is out of reach. You can stop the producer influencing the checker, and that is measurable. All of these are required future evidence, not results. Honest limits

This is the part that would be left out of a sales document. It is here because a plan you can stand behind has to include what it cannot do.

  • One model family, throughout. Every author and every reviewer of this package shares a model family. The independence is procedural — no reviewer read the authors' self-assessments — not statistical. The standard this work holds itself to asks for a panel across at least two distinct model families; that is not met anywhere in the package, because no non-Anthropic model is reachable from inside the tooling.
