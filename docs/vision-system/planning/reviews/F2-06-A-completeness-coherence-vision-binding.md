## W1 — completeness for unknown future jobs: **insufficient**

### What holds

The admission chain is specified end to end and every link names an authority. §2 derives the
**reached class set before the kind**, so mistyping into the nearest existing kind cannot lower the
class. Typing returns exactly **matched / `no_match` / `budget_exhausted`**, with "typed into the
nearest existing kind" a recorded failure and never a success, and no confidence threshold anywhere.
An unresolvable class dimension resolves to the **ceiling**, never a default. Refusal is a terminal
state of the same record requiring a **named failed predicate** and its own acceptance owner, and
silence is not refusal. `unclassified-work/v1` has a written adaptive envelope with **effect classes
= none** and a **declared maximum age whose breach is itself admitted work**. `unclassified-effect`
**refuses the omnibus standing grant** and admits **no effect steps at all** until the founder sets
two ceilings (MD-10). Fixture 17 of §9 is a **false-exclusion control that must pass**, so the cost
of the three-outcome typing is priced rather than hidden.

Walked: the protocol's own customs-classification probe reaches C3 and C4, types `no_match`, and at
the two highest classes is **refused with a reason plus a decision packet to `04`'s `AuthorityMatrix`**.
That terminates. It is not an unowned queue and it is not "the founder adds a new agent, skill or
route." It does require "the founder staffs a role", which is a different act, and R-D09 makes it
visible by **stopping admission** in that duty class rather than letting work accumulate.

### F6A-01 · class (d) · no admission rule for a standing holder of a duty class no frozen fixture exercises

**Governing requirement:** protocol §2 W1 — an admission, typing, staffing rule and acceptance owner
that operate without a human redesigning the system; §5 example 2 (no authority named for a
handoff's acceptance).

**Passages.** `05-work-agents-skills.md` §12: *"A holder exercised by no frozen fixture is
unjustified and is refused on sight"*, and the holder table's last row, *"Any other proposed holder |
— | refused on sight"*. `05` §2: at the **middle two reached classes** work nothing can handle
*"escalates on a timer to the standing holder for that duty class (§12)"*.

**Expected behaviour.** Every reached-class band has a terminus that exists.

**Counterexample.** Supplier insolvency during a paid pre-order reaches {C2 external contact, C3
economic}. That is the middle band. No frozen fixture exercises supplier insolvency, so §12 refuses
a holder for it on sight; the four admitted holders are personal-data/deletion, grievance and rights,
continuity and on-call, and a reserved lane, and none covers it. The escalation therefore has no
recipient. §6's fourth silent-drop counter promises *"the terminus of every ladder is an explicit
awaiting-principal state with a deadline and a standing owner (§12)"* — §12 supplies none.

**The asymmetry is the discriminating evidence.** The two lowest classes **park with an owner and a
review date**; the two highest **refuse with a packet to a named human authority**. Only the middle
band routes to §12, and only the middle band has no fallback. Computed:
`StandingHolder.payload` carries a **required** `exercising_fixture_id` in `records.schema.json`, so
the fixture gate is mechanical, not rhetorical. The fixtures are frozen by protocol §3 and negative
control 10 voids any authored after a candidate's result is known, so the admitted holder set cannot
grow. §12's removal criterion and per-holder retirement rule let holders leave. Nothing lets one
arrive.

**Missing decision.** What receives a middle-class escalation when the duty class has no staffed
holder, and by what criterion a holder for a new duty class is admitted.

**Required contract.** Either (i) name the default recipient for that band — `04`'s `AuthorityMatrix`,
as the high band already uses, or `03`'s ordered custody resolver, which already has a terminal
`maintenance_custodian` tier — or (ii) state an admission criterion for a new holder that does not
require membership in a set frozen on 2026-09-13, and say which party applies it.

## W2 — coherence with the fixed boundaries: **insufficient**

### The two-implementer test

I handed `05` §1/§11 and `02-authority-recovery.md` §4/§8.2 to two readings and asked who acts, what
"accepted" means, and what remains owed after a shed.

**Who acts — they agree.** L1 computes the **set** of reached classes from one deterministic function
over four named inputs, recomputed at release on the frozen bytes, parking on mismatch and never
resolving downward; every reached class's gates apply; **no rank, no ordering, no severity score, no
seventh class.** `02` §4 carries the matching restatement, `guard.class.reached_set_gates_all` carries
the predicate, and negative fixture `r16-01` deletes the containment conjunct while leaving the size
comparison standing, with a paired benign fixture. That is a real control, not an assertion.

**"Accepted" — they agree.** `05` §1: a committed, independently witnessed transition, **distinct
from** output acceptance, effect observation, customer acceptance and duty discharge.
`AcceptanceInterval` adds `declared_done → in_acceptance → accepted | rejected` with the terminal edge
written by the acceptance owner and **rejected when the resolution reason is empty**.

**What remains owed after a shed — they agree.** `ShedDecision`, written once, records the lane, the
moved obligations **with named custodians**, the stated minimum, whether it was breached, the overflow
count and the lapsed-unconsumed quantity; the holder's minimum is **evidence, not consent**. Open and
routed: MD-07 (halt authority) and MD-08 (lane sizes).

**Layer precedence composes.** *"Class may raise what a procedure permits and never lower it"* is
consistent with `02` §4's *"an action may satisfy multiple classes and must meet all their gates"*, and
introduces no ordering.

### F6A-06 · class (d) · the "declared floor" has no representation in the capability contract

**Governing requirement:** protocol §2 W15 — production mode and acceptance owner named per capability,
every consequential choice traceable; failure condition *"a link to a whole chapter in place of an
operative answer."*

**Passages.** `05` §1 and §11, and `02-authority-recovery.md` §4's F2 restatement: the class set is
computed *"by one deterministic function from … the **owning capability contract's declared floor**, and
from nothing else."* `05` §11: *"It is therefore **floored by the capability**: CAP-13, CAP-14, CAP-15
and CAP-16 floor at the economic/contractual class."* `record-registry.json`:
`ConsequenceDerivation.payload.declared_floor_ref: Ref<ConsequenceClassDefinition>`, **`required: true`**.

**Counterexample, computed.** `planning/specification/capabilities.json` has **no floor field of any
name** — a search for `floor` returns **0** — and **zero** occurrences of any `C0`–`C5` token. What it
does carry is `consequence_classes`, a **53-term free-text domain vocabulary** (`direction`, `financial`,
`contact`, `personal-data`, `publication`, `grievance`, …). CAP-13's value is
`internal-artifact / public-claims / disclosure`; CAP-15's is `customer-promise / financial /
relationship`. Nothing anywhere in the subject maps that vocabulary onto C0–C5.
`ConsequenceClassDefinition` exists and defines a class (`class_id: Enum<C0..C5>`, predicate, required
gates) — but nothing binds a capability to one. There is **no `CapabilityContract` record in the
registry at all**: a search over all 188 record names for `Capab` returns the empty set.

**"The mapping table" is named three times and does not exist.** `05` §11: *"The function, the **mapping
table**, the holder set and every declared floor are C5 protected control changes."* `05` §296 repeats
it. `08-improvement-implementation.md` §13 enumerates *"the class **mapping table**"* as one of six
protected control objects. No such table is in the package.

**Resulting failure.** An implementer deriving the class of an action under CAP-17 (who accepts a
refund), CAP-22 (a privacy request from a non-customer) or CAP-24 (who signs a supplier commitment) must
populate a required field and has no rule for which class definition to point at. Two implementers will
map 53 domain terms onto six classes differently, and the mapping decides **who may act** — the one
thing layer 1 exists to decide. MD-21 defers *which of the four named capabilities* carry the promise
floor, with a stated default of all four; it does not defer, and does not mention, where a floor lives
or how the other 42 get one.

**Required contract.** Either (i) a `declared_floor` field holding a C0–C5 value on every row of
`capabilities.json`, with a stated behaviour when absent; or (ii) the mapping table from
`consequence_classes` to C0–C5, with a named writer and its C5 protected-change registration. Either
way, the floor must be reachable from the capability contract the derivation names.

### F6A-07 · class (a) · all fifteen new records declare `ContextManifest`'s payload in the registry

**Passage.** `planning/specification/contracts/record-registry.json`,
`<Record>.fields.payload.type_fields` and `.required_fields`, for **every one** of `AcceptanceInterval`,
`AdmissionRecord`, `ArmedSet`, `ConsequenceDerivation`, `ConstraintSet`, `EffectIdentity`,
`ExistenceJustification`, `FieldAuthority`, `GrantDeliveryReceipt`, `InstrumentCalibration`,
`ProjectionGrant`, `ShedDecision`, `StandingHolder`, `StandingInterest`, `UnmatchedPoolEntry`.

**What is there.** Each declares, verbatim, `ContextManifest`'s fifteen payload fields —
`work_order_ref, loader_identity_ref, loader_profile_ref, source_refs, blob_refs, captured_inputs,
epochs, transformation_refs, destination_contract_refs, purpose_ids, native_ancestry, omissions,
allocations, closure_snapshot_ref, expires_at` — and lists all fifteen as **required**.

**Why it is unambiguously wrong rather than a convention I misread.** Of 188 records, **165 carry
`type_fields` only**. **23 carry both `type_fields` and `fields`**: the 8 the amendment extended plus
the 15 it created. For all 8 extended records the convention is exact — `WorkflowDefinition.type_fields`
is its own original payload and `.fields` is the six added fields; `AgentInstance.type_fields` is its own
and `.fields` is the three added. For all 15 new records `type_fields` is `ContextManifest`'s.

**Consequences the registry therefore states.** `StandingHolder` — the record §12 defines as the party
whose clock runs with no case admitted and that survives closure — is **required** to carry a
`work_order_ref` and an `expires_at`. `ConsequenceDerivation` is **not** required to carry
`declared_floor_ref`, `reached_class_ids`, `frozen_payload_digest` or `release_path_component_id`.
`AdmissionRecord` is not required to carry `origin` or `arrival_seq`, which §2 gives as its identity.

**Mitigation, and it is real.** `records.schema.json` is generated from `fields` and is **correct** for
all four I spot-checked: `ConsequenceDerivation` requires 11 fields including `declared_floor_ref`;
`StandingHolder` requires 16 including `exercising_fixture_id` and `carries_model_memory`;
`AdmissionRecord` requires 12 including `origin` and `arrival_seq`; `AcceptanceInterval` requires 12
including `submission_seq` and the criteria-freeze fields.

**Why nothing caught it.** `validate_contracts.py:199` reads
`SCHEMAS["records.schema.json"]["$defs"][record_name]["properties"]["payload"]` and never compares it to
the registry's `type_fields`. `05` §10 names the registry as *"the authority for which transitions
exist"*, so the declared authority and the enforced schema disagree for 15 of 188 records with no check
between them. `02` §9 reopen condition 7 makes a registry defect *"a lint failure, not a review
finding"*; this arrived as a review finding.

**Required contract.** Delete `type_fields`/`required_fields` from the fifteen new records, or set them
to each record's own payload, and add a check that `record-registry.json` and `records.schema.json`
agree on every record's payload.

## W11 — vision preservation: **sufficient**

### The plain-language test

Renamed into ordinary business terms, with no architecture vocabulary:

> A company that takes work in through named channels and **turns some of it away with a written
> reason**. Every action is first sorted by **how much damage it could do**, before anyone decides what
> kind of work it is, so an unfamiliar job cannot be quietly filed as something small. Work runs on
> **written standard operating procedures** that say, step by step, which files each step may open — and
> a clerk delivers those files and **writes down what it actually delivered**, rather than the worker
> saying what it read. A **standing watch list** fires automatically when all the conditions for
> something becoming due are met, so a deferral is a visible state with a deadline rather than an
> oversight nobody can tell apart from a decision. **Temporary staff are hired per job**, and only
> against a written justification naming one of six reasons, the thing that reason is about, and **a
> test that could prove the justification wrong**. Four **permanent desks** an outsider can write to by
> name survive the jobs that created their duties. One owner sees a fixed cycle of real customer
> material, a taste decision, an unfamiliar financial explanation, a rehearsal, and the decisions only
> he can make.

A reader who knows business and not this architecture describes **a company operating end to end**. Not
a coding tool, not a dashboard, not a workflow product.

### The roster narrowing, refused mechanically

This is the narrowing the founder rejects by name, and it is the strongest part of the amendment:

- **Specialized knowledge is refused as a reason to create a worker**, with its evidence: across four
  model families, 162 roles and 2,410 questions, adding a persona gave no benefit and picking the best
  persona was no better than random. A general worker loads a versioned, tested procedure instead.
- **Attributable identity is mandatory for every worker and therefore never a reason** — *"a property
  every worker must have cannot license creating one."*
- **The profile-convergence probe is declared as a pre-declared negative control against this design's
  own thesis**: count distinct worker profiles for one month, and *"if they converge, this design has a
  roster it discovered rather than declared."* `02` §9 reopen condition 6 carries it, and the honest
  response named there is to stop calling them ephemeral and design the standing form properly.
- **Carried model memory is refused as a property of a standing party**, on the ground that it is the
  central claim in the round with zero supporting evidence, with the two most-cited deployments named
  against it. What is kept is **reachability**, which is separable from memory and is what CAP-42 rides
  on.

Layer 5 is not a roster: four desks, each with a named frozen fixture exercising it, and *"any other
proposed holder — refused on sight."* That is a smaller standing structure than any candidate proposed,
and it is the same rule that produces F6A-01, which is the price of it.

### F6A-12 · class (c) · the founder-attention figure the design requires of itself is not produced

**Passages.** `05` §12: *"Holder review is **priced**: a review-period length and an estimated
holder-hours-per-period figure **with its denominator**, plus a stated behaviour when holder review
collides with a capacity-pressure window."* `02` §8 repeats it as the second of two unpriced items —
*"which is the discipline the workflow-first candidate stated and did not apply to itself."*

**Counterexample, computed.** No such figure appears in `05`, `02`, `04` or `07`. Searching for
`hours-per-period`, `holder-hours` and `review-period length` returns exactly the two sentences that
*require* the figure. `04` §105's `ParticipationPlan` has a `maximum decision-batch minutes` field and
§107's default is one cycle per five operating days *"within the chosen minutes"* — both are containers,
neither is a value.

**Why the figure is load-bearing, computed from the binding.** There are **46** capabilities, **46
distinct named acceptance roles**, and **41 of 46** carry `human` among their production modes. R-X09
makes *"every acceptance-owner role … a record"* with a reachability route, a capacity reservation, an
alternate and a review date, and **admission in that duty class stops while it is unfilled**. `Q-017`
(MD-02) asks which of those are staffed by a person other than the founder; status `Open`, owner
`founder`, `asked: null`.

Until it is answered the arithmetic has two branches, and the chapter states neither: the founder
occupies 46 roles plus 46 alternates from a population of one — which the word *alternate* forbids — or
admission stops in every unstaffed duty class and the company admits almost nothing. `02` §9 reopen
condition 3 names this exact dynamic, but only for **layer 5's four holders**, not for the 46
acceptance-owner roles R-X09 creates. `05` §13 names three unrun measurements — the rate of genuinely
unfamiliar admitted work, the deterministic-predicate fraction, the account entitlement behind the
concurrency pin — and this is not among them.

**Class (c), and why W11 is still sufficient.** The design does not *claim* a founder load it cannot
support; it commits to reporting the number and has not run it, and `Q-017`'s packet states
`if_no_answer` as a **visible recorded narrowing** rather than a silent overload. On the facts stated,
this is not a full-time founder review job. On the facts not stated, nobody can say — and the chapter is
the party that said it would say.

**Required contract.** The review-period length and holder-hours-per-period figure with its denominator,
over the 46 acceptance-owner roles as well as the four holders, with a baseline, a unit, a repetition, a
stopping rule and an owner. Add it to §13's list of missing measurements.
