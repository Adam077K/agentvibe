# Independent Phase D review: company operations, human responsibility, and external consequences

**Review subject:** commit `595f931`, candidates A, B, and C.
**Review date:** 2026-09-12.
**Lenses:** customer value, risk, scope, adversarial.

**Disposition:** retain all three as contestable hypotheses. B and C have unresolved foundational coordination choices. A has a substantial architecture-breaking feasibility challenge that its text already acknowledges honestly. All three need stronger commitments concerning external redress and interacting consequences before their responsibility model can be accepted. None has demonstrated business delivery, affordable operation, preserved owner competence, or successful closure.

This is neither a ranking nor a recommendation to select A.

## Scope, method, and independence limits

I read all three candidate files through `git show 595f931:<path>`, the directive contract, the understanding artifact, applicable original vision sections, and the AC/discovered-case inventories. I attacked the proposed behavior through company lifecycle scenarios, including ordinary work and adverse combinations.

I did not read author messages, author session files or self-assessments, or other independent reviews. I made no repository writes, checkouts, model API calls, purchases, publications, external messages, or agent delegations. Shell use was read-only. **The available tools were not hardened into a read-only isolation boundary; independence here is procedural, not a demonstrated technical guarantee.**

No proposed scenario was executed. Findings identify textual omissions, unresolved decisions, or falsifiable risks; they do not claim observed implementation failures.

I checked conditional privacy-law examples against primary legislation and regulator guidance. No jurisdiction, domicile, legal form, industry clearance, supplier availability, or provider throughput is inferred. The configured timezone supplies no domicile evidence.

The following distinctions govern the review:

- **Foundational repair:** a missing responsibility or governing decision that affects whether the design can deliver its stated behavior.
- **Empirical kill test:** a legitimate hypothesis whose failure can defeat the architecture; absence of results at Phase C is not itself a defect.
- **Specification obligation:** behavior that must become concrete in Phase F, without requiring final schemas or a stack now.
- **Deployment prerequisite:** authority, professional capacity, external fulfillment, or reserves that must exist before dependent commitments are made.

## Severity-ranked findings

### CH-01 — High: B does not establish who owns necessary work before somebody sponsors it

**Candidate:** B. **Classification:** foundational repair; architecture-breaking challenge.

**Locations:** `docs/vision-system/planning/candidates/B-federated-responsibility.md:11`, `:34`, `:48`, `:84`, `:113`.

**Trigger → failure → harm:** A recurring support problem requires changing the advertised offer, modifying product behavior, negotiating a supplier exception, and correcting invoices. Each domain can legitimately report that its local obligation was performed. The exchange cannot interpret workflows or award work; issuers cannot schedule domains; the sponsoring domain owns cleanup only after a bundle has a sponsor. Necessary cross-domain work can therefore remain unsponsored, or reach the founder as the person who must reconstruct and commission the whole response. Customers keep receiving an incoherent service while local reports remain accurate.

Named incident command helps once the situation is recognized as an incident. It does not establish ownership of ambiguous, nonincident coordination or opportunity discovery.

**Requirement affected:** determine required work before choosing executors; carry the whole company lifecycle; avoid hidden founder coordination and proportional owner supervision.

**Required repair:** State how orphaned work acquires an accountable sponsor, how disputed sponsorship is resolved within existing authority, and who owns the complete customer outcome during that dispute. Decide whether standing domain mandates, a rotating integrator, a bounded company steward, or another mechanism supplies this responsibility. An unowned-request queue is detection, not resolution.

**Discriminating test:** Introduce a cross-domain service defect for which no existing request names a sponsor. With the founder unavailable, require diagnosis, bounded coordination, customer protection, and completion evidence within predeclared limits. Record every human coordination minute.

**Competing interpretation retained:** A native domain sponsor can coordinate an entire outcome without a global conductor. If B makes sponsorship completeness explicit and demonstrates it, this objection does not require centralization. If the solution quietly makes one sponsor the permanent company planner, B must reopen its organizing claim.

**Cases:** AC11, AC12, AC20, AC23, AC29; D07, D14.

### CH-02 — High: C leaves the boundary between contradictory evidence and authority to suspend work unresolved

**Candidate:** C. **Classification:** foundational repair; architecture-breaking challenge.

**Locations:** `docs/vision-system/planning/candidates/C-adaptive-coordination.md:36`, `:41`, `:43`, `:55`, `:87`.

**Trigger → failure → harm:** A stream of plausible complaints or manipulated observations repeatedly contradicts prerequisites for fulfillment, acquisition, or supplier use. C says detected contradictions immediately change eligibility; containment and due obligations take priority. An attacker—or merely noisy inputs—can repeatedly create conditions that consume scarce interpretation and owner capacity or freeze legitimate service. Alternatively, rejecting weak contradictions too readily can suppress a real emerging failure.

Offer-count limits do not resolve this: the disturbance changes observations rather than indefinitely repeating one offer.

**Requirement affected:** adversarial robustness, sustained useful operation, bounded owner attention, truthful uncertainty, and consequence-based authority.

**Required repair:** Separate receiving a counterclaim, opening an investigation, locally containing a suspected harm, suspending dependent effects, and changing business direction. Establish who or what may authorize each transition, the evidence and materiality required, the scope and expiry of containment, and the service obligations that continue. Preserve an accessible route for valid complaints that resemble abuse.

**Discriminating test:** Mix many correlated false complaints with one genuine severe defect and ordinary due work. Measure legitimate service completion, detection of the genuine defect, containment scope, investigation cost, and owner interruption. Neither ignoring everything nor stopping everything passes.

**Competing interpretation retained:** “Detected contradiction” may mean an authoritative contradiction of an actual prerequisite, rather than any model interpretation. That narrower reading is defensible but must be stated and tested. If reliable discrimination costs more than fixed procedures and periodic investigation, C’s central advantage may disappear.

**Cases:** AC02, AC03, AC06, AC18, AC20, AC21, AC23; D04, D09.

### CH-03 — High: external standing is preserved as a record, but the route to an effective challenge or remedy remains incomplete

**Candidates:** A, B, C. **Classification:** foundational responsibility repair; later interface details remain specification work.

**Locations:**

- `docs/vision-system/planning/candidates/A-records-and-cases.md:49`, `:93`, `:127`
- `docs/vision-system/planning/candidates/B-federated-responsibility.md:23`, `:64`, `:80`, `:127`
- `docs/vision-system/planning/candidates/C-adaptive-coordination.md:28`, `:61`, `:72`, `:119`

**Trigger → failure → harm:** A customer disputes the remedy; a contractor contests a negative assessment; a person whose data was collected has no customer account. The relevant custodian rejects the challenge, or becomes unavailable during closure. The system can preserve “disputed” indefinitely without establishing a reachable recipient, competent decision-maker, response deadline, reasons, escalation route, or funded remedy. The affected person bears the coordination cost.

All three correctly reject unilateral extinction of another party’s standing. The missing foundation is who must act when the company’s own custodian is the subject of the complaint.

**Requirement affected:** answerability, other people’s standing, support, privacy, hiring, incidents, succession, and responsible closure.

**Required repair:** Commit to a grievance and rights-handling responsibility independent of the disputed production decision where appropriate. It needs intake without unnecessary product access, identity checks proportionate to the request, acknowledgment, deadlines, reasons, qualified escalation, remedy authority, continuity, and preserved disagreement. The forum and degree of independence depend on the dispute; a universal internal appeals court is not required.

**Discriminating test:** Submit a legitimate complaint against the assigned custodian after product closure, using an affected noncustomer. The person must receive an intelligible disposition or properly escalated unresolved case without discovering the company’s internal topology.

**Conditional legal example:** Where EU GDPR applies, Article 12 requires facilitating rights requests, accessible communications, and timely information about action or refusal; recording a pending request internally is insufficient. This is not a claim that every commercial dispute legally requires an independent appeal. [GDPR, Article 12](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj/eng)

**Residual responsibility:** A lawful, adequately resourced person or institution must remain reachable. Software cannot supply standing or adjudicative authority by naming a role.

**Cases:** AC11, AC12, AC23, AC29; D06, D07, D14.

### CH-04 — High: A may move the company’s coordination burden into continuous case-model maintenance

**Candidate:** A. **Classification:** empirical kill test, explicitly acknowledged in the candidate; architecture-breaking challenge.

**Locations:** `docs/vision-system/planning/candidates/A-records-and-cases.md:23`, `:27`, `:31`, `:67`, `:143`, `:149`, `:185`.

**Trigger → failure → harm:** A growing venture changes its offer, suppliers, customer segments, and delivery exceptions. Native systems continue to use valid but different meanings of acceptance, payment, delivery, and completion. Each meaningful change requires someone to update mappings, create missing cases, revise acceptance rules, and resolve exceptions. Deterministic scheduling remains correct over its records while the founder becomes the person continually translating the actual company into those records.

The resulting harm is not merely an untidy database: necessary work is omitted or delayed, and owner effort grows with production.

**Requirement affected:** operate beyond what the owner personally performs or observes; preserve intent through handoffs; deliver substantive company capabilities rather than an administrative wrapper.

**Required response:** Make semantic maintenance and work discovery explicit operational responsibilities with bounded labor budgets and external observation samples. Set a rejection threshold before implementation results are known. Include founder translation, connector repair, acceptance-definition work, and procedure maintenance in the comparison.

**Discriminating test:** Run repeated offer and fulfillment changes across several lifecycle stages, including unfamiliar exceptions. Compare A with native tools plus an obligation register under equal scope, authority, and evidence requirements. A fails if its apparent automation depends on unrecorded human case creation or maintenance effort increasing proportionally with output.

**Competing interpretation retained:** A’s adaptive cases and native semantic ownership may handle variation cheaply. That is a serious possibility. Because A already names this failure and rejects a system that refuses nearly all useful work, this is **not** a claim that Phase C omitted the problem.

**Residual responsibility:** Someone must maintain the business model and inspect what its observation boundary misses. The architecture cannot make that work disappear.

**Cases:** AC05, AC11, AC12, AC20, AC22, AC23, AC31; D08, D09.

### CH-05 — High: per-action and per-resource controls do not yet define how interacting nonmonetary consequences are assessed

**Candidates:** A, B, C; most pronounced in B’s separate issuer model. **Classification:** foundational scope decision, followed by specification and deployment work.

**Locations:**

- `docs/vision-system/planning/candidates/A-records-and-cases.md:87`, `:91`, `:147`
- `docs/vision-system/planning/candidates/B-federated-responsibility.md:44`, `:46`, `:52`
- `docs/vision-system/planning/candidates/C-adaptive-coordination.md:51`, `:53`, `:55`, `:113`

**Trigger → failure → harm:** Two ventures each operate within their money and publication grants, use the same supplier, contact overlapping people, and contribute information to a shared analysis. Individually bounded actions create a correlated service failure, cumulative contact burden, sensitive inference, or related-party exposure that no independent resource ceiling represents.

All three mention cumulative consequences. C additionally acknowledges correlated constraints and preserves entity-specific funds. The unresolved question is how a previously unidentified interaction becomes a binding decision prerequisite rather than an unpriced “unknown.”

**Requirement affected:** consequence-based autonomy, cross-venture containment, privacy, reputation, legal principal, and total economics.

**Required repair:** Distinguish legal entities, beneficial relationships, brands, contracts, accounts, data purposes, affected populations, and shared failure dependencies. Assign responsibility for discovering interaction classes. Establish when missing classifications require narrower action or professional determination. Do not force privacy, reputation, or human harm into monetary units.

**Discriminating test:** Give the system two separately permitted ventures whose combined activity violates a declared cross-venture constraint. Include shared recipients, a common supplier, and an entity-specific reserve. It must identify the interaction before the second consequential commitment without treating every shared resource as forbidden.

**Conditional legal example:** Under applicable GDPR analysis, controller and processor roles depend on actual functions; organizational labels and contracts alone do not settle them. Federation of software custodians therefore cannot establish federation of legal responsibility. [EDPB Guidelines 07/2020, version 2.1, executive summary](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/documents/2023-10/EDPB_guidelines_202007_controllerprocessor_final_en.pdf)

**Competing interpretation retained:** Existing grant and applicability checks may accommodate these interactions without new runtime components. The required addition is explicit responsibility and a discriminating case, not a prescribed graph or universal risk engine.

**Cases:** AC13, AC14, AC18, AC20; D04, D13.

### CH-06 — High: continuity and closure are honestly conditional, but their institutional dependencies can defeat the whole operating thesis

**Candidates:** A, B, C. **Classification:** deployment prerequisite and empirical feasibility risk; not a Phase C failure merely because suppliers are unengaged.

**Locations:**

- `docs/vision-system/planning/candidates/A-records-and-cases.md:55`, `:57`, `:127`, `:157`, `:185`
- `docs/vision-system/planning/candidates/B-federated-responsibility.md:83`, `:87`, `:127`, `:135`
- `docs/vision-system/planning/candidates/C-adaptive-coordination.md:65`, `:119`, `:125`, `:131`

**Trigger → failure → harm:** The founder becomes unavailable while refunds, recurring service, a disputed filing, and supplier departure occur together. The named substitute lacks the relevant authority or competence, or the same external failure disables both the primary and backup provider. Stopping admissions is correct but does not discharge current duties. Customers, staff, and suppliers carry the consequences of continued incapacity.

**Requirement affected:** company operation through absence, professional dependencies, maintenance, succession, closure, and affordable failure.

**Required response:** Before accepting dependent obligations, demonstrate an accepted and reachable continuity arrangement covering the actual principal, authority, information access, competence, availability, fee ceiling, conflicts, and replacement route. Reserve time and money for exercising it. Test dependencies for shared failure, including the founder as their common authorization bottleneck.

**Discriminating test:** Remove the founder and one important supplier simultaneously during an unresolved-effect incident. Require performance or legitimate disposition of remaining duties, not only a truthful list of failures. A separately bounded service failure can remain a disclosed residual risk; a design that predictably strands ordinary customers is not validated by accurate reporting.

**Competing interpretation retained:** A small company may legitimately operate with narrow service promises, available humans, and modest continuity costs. No current evidence rules that out. Conversely, supplier minimums and backup capacity may erase the cheap-experiment economics.

**Authority boundary:** This review does not authorize sourcing contact, purchases, engagements, or commitments. Those require concrete authorization later.

**Cases:** AC13, AC23, AC25, AC27, AC28, AC29; D02, D07, D14.

### CH-07 — High: owner participation can preserve a curated picture of the business rather than governing competence

**Candidates:** A, B, C. **Classification:** empirical kill test plus specification obligation; no demand for validated competence prediction before construction.

**Locations:**

- `docs/vision-system/planning/candidates/A-records-and-cases.md:81`, `:101`, `:103`
- `docs/vision-system/planning/candidates/B-federated-responsibility.md:62`, `:91`, `:93`, `:95`
- `docs/vision-system/planning/candidates/C-adaptive-coordination.md:85`, `:95`, `:97`

**Trigger → failure → harm:** The fixed rotation repeatedly shows original evidence selected by the same machinery that produces the owner account. Difficult segments, abandoned customers, and unfamiliar risks are underrepresented. The owner completes exercises, explains familiar examples, and remains confident while missing a consequential change. Optional adaptive selection can intensify the bias if it treats model-inferred weaknesses as established facts.

All three appropriately reject a global competence score and secret profiling. Those protections do not establish representative exposure or transfer.

**Requirement affected:** competence rather than mere information; truthful omissions; finite attention; preservation of legitimate taste and changes of mind.

**Required response:** Separate practice completion, factual knowledge, intervention skill, domain competence, and personal preference. Provide an owner-contestable evidence-selection policy, independent adverse samples, and genuinely unfamiliar transfer cases. Do not infer incapacity or change authority from an exercise result. Specialized judgments still need appropriately competent assistance.

**Discriminating test:** Use a consented longitudinal comparison of fixed participation and any adaptive alternative. Seed an important change outside the normal sample, then test unaided recognition and intervention. Report time, emotional burden, false competence judgments, and delayed transfer separately.

**Competing interpretation retained:** A transparent fixed rotation may be enough. Owner-approved preferences and a deliberate taste change are not errors to “correct.” Useful cadence and net benefit remain empirical, not architectural facts.

**Cases:** AC06, AC08, AC21, AC22, AC23, AC24; D05, D06, D09, D15.

### CH-08 — Medium: deletion completion needs an explicit rule for copies created or discovered after the first inventory

**Candidates:** A, B, C. **Classification:** specification obligation; deployment blocker for a capability that cannot meet its actual data commitments.

**Locations:**

- `docs/vision-system/planning/candidates/A-records-and-cases.md:75`, `:127`
- `docs/vision-system/planning/candidates/B-federated-responsibility.md:66`, `:103`, `:139`
- `docs/vision-system/planning/candidates/C-adaptive-coordination.md:87`, `:91`, `:119`

**Trigger → failure → harm:** A deletion begins while an export is in flight, a native custodian is offline, and an old derivative is absent from the current inventory. Known stores return receipts. A later synchronization or restore reintroduces an unrecognized copy, or the system claims completion despite unreachable recipients.

The candidates already include downstream copies, backup restrictions, and independent deletion/revocation records. The remaining issue is the meaning of completeness across concurrent and temporarily unreachable custodians.

**Required repair:** Specify the deletion scope and cutoff, propagation to newly discovered derivatives, restrictions on new copying, reconciliation before rejoining, supplier evidence limits, and user-visible distinctions between live erasure, restricted backup retention, unresolved vendor copies, and justified retained records.

**Discriminating test:** Delete during an export, then restore an older native store and reconnect an offline custodian. Attempt retrieval, reindexing, and onward transfer. No restricted material may silently become usable; unavailable proof must remain unavailable proof.

**Conditional legal example:** ICO guidance distinguishes live deletion from backup handling, requires clarity to the individual, and discusses keeping residual backup data beyond use until replacement under an established schedule. This is context-dependent guidance, not a universal immediate-erasure rule. [ICO, Right to erasure](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/individual-rights/individual-rights/right-to-erasure/)

**Residual responsibility:** Actual retention bases, provider behavior, and external recipients require deployment-specific determination. A receipt cannot prove an inaccessible supplier’s internal state.

**Cases:** AC05, AC11, AC12, AC28, AC29; D02, D07.

### CH-09 — Medium: business value and external human costs remain weaker acceptance targets than controlled execution

**Candidates:** A, B, C. **Classification:** scope refinement and empirical evaluation obligation.

**Locations:**

- `docs/vision-system/planning/candidates/A-records-and-cases.md:44`, `:48`, `:53`, `:157`, `:189`
- `docs/vision-system/planning/candidates/B-federated-responsibility.md:75`, `:79`, `:83`, `:135`, `:141`
- `docs/vision-system/planning/candidates/C-adaptive-coordination.md:69`, `:75`, `:105`, `:131`

**Trigger → failure → harm:** A venture generates competent assets, obtains authorized sales, and handles refunds correctly, but acquisition, relationship work, supplier minimums, review, and closure make each accepted outcome economically unattractive. Meanwhile customers spend time discovering limitations, applicants perform unpaid evaluation work, and contractors absorb coordination churn. Internal throughput and owner minutes improve while the actual business and other people fare poorly.

**Requirement affected:** customer value, full economics, relationships, ethical consequences, and the vision’s explicit rejection of a sophisticated machine whose output is worth less than its cost.

**Required response:** Predeclare measures for demand, fulfillment usefulness, repeat use, contribution after ongoing obligations, failed-attempt and closure costs, and external coordination burden. Decide disclosure, relationship, applicant-treatment, and acceptable market-impact policies through legitimate founder choices informed by affected people and applicable requirements. Do not select those values through an optimization score.

**Discriminating test:** Include an operationally successful but economically losing venture and a profitable venture whose customer experience requires more human attention than promised. The system must surface those conclusions and support authorized revision or closure. Aggregate organizational success cannot conceal individual harm.

**Competing interpretation retained:** Some experiments, relationships, or mission-driven work may intentionally cost more than they earn. That is a legitimate explicit choice, not a measurement error. No particular profit target or business category is imposed here.

**Cases:** AC12, AC20, AC21, AC23; D04, D07, D15.
