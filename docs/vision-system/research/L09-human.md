# L09 — Human operation, attention, and competence

The human problem is maintaining effective judgment over consequential work without making its owner inspect every output. More visibility can help, but visibility alone supplies neither understanding nor intervention capacity. The strongest evidence supports specific failure mechanisms and bounded countermeasures; it does not establish that one person can sustainably govern an automated company.

**Founder constraints:** the vision, rewritten 2026-09-09, and directive contract dated 2026-09-12 require consequence-based authority, preserved competence, truthful omissions, bounded attention, and interfaces derived from operator jobs. These are requirements, not empirical findings. The following implications are proposals for competing designs; no architecture, roster, or page inventory is selected.

## Operator jobs before surfaces

| Job | Information needed | Successful outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Set direction and change it | Purpose, exclusions, promises, trade-offs, affected work | The interpretation is inspectable and a correction changes dependent work |
| Decide what only this person can decide | Exact choice, alternatives, consequences, uncertainty, deadline | A considered decision or explicit deferral within existing authority |
| Understand unwatched work | Results, failed attempts, omissions, stale evidence, unresolved consequences | A defensible account with routes to original evidence |
| Intervene and recover | Current execution, external effects, available controls, acknowledgments | Actual containment and a known remaining obligation |
| Stay capable and connected | Customer language, representative work, taste examples, outcome feedback | The owner can explain, challenge, and redirect the business |
| Allocate scarce capacity | Attention demand, commitments, service/account limits, absence plans | Work is declined, delayed, delegated, or ended before overload |

These jobs span discovery, sales, support, finance, operation, and closure. A refund dispute and a failing deployment need different evidence but share the need to distinguish intention, authority, execution, and confirmed consequence. A second founder introduces explicit decision ownership and handover; silently routing everything to the most responsive person is not shared responsibility.

## Findings and competing interpretations

**F1 — Presence in the loop is insufficient.** Mosier and Skitka describe automation bias as substituting automated cues for verification, including action despite contrary evidence; their research overview reports comparable automation error rates across expert and novice populations.[^1] Endsley and Kiris found that reduced situation awareness accompanied slower decisions after expert-system failure in a navigation experiment.[^2] These are bounded findings about human–automation interaction, not estimates of business failure rates. Requiring a click does not establish comprehension, and expertise does not guarantee immunity.

**F2 — Competence can be lost, transformed, or developed.** Bainbridge’s original analysis identifies the difficulty of leaving rare, difficult recovery work to operators whose practical involvement has diminished.[^3] A survey of 319 knowledge workers associated higher confidence in generative AI with less reported critical thinking, while describing a shift toward verification, integration, and task stewardship.[^4] It measured reports and associations, not longitudinal skill decay. Conversely, Drydakis followed 121 underrepresented small-business entrepreneurs before training in 2023 and one year later in 2024; digital competencies and business outcomes improved together. Its pre/post design lacks an untreated comparison, and important measures are self-reported.[^5] This is relevant longitudinal small-firm evidence, but it evaluates training, not autonomous-company supervision. Neither inevitable deskilling nor guaranteed competence preservation is established.

**F3 — Friction trades error reduction against acceptability.** In a short online experiment with 199 retained participants, Buçinca and colleagues found that cognitive forcing reduced overreliance relative to simple explanations, without eliminating it. More effective interventions were less liked, and benefits varied with motivation for effortful thinking.[^6] This supports testing selective independent assessment before revealing advice; it does not support forcing every approval through quizzes. Explanations may be persuasive while failing to improve discrimination between correct and incorrect recommendations.

**F4 — Speed can conceal attention costs.** Mark and colleagues’ simulated office experiment found faster interrupted work alongside greater stress, frustration, effort, and time pressure.[^7] The authors explicitly could not infer adaptation over time. Google’s operational account advocates actionable, low-noise alerts and describes reducing excessive Bigtable alerting to recover improvement capacity.[^8] Together they challenge throughput and response speed as sufficient success measures. They do not yield a universal number of decisions per day or a scientifically established founder attention budget.

**F5 — Control includes reasons and practical ability.** Santoni de Sio and van den Hoven’s philosophical account requires responsiveness to relevant human reasons and traceability to humans who understand their responsibilities.[^9] This is a normative framework, not experimental validation or a legal safe harbor. Horvitz’s mixed-initiative work combines automation with direct invocation, termination, refinement, and responses proportionate to uncertainty.[^10] Its LookOut scheduling prototype is a useful mechanism precedent, not evidence that inferred attention or animated assistants improve company governance.

The live disagreement is whether active participation preserves valuable judgment or spends scarce attention on skills no longer needed. The relevant distinction is between retaining every production skill and retaining the ability to set standards, recognize consequential errors, understand customers, and obtain capable help. Which skills remain essential depends on the business and its recovery obligations.

## Mechanisms worth testing and mechanisms to reject

**Decision packets — design proposal.** Begin with the exact decision and why this person is needed. Include the latest responsible decision time, options including delay or abandonment, consequences, evidence and counterevidence, uncertainty, reversibility, recommendation, behavior without an answer, and independent work that continues. Bind approval to the concrete artifact, scope, recipient, account, and version; material changes require a new decision. Separate authorization from acknowledgment and from mere silence. Layer supporting detail while retaining consequential omissions in the initial account. Unknown confidence must remain unknown rather than becoming an invented percentage.

**Attention admission — design proposal.** Use owner-declared availability, observed decision backlog, deadline slack, and sampled handling time to estimate whether accepted work fits. Include customer contact, recovery, and learning in that capacity. Reserve headroom for incidents; the appropriate amount is unknown. Group requests by the decision they require, remove duplicates, and schedule routine batches. If capacity is exceeded, reduce incoming commitments under the agreed policy. Hiding an alert does not remove its obligation; record suppression, reason, expiry, and escalation. Reject automatic permission expansion as a remedy for overload.

**Competence-preserving participation — hypothesis.** Agree a small, owner-controlled allocation for authentic customer contact, reviewing representative successes and failures, stating a taste judgment before seeing suggestions, and rehearsing selected recovery actions in a safe environment. Tie each activity to a real decision or known recovery need, and evaluate whether it transfers to later independent judgment. Allow alternative participation modes and revision of the arrangement. Reject compulsory busywork, secret attention surveillance, productivity scoring from response latency, and punishment for disagreement. If the owner lacks domain expertise, extra reading cannot substitute for an appropriate professional.

**Return from absence — design proposal.** Provide changes since the last acknowledged state, altered commitments, decisions waiting, actions already taken, stale assumptions, and remaining external consequences. Preserve the interrupted task’s place and reasoning. An absence policy must define safe continuation and stopping before illness occurs. Silence must not become consent because a timer expired.

## Interfaces as projections of those jobs

Official product documentation establishes the following affordances; they were not tested in authenticated deployments.

| Precedent | Useful mechanism | Limit on inference |
|---|---|---|
| GitHub CLI | `gh run watch` offers live progress and compact relevant/failed steps; `gh auth status` identifies active accounts and authentication problems.[^11] | A terminal and structured output can share operational objects; this does not establish usability for nontechnical owners |
| Temporal CLI | Termination records a reason and stops workflow execution; cancellation permits workflow cleanup that termination cannot.[^12] | A stop request, completed stop, and reversal of external effects are distinct |
| Stripe Dashboard | Business/account views connect transactions, balances, request outcomes, resources, and mobile operation.[^13] | Vendor representations need freshness and scope checks; request success is not business success |
| Gather | Availability and optional app status connect avatars to selected signals; classic documentation explicitly says idle time can mean working elsewhere.[^14] | Presence, app focus, activity, and useful progress cannot be equated |

**Derived requirements, not selected pages:** a terminal must preserve conversation, executable commands, plans, concurrent work, code/diffs/tests, failures, environment/account context, approvals, evidence, checkpoints, and recovery across task switches. Live control must disclose observation age and separately show “requested,” “acknowledged,” and “confirmed”; disconnection means unknown state. Account views must distinguish money spent, allowance consumed, reset time, queue pressure, permission, and identity. Displaying an available account must not silently authorize switching providers or paid fallback.

A phone should support urgent triage, bounded decisions, and stopping safely, with a persistent handoff for work requiring more context. Thirty seconds is an operating scenario, not a blanket decision-time target. Reduced space cannot justify concealing recipients, monetary exposure, or unresolved consequences. WCAG 2.2 supplies testable requirements for keyboard access, focus, reflow, target size, status messages, and alternatives to dragging.[^15] Screen-reader access, reduced motion, plain-language errors, and non-color state distinctions belong in the acceptance conditions; conformance alone does not prove usability under stress.

An optional playful office can make location and grouping memorable, but every meaningful animation needs an explicit mapping to observed state, timestamp, uncertainty, and inspectable work identity. An animated worker must not imply reasoning quality, sentient welfare, or continuing execution after telemetry stops. Preserve the same facts and controls in a text/list representation; movement must never be required to approve, inspect, or stop. Reject fictional busyness and completion celebrations before verification. Its value over a plain account remains a hypothesis.

## Failures, evaluation, and unanswered questions

Candidate designs should survive a burst of approvals, correlated summary errors, a stale phone session, a lost stop acknowledgment, a provider/account switch, a month-long absence, and an owner approving outside their competence. A producer-controlled summary could omit precisely what would change the owner’s judgment. Countermeasures therefore need inspectable selection/omission records and independent evidence access, not simply more fluent summaries.

Evaluate decision correctness, missed consequences, time to regain context, confirmed intervention latency, interruption burden, sustainable hours, and ability to explain the business after time away. Compare plain periodic accounts, mixed-initiative assistance, and continuous control using the same operator jobs. Track whether source inspection detects seeded mistakes and whether learned judgment transfers to new cases. Use consensual operational samples and explicit exercises; avoid undisclosed deception in consequential work. Longer trials must include ordinary successful weeks, unpleasant exceptions, and winding down, not only demonstrations.

**Assumptions:** the owner can declare availability, some consequences permit delay, authentic customer evidence is accessible with consent, and useful authority boundaries can be expressed. **Unknowns:** sustainable scale; competence-maintenance dosage; effects across disabilities, languages, expertise, and device constraints; whether office representation helps beyond novelty; whether suppressed information remains discoverable in practice. **Missed questions:** who supports an isolated owner emotionally; who notices the owner’s goals becoming unwanted; who can take over when the owner cannot evaluate their own impairment; which relationships require human presence; and how much business complexity should be refused outright? These remain open rather than being assigned to an interface by default.
