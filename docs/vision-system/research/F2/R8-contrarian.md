## 2. Steelman A · Steelman B · The thesis's weak points

### Steelman A — a company of chartered persistent agents

**A-1. INFERENCE. The middle position already contains five of the six properties a charter would add, and the argument is therefore about two properties, not about persistence.** `AgentTemplate` is "a versioned production recipe — not an identity with standing", bound per work order (CP-74). But a versioned template carries a stable identity (its version), an instruction scope (CP-106), tests and a maintenance owner (CP-109), evaluation as a named subject (CP-144), and a retirement procedure requiring reassignment of work and revocation of tools (CP-78). Charter, evaluation, suspension and retirement are already there. The two properties a charter would add and the package refuses are **carried-over memory across work orders** and **reserved capacity bound to a named holder**. Anyone arguing this question as "roster versus no roster" is arguing about the wrong five-sixths.

**A-2. SOURCE CLAIM, conflicted vendor. The one published quantitative result favouring multiple executors is confined to research-shaped work and explicitly excludes most coding work.** Anthropic reports a "multi-agent system with Claude Opus 4 as the lead agent and Claude Sonnet 4 subagents outperformed single-agent Claude Opus 4 by 90.2% on our internal research eval", while stating in the same post that "most coding tasks involve fewer truly parallelizable tasks than research" and that "for economic viability, multi-agent systems require tasks where the value of the task is high enough to pay for the increased performance" [S5]. The scope of this company is whole-company operation, of which research-shaped work is a real and recurring part (CAP-01, market research, discovery). A steelman for A can legitimately claim the one measured win in the literature; it cannot generalise it to construction.

**A-3. SOURCE CLAIM, secondary. Team familiarity has a measured positive effect on performance in a large services setting, and conventional individual experience does not.** Huckman, Staats and Upton report that "the level of team familiarity ... has a significant positive effect on performance", while "conventional measures of individual team members' experience ... are not consistently related to performance", and that role experience is associated with better team performance [S24]. This is the strongest non-vendor support for continuity as a mechanism. **Its transfer is unvalidated** and I could not open the publisher's page; the finding reached me through a search-result summary of the abstract, so it is recorded as secondary at medium confidence.

**A-4. INFERENCE, bound to the package. A class-based reservation cannot be negotiated at demand pressure; a holder-based one can.** `07` §6 reserves capacity by *class* — due service, grievance, recovery, maintenance, discretionary. Fixture F-2's adverse variation requires that "one lane must be shed", by a named authority, with a record of what the founder sees. A class has no representative to ask. A chartered agent has a mandate, a holder and a suspension procedure (DIRECTIVE §8.8 names suspension, demotion, replacement and deletion as required definitions). **This is the single place in the six fixtures where a charter does work the middle position has no mechanism for.**

**A-5. INFERENCE. Two of review D's unresolved high findings ask for a reachable named party that survives the work, which is what a charter is.** CH-03 requires "a grievance and rights-handling responsibility independent of the disputed production decision", with intake, deadlines, escalation and continuity; CH-06 requires "an accepted and reachable continuity arrangement". The middle position's answer is the ownership resolver assigning a custodian by rule (CP-06, CP-14). An assignment rule produces a custodian at the moment it is asked. A complaint from a non-customer six months after closure needs a party that already existed and can be reached. A does not solve these, but it is the shape of answer they are asking for.

**A-6. INFERENCE. Legibility to the owner is a first-class requirement here, and a roster is the most legible structure a non-specialist can hold.** W11 fails a candidate if a business-literate reader describes "a roster of digital employees". W12 fails it if the founder becomes a full-time reviewer or has no competence mechanism. These two pull in opposite directions: the structure that is easiest for a founder to hold in his head is precisely the one W11 rejects by name. A steelman for A is entitled to point out that W11 encodes a conclusion the round has not reached, and to ask what evidence supports rejecting the most legible structure available. **This is a criterion tension, not a defect of A.**

**A-7. UNKNOWN, and it is the load-bearing hole in A.** No source found in this lane measures whether persistent model identity improves accepted outcomes. The two most-cited public deployments of "persistent agent" and "AI employee" products both fail to support it. An independent one-month review of Devin reports: "Out of 20 tasks we attempted, we saw 14 failures, 3 inconclusive results, and just 3 successes", and attributes the failures to persistence itself: "The autonomous nature that seemed promising became a liability — Devin would spend days pursuing impossible solutions rather than recognizing fundamental blockers" [S19]. Klarna's own press release claims its assistant "is doing the equivalent work of 700 full-time agents" and "$40 million USD in profit improvement" [S20]; fifteen months later the company reversed and rehired, with its chief executive quoted saying cost-driven automation produces "lower quality" [S21, secondary]. **Neither refutes chartered persistence. Both refuse to support it, and A must carry that.**

### Steelman B — zero agents: durable workflow plus model calls as steps, plus one long session

**B-1. SOURCE CLAIM, primary, vendor-neutral in direction. The durable-workflow pattern supplies, as its execution model, four of the things dimension W7 asks a candidate to add.** Temporal's reference architecture states "All non-deterministic I/O belongs in Activities, not Workflows", that "Workflow code is replayed from history during failure recovery", that "Activities are side effects that are recorded once and replayed from history", and that a durable wait "is durable — no worker thread is consumed while waiting", supporting "a one-shot approval gate" and "a multi-turn interactive loop" [S22]. Checkpointing, idempotency, resumption and a human-approval wait are properties of the substrate, not components someone must remember to add. The middle position has the same primitive in one rule (CP-68: only C02 advances the cursor, in the same protected transaction), which is correct and narrower.

**B-2. SOURCE CLAIM, conflicted vendor, arguing against its own product category. The strongest published argument against splitting is that splitting manufactures conflicting implicit decisions.** Cognition's two principles are "Share context, and share full agent traces, not just individual messages" and "Actions carry implicit decisions, and conflicting decisions carry bad results", with the failure mode stated as subagents whose "work ends up being inconsistent with each other" because of "conflicting assumptions not prescribed upfront" [S14]. **Conflict of interest note, and it cuts the unusual way:** the author sells a persistent autonomous agent, so this is a vendor arguing *against* the architecture that would flatter its category. That makes it more credible on this specific point, not less.

**B-3. SOURCE CLAIM, primary, academic. Measured gains from multi-agent systems on published benchmarks are often minimal, and their failures are systematic.** MAST reports: "Despite enthusiasm for Multi-Agent LLM Systems (MAS), their performance gains on popular benchmarks are often minimal", from a dataset of "1600+ annotated traces collected across 7 popular MAS frameworks", with 14 failure modes and inter-annotator agreement of κ = 0.88 [S16].

**B-4. SOURCE CLAIM, primary, vendor. The vendor's own guidance is B's guidance.** "Start with simple prompts, optimize them with comprehensive evaluation, and add multi-step agentic systems only when simpler solutions fall short", and "Agentic systems often trade latency and cost for better task performance, and you should consider when this tradeoff makes sense" [S6].

**B-5. DISAGREEMENT, preserved. The best randomized evidence on AI-assisted work is a slowdown, and the same organisation now says its own result is probably no longer current.** METR's randomized controlled trial of 16 experienced developers across 246 real tasks found that "when developers are allowed to use AI tools, they take 19% longer to complete issues", against a forecast speed-up of 24% [S17]. In February 2026 METR wrote: "we believe it is likely that developers are more sped up from AI tools now — in early 2026 — compared to our estimates from early 2025", while warning that its newer data "is only very weak evidence for the size of this increase" [S18]. **Neither figure may be cited without the other.** Per protocol §7, this is model-generation dependent and both halves are retained.

**B-6. DIRECT OBSERVATION, and it contradicts a strength the frozen protocol grants B0.** Protocol §6 grants B0 "the lowest plausible consumption of scarce subscription capacity" among its legitimate strengths. Anthropic's own documentation gives four mechanisms by which a single long session consumes *more* plan allowance than its activity suggests: "Claude Code sends your full conversation with every request, and each time Claude uses tools it sends another request carrying that batch of tool results"; "your first message after a break longer than the cache lifetime misses the cache and reprocesses your full context", where "the lifetime is an hour on a subscription"; "`/compact` reads the conversation it summarizes, so compacting a large context is itself a large request"; and scheduled tasks and idle check-ins each send "your full context" while the session sits idle [S3]. **B0 minimises *launches*. It does not minimise the *weekly bucket*, and the weekly bucket is what binds.** The protocol is frozen and I am not amending it; I am reporting a primary source that falsifies one clause of a granted strength, which §6's own instruction to demonstrate rather than assert invites.

**B-7. INFERENCE. Determinism is the only lever that actually reduces subscription consumption, and it belongs to every position.** `05` §7 states "no model runs merely to notice time" and CP-69 requires deterministic transformations to execute without a model. Every step moved out of a model call costs zero provider allowance. B maximises that share by construction. **A candidate that adds executors while leaving deterministic work in model calls has not paid for its executors; it has paid for the wrong thing twice.**

### The thesis's weak points

**W-1. The "thirty agents in a department" cost claim is directionally supported by the vendor and materially misdescribed by the founder, because at CP1 it is not a cost claim at all.** Anthropic states that "agent teams use approximately 7x more tokens than standard sessions when teammates run in plan mode", that "token costs scale linearly: each teammate has its own context window and consumes tokens independently", that there are "diminishing returns: beyond a certain point, additional teammates don't speed up work proportionally", and recommends "3-5 teammates for most workflows", adding "three focused teammates often outperform five scattered ones" [S3, S4]. Separately: "agents typically use about 4× more tokens than chat interactions" and "multi-agent systems use about 15× more tokens than chats" [S5], which is roughly 3.75× a single agent, not 30×. **But `07` §6 fixes CP1 at one native job plus one external job. Thirty executors under CP1 cannot run concurrently; they run in sequence. Sequential executors do not multiply concurrent context windows. What thirty executors buy you at CP1 is thirty serialised launches, which is an elapsed-time cost and a handoff-loss cost, not a token-multiplication cost.** The founder's cost intuition is right about a system with thirty concurrent workers and is describing a system this specification does not permit.

**W-2. A mechanism the package does not currently carry makes the multi-executor capacity penalty larger than the token count implies.** "An in-process teammate's requests fall outside the main conversation's cache TTL bucket, so its cache holds for five minutes by default, including on a Claude subscription" [S4]. A parallel executor therefore pays cache-miss reprocessing that a continuation of the same context does not. **REGISTRABLE.** Proposed: *a parallel executor's prompt cache expires in five minutes against the main session's one hour on a subscription, so splitting work costs more scarce allowance than the token arithmetic predicts.* `valid_until` 2026-12-13; invalidator: a change to `subagentPromptCacheTtl` defaults or to subscription cache lifetimes.

**W-3. The "shared source of truth" claim is contradicted by the package in three places and by measurement in one.** The package: native meanings stay authoritative and shared meaning covers only actual cross-boundary decisions (CP-22); workers read "authorized peers' work projections" and "cannot see all company context merely to coordinate" (CP-84); a query against all records followed by client-side filtering is prohibited (CP-135). The measurement: Chroma's report across 18 models finds "Models do not use their context uniformly; instead, their performance grows increasingly unreliable as input length grows", holding "task complexity constant while varying only the input length", with "even a single distractor" reducing performance and full prompts with irrelevant context significantly underperforming focused prompts [S15]. **A larger authoritative view delivered to every executor makes every executor measurably worse.** The founder's shared-truth requirement therefore cannot mean one store read by everyone; it must mean one *authority* over each field, with delivery narrowed per attempt. Those are different designs and the thesis does not distinguish them. Note the live disagreement: Cognition prescribes the opposite, "share full agent traces, not just individual messages" [S14]. Retained unresolved.

**W-4. Capability-differentiation is a roster by another name unless someone states whether the partition is recomputed or persisted, and nobody has.** Conway: "Any organization that designs a system will inevitably produce a design whose structure is a copy of the organization's communication structure", and "To the extent that an organization is not completely flexible in its communication structure, that organization will stamp out an image of itself in every design it produces" [S23]. The five criteria (parallel work, distinct tools or permissions, isolated context, specialized knowledge, independent verification) are a partition function. Applied to a stable workload, a partition function yields a stable partition. **The distinguishing test is not vocabulary and not the criteria; it is whether the set of instantiated executor profiles is recomputed per work order or converges.** If over a month the same six profiles recur, the system has a roster it discovered instead of declared, and negative control 8 should catch it. Nothing in the package measures this. The package's own answer, CP-76, uses *four* reasons rather than the founder's five, and drops "parallel work" and "isolated context" as qualifiers for a specialist — which is itself an unremarked contradiction between the package and the thesis.

**W-5. FOUNDER CONSTRAINT, unenforced. The no-silent-metered-fallback rule is defeated by a documented in-product affordance at both providers.** Claude Code: `/usage-credits` opens the credits setting, and "on Pro and Max plans, when you reach your spend limit with usage credits still available, Claude Code prompts you to raise or remove the limit without leaving the CLI"; turning credits on also drops the prompt-cache lifetime from an hour to five minutes [S3]. Codex: "ChatGPT Plus and Pro users who reach their usage limit can purchase additional credits to continue working without needing to upgrade their existing plan" [S10]. **The subscription-to-metered path is one setting away on both providers, is surfaced at exactly the moment of exhaustion, and the package has no mechanism that observes or blocks it.** CP-173 states the policy; nothing enforces it. This is the conformance gap in TC-26.

**W-6. DIRECT OBSERVATION. The denominator for "scarce capacity consumed" does not exist on the Claude side.** The current Max plan page states only "20 times more usage per session than the Pro plan", "Max plans also have a weekly usage limit that applies across all models", "The weekly limit resets at a fixed time each week that is assigned to your account", and "to manage capacity and ensure fair access to all users, we may limit your usage in other ways, such as weekly and monthly caps" [S1]. No absolute figure appears on that page, on the usage-and-length-limits page [S2], on the best-practices page [S8], or on the Claude Code Pro/Max page [S9], all fetched 2026-09-13. Anthropic did publish absolute figures in July 2025 — Pro "40 to 80 hours of Sonnet 4", Max $100 "140 to 280 hours of Sonnet 4 and 15 to 35 hours of Opus 4", Max $200 "240 to 480 hours of Sonnet 4 and 24 to 40 hours of Opus 4", effective 2025-08-28, said to affect "less than 5% of subscribers" [S11, S12, both secondary]. Codex, by contrast, still publishes approximate per-window message ranges by plan, with the caveat that these "are not fixed message limits" [S10]. **Consequence for this round: every capacity comparison it can perform is a ratio between arms. No arm can be expressed as a fraction of the week. "This design consumes 40% of the allowance" is not a sentence anyone here can write.**

**W-7. FOLKLORE, with its propagation traced. The withdrawn 2025 figures are circulating as fresh independent measurement.** A July 2026 third-party page presents "~140–240 Sonnet hrs + 15–35 Opus hrs" for Max 5x and "240–480 Sonnet hrs + 24–40 Opus hrs" for Max 20x, attributing them to "independent testing" with "actual usage varies" [S13]. The Max 20x range is character-identical to what Anthropic published in July 2025 [S11]. **INFERENCE, medium confidence:** this is a fourteen-month-old vendor estimate relabelled as measurement, not an independent observation. It is exactly the upgrade-by-repetition that protocol §7 forbids, and it is the most likely way a stale number enters this round.

**W-8. The thesis's cost argument and its shared-truth argument are in tension with each other.** Handoff loss is the reason to split less. Context rot is the reason to load less. The first says put more in one context; the second says put less. **The thesis asserts both without naming the quantity that trades them off.** That quantity is: how much of the load-bearing context survives a transfer, versus how much accuracy is lost by carrying it all. CP-132 (`SummaryLoss`) and CP-128 (server-recorded manifests) are the instruments; nothing has been measured with them.

**INFERENCE, and it is the lane's central economic finding. Under subscription capacity, architecture does not change what the company spends. It changes the date on which work stops.** Money is fixed at the subscription step; the variable that architecture moves is the rate at which a non-refundable, non-rolling weekly allowance is consumed. This inverts the usual reading of TC-02 and TC-20: the question is not "which design costs more" but "which design reaches the wall sooner, and what does it do when it gets there". F-2's adverse variation is the only fixture that asks the second half, and neither end nor the middle currently answers it with a named holder.

**INFERENCE, unpriced by any lane so far. Unused allowance is destroyed, not saved.** The five-hour session window resets on schedule whether consumed or not [S1]. A conservative scheduler that holds capacity back does not bank it. Reserved-but-unconsumed capacity under `07` §6 is therefore a real loss, not a prudent hold — the opposite of how a reserve behaves under per-token pricing. **REGISTRABLE.** Proposed: *subscription allowance does not roll over across its reset window, so unconsumed reserved capacity is destroyed rather than saved, and reservation policy must be priced as waste rather than as thrift.* `valid_until` 2026-12-13; invalidator: a provider introducing rollover or banked allowance.

## 5. Per-claim verdicts for every R8 claim

**TC-12** — *"A set of persistent agents without shared state produces worse accepted outcomes, or more owner labour, than the same capabilities coordinated around one shared source of truth."*
**Verdict: NOT MEASURABLE AS WRITTEN, and the defect is methodological rather than a capacity limit.** The claim moves two variables at once — persistence and state location — so no result can attribute a difference to either. It needs a 2×2: persistent/ephemeral × shared-record/own-context. Its stated fixture F-6 (founder absent) is not concurrency-bound, so the comparison *can* run at CP1 once decomposed; this claim is **not** unmeasurable at CP1. The phrase "the same capabilities" is also not holdable constant: capabilities in the persistent arm include accumulated state, which is the treatment. **Evidence status:** the package records none as a comparison; L03 declines to license the converse. This lane found none either. **Remains a hypothesis.**

**TC-20** — *"Beyond some number, adding executors within one domain raises capacity per accepted outcome and lowers context quality, with no compensating gain."*
**Verdict: DIRECTION CORROBORATED BY A CONFLICTED PRIMARY SOURCE. TURNING POINT UNMEASURABLE AT CP1.** Direction: Anthropic states linear token scaling with team size, diminishing returns beyond a point, and recommends 3-5 teammates [S3, S4]; context quality degrading with input length is measured across 18 models [S15]. Turning point: the claim's own measure sweeps executor counts 1, 3, 7, 15, 30. At one native plus one external job, counts above 2 are serialised, so the sweep measures **serialisation, not coordination overhead**. The two have different shapes and would be confused. **Unmeasurable at CP1 for its stated measure.**

**TC-21** — *"One undifferentiated executor with all tools and all context cannot deliver the whole-company scope at acceptable safety and cost."*
**Verdict: THE SAFETY CLAUSE IS ENTAILED BY THE FROZEN BOUNDARIES AND MUST NOT BE SCORED AS AN EMPIRICAL RESULT. THE COST CLAUSE IS UNKNOWN.** F-3 requires attenuation on delegation and a record of which identity performed which effect; a single executor holding the union of three permissions cannot exhibit attenuation, because there is nothing to attenuate from. F-4 requires an acceptor with no stake, and CP-82 and CP-147 forbid the producer becoming its own acceptor by relabelling. **So the safety half is true by the definitions already frozen, at any demand level, and no experiment is needed or possible.** The cost half is genuinely open and is the half the claim's own measure addresses. Its stated fixture (F-3 with the single executor holding all three permissions) tests only the entailed half. **Recommend the round split this claim; do not let a definitional truth stand as evidence for the empirical one.**

**TC-22** — *"For this system's scope, some multi-executor arrangement beats the best single-executor arrangement on accepted outcomes at matched capacity."*
**Verdict: SPLITS. The independent-verification half is measurable at CP1 today. The parallelism half is unmeasurable at CP1.** F-4 needs two executors, sequentially, which CP1 permits; the known-defect control and the joint false-acceptance measurement can run now. The parallelism half needs concurrency the CapacityPlan does not admit. **Evidence is in genuine conflict and stays that way:** S5 reports a large win on a research eval and excludes coding; S16 reports minimal gains on popular benchmarks; S14 argues the split itself manufactures the failures. All three are 2025-vintage and model-generation dependent. **Retained as contested; forced consensus would be a finding against the round.**

**TC-26** — *"Internal execution economics are measured in subscription allowance, provider quota buckets, concurrency and owner attention, tracked separately; no silent fallback to a metered path is permitted."*
**Verdict: CONFORMING AS A UNIT SYSTEM, UNENFORCED AS A CONSTRAINT, AND MISSING ITS DENOMINATOR.** Three separate findings. (a) The unit system is correct and is what `07` §6 and AD-007 specify. (b) The no-fallback rule has no mechanism: both providers surface a purchase-credits path at the exact moment of exhaustion, and Claude Code prompts the user to raise or remove a spend limit in the CLI [S3, S10]. Whether that path is disabled is an **observable account setting, not a property of any architecture**, and nothing in the package observes it. (c) The Claude-side allowance is no longer published in absolute units [S1, S2, S8, S9 — direct observation], though it was in July 2025 [S11, S12], so capacity-per-accepted-outcome can only ever be reported as a ratio between arms in this round. **This claim is where the round's cost comparisons either become honest or become arithmetic about tokens.**

**TC-39** — *"The thesis's costs are asserted without a scale; it is unknown whether they bind at three executors, thirty, or only above some throughput."*
**Verdict: CONFIRMED UNKNOWN, UNMEASURABLE AT CP1, AND CIRCULAR AS SPECIFIED.** Its measure is the TC-20 sweep, which CP1 forbids. Raising CP1 is "a reviewed CapacityPlan revision after measured resource and provider allowance evidence" (`07` §6) — that is, **the measurement that would establish the threshold requires first changing the parameter whose effect is being measured.** The circularity is not fatal but must be stated: either the round accepts a staged CapacityPlan revision as an experiment with its own stopping rule, or it records the threshold as permanently unknown in this system. **Additional finding:** the threshold is not one number. Executor count, concurrency and total work admitted per period have different thresholds under a weekly bucket, and the claim's own measure already says so. The weekly bucket makes *admitted work per period* the binding one, and that one is measurable at CP1 today.

**Plainly, which thesis claims are unmeasurable at CP1 concurrency.** Wholly: **TC-20** and **TC-39**. In part: **TC-22** (parallelism half only). Not unmeasurable at CP1, contrary to what a quick reading suggests: **TC-12** (blocked by its own design, not by concurrency), **TC-21** (its safety half needs no measurement at all), **TC-26** (a conformance and observation question). Outside my lane but on the same mechanism, and named here because the position ledger's own closing note flags them: **TC-02**, **TC-03** and **TC-07** all have concurrency in their measures.

## 6. Assumptions · Unknowns · Competing interpretations

**Assumptions.** That the $400/month figure is a planning baseline and not a verified invoice, tier or entitlement (CP-166, L10). That CP1 remains one native plus one external job for the period in which this round's comparisons would run. That the account in use is an individual subscription, not Team or Enterprise, since the seat-allowance mechanics differ [S3]. That the six fixtures are the evaluation surface; I judged both ends against those and not against others.

**Unknowns.** The absolute weekly allowance on the Claude side (W-6). Whether persistent model identity improves any accepted outcome (A-7). Whether team familiarity transfers from human teams to model executors at all (S24's transfer is unvalidated). Provider concurrency entitlement: **no inspected page establishes any account-level concurrent-worker limit**, so CP1 is a self-imposed policy, not a provider constraint, and nothing found here says whether a higher CP1 is permitted or merely untested. Devin-class per-unit economics, because every pricing page refused the fetch. The NTSB crew-familiarity statistic that would have strengthened A-3: sought, document located, fetch returned unparseable binary, **not found and not asserted**.

**Competing interpretations, preserved.**
1. *Share everything* [S14] versus *narrow every delivery* [S15, CP-84, CP-135]. Both are supported. They cannot both be right about the same context, and the discriminating quantity is how much load-bearing content survives narrowing, which CP-132 could measure and nobody has.
2. *Multi-executor wins* [S5] versus *gains are minimal* [S16]. The likeliest reconciliation is task shape: research-like work parallelises, construction-like work does not, and S5 says so itself. That reconciliation is an inference, not a measurement, and I decline to call it settled.
3. *AI slows experienced developers* [S17] versus *that is probably no longer true* [S18]. Same authors, eighteen months apart, with the later one honest that its own evidence is weak. **Neither figure is usable alone.**
4. *Charters are legibility* (A-6) versus *charters are the narrowing W11 forbids*. The protocol takes a side; the evidence does not.

## 7. Mechanisms each end contributes that the middle position lacks · Mechanisms that do not survive scrutiny

**From A, three mechanisms the middle lacks.**
1. **A shed unit with a holder.** F-2's adverse variation requires shedding a lane by someone's authority. `07` §6 reserves by class; a class cannot be asked to yield. A charter can be suspended, and DIRECTIVE §8.8 already requires suspension to be defined if persistent agents are justified. The middle needs this mechanism whether or not it adopts charters.
2. **A party that outlives the work.** CH-03's grievance route and CH-06's continuity arrangement both need someone reachable after the case closed. An assignment rule produces a custodian when invoked; it does not maintain a relationship. CP-06 forbids an unowned queue but does not supply continuity.
3. **A subject for longitudinal evaluation.** CP-144 evaluates agent instances and templates. Drift of a *performer* is not measurable without a performer that persists. The middle can measure template drift, which is a different and narrower thing, and should say so.

**From B, three mechanisms the middle lacks.**
1. **Durable execution as the substrate rather than as a rule.** Replay from history, activities recorded once, waits that consume no worker [S22]. CP-68 is the same idea implemented once in one component. Taking the substrate rather than the rule supplies W7, W9 and F-5 by construction rather than by discipline.
2. **One record a person can read end to end.** This serves W12 better than any projection, as protocol §6 already grants, and the middle's permission-filtered projections (CP-84) structurally cannot supply it to the founder without a separate unfiltered surface that nothing currently specifies.
3. **Maximising the zero-capacity share.** Every deterministic step costs no allowance (CP-101, CP-69). B treats that as the default. **This is the only lever that reduces subscription consumption without reducing work, and it is free to all three positions.**

**Mechanisms that do not survive scrutiny.**
- **"Persistent identity improves quality."** No supporting evidence found; the two most-cited deployments fail to support it and one shows persistence as the failure mode [S19, S20, S21].
- **"More executors give independent verification."** Refused by CP-32, CP-153, CP-171 and by S16. A second executor of the same family is a second sample.
- **"One shared store prevents contradiction."** Refused by CP-22, CP-84, CP-135 and measured against by S15.
- **"A single long session is the cheapest in scarce capacity."** Refused by the provider's own mechanism list [S3, B-6].
- **"Capability differentiation is not a roster."** Not yet a mechanism. It becomes one only when someone states whether the partition is recomputed or persisted (W-4).
- **"Thirty agents cost thirty times as much."** Not at CP1. At CP1 they cost thirty times the *elapsed time* and thirty handoffs (W-1).

## 8. Failure cases for each end

**A fails when:** charters idle under CP1 and reserved capacity is destroyed at the reset window rather than saved, so the roster's main artifact is waste; an accumulated good record silently moves an action into a lower supervision class, which CP-83 and TC-25 forbid outright and which a persistent evaluated identity makes tempting; retirement removes a name while live obligations remain, which CP-78 anticipates and a charter makes more likely because more attaches to the name; the charter set becomes the design, per Conway [S23]; the founder's review load scales with the roster rather than with the work, failing W12; and a charter defends its mandate against admission narrowing, which CP-13 requires and no charter is obliged to accept.

**B fails when:** F-3 is run at all, because attenuation has nothing to attenuate from; F-4 is run at all, because the producer cannot be the independent acceptor; demand reaches 2× and elapsed time is the only lever; context grows and accuracy degrades measurably [S15]; the founder becomes the continuous translator predicted by CH-04; and — the subtle one — the durable-workflow half grows enough to need admission, typing, staffing and acceptance rules, at which point **B has rebuilt C02 and is no longer B**. Any honest B0 comparison has to say at what point that happened.

**Both ends fail the same way at the wall.** Neither has a rule for what a partially completed external effect means when the weekly allowance ends mid-attempt. F-5's adverse variation — a pre-reset summary that omits a decision, a source fact changed in the gap, one external effect already released — is the fixture that discriminates, and it discriminates on record design, not on agent count.

## 10. Questions other lanes may have missed

1. **Is the set of instantiated executor profiles stable over time?** Run the system for a month and count distinct profiles. If they converge to a small stable set, the middle position has a roster it discovered rather than declared, and negative control 8 should catch it. **Nothing currently measures this, and it is the only honest test of W-4.**
2. **Who owns a reservation?** `07` §6 reserves by class. F-2 requires shedding a lane by someone's authority. A class has no representative. Which component yields when two protected classes both need the same slot?
3. **What is the denominator?** The weekly allowance has no published figure [W-6]. Can this round state its capacity findings as ratios only, and does any downstream decision depend on a fraction nobody can compute?
4. **What does idle reserved capacity cost?** Allowance does not roll over. A conservative scheduler destroys what it withholds. Has any lane priced a reservation as waste rather than as thrift?
5. **Is the metered path disabled on the actual accounts, today?** TC-26 is a founder constraint that currently rests on nobody having pressed a button the provider surfaces at the moment of exhaustion [S3, S10]. Who observes the setting, and how often?
6. **If a provider doubles or halves the allowance overnight, does any decision in this round change?** Anthropic doubled the five-hour limits on 2026-05-06 [S7] and withdrew its published weekly figures at some point after July 2025 [S11 versus S1]. If no decision moves, the capacity argument was never load-bearing and should stop being cited as one.
7. **Is CP1 a provider constraint or a policy?** No inspected page establishes an account-level concurrency entitlement. If CP1 is policy, then every "unmeasurable at CP1" verdict in §5 is a self-inflicted limit with a named owner, and that owner can authorise a bounded capacity trial with a stopping rule. If it is a provider constraint, several thesis claims are permanently unmeasurable here and the round should say so in those words.
8. **When does B0 stop being B0?** Give the simple baseline its deterministic scripts, its retrieval, its checklists and its human review, as §6 requires, and then ask at what added mechanism it has rebuilt the case admission authority. Naming that point is the fairest comparison this round can make, and no artifact currently names it.
