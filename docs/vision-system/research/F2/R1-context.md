## 2. Findings

### Loss under summarization, compaction and handoff

**F-01. FACT (vendor-documented behavior).** Compaction in a shipping harness has deterministic, documented loss rules, and the model is not told what was lost. Claude Code's published table states that after compaction, files Claude read or edited are re-read "up to five, most recently modified first"; invoked skill bodies are "Re-injected, capped at 5,000 tokens per skill and 25,000 tokens total; oldest dropped first"; and the skill index "is not re-injected after `/compact`. Only skills you actually invoked get preserved" (R1-S03). The same vendor states of automatic compaction: "It clears older tool outputs first, then summarizes the conversation if needed. Your requests and key code snippets are preserved; detailed instructions from early in the conversation may be lost" (R1-S04). Anthropic's engineering account adds that "overly aggressive compaction can result in the loss of subtle but critical context whose importance only becomes apparent later" (R1-S01).

**F-02. FACT.** The loss is quantified in one place and only as a count. The Anthropic context-editing API returns `context_management.applied_edits` carrying `cleared_tool_uses` and `cleared_input_tokens`, and "The API replaces each cleared result with placeholder text indicating to Claude that it was removed" (R1-S06). So the system reports how much was removed and that something was removed. It does not report which facts left, and the edit happens server-side, after any loader has assembled and recorded its inputs.

**F-03. FACT.** Shared-state indexes truncate silently at the read boundary. Claude Code loads "The first 200 lines of `MEMORY.md`, or the first 25KB, whichever comes first" and warns that an over-limit write still succeeds "because everything past the limit is dropped on the next load" (R1-S02). The remedy the vendor implements is a size check at write time, not at read time. This is a transferable pattern: an index that is trimmed on read is an index whose omissions nobody observes.

**F-04. SOURCE CLAIM, high confidence for the reported result, model-generation dependent.** Replacing full context with a bounded self-managed memory costs measured accuracy on exactly the task that a handoff must survive, which is a fact that changed. On the LongMemEval knowledge-update oracle split, accuracy fell from 82 percent to 63 percent for one mid-size model and from 92 percent to 77 percent for a frontier model, p equals 0.0033, and the authors conclude "The gap is significant, and a bigger model does not close it" (R1-S12). In the same work, extending the conversation twenty-four times dropped accuracy from 68 percent to 28 percent, and proportionally expanding the memory budget produced "no detectable recovery (28%→28%)", from which the authors conclude "The failure tracks conversation length, not the compression ratio" (R1-S12).

**F-05. SOURCE CLAIM, high confidence, historical generation.** Omission in long-form summarization is systematic and positional, and automatic raters do not catch it. FABLES collected 3,158 human claim annotations over summaries of 26 books and reports that "none correlates strongly with human annotations, especially with regard to detecting unfaithful claims" for the LLM raters they built, and that they "develop a typology of omission errors related to crucial narrative elements and also identify a systematic over-emphasis on events occurring towards the end of the book" (R1-S21).

**INFERENCE from F-01 to F-05.** Compaction loss in this system will be of a specific, predictable shape: early material, middle material, and material that the summarizer cannot see is load-bearing. That is the shape of an exclusion, a rejected alternative, and a promised remedy. The package's `SummaryLoss` categories in chapter 06 section 2 name exactly those, which is the right list. Nothing in the evidence suggests a model can produce that list about its own summary reliably, and F-05 is direct evidence against using a model to check it.

### Long-context degradation as the competing explanation

**F-06. SOURCE CLAIM, high confidence for the reported experiments, vendor-conflicted, 2025 generation.** Chroma's context-rot study tested 18 models across needle variants, a 306-prompt LongMemEval condition comparing roughly 113,000-token full input against roughly 300-token focused input, and a repeated-words task, concluding "Models do not use their context uniformly; instead, their performance grows increasingly unreliable as input length grows" (R1-S05). The publisher sells a retrieval database, so the finding favors its product.

**F-07. SOURCE CLAIM, high confidence, current generation.** LOCA-bench, February 2026, from an academic group with no memory product, evaluates agents rather than single-shot retrieval under controlled context growth on Claude Opus 4.5, Claude Sonnet 4.5, GPT-5.2, Gemini 3 Flash and Gemini 3 Pro, and concludes: "While agent performance generally degrades as the environment states grow more complex, advanced context management techniques can substantially improve the overall success rate" (R1-S27).

**INFERENCE.** F-06 and F-07 together mean the single-long-context comparator B0 does not win by default and does not lose by default. It degrades with length on current models, and the degradation is partly recoverable by context management, which is a scaffold property rather than a model property. This is the empirical basis for the package's CP-133, "Longer accepted context is not proof of utilization," and it now has current-generation support rather than only the 2023 lost-in-the-middle result.

### Agent count against context quality

**F-08. SOURCE CLAIM, high confidence, current generation, non-vendor.** Tran and Kiela, Stanford, April 2026, held the reasoning-token budget constant across budgets of 100, 500, 1,000, 2,000, 5,000 and 10,000 tokens on FRAMES and 4-hop MuSiQue, across Qwen3-30B, DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-70B and Gemini 2.5 Flash and Pro, comparing sequential, subtask-parallel, parallel-roles, debate and ensemble multi-agent arrangements against a single agent. Single-agent matched or beat every multi-agent variant at matched budget, for example 0.700 against 0.680 for the best multi-agent arrangement on one cell. Multi-agent became competitive only under heavy deliberate degradation of the single agent's context, at substitution level alpha equals 0.7. The paper also "identifies artifacts in API-based budget control and benchmark vulnerabilities that inflate apparent multi-agent gains" (R1-S22).

**F-09. SOURCE CLAIM, vendor-conflicted, 2025 generation.** Anthropic's multi-agent research system reports a 90.2 percent improvement over single-agent Opus 4 on an internal research evaluation, alongside roughly 15 times the token use of chat and roughly 4 times for single agents, and reports that three factors explained 95 percent of performance variance on BrowseComp with "token usage alone" accounting for 80 percent (R1-S24). The same post states that "some domains that require all agents to share the same context or involve many dependencies between agents are not a good fit."

**INFERENCE, medium confidence.** F-09's headline gain is measured at roughly fifteen times the resource, which is exactly the confound F-08 identifies and controls for. Read together, the two are not in conflict: the multi-agent gain in F-09 is consistent with being a resource effect that F-08 removes by matching budgets. Anthropic's own variance decomposition, 80 percent from token usage, points the same way. This is a disagreement about attribution, not about observation, and I retain it rather than resolving it, because neither study varied shared-state quality, which is the axis TC-05 actually names.

### Contamination, poisoning and correction

**F-10. SOURCE CLAIM, high confidence for the reported experiments, single-author preprint with a commercial interest in the problem.** A poisoning attack that injects only false statements in ordinary conversational form, with "no instruction, no override, no role manipulation", passed a four-stage write-path content screen entirely: it "refused 0 of 360 poisoned memories", while the same screen achieved 0.832 recall on indirect prompt injection and flagged only 1.5 percent of benign trigger-word-laden text. At 1.2 percent of the corpus the attack "removes two-thirds of an agent memory's value on LongMemEval (accuracy 0.850 to 0.300)". The read-path provenance-ranking defense at its shipped weight was "statistically indistinguishable from no defense (p=0.80)"; at a corrected weight it became a hard exclusion filter, and with answer-bearing evidence marked untrusted accuracy fell "from 0.8583 to 0.0417" with "evidence recall [fell] to exactly 0.00%" (R1-S15).

**F-11. SOURCE CLAIM, high confidence, historical generation.** ClashEval, over 1,200 questions across six domains and six models including GPT-4o, found that "LLMs are susceptible to adopting incorrect retrieved content, overriding their own correct prior knowledge over 60% of the time" (R1-S20).

**F-12. SOURCE CLAIM, medium confidence.** Under realistic conditions in which legitimate memories already exist, the MINJA-class injection success rates of above 95 percent injection and about 70 percent attack success "dramatically reduced", and the authors conclude that "Effective memory sanitization requires careful trust threshold calibration to prevent both overly conservative rejection (blocking all entries) and insufficient filtering (missing subtle attacks)" (R1-S16).

**INFERENCE, high confidence.** F-10, F-11, F-12 and L05's Memora result converge on one conclusion that bears directly on the package's current position: the two mechanisms chapter 06 leans on against a false entry, a `SourceRecord` with trust classification and a ranking that weights source applicability, are measured as ineffective against a false statement that carries no attack signature, and as utility-destroying at the weight that makes them effective. The false entry that matters is not an injected instruction. It is a plausible wrong fact, and screening built to catch instructions does not see it.

**F-13. SOURCE CLAIM, high confidence for the reported result.** A correction that is stored is not a correction that takes effect. This is F-04's knowledge-update gap seen from the other side, and it corroborates L05's Memora finding of recurring invalid-memory reuse across four models and six memory agents. The package's CP-142, which requires an understanding check by a new bounded case or a direct critical-field check because "storing the correction alone is insufficient", is the right mechanism and is, as far as I can find, unimplemented anywhere in the measured literature.

### Disclosure under permission filtering, which is the narrow thesis

**F-14. SOURCE CLAIM, high confidence, current generation, Microsoft Research.** CI-Work simulates enterprise workflows across five information-flow directions and asks whether agents "convey essential content while withholding sensitive context in dense retrieval settings". Its abstract states: "Our evaluation of frontier models reveals that privacy failures are prevalent (violation rates range from 15.8%-50.9%, with leakage reaching up to 26.7%) and uncovers a counterintuitive trade-off critical for industrial deployment: higher task utility often correlates with increased privacy violations... Simply increasing model size or reasoning depth fails to address the problem. We conclude that safeguarding enterprise workflows requires a paradigm shift, moving beyond model-centric scaling toward context-centric architectures" (R1-S17).

**F-15. SOURCE CLAIM, high confidence for the abstract, medium for the specific percentages.** PiSAs is the only source I found that directly compares agent topologies and memory regimes on cross-user spillage. Its abstract states: "We find that, although system design improves CI compliance, results are bottlenecked by incorrect LLM judgment calls: even state-of-the-art models fail to reliably filter inappropriate content or restrict transmission to authorized users" (R1-S18). The figures reaching me through the summarizing layer, and therefore medium confidence: single-agent appropriateness violations above 77 percent on every backbone tested, including a current closed frontier model; multi-agent topologies reduced violations by 20 to 50 percentage points while remaining above 75 percent; with hybrid memory, visibility violations rose from a 36 to 47 percent band to a 63 to 90 percent band; prompt-level defenses cut violations by up to 50 percent in communication channels but only 28 to 29 percent in memory.

**F-16. SOURCE CLAIM, high confidence, historical generation.** ConfAIde found GPT-4 and ChatGPT flow information to inappropriate actors 39 percent and 57 percent of the time respectively, and reveal secrets 22 percent and 93 percent of the time in its third tier, "even though they are directly instructed to preserve privacy", and that leakage persists under privacy-inducing prompts and chain-of-thought (R1-S19).

**INFERENCE, high confidence, and this is R1's sharpest result.** The narrow version of the thesis, in which workers see permission-filtered peer projections rather than all company context, is measured, and it does not do what it is being asked to do. Filtering at the loader is necessary and is the direction all three sources point. Filtering by the model, after the material is in the window, fails at rates between roughly 16 and 51 percent on current frontier models. And the specific mechanism the narrow thesis introduces, a shared memory of peers' work, is the component where defenses work worst: 28 to 29 percent reduction in memory against up to 50 percent in channels (F-15).

### Contradictory and duplicated action

**F-17. SOURCE CLAIM, vendor-conflicted, current generation, and the most surprising finding in this lane.** Anthropic's August 2026 multiagent research reports that in one experiment "18 out of 30 agents decided to create a git branch with the exact same branch name"; that in a job-queue experiment "there were 2.4 million job requests and only 117 jobs accepted"; that "transmitting context is about as costly as acting on it, and an agent can be forked or repurposed at will"; and that agents lack the social technologies humans use, having "reputation to lose, no court to appeal to, and no colleague who remembers them" (R1-S23). The same source reports that on a parallelizable task, "the simple independent parallelized method produces 21 vulnerabilities over a 6.5 million token run, while the coordinating agent swarm found 266 vulnerabilities over a 27 million token run", with "only 12 vulnerabilities in common between them", and reports that in hidden-profile tasks agents fail to surface private information contradicting group consensus.

**INFERENCE, high confidence.** TC-13 assumes the cause of contradictory and duplicated action is divergent context. F-17 measures a second, larger cause: correlated behavior. Thirty agents reading nothing about each other collided on one branch name because they are the same model reasoning identically, not because their contexts disagreed. A single authoritative view of next actions does not fix this, and the hidden-profile result suggests it makes the related failure worse by giving every executor the same consensus to conform to. This is a genuine counter-mechanism to the founder's thesis, and I found no measurement of it under a shared-state arrangement.

**F-18. SOURCE CLAIM, 2025 generation.** MAST annotated 1,600 or more traces across 7 multi-agent frameworks into 14 failure modes in 3 categories, one of which is inter-agent misalignment, with inter-annotator kappa of 0.88 on 150 traces (R1-S26). This establishes that context-divergence failures are real and frequent; it does not quantify them against the correlated-behavior failures of F-17, because its traces are drawn entirely from multi-agent systems with no single-context arm.

**F-19. SOURCE CLAIM, practitioner, vendor blog, not a measurement.** Cognition's position is two principles: "Share context, and share full agent traces, not just individual messages" and "Actions carry implicit decisions, and conflicting decisions carry bad results" (R1-S25). Under protocol section 7 this is folklore with high propagation and must not be upgraded by its wide repetition. It agrees with the founder's thesis and is the single most-cited source for it in practitioner writing.

### Shared state as an artifact, and whether anything binds an attempt to it

**F-20. FACT, vendor-documented, and it is the protocol's negative control number 2 answered directly.** Of its own canonical instruction file, the vendor states: "Claude treats them as context, not enforced configuration. To block an action regardless of what Claude decides, use a PreToolUse hook instead"; "CLAUDE.md content is delivered as a user message after the system prompt, not as part of the system prompt itself. Claude reads it and tries to follow it, but there's no guarantee of strict compliance, especially for vague or conflicting instructions"; and "If two rules contradict each other, Claude may pick one arbitrarily" (R1-S02). The AGENTS.md format, used by more than sixty thousand projects and stewarded under the Linux Foundation, states its precedence rule as "The closest AGENTS.md to the edited file wins; explicit user chat prompts override everything" and offers no compliance guarantee (R1-S09). Gemini CLI concatenates a hierarchy of files, shows a count in the footer and offers `/memory show` to display "the full, concatenated content of the current hierarchical memory", with no statement that the model will follow it and no record of what was loaded into any particular turn (R1-S10).

**INFERENCE, high confidence.** Every shipping implementation of "the file everyone is supposed to trust" is exactly the artifact the acceptance protocol's negative control number 2 says must fail: a canonical document with no mechanism binding any attempt to it. Three independent vendors converge on the same design and two of them say in their own documentation that it is not enforcement. A candidate that proposes shared state as instruction text has proposed the thing that has already been built three times and is documented as non-binding.

**F-21. FACT.** No standard in the path carries what section 8.12 requires. MCP resources define `uri`, `name`, `title`, `description`, `mimeType`, `size`, plus annotations `audience`, `priority` and `lastModified` (R1-S08). Of section 8.12's nine manifest fields, `lastModified` touches age and nothing touches version, trust level, omission, summarization or possible loss. The OpenTelemetry GenAI attribute registry defines a `gen_ai.data_source.id` and no attribute recording omitted, truncated, summarized, compacted or dropped content; its only relevant notes are that instrumentations "MAY provide a way for users to filter or truncate input messages", which is a capability rather than a record, and the conventions are at development stability and marked as moved (R1-S07).

**F-22. FACT, external obligation.** EU AI Act Article 12(1) states: "High-risk AI systems shall technically allow for the automatic recording of events (logs) over the lifetime of the system," with traceability appropriate to the intended purpose (R1-S30). Whether this system's uses fall in scope is undetermined and outside R1. It is recorded because it makes the manifest a possible legal obligation rather than only a design preference, and because it invalidates any future argument that the manifest can be dropped on cost grounds without checking scope first.

### One source of truth, and what the phrase can mean

**F-23. SOURCE CLAIM, high confidence, dated, non-vendor.** One reading of the phrase has a production existence proof for exactly one class of fact. Google Zanzibar is a single global authority over authorization facts consumed by hundreds of client services, operating over trillions of access control lists at millions of requests per second with 95th-percentile latency under 10 milliseconds and availability above 99.999 percent over three years, with a purpose-built consistency mechanism for causal ordering (R1-S28).

**INFERENCE.** Zanzibar establishes that "one authority over many native stores" is implementable, and simultaneously shows its price: a dedicated global service, a bespoke consistency token, and years of operation, all for one narrow fact class. It is evidence for reading three of TC-31 at the granularity of a fact class, and evidence against reading three at the granularity of a company.

**UNKNOWN.** I did not find primary sources establishing an agreed definition of "single source of truth" in enterprise architecture. What I saw through search was mutually inconsistent definitions across secondary and vendor sources, one treating it as each data element being mastered in exactly one place and another treating it as a single analytical view across departments, which are different designs. I did not fetch those pages and I do not cite them as evidence. The honest finding is that the ambiguity TC-31 identifies in the founder's sentence also exists in the industry vocabulary he borrowed it from, and that I have not evidenced it to this round's standard.

### Vendor dispute retained, not resolved

**F-24. DISAGREEMENT, preserved per directive section 6.** Mem0 reports against a full-context baseline on LoCoMo: 26 percent relative improvement on an LLM-as-judge metric, 91 percent lower 95th-percentile latency, roughly 90 percent token savings (R1-S13). Zep, a direct competitor, responds that LoCoMo conversations of 16,000 to 26,000 tokens sit inside modern context windows, that the benchmark "lacks questions designed to test knowledge updates", that one question category was "unusable due to missing ground truth answers", that the Zep implementation used in the comparison was misconfigured, and, most relevant here, that "a simple full-context baseline...achieved a J score of ~73%, compared to Mem0's best score of ~68%" (R1-S14). Both parties sell memory systems and each has an interest opposite the other's.

**INFERENCE, medium confidence.** The one claim in this dispute that neither party has an interest in inflating is the one that matters to this round: on the benchmark used to sell memory architectures, the unarchitected full-context baseline scored higher than the architected system. That is a datum for comparator B0 and it comes from B0's opponent.

## 4. Per-claim verdicts

**TC-04, facts lost when work is split, at a higher rate than a single context fails to use them.** **Partially supported, model-generation-dependent.** Supported in direction by F-04: at fixed task length, a bounded self-managed memory loses 19 and 15 accuracy points against full context on the knowledge-update task, and a larger model does not close the gap. The control half of the claim is also measured and is not small: F-06, F-07, F-11 and F-05 all measure facts present and not used, or present and misused, and F-11's rate of above 60 percent is higher than any handoff loss rate I found. F-04's scale study is the decisive nuance: the failure tracks conversation length, and expanding the memory budget recovers nothing. **No source measured both rates on one fixture with the decision probe TC-04 specifies.** The ordering can reverse: F-07 finds that on current models, in agentic settings with growing context, context management substantially improves success, which is the split arm winning.

**TC-05, agent count matters less than context quality.** **Supported in direction on the axis that was measured, unmeasured on the axis TC-05 names.** F-08 is the closest executed experiment: at matched reasoning budget, the agent-count main effect is at or below zero across three model families and five multi-agent arrangements, while deliberately degrading the single agent's context both moves the outcome and flips the ranking. That is the interaction TC-05 predicts. Two caveats block a stronger verdict. First, the context axis measured is length and corruption, not shared-state strength, so the 2x2 TC-05 specifies has not been run. Second, F-09 reports the opposite ordering at fifteen times the resource, and I retain that disagreement rather than resolving it, while noting that F-09's own variance decomposition attributes 80 percent of variance to token usage.

**TC-09, executor isolation prevents disclosure that a permission-filtered single context does not.** **Partially supported and refuted as stated.** Supported: F-15 finds multi-agent topologies reduce appropriateness violations by 20 to 50 percentage points against single-agent. Refuted as a prevention claim: the residual stays above 75 percent, F-15's abstract attributes the bottleneck to "incorrect LLM judgment calls" rather than topology, and F-14 finds frontier-model violation rates of 15.8 to 50.9 percent with the explicit conclusion that model scaling does not fix it. **The most consequential sub-finding is adverse to the narrow thesis:** F-15's hybrid-memory condition raised visibility violations from a 36 to 47 percent band to a 63 to 90 percent band, and prompt-level defenses were weakest in memory, 28 to 29 percent, against up to 50 percent in channels. Shared peer projections are the component where this fails worst.

**TC-13, one authoritative view produces fewer contradictory and duplicated actions.** **Partially supported, with a measured counter-mechanism the claim does not anticipate.** Supported for divergence-caused contradiction by F-18 and, as folklore, F-19. Counter-evidenced by F-17: eighteen of thirty agents duplicated one branch name through correlated reasoning rather than divergent context; a job queue received 2.4 million requests and accepted 117; and in hidden-profile tasks agents failed to surface private information contradicting group consensus, which is the failure a single shared view would amplify. **No source measured contradictory actions and duplicated external effects under both arrangements**, which is what TC-13's measure requires. The claim as written attributes one cause to a phenomenon that has at least two, and the unnamed cause is the one with current-generation measurement behind it.

**TC-14, the five constituents are together sufficient.** **Refuted as stated, under the natural reading, by coverage argument with measured consequence.** At least one constituent outside the five is load-bearing and its absence is the dominant measured failure in this lane: authorization and recipient scope, that is, who may see and receive a given fact (F-14, F-15, F-16). It is not a goal, a decision, a canonical file, verified knowledge, or a next action. Three further candidates are supported by evidence rather than by preference: temporal validity distinguishing when a fact entered the record from when it applies (L05's XTDB model, and F-04's supersession failure); explicit contradiction state rather than a resolved winner (chapter 06's own `ContradictionSet`, corroborated by F-11's 60 percent override rate); and surviving obligations that outlive the case carrying them, which the package already treats separately. The package's CP-124 already carries six memory jobs where the thesis names five constituents, so the package is ahead of the thesis here and should not be revised toward it.

**TC-27, the context manifest.** **Supported as a requirement, unprecedented in practice, and partially unachievable through today's provider surface.** Supported as an obligation: F-22 makes automatic event logging a legal requirement for high-risk systems, so the manifest may be more than a design preference. Unprecedented: F-21 shows neither MCP nor the OpenTelemetry GenAI conventions carry version, trust, omission, summarization or loss; F-02 shows the one production mechanism that reports loss reports a count and a placeholder, not an identity; F-01 and F-03 show documented deterministic loss with no record at all. **The important structural finding is this: chapter 06's rule that "the server discovers and records actual delivered inputs" is satisfiable for the loader's own server and is not satisfiable across the provider boundary**, because provider-side context editing occurs after the loader has handed off and reports only aggregate counts. A manifest that claims to record actual delivered inputs, while the provider silently edits them afterwards, is a manifest that can be wrong in the direction that matters. The remedy is available today and is narrow: read `context_management.applied_edits` from each response and reconcile it against the manifest, treating an unaccounted edit as a defect rather than as housekeeping.

**TC-29, the designed return of work to the founder.** **Supported as a requirement, dose unknown, and the specified measurement has been run by nobody.** F-25, that is R1-S29: a survey of 319 knowledge workers reporting 936 real use cases found higher confidence in the tool associated with less critical thinking and higher self-confidence associated with more. This is self-report, not a performance test, and the package is already correct to say that attendance, confidence and agreement are not competence. **The delayed unfamiliar-transfer test that TC-29's measure specifies is, as far as I can find, unexecuted in the literature.** It is the right instrument and it is untried, which makes it a (c)-class empirical hypothesis needing a protocol, a baseline, a unit, a stopping rule and an owner, not a (d)-class blocker.

**TC-31, what one shared source of truth means.** **Unresolved, as the claim itself states, with one reading now carrying an existence proof and a price tag.** F-23 establishes that reading three, one authority over many native stores, is implementable at extreme scale for a single fact class, at the cost of a dedicated global service and a bespoke consistency mechanism. That is evidence for the reading at fact-class granularity and against it at company granularity. **I could not evidence the definitional disagreement in the industry vocabulary to this round's standard and I record that as a gap rather than asserting it.** The package's existing answer in chapter 02 section 5, that native systems remain authoritative in their domains and shared meaning covers only cross-boundary decisions, is closer to reading four and is not contradicted by anything I found.

**TC-32, the thesis makes context engineering central and supplies no measure.** **Supported, trivially on the text, and usefully on the remedy.** Four candidate metrics survive the sham-change requirement that TC-32 imposes, and one popular family fails it. Surviving: facts-present rate by recall probe; facts-used rate by decision probe, which is the split LongMemEval established when it showed retrieval success does not guarantee correct use; load-bearing-fact loss per transformation by deterministic exact-field comparison; and stale-fact use rate, which F-04 shows is separately measurable and separately fails. Failing the instrument test: any metric whose judge is a model, because F-05 found that no LLM rater they built correlated strongly with human faithfulness annotations, "especially with regard to detecting unfaithful claims". RAGAS-style context precision, recall and utilization are well defined (R1-S31) but each requires either a reference answer or the generated response, which makes them retrieval metrics rather than manifest-completeness metrics; they do not measure omission and must not be presented as if they do.

**TC-42, how a false entry is detected, corrected, and stopped from propagating.** **Supported, and it is the finding most adverse to the current position.** F-10 measures the two defenses chapter 06 relies on and finds both wanting against the attack that matters. Write-path content screening refused zero of 360 poisoned memories because the poison carried "no instruction, no override, no role manipulation", while the same screen detected indirect prompt injection at 0.832 recall. Read-path provenance ranking at its shipped weight was statistically indistinguishable from no defense at p equals 0.80, and at the weight that made it effective it destroyed utility, taking accuracy from 0.8583 to 0.0417 and evidence recall to exactly zero. Blast radius is measured: 1.2 percent of the corpus took accuracy from 0.850 to 0.300. Propagation after correction is measured separately by F-04 and by L05's Memora. F-11's above-60-percent override rate measures how hard a wrong entry hits once it is trusted. **The package's CP-142 understanding check is the right mechanism and is the only one in this list that the evidence does not refute, and it is untested anywhere.**

## 5. Assumptions, unknowns, competing interpretations

**Assumptions I made and did not verify.** That benchmark results on conversational memory transfer to company operating records; the corpora are synthetic or conversational and the company's are neither. That violation rates measured on simulated enterprise workflows transfer to a one-founder company with four ventures; the scale and the counterparty structure differ. That the summarizing fetch layer reproduced quotations faithfully.

**Unknowns, held open.** How much is actually lost in one compaction event in this harness, in load-bearing facts rather than tokens, is unmeasured by anyone including the vendor. Whether the correlated-duplication failure of F-17 gets better or worse under a shared authoritative view is unmeasured, and it is the single most valuable experiment named in this report. Whether any dose of returned work preserves founder competence is unknown, and X07 already records that. Whether the industry's "single source of truth" vocabulary has an agreed meaning is unresolved by me, and I did not fetch the sources that would settle it. What a permission-filtered loader costs in false exclusion, that is, correct work refused because material was filtered out, is unmeasured; every source I found measures leakage and none measures the matched benign case, which is the pairing the acceptance protocol's section 4 demands.

**Competing interpretations, retained.** On agent count: F-08 attributes multi-agent gains to unmatched budgets; F-09 attributes them to the architecture. Both were run on different tasks by parties with opposite interests, and neither varied shared state. On memory architecture: F-24's two parties disagree about a benchmark whose full-context baseline may have beaten both of them. On isolation: F-15 shows topology helps and the abstract says the bottleneck is model judgment, which can be read either as "build more topology" or as "topology is not the lever". I prefer neither reading; the discriminating measurement is the one PiSAs itself did not run, which is a loader-filtered single context against a topology-separated one at matched utility.

## 6. Useful mechanisms, and rejected mechanisms

**Useful, each with the named problem it solves.**

1. **Filtering before the model sees the material, never after.** Solves the 15.8 to 50.9 percent model-judged withholding failure (F-14, F-15, F-16). This is what CI-Work means by moving "toward context-centric architectures", and it is what chapter 06's CP-135 retrieval order already specifies.
2. **Reconciling the provider's reported edits against the manifest.** Solves undetectable provider-side loss (F-02 against F-01). Available today; costs one field read per response.
3. **A write-time size oracle on any shared index.** Solves silent read-time truncation (F-03). The pattern is proven in a shipping product.
4. **Supersession as an operation rather than an append, with a critical-field probe after it.** Solves the memory-update gap (F-04, F-13). This is CP-142 and it is the one specified mechanism the evidence does not undermine.
5. **The decision probe as the second metric beside recall.** Solves retrieval success being mistaken for correct use (F-05, F-11, and LongMemEval's original finding).
6. **Deterministic exact-field comparison for critical preservation, with the model used only for the semantic challenge.** Solves the instrument failure in F-05.
7. **Per-fact-class authority rather than one company-wide store.** Solves TC-31's ambiguity at a known price (F-23).
8. **Deliberate decorrelation where two executors may produce one external effect: idempotency keys, reserved name ranges, an allocator.** Solves the eighteen-of-thirty collision (F-17). This mechanism is in tension with the shared-truth thesis and must be designed alongside it, not after it.
9. **A clean-case control on every filtering and trust mechanism, run at the same time as the adverse case.** Solves the utility collapse in F-10, where the defense that worked took evidence recall to zero.

**Rejected, with why.**

1. **Provenance or trust-weighted ranking as the primary defense against a false entry.** Measured indistinguishable from no defense at its shipped weight and utility-destroying at the effective weight (F-10).
2. **Write-path content screening as the defense against false facts.** It detects instructions, not falsehoods: zero of 360 refused (F-10).
3. **Prompt-level or instruction-level privacy control as the disclosure boundary.** Fails at 39 and 57 percent historically and at 15.8 to 50.9 percent currently, and is weakest exactly in memory (F-14, F-15, F-16).
4. **A larger model or deeper reasoning as the fix for leakage.** The source states explicitly that it fails (F-14).
5. **A model judge as the instrument for summarization loss.** No rater correlated strongly with human annotation, worst on the errors that matter (F-05).
6. **A canonical document with no mechanism binding an attempt to it.** Three vendors have built it; two document that it is not enforcement and that contradictions are resolved arbitrarily (F-20). This is the protocol's negative control number 2 and it now has a primary source showing it fails.
7. **Graph memory accepted as better without a matched comparison.** The dispute in F-24 and L05's Selective Forgetting result both refuse it.
8. **Token count as the capacity or cost model.** F-09's headline figures are token multipliers and predict nothing about a subscription ceiling; TC-26 already forbids this and F-09 is the source most likely to be cited against it.

## 7. Failure cases

Each states what breaks and how it is detected.

1. **The index that is trimmed on read.** Shared state grows past the loader's limit; everything beyond it stops arriving and no reader observes the boundary. Detected by a size check at write time against the read limit, refusing or erroring rather than warning.
2. **The compaction that drops the exclusion.** A promised remedy or a rejected alternative stated early in a run does not survive into the summary. Detected by re-asserting load-bearing facts from durable storage after every compaction event, and by a recall probe on those facts before the next consequential action. Not detected by reading the summary.
3. **The unaccounted provider edit.** The loader records a manifest; the provider then clears tool results server-side; the manifest is now false about delivered inputs. Detected by reconciling the response's applied-edits field against the manifest and treating any unaccounted edit as a manifest defect.
4. **The correction that does not bite.** The founder corrects a fact; the correction is stored; the next worker acts on the superseded value. Detected by a critical-field probe on a new bounded case after the correction, never by confirming the correction is in the store.
5. **The plausible false entry.** A wrong price, a closed duty recorded as live, a fabricated counterparty term, carrying no attack signature. Detected only out of band: against destination state, against original customer evidence, or by a deterministic counterexample. Not detected by screening, and not detected by provenance rank.
6. **The defense that empties the well.** Trust weighting is tightened after an incident; answer-bearing evidence is marked untrusted; the system becomes safe and useless. Detected by the paired clean-case control, run in the same session as the adverse control.
7. **The correlated duplicate.** Two or more executors, reading the same authoritative next-action view, take the same external action. Detected by idempotency-key collision and by rate anomaly on the shared resource. Not detected by reading shared state, because shared state is the cause.
8. **The filtered context that leaks anyway.** The loader filtered correctly; the model emitted the private term in an outward query or in tool stdout. Detected by a seeded canary term scanned across every disclosure destination, which chapter 06's CP-129 already enumerates correctly.
9. **The shared record that binds nobody.** A canonical file exists, every executor is told to read it, and adherence is discretionary. Detected by the manifest recording actual delivered inputs and by a planted contradiction: put two conflicting rules in the record and observe whether the system reports the conflict or silently picks one.

## 8. Implications for this system

**Against the fixed boundary of consequence and release.** F-14, F-15 and F-16 say the model cannot be the boundary. Any design in which an executor holds material it must not disclose and is instructed not to disclose it has placed a release boundary inside a component with a measured 16 to 51 percent failure rate. The boundary must be the loader and the outbound channel. This does not reopen the boundary; it constrains where it may be implemented.

**Against evidence and acceptance.** F-05 removes the model judge from the critical-preservation instrument. F-10's clean-case collapse means every filtering or trust mechanism must be accepted on a paired adverse and benign result, never on the adverse one alone. Chapter 06 section 7 already requires this and it is now the difference between a safe system and a system that refuses its own evidence.

**Against recovery.** F-02 and F-21 together mean that a resumed attempt cannot establish, from anything the provider surface offers, what its predecessor actually held. It can establish what the loader delivered and how many tokens the provider later removed. Chapter 06's rule that a resumed session may be reused only when every retained input is allowed for current recipients is therefore not just a privacy rule: it is the only rule under which the resumed attempt's context is knowable at all. The portable Continuation is the safe default and the opaque route is the exception, not the reverse.

**Against human responsibility and founder competence.** R1-S29 supports the mechanism and does not calibrate it, and the transfer test TC-29 names is unexecuted anywhere. The honest position is a (c)-class hypothesis with a protocol, not a claim.

**Against the six fixtures.**

- **F-1, the unknown job.** The admission rule must be able to refuse, and the refusal must be distinguishable from a failure to search, which chapter 06's CP-126 already handles. R1 adds nothing to the typing rule and flags that the malformed-report variant is the poisoning case of F-10, not an injection case: it may contain no instruction at all.
- **F-2, demand at 4x.** F-08 predicts that at matched capacity the multi-executor arm does not win on quality, and F-17 predicts that the coordination cost at 4x is dominated by transmitting context, which the vendor prices at about the cost of acting. Measure coordination minutes and transmitted context bytes separately from work performed.
- **F-3, three permissions.** R1's contribution is negative and specific: a permission-filtered single context is not equivalent to three separated executors, and the gap is 20 to 50 percentage points in one direction and still above 75 percent residual in the other (F-15). Neither arrangement discharges the requirement alone.
- **F-4, verification the producer must not influence.** Out of lane, except that F-05 removes one candidate checker: a model rating faithfulness does not correlate with human judgment on the errors that matter.
- **F-5, the capacity reset.** This is R1's central fixture. The resumed attempt may trust the portable Continuation and the loader's manifest; it may not trust a provider-resumed session, because F-02 shows the provider may have edited it and reports only a count. The changed source fact is detected by the critical-field probe, not by re-reading the summary. The already-released external effect is detected by an idempotency record on the effect, not by state in the shared record.
- **F-6, the founder absent.** F-17's hidden-profile result is the warning: a next-action view that everyone trusts suppresses the dissenting private fact rather than surfacing it. The re-entry brief must include what was omitted and what was contested, not only what was decided.

**Against comparator B0.** Three separate findings favor B0 more than this package currently assumes: F-24's full-context baseline outscoring the architected memory systems on their own benchmark; F-08's matched-budget result; and F-04's finding that the bounded-memory arm loses to full context on knowledge updates. Three findings cut the other way: F-06 and F-07's length degradation, F-15's isolation benefit, and F-03's silent truncation, which B0 also suffers. B0 is stronger than a naive reading of this package would suggest, and the round should say so.

## 9. Questions other lanes may have missed

1. **Who measures false exclusion?** Every privacy and filtering source I found measures leakage. None measures correct work refused because the loader filtered too hard. F-10's evidence recall of exactly zero is the extreme case. A design accepted on leakage alone is a design that will be tuned into uselessness after the first incident.
2. **Does shared truth increase duplicated external effects?** F-17 measures correlated duplication with no shared state at all. Nobody has measured whether a shared authoritative next-action view makes it better or worse. If it makes it worse, the thesis's central mechanism buys coherence and pays in duplication, and nobody has priced that trade.
3. **What is the writer authority on shared state, and what happens on a conflicting write?** The acceptance protocol lists this as (d)-class example 8. I found a paper naming last-writer-wins as the defect and no measurement of its frequency. The lane that owns state should answer it before any candidate names a shared record.
4. **Is the manifest's "actual delivered inputs" rule satisfiable across the provider boundary at all?** F-02 says partially, by counts. If a candidate asserts the rule without naming the reconciliation, it has asserted something the provider surface does not support.
5. **What is the unit of a "peer work projection", and who decides its recipient scope?** The narrow thesis assumes a projection can be permission-filtered. F-15 says the memory component is where filtering works worst. Someone must say whether a projection is filtered at write, at read, or at both, and what happens to a projection already written when a grant is revoked.
6. **Does any fixture actually put conflicting context in one window?** Negative control 4 requires that isolated context not be claimed where no fixture exercises it. Of the six fixtures, only F-3's adverse variation comes close, and it tests permissions rather than conflicting knowledge. If the answer is no, then TC-09's isolation criterion is being claimed on evidence the round did not generate.
7. **What is the retention and deletion story for a peer projection?** L05 established that erasure does not propagate to derivatives by default. A projection is a derivative of another venture's work and may outlive the grant that justified it.
8. **Is the EU AI Act record-keeping obligation in scope for any of the four ventures?** If yes, the manifest stops being a design choice and its cost argument closes. Nobody in this round appears to own that determination.
