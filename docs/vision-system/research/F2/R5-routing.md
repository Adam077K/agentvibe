## 2. Findings

Kind labels follow DIRECTIVE §6. Source ids resolve in §3.

**R5-01. The founder's thesis describes a named 1970s architecture, and that architecture deliberately does not specify who decides what happens next. [Source claim + Inference]** The blackboard model is "a global database called the blackboard" with "logically independent sources of knowledge called the knowledge sources", and the figure caption states: "Note that there is no control flow; the knowledge sources are self-activating" [S27]. Its own author's footnote is the load-bearing sentence: "There is no control component specified in the blackboard model. The model merely specifies a general problem-solving behavior. The actual locus of control can be in the knowledge sources, on the blackboard, in a separate module, or in some combination of the three" [S27]. Nii also warns that "the blackboard model is a conceptual entity, not a computational specification" and that "a sketch is a long way from a working system" [S27]. **Inference:** "coordinated capabilities around a shared source of truth" is the blackboard model restated, and TC-41 is not an imprecision in the founder's wording. It is the exact hole the original model left open, unclosed for forty years.

**R5-02. Adding a coordinator to a blackboard is not free, and the source says what it costs. [Source claim]** In Nii's jigsaw analogy a monitor is introduced to serialize access, and "the monitor has broad executive power. The monitor could, for example, force the puzzle to be solved systematically from left to right; that is, the monitor has the power to violate one essential characteristic of the original blackboard model, that of opportunistic problem solving" [S27]. **Inference:** the package's choice of an explicit admission authority (C02) is one of the three readings TC-41 names, and its cost is already documented: concentrated agenda authority can suppress the opportunism that made the shared record valuable. `02 §2` names that same cost independently.

**R5-03. Every operating system for unanticipated work resolves ownership by default rule at the instant of arrival, before typing. [Source claim]** The US national incident system states it as a rule of organization: "Responsibility for functions that subordinates perform defaults to the next higher supervisory position until the supervisor delegates those responsibilities" [S1]. The organization "develop[s] in a modular fashion based on an incident's size, complexity, and hazard environment", and the five functional areas are "staffed as needed" with a sixth "only used when the incident requires these specialized capabilities" [S1]. **Inference:** a capability slot that exists and is unstaffed is not the same object as an agent that must be invented. Expansion happens by delegating an already-held responsibility downward, never by creating an authority that did not exist.

**R5-04. Incident practice biases admission toward accepting, and types afterwards. [Source claim]** "Any PagerDuty employee has the ability to trigger our incident response process at any time", and "If you are unsure of whether response is required, trigger our incident response process" [S8]. Roles are assumed contextually rather than pre-staffed: "It is not intended that every role be filled by a different person for every incident" [S9].

**R5-05. The same literature contains the matched refusal rule, so admission bias is not admission of everything. [Source claim]** "Resources that authorities do not request should refrain from spontaneous deployment to avoid overburdening the recipient and compounding accountability challenges" [S1]. **Inference:** negative control §4.9 of the protocol ("a router that admits everything") has a real-world analogue, and the real systems pair a low admission threshold with an explicit prohibition on self-dispatch. The threshold is low for *reporting*, not for *acting*.

**R5-06. Adaptive case management admits new work at runtime, but only from a pre-declared envelope, under role authorization, gated by data conditions. [Source claim]** CMMN states "Planning is a run-time effort" and defines a `DiscretionaryItem` as identifying a plan item definition "of which instances can be planned, to the 'discretion' of a Case worker that is involved in planning" [S2]. The act is defined precisely: "Users (Case workers) are said to 'plan' (in run-time), when they select DiscretionaryItems from a PlanningTable, and move instances of their associated PlanItemDefinitions into the plan of the Case (instance)" [S2]. Authority is explicit: "It is possible to authorize Roles for planning of certain DiscretionaryItems and sub-PlanningTables" [S2]. Applicability is data-driven: "TableItems for which an associated ApplicabilityRule evaluates to FALSE will not be exposed to Case workers for planning purpose" [S2]. **Inference, and this is the finding that matters for F-1:** the standard most often cited for "handling unanticipated work" does not admit an unanticipated *kind* of work. It admits a pre-declared item that was not pre-scheduled. `02 §8`'s retention of adaptive cases is therefore weaker support for TC-19 than it reads.

**R5-07. The workflow-patterns literature states the ceiling in one sentence. [Source claim]** "It is only possible to specify handlers for expected types of exception" [S23]. That paper classifies exception events into five groups: work item failure, deadline expiry, resource unavailability, external trigger, constraint violation [S23]. Resource unavailability explicitly covers the unstaffable case: problems arise if "at distribution time, no resource can be found which meets the specified allocation criteria for the work item" [S23]. Handling is a triple of work item disposition, case disposition and recovery action, giving "135 possible patterns", with case-level options continue, remove current case, remove all cases, and recovery actions no action, rollback, compensate [S23].

**R5-08. Durable workflow engines route by pre-registered handler, and their answer to "no handler" is to hold the work and wait for a person to deploy one. [Source claim]** A Temporal task queue is "a lightweight, dynamically allocated queue that one or more Worker Entities poll for Tasks", and "You must ensure that if a Worker accepts a Task that it can process that task using one of its registered Workflows, Activities, or Nexus Operation handlers" [S13]. Work is not dropped when nobody can take it: "Workflow and Activity Tasks persist in a Task Queue. When a Worker Process goes down, the messages remain until the Worker recovers and can process the Tasks" [S13]. When the handler exists but fails in a way the engine reads as a defect, "The Workflow Task retries indefinitely, letting you fix the bug and redeploy without losing Workflow state" [S14]. **Inference:** the most mature durable-execution designs answer F-1's staffing question with *park indefinitely and wait for a human edit*. That is the direct opposite of TC-19 and it is a deliberate choice, not an oversight.

**R5-09. The catch-all is never total, and the vendor documents the residue. [Source claim]** `States.ALL` is "A wildcard that matches any known error name", and in the same page: "The States.ALL error type must appear alone in a Catcher and cannot catch the States.DataLimitExceeded terminal error or Runtime error types" [S11]. **Inference:** a design whose completeness argument is a catch-all branch must state what its catch-all cannot catch, or the argument is decoration.

**R5-10. Unroutable work is given a named destination, an owner and an alarm rather than being dropped. [Source claim]** Dead-letter queues let you "isolate unconsumed messages to determine why processing did not succeed"; a redrive policy sets `maxReceiveCount`, the number of failed receives before the move; a redrive allow policy names which sources may use it; and "it is a best practice to always set the retention period of a dead-letter queue to be longer than the retention period of the original queue" [S10]. AWS documents alarming on the queue as a companion practice [S10]. **Inference:** three separable obligations fall out. Name the destination, bound the retention so the destination is not itself a slow drop, and alarm on arrival so the destination has a reader.

**R5-11. Deterministic ownership resolvers are cheap and reproducible, and their default is where they fail. [Source claim]** GitHub's rule is a single sentence: "Order is important; the last matching pattern takes the most precedence" [S15]. For paths no rule matches, changes "can be made with the approval of any user who has write access to the repository" [S15]. **Inference:** last-match-wins is exactly the reproducibility property `03`'s tie-break is reaching for, and the unmatched-path behaviour is the failure the same section is trying to avoid. Universal ownership and no ownership are the same state.

**R5-12. Escalation by timeout is the standard remedy for unacted work, and it terminates in a silent state. [Source claim]** "An escalation policy is an ordered set of rules"; each rule carries a timeout, default thirty minutes; "If no one takes action before the timeout elapses, the incident escalates to the next rule"; "An escalation policy can be repeated up to nine times", after which "the incident will stay assigned to the last user and will not continue to notify" [S7]. **Inference:** the ladder converts unowned work into owned-but-unacted work and then stops making noise. Assigned and silent is not resolved, and a design that treats the end of the ladder as an end state has reinvented the unowned queue with an owner's name on it.

**R5-13. The most widely deployed issue tracker separates "the state it is in" from "the reason it is finished", and makes the separation optional. [Source claim]** "In Jira, a work item's Resolution field describes the reason it was moved to Done"; "Done work items don't have to have a resolution"; and "the Open work items filter shows work items that don't have a resolution" [S5]. A workflow is "a set of statuses and transitions that a work item moves through" [S6]. **Inference:** a Done item with an empty resolution is simultaneously finished and open in the same product. (d)-class example 3 in the protocol, incompatible meanings of "done", is not hypothetical; it ships.

**R5-14. Admission is a first-class state with a named owner in current tools, not a folder. [Source claim]** "Triage is a special inbox for your team", for issues "from integrations and non-team members"; "Enable triage responsibility to define who handles incoming issues", with rotation optionally driven by the on-call system; declining "will update the issue to a Canceled status type" [S3]. Triage is one of the fixed status categories alongside backlog, unstarted, started, completed, canceled and a reserved duplicate state [S4]. **Inference:** refusal is recorded as a terminal state of the same object, not as deletion. The spurious job in F-1 leaves a record.

**R5-15. Typing unfamiliar work is measurable, and the measurement is unflattering exactly where F-1 lives. [Source claim]** The CLINC150 study exists because "current text classification corpora only define label sets that cover every example" [S17]. Its result: "while the classifiers perform well on in-scope intent classification, they struggle to identify out-of-scope queries", with BERT "scoring 96% or above" in-scope but "the best system scoring 66% out-of-scope recall" even with a large number of out-of-scope training examples; reducing out-of-scope training examples from 250 to 100 dropped out-of-scope recall from 59.2% to 40.3% [S17]. **Model-generation dependence: yes.** These are 2019 classifiers. The structure of the result transfers; the numbers do not.

**R5-16. Refusing to act when nothing applies is now a separately scored capability, and its benchmark is dominated by the negative class. [Source claim]** The Berkeley Function-Calling Leaderboard defines irrelevance detection as "The scenario where none of the function choices provided are relevant to the user query and none should be invoked", against relevance detection, "The scenario where at least one of the function choices provided are relevant" [S18]. Its Live split carries 882 irrelevance entries against 18 relevance entries [S18]. **Inference:** the base rate in the benchmark is the opposite of the base rate in a company. A recogniser tuned where most inputs are out of scope will not behave the same where almost all work is familiar, and neither will its error costs.

**R5-17. Routing between executors has an established cost-quality instrument. [Source claim]** RouteLLM defines performance gap recovered as "how much of the performance difference between the weak and strong models is recovered by the router model", average performance gap recovered as "an overall measure of the router's ability to recover the performance gap under different cost constraints", and call-performance threshold as "the minimum percentage of calls to the strong model needed to reach the desired PGR" [S16]. The abstract reports cost reduction "by over 2 times in certain cases", and the rendered results report cost-saving ratios of 3.66, 1.41 and 1.49 on MT Bench, MMLU and GSM8K while retaining 95%, 92% and 87% of the strong model's quality [S16]. Routers transferred to unseen model pairs "with performance comparable to that of the original model pair" [S16]. **Confidence caveat:** I read a rendered HTML summary, not the tables directly; treat the three ratios as reported figures pending recomputation.

**R5-18. Selection over a growing catalog degrades, and the vendor states the threshold without publishing a method. [Source claim, conflicted]** "Claude's ability to pick the right tool degrades once you exceed 30–50 available tools"; a typical five-server setup "can consume ~55k tokens in definitions before Claude does any work"; search-then-load "typically reduces this by over 85 percent" [S20]. **This is a source claim, not a measurement**, because no task distribution, no sample and no method are published. It is directly relevant to the package's 10/100/1000 sweep in `05 §8`, and it is weaker evidence than that sweep would produce.

**R5-19. Routers under capacity pressure drop work by design, quietly, and need a separate force to stay balanced. [Source claim]** In sparse expert models, expert capacity is set by a capacity factor, and "If too many tokens are routed to an expert (referred to later as dropped tokens), computation is skipped and the token representation is passed directly to the next layer through the residual connection"; the authors report dropped-token rates "typically <1%" and use an auxiliary load-balancing loss to keep routing uniform [S19]. **Inference:** the transferable lesson is not about models. Any router with a bounded per-destination capacity must name an overflow destination and count overflows, or the overflow becomes a pass-through that looks like completion. For tokens a residual pass-through is benign. For an obligation it is a silent drop.

**R5-20. Re-routing is not simply a defect, and treating it as one optimises the wrong thing. [Source claim]** A large-scale study of Windows Vista bug reassignments found "reassignments are useful to determine the best person to fix a bug, contrary to the popular opinion that reassignments are always harmful", and categorised "five primary reasons for reassignments: finding the root cause, determining ownership, poor bug report quality, hard to determine proper fix, and workload balancing" [S24]. It names the genuinely harmful shape: "there are certain harmful patterns of reassignments, like cycles at the end of a sequence" [S24]. It also states the metric it is arguing against: "In ticket routing, a new ticket needs to find its resolver with as few steps as possible—any assignment to someone who cannot resolve the ticket is considered as inefficient" [S24]. A separate study reports that between 37% and 44% of bug reports are reassigned in Mozilla and Eclipse [S25]; **I did not read that paper and record the figure as unverified.**

**R5-21. There is one published account of an agent system absorbing task types nobody specified, and its enabling condition does not hold here. [Source claim + Inference]** Voyager proposes its own tasks through an automatic curriculum, stores successful action programs in a skill library indexed "by the embedding of its description", and retrieves by querying "with the embedding of self-generated task plans and environment feedback" [S22]. It reports 3.3 times more unique items, 2.3 times longer distances travelled, and tech-tree milestones "up to 15.3× faster than prior SOTA" [S22]. **Inference:** the mechanism that makes this work is not the curriculum. It is that the environment returns a cheap, automatic, non-negotiable verdict on every attempt. A customs classification dispute or a supplier's deletion obligation returns no such verdict, and the acceptance owner the protocol demands is precisely the thing the game supplies for free.

**R5-22. Orchestrator-to-worker delegation has published failure modes that are routing failures, not execution failures. [Source claim, conflicted]** Anthropic reports that a lead agent that "decomposes queries into subtasks and describes them to subagents" produced instructions vague enough that "subagents misinterpreted the task or performed the exact same searches as other agents", that "Token usage by itself explains 80% of the variance", and that "Some domains that require all agents to share the same context or involve many dependencies between agents are not a good fit for multi-agent systems today" [S21]. The headline 90.2% improvement over a single agent [S21] is a vendor self-report on an internal evaluation and may not be the sole support for any mechanism.

**R5-23. The next-action view in the one system designed for genuinely unforeseen work has an author, an approver, and an expiry. [Source claim]** "Personnel managing the incident develop an IAP for each operational period"; "Based on concurrence from all elements at the end of the Planning Meeting, the Incident Commander or Unified Command approves the plan"; each period starts with an Operational Period Briefing at which assigned personnel receive it [S1]. The initial version is deliberately thin: "During the initial stage of incident management, the Incident Commander typically develops a simple plan and communicates the plan through concise oral briefings" [S1]. And on waiting for better information: "Personnel develop the IAP using the best information available at the time of the Planning Meeting. Personnel should not delay planning meetings in anticipation of future information" [S1]. Handover carries a mandatory briefing: "the transfer process includes a briefing that captures essential information for continuing safe and effective operations" [S1]. Staffing has a stated ratio: "The optimal span of control for incident management is one supervisor to five subordinates" [S1].

**R5-24. The rate at which unanticipated kinds of work arrive is not established by any source I located. [Unknown]** I searched for a measured rate of work items that match no existing category in real operations and found only vendor marketing figures [S30], which I record as folklore and do not use. The peer-reviewed ticket-classification work I found states the problem, "Manual classification of IT service desk tickets may result in routing of the tickets to the wrong resolution group" [S29], without a novelty rate. TC-18 therefore rests on the founder's testimony plus the structural observation that every mature work system builds an unanticipated-work path. That is evidence that the path is necessary, not evidence of its load.

## 4. Per-claim verdicts for every R5 claim

**TC-18 — unspecified kinds of work arrive often enough to be a primary design requirement.**
Verdict: **the requirement is supported, the rate is not established.** Every system examined that operates under real uncertainty carries a dedicated path for work its model did not anticipate: an incident category defined by being unplanned [S8], a triage state [S3], a discretionary-planning envelope [S2], a dead-letter destination [S10], and an exception taxonomy whose author states handlers exist only for expected exceptions [S23]. That is convergent structural evidence that the path is load-bearing. It says nothing about frequency. No source I found measures the fraction of admitted work matching no existing capability contract [S24 is about misrouting, not novelty; S30 is folklore]. The claim as written ("often enough") is therefore **unresolved**, and the measure in the claims file is the right one. One design consequence follows regardless of the rate, from R5-16: the base rate decides whether a recogniser can work at all, so the rate must be estimated before any typing mechanism is evaluated.

**TC-19 — a new kind of work can be admitted, typed, staffed and accepted with no person editing a capability catalog, routing table or agent definition.**
Verdict: **contradicted as a single claim; it must be split into four, and they have different answers.**
- *Admitted:* **supported.** Triage [S3], unconditional trigger rights [S8], dead-letter destinations [S10] and the persistent task queue [S13] all admit work with no edit. Admission is the easy step and no candidate should earn credit for it.
- *Typed:* **partially supported and measurable.** The mechanism exists; the measured failure is silent mistyping, not refusal [S17], and the operating base rate changes the answer [S18].
- *Staffed:* **contradicted in the strong form.** No system located staffs a genuinely novel work type without either a pre-declared envelope [S2], a pre-registered handler [S13], or a human redeploy [S14]. The only counterexample is Voyager, and it writes a new *skill* rather than a new executor, with an automatic success oracle the business case lacks [S22]. The package's own text agrees: `05 §4` routes a role gap through the maintenance custodian proposing and the capability owner approving.
- *Accepted:* **the claim is mis-stated.** Every system resolves an acceptance owner by a pre-declared default rule to a standing owner: default upward to the next supervisory position [S1], the last matching path rule [S15], escalation order [S7], the promise sponsor then service mandate then maintenance custodian in `03`. Resolving by rule is *not* a human editing the system. So the correct reading of TC-19 is: **zero edits are achievable for admission, typing and acceptance-owner assignment, and not for capability creation.** A candidate that claims otherwise should be asked to exhibit the mechanism that writes a new capability contract without an approver, and then asked who accepts its first outcome.

**TC-34 — who produces the next-action view, whether it binds, and what happens when it is wrong.**
Verdict: **the package's answer is corroborated and the thesis's phrasing is under-specified.** Three properties appear in every working instance. It has a named author and a named approver, and those are different roles [S1]. It has an explicit validity window, the operational period, and is re-issued rather than edited [S1]. Only one component advances the cursor, which in Temporal is the engine and in the package is C02 [S12, `05 §3`]. Two further constraints come from the sources. Waiting for better information is prohibited: "Personnel should not delay planning meetings in anticipation of future information" [S1]. And the blackboard source warns that the view and the record must not be the same object, because a controller with the power to order the next action can override the opportunism the record exists to enable [S27]. **The thesis phrase "an updated view of what should happen next" names an artifact with no author, no approver and no expiry, and that is the (d)-class shape.**

**TC-40 — the recogniser, its evidence and its failure modes are unspecified.**
Verdict: **supported that it is unspecified, and it is now specifiable.** The instrument is a confusion matrix over three classes, familiar / genuinely new / familiar-in-unfamiliar-wording, exactly as the claims file says. The discriminating quantity is out-of-scope recall, which lags in-scope accuracy by a wide margin: 96% or above in-scope against 66% best out-of-scope recall, falling to 40.3% when out-of-scope training material is reduced [S17]. Irrelevance detection is a separately scored category with its own dataset [S18], and the class balance of that dataset is the inverse of an operating company's [S18], so its scores do not transfer without re-weighting. Selection over a catalog degrades with catalog size [S20], which means the recogniser's error rate is a function of how much the company knows how to do, not only of the input. `05 §10`'s distinction between `SkillSelection.no_match` and `budget_exhausted` is the right shape one level up, and the missing third member is *typed into the nearest existing kind*, which must never be scored as success.

**TC-41 — whether coordination is a component, a shared record, or emergent.**
Verdict: **a real open decision with a named 1986 source that declines to resolve it and states the cost of each resolution.** "There is no control component specified in the blackboard model ... The actual locus of control can be in the knowledge sources, on the blackboard, in a separate module, or in some combination of the three" [S27]. The three readings in the claims file are exactly those three loci. The evidence adds two things the claim does not have. First, the cost of the component reading is documented: broad executive power that can violate opportunistic problem solving [S27], which is the same cost `02 §2` names as concentrated agenda authority. Second, the pairwise-negotiation reading has published failure data in this generation: vague delegated instructions produced duplicated work across subagents [S21], and inter-agent misalignment is a named family in the MAST taxonomy `L03` already carries. **Disagreement preserved: no source resolves the trichotomy, and the measure in the claims file, contradictory actions and duplicated effects and owner minutes under each reading, remains the way to settle it.**

## 5. Assumptions · Unknowns · Competing interpretations

**Assumptions.** That mechanisms from incident command, case management, issue tracking and durable execution transfer to a company operated mostly by models. That the fixed boundaries hold, in particular that C04 alone releases consequences, so nothing in routing releases an effect. That an acceptance owner is always a standing owner, human or otherwise, with actual authority. None is established by this lane.

**Unknowns.**
1. The arrival rate of genuinely novel work kinds (R5-24). No source located. This blocks calibration of any recogniser and is the single most valuable measurement the founder could take from his own history, by counting admitted items over a past window against the existing capability list.
2. Whether the 37%-44% reassignment figure [S25] holds as stated. Unverified; I did not read the paper.
3. Whether the ICS default-upward rule has an analogue that is safe here. Defaulting upward means defaulting toward *more* authority, and the package's tie-break deliberately defaults toward the *narrowest sufficient* mandate. Both are reasoned; nothing I found decides between them.
4. **Instrument gap, reported as owed work.** The claim-ledger append tool was unavailable in this session, so none of these findings is registered as a durable claim with an expiry and an invalidator. The five strongest candidates for registration are R5-08 (durable engines park unhandleable work pending human deploy), R5-15 (out-of-scope recall lags in-scope accuracy), R5-09 (catch-all residue), R5-06 (CMMN admits unscheduled, not unanticipated, work), and R5-13 (Done and resolved are separable in shipped software). Whoever holds the tool should register them with the quotes in §2, which are recorded verbatim for that purpose.

**Competing interpretations, preserved.**
- **Three incompatible dispositions for work nothing can handle.** Park indefinitely and wait for a human [S14]; refuse with a recorded reason [S3]; assign by default and escalate on a timer [S1, S7]. Each has a different failure: an obligation with a deadline rots in the first, a real duty is declined in the second, and the third ends assigned and silent. The sources genuinely disagree and the right choice is almost certainly per consequence class, not global.
- **Whether re-routing is a defect.** The ticket-routing literature treats any assignment to a non-resolver as inefficient; the Vista study argues reassignment is often how the right owner is found, and that the harmful shape is cycles [S24]. A design that minimises re-routing may be suppressing diagnosis.
- **Whether a new kind of work needs a new executor.** Three sources answer three ways: a new skill [S22], a new assignment under an existing function [S1], a new plan item from a declared envelope [S2]. None creates a new standing role. That convergence is itself a finding against the departmental reading and is consistent with `05 §4`.

| Mandatory briefing on transfer of command | Handover losing what the receiver needed | S1 |
| Automatic curriculum plus indexed skill library | Absorbing a new task *where a cheap success oracle exists* | S22 |

**Rejected mechanisms, with the reason.**

1. **A catch-all branch offered as a completeness argument.** The best-documented catch-all in production cannot catch two named error classes [S11]. A catch-all whose residue is unnamed is decoration.
2. **Indefinite retry as an admission policy.** Correct where the blocker is a defect awaiting redeploy [S14]; wrong for a supplier obligation with a deadline, which would sit in a retry loop with no deadline owner. F-1 is the second case.
3. **Pure shared record with no control locus.** Its own source states the model specifies no control component and is not a computational specification [S27]. This is negative control §4.2 of the protocol, a shared source of truth nobody is forced to read.
4. **Confidence-thresholded auto-typing with no abstain class.** Out-of-scope recall is the weak axis and it degrades fastest when novel examples are rare [S17], which is precisely the operating regime here.
5. **Capacity shedding by pass-through.** Acceptable for tokens [S19]; for obligations it produces completion-shaped silence. Any shed must have a destination and a count.
6. **"Assign to the team" as the default owner.** The unowned-path behaviour of the best-known ownership resolver is that anyone with write access may act [S15]. Universal ownership and no ownership are one state.
7. **Routing accuracy as the sole metric.** It scores the diagnostic path as waste [S24].
8. **Delegation by short natural-language description.** Published as producing misinterpretation and duplicated work [S21].

## 7. Failure cases

**Mis-routing.** Typed into the nearest existing kind and worked by a competent executor who solves the wrong problem. The measurement that exposes it is out-of-scope recall, not overall accuracy [S17]. It is the failure that looks most like success, because every downstream record is well-formed.

**Silent drop.** Four distinct shapes located. Capacity overflow passed through untouched [S19]. Work resting in a queue nobody polls, which persists rather than erroring [S13]. An item sitting past a dead-letter retention window because the destination's retention was shorter than the source's [S10]. And an escalation ladder that exhausted its nine repeats, leaving the incident "assigned to the last user" with no further notification [S7]. Only the last of these has a named owner, and it is the one most likely to be reported as handled.

**Duplicate admission.** Two routes. The same external report arriving through two channels and typed into two work orders, which the duplicate state in trackers exists to absorb [S4]; and redrive replaying dead-lettered messages whose external effects already occurred, which is L08 F2's exactly-once boundary problem [`L08`, S10].

**Unowned work.** Three shapes: no rule matches, so the default is everyone [S15]; a rule matches a mandate that no longer exists; and the triage queue itself, which is an owned inbox whose backlog can become a second unowned queue if triage responsibility is a rotation with no service level [S3].

**Ping-pong.** Named in the literature as the harmful reassignment shape, "cycles at the end of a sequence" [S24]. A custody rule that permits dispute to suspend custody generates these; `03`'s decision that "a contested provisional custody is still custody" is the correct guard and should be read as load-bearing rather than procedural.

**Refusal as a disguised drop.** A spurious job declined with a reason is a recorded outcome [S3]. A spurious job declined by silence is indistinguishable from a lost one. The adverse variation in F-1 is therefore two tests, not one: the refusal must happen *and* leave a record with an owner.

**Done without resolution.** A work order marked complete with no acceptance reason, which the most widely deployed tracker permits [S5].

## 8. Implications for this system

Bound to the fixed boundaries and the six fixtures. These are implications and required showings, not recommendations; the decisions are the founder's.

**F-1, an unknown kind of job appears mid-build.** The evidence says the fixture decomposes into four questions with four different answers, and a candidate that answers them as one has not answered them.

- *Admission.* Achievable with no human edit, and the package already has the shape: intake mandate plus temporary protective custody (`03`). The showing required is that admission is a **state with an owner**, not a folder, and that the refusal branch is a terminal state of the same record [S3].
- *Typing.* Achievable and measurable. The showing required is a three-way confusion matrix that separates honest no-match from silent mistyping, plus the base rate used to calibrate it. `05 §10`'s `SkillSelection.no_match` / `budget_exhausted` distinction is the right instrument one level up, and needs a third member for mistyping [S17, S18].
- *Staffing.* **This is where the fixture bites and where TC-19 fails as written.** Nothing located staffs a novel work type without a pre-declared envelope, a pre-registered handler, or a human deploy [S2, S13, S14]. A candidate claiming zero edits here must exhibit the mechanism and name who accepts the first outcome of a capability nobody approved. The honest alternative, which the package already implements, is that the *ownership* of unknown work needs no edit while the *capability* to perform it does.
- *Acceptance.* The ordered resolver in `03` is the right class of mechanism, and it is stronger than TC-19's framing suggests: promise sponsor, else service mandate, else designated maintenance/discovery custodian, with least-authority-sufficient as the overlap tie-break and `custody_tie_break_reason` recorded so two implementations are comparable. Two gaps the evidence exposes. First, the terminal default must be a **standing owner with authority to act**, not a queue; ICS achieves this by defaulting upward to a position that already holds the authority [S1], while the package defaults to the narrowest sufficient mandate, which is the opposite direction and needs a stated reason for the case where no mandate covers the harm at all. Second, the losing-mandate record and the "contested custody is still custody" rule are the two provisions that prevent the ping-pong failure [S24]; both should be treated as load-bearing, not as bookkeeping.
- *The adverse variation.* The malformed report from an untrusted channel and the spurious job are separate tests. The first is a context-integrity test that belongs with W3 and W10 and must not release any effect on the strength of the report alone, which the C04 boundary already forbids. The second is a refusal test with a measurable analogue [S18], and negative control §4.9 means a candidate that admits it fails whatever else it achieves.

**F-2, demand at 4×.** Two transfers. Every router with a bounded per-destination capacity needs a named overflow destination and an overflow **count**, because the default behaviour of capacity-bounded routing is a silent pass-through [S19]. And shedding is an authority question before it is a capacity question: the ICS ratio of one supervisor to five subordinates [S1] and the package's WIP default of one running work order per temporary executor (`05 §2`) are staffing rules whose interaction with CP1 concurrency (`07 §6`) decides what can be shed at all. Shed work must remain owned; an unshedded owner is the only thing that distinguishes shedding from dropping.

**F-3, three tool permissions.** Mostly R4's. One transfer from here: CMMN authorizes *roles for planning* separately from roles for doing [S2]. The authority to add work to a live case is a distinct permission from the authority to perform it, and the package does not currently name it as one.

**F-4, verification the producer must not influence.** Out of lane. One note: if a router selects the verifier, the router is inside the verification boundary, and a candidate whose routing component can choose who checks its own work has not achieved procedural independence.

**F-5, handoff across a capacity reset.** Two transfers. Work persists in a queue while nothing can take it, which means "still queued" after a reset is a normal state and not evidence of loss [S13]. And transfer of command requires a briefing capturing what the receiver needs to continue [S1], which is the same obligation `05 §5` places on Continuation. The dead-letter retention trap is the reset-specific hazard: a holding destination whose retention is shorter than the interruption converts parked work into lost work [S10].

**F-6, founder absent for a week.** The escalation ladder terminates [S7], so the candidate must say what happens at the end of the ladder when the only remaining escalation target is the absent founder. The IAP pattern is the strongest available answer to the re-entry brief: a plan re-issued each period by a named author and approved by a named authority, with the explicit instruction not to delay planning in anticipation of information that has not arrived [S1]. That maps directly onto latest-responsible-decision-time behaviour and onto what `04` owes the founder on return.

**Coherence with the fixed boundaries.** Nothing in this lane's mechanisms releases an external effect, and none should be read as doing so. Escalation, custody assignment and triage disposition are all assignments of responsibility, not grants of authority; `03`'s sentence that "The resolver assigns ownership, not new grants" is the right boundary and every mechanism above respects it. The one place to watch is the DLQ analogue: a holding destination with an alarm implies a reader with standing to act, and if that reader is created by the routing layer rather than granted by C01, the layer has minted authority.

## 9. Questions other lanes may have missed

1. **Is the thesis a blackboard architecture?** If it is, TC-41 and TC-34 are one question with a forty-year-old primary source that declines to answer it and states the price of each answer [S27]. R8 and R1 are both working near this and neither claim set names it.
2. **What is the base rate of novel work?** A recogniser with 96% in-scope accuracy and 66% out-of-scope recall behaves completely differently at one novel job in five than at one in two hundred. Nobody has asked for the denominator, and TC-18's measure produces it. This should be settled before any typing mechanism is evaluated, because it decides whether the mechanism can work at all.
3. **Does the refusal path have its own acceptance owner?** A communicated refusal is an outward effect. F-1's spurious job is refused with a reason; who accepts that the refusal was correct, and what happens when it was not?
4. **Is re-routing scored as a defect anywhere in the package?** If so, the system will be tuned to suppress the mechanism by which the right owner is found [S24].
5. **Who owns the triage queue's own backlog?** Every admission mechanism located creates a second queue whose owner is a rotation. A rotation with no service level is an unowned queue with a name.
6. **What is the alarm on the unroutable destination, and who is paged by it?** A named destination with no reader is the original failure with better records [S10].
7. **When no mandate covers the harm at all, does the package's least-authority default have a terminal element, and is that element a person?** `03` names the maintenance/discovery custodian. Whether that custodian is an agent, a standing human role or the founder changes F-6 completely.
8. **Is the authority to add work to a live case separately granted from the authority to perform it?** CMMN separates them explicitly [S2]; the package does not appear to.
9. **Does any mechanism in the package count overflows?** Shedding without a count is indistinguishable from a silent drop after the fact, and F-2 is the fixture where this becomes visible.
10. **What does "done" mean when the resolution field is empty?** The shipped precedent permits the contradiction [S5], and (d)-class example 3 says it blocks.
