## 2. Strongest mechanism and most dangerous flaw

**C1 — strongest: independently settled Outcome Contracts.** Acceptance connects spending, confidence and delivered outcomes.

**Dangerous flaw: a convincing market without independent information.** Synthetic bidders need not supply independent knowledge. Sparse outcomes weaken calibration. Unvalidated prices must not acquire authority to freeze ventures.

**C2 — strongest: judgment compiled into Standing Orders.** Repeated decisions become delegation; separate founder/own preferences preserve dissent.

**Dangerous flaw: institutionalised error.** Incarnations share one Mind; an 85% historical-agreement gate can reject improvement. Preserve constraints, not mistakes. Its A4 example also requires routine refund approval: a founder bottleneck at volume.

**C3 — strongest: artifact-bound acceptance through an enforced effect gateway.** Permission attaches to current premises and actions, including under crashes and retries.

**Dangerous flaw: locally correct work without a coherent venture bet.** Goal links do not establish strategic ownership. Its co-founder function recommends but cannot commit peers. Add a replaceable strategic seat with bounded allocation authority.

**C4 — strongest: preregistered experiments settled against authoritative outcomes.** Fixed criteria, preserved null results and controlled organisational trials make learning falsifiable.

**Dangerous flaw: an evidence timetable mistaken for business reality.** Delayed retention can kill sound theses prematurely. Distinguish disproved, underpowered and not-yet-observable bets. Expiry must preserve customer obligations.

**C5 — strongest: mandatory return of tested assets to the backlot.** Completed productions leave capabilities that can lower subsequent delivery costs.

**Dangerous flaw: production templates becoming the core playbook.** Fixed crews and commercial exits impose assumptions; research should not require paying users. A completion guarantor cannot silently redefine the founder’s outcome.

## 3. Best combination

**One operating system, with separate authority for intent, allocation, execution and acceptance.**

| Source | Graft | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **C3: spine** | World model, needs, fenced leases, receipts, effect gateway | Temporary workers cooperate and recover through durable, enforceable state. |
| **C2: continuity** | Venture Mind, wagers, founder/own-view split, Standing Orders | An on-demand strategic seat owns the thesis and allocates its authorised mission budget. |
| **C4: learning** | Uncertainty map, preregistration, interference graph, null results, configuration trials | Test consequential unknowns without turning every operational task into an experiment. |
| **C1: economics** | Cost/confidence estimates, paid auditions, independent settlement | Compare approaches where uncertainty justifies competition. |
| **C5: capability and taste** | Versioned backlot, mandatory asset recovery, dailies, circled takes | Reuse proven work and present concrete choices to the founder. |

The strategic seat funds missions; workers claim contributions. Acceptance precedes effects; receipts update state; later outcomes settle predictions. Tested lessons update skills and Standing Orders. The founder receives capital choices, intent changes and authority exceptions.

**Conflicts and resolutions:**

1. **C2 central judgment versus C3 decentralisation.** The seat owns thesis and allocation; workers choose methods. Its authority expires, allows challenges and excludes acceptance.
2. **C1 auction versus C4 allocator versus C3 admission.** One ledger reserves obligations and recovery, then funds hypotheses. Auditions select approaches inside funded missions. Capacity cannot be spent twice.
3. **C4 kill dates versus C5 ongoing Series.** Stop failed hypotheses; fund remaining promises through continuation or wind-down missions.
4. **C2 fingerprint versus C4 improvement.** Preserve constraints; let outcomes overturn historical choices. Test continuity and prospective performance.
5. **C1 royalties versus C3 forgetting versus C5 reuse.** Use one provenance record. Test cited assets’ benefit; quarantine defects; retain obligations separately.
6. **Cross-family review versus provider availability.** Both families remain equal. Require independent evidence and permissions. During outages, continue verifiable authorised work; queue effects missing required checks.
7. **C5 dailies versus founder attention.** Surface decision-relevant artifacts; keep other reels on demand. Parallel output cannot demand parallel founder attention.

Test an autonomous week, unfamiliar research and a multi-resource change under duplicates, outages and uncertain effects. Compare outcomes, fulfilled obligations, damage, cost and founder minutes against a lead-and-workers baseline at matched resources. Sequence proofs without shrinking the destination.

## 4. What all five missed

None specifies these mechanisms. They extend adjacent ideas; they are not claims of industry novelty.

1. **Correlated-failure budgeting.** Map ventures to shared models, skills, gateways, suppliers and demand channels. Stress simultaneous failures and limit aggregate exposure. Separate venture caps cannot contain one defective skill deployed everywhere.
2. **Missing-evidence audits.** Independently sample abandoned checkouts, silent customers and unreported failed missions. Record collection coverage beside outcomes. An authoritative database can still describe a biased slice of reality.
3. **Complementary-investment funding.** Evaluate bundles whose parts have little standalone value: instrumentation, distribution and an offer, for example. Fund dependency milestones against the bundle’s outcome. Isolated contract and experiment rankings can reject every necessary prerequisite.
4. **Relationship repair.** After harm, identify affected customers, correct promises, assign remedy authority and verify restored service and customer acceptance. A rollback or refund receipt alone does not establish repaired trust. Rehearse disputes in the twin.
5. **Transferable operating-company packages.** Export authority, assets, obligations, data permissions and operating evidence into a handover rehearsal with a different operator. Measure undocumented founder dependence. Make creating, acquiring and transferring businesses a tested capability.

## 5. Evidence checks against Round 0

These compare supplied research, not independent replications. Unsupported guarantees differ from contradictions.

- **C1: market authority exceeds the evidence.** R0-D §1 reports losses for all six Prediction Arena trading models. This does not disprove internal markets; it leaves their allocation quality unproven. Require calibration before market-driven freezes.

- **C5: abstraction is not a privacy guarantee.** “Nothing leaks” through abstract craft notes exceeds R0-C §6, which identifies safe cross-venture abstraction as unresolved. Add disclosure tests and enforceable boundaries.
- **C1/C3: topology results have limited scope.** C1 correctly quotes R0-A §2’s 17.2× versus 4.4× error-amplification figures. They do not establish a universal clearing-house reliability multiplier. C3’s distributed planning still requires integrated verification.

- **All five: autonomy and improvement remain hypotheses.** R0-E §§2–3 supports bounded skill transfer and external feedback, not indefinite organisational improvement or simulated purchasing validity.
