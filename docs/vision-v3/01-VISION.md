# 01 — Vision: vibe startuping

*Chief architect, Round 5, 2026-09-30. Obeys `00-CANON.md`. Numbers are marked target, illustration, parameter or
measured (with source).*

## 1. What vibe startuping is

**Vibe startuping is one founder running an organisation of AI workers that conceives, builds, runs, grows and learns
across as many ventures as his judgment can steer, and competes with companies of thousands of people.** The founder
supplies direction, taste and the few decisions that are truly his. The organisation supplies everything else:
research, design, engineering, selling, support, operations, accounting, strategy, and the discipline to check its own
work.

The name borrows from "vibe coding", where a person states intent and a model writes the code. Vibe startuping keeps
the ease and drops the carelessness. The founder states intent at the altitude he chooses — a sentence, a circle on a
draft, a veto — and an organisation turns it into ventures. That organisation has separated powers, a memory that is
actually read, acceptance by an independent referee, and brakes it cannot remove.

It handles **any kind of work**:

| Kind | What the organisation does | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Startup | Finds a pain, tests demand, builds, sells, supports, iterates, raises or sells | A tax-savings product for freelancers, from probe to Series mode |
| Agency | Wins clients, delivers, bills, keeps promises | A conversion-optimisation agency run at A3 with the founder on the first ten sales calls |
| Business | Runs an acquired or built operating company | A bought niche SaaS with an agent workforce and forced-leave audits |
| Research | Poses questions, runs studies, publishes original findings | A study of churn causes across its own ventures' anonymised, pooled priors |
| Learning | Compresses a new field for the founder | A Field Compressor turns a new domain into a two-week curriculum built on what he already knows |
| Any idea | Buys a measure of success first, then works on it | "Could I run a physical-product brand?" → a Framing Contract → probes → decision |

Most of this work has **no playbook**. So the organisation is not built on playbooks. It reasons, researches, imagines,
challenges itself, plans, executes, documents, remembers and improves on open-ended work (`03-MISSION-ENGINE.md`), and
anything it learns returns as optional priors and tested policy, never as a compulsory procedure.

## 2. The bet underneath it

Three facts, one consequence.

1. **Labour is becoming nearly free, parallel and tireless.** A Claude Code or Codex session with the right memory,
   context, sandbox and skills does in minutes what took a specialist days. Both families are equal workers; neither is
   "the reviewer". (Founder direction 6.)
2. **Judgment, direction and taste are not becoming free.** Deciding what matters, telling good from plausible, and
   carrying the consequences remain scarce. With cheap labour, *verification* becomes the scarce input:
   who can confirm, cheaply and independently, that the work is right? (R1 synthesis; R3 expander §0.)
3. **Organisations were designed for expensive labour.** Hierarchies, departments and spans of control ration human
   hours and human attention. Keeping those shapes wastes most of what cheap labour makes possible (R0-B; R1 decision 7).

**Consequence:** the winning organisation is designed around its real constraints — founder judgment, verifier
capacity, trust, cash and reputation — and puts effort into **manufacturing more of them**, not only rationing them.
That is clause 3 of the organising principle (`00-CANON.md` §1): *separate the powers, compile them into one answer, and
grow the scarce inputs.*

## 3. Principles

Fifteen principles. Each names the mechanism that makes it true, because a principle with no mechanism is a wish.

## 4. "Beyond a billion-dollar company" — in numbers

**Every figure in this section is a TARGET or an ASSUMPTION, not an evidenced forecast and not a claim about any real
company.** The canonical table is `00-CANON.md` §7; this section explains it.

A single definition can be gamed, so "beyond a billion-dollar company" has **three** definitions, and v3 must meet all
three:

| Definition | What it measures | Year-5 target | Why it cannot stand alone |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Value** | Portfolio enterprise value | ≥ $1B (at an *assumed* 5–8× ARR on ~$200M ARR, to be re-sourced from real transactions before quoting) | Multiples move; value can be borrowed from hype |
| **Output** | Matched, accepted work, as FTE-equivalents | ≥ 3,000 FTE-equivalent per week | Output can be busywork |
| **Capability** | Things no billion-dollar company does | 15,000 demand probes a year, <24 h wish-to-ship for every customer, 30 original studies, 50% of model calls on its own model family | Novelty can be useless |

**How output is counted** (owned by `09b`): FTE-equivalent = accepted, matched human-equivalent hours ÷ 40 per week,
with rework debited and a baseline range. "Accepted" means accepted by Acceptance, not claimed by a worker. "Matched"
means compared with what a human team would have needed for the same deliverable.

**The trajectory (targets):**

```
                     Year 1        Year 3         Year 5
ARR                  $2M           $40M           $200M
FTE-equivalent/wk    60            800            3,000
Accepted outcomes/mo ~600          ~8,000         ~30,000
Flagships / micro    3 / 12        6 / 80         10 / 300
Probes per year      1,000         5,000          15,000
Deterministic Share  40%           75%            90%
Founder decision min ≤45/day       ≤40/day        ≤30/day
Founder sec/outcome  ~135          ~9             ~1.8
```

**The arithmetic that forces the design.** At Year 5, 30 founder minutes a day spread over 30,000 accepted outcomes a
month is about **1.8 seconds of founder time per outcome**. The founder therefore cannot touch 98% or more of outcomes.
Those outcomes must settle through Standing Orders, mandates and deterministic verifiers with no founder contact. That
is why the Verifier Foundry, the Probe Mandate, fleet governance of micro-ventures and the Decision Supply Bench are not
optional. They are what the numbers require. (Expander §3, adopted.)

**The compute line.** Illustrative all-in compute and tools cost falls from about $5 per matched hour (Year 1, S09's
illustration) to about $2 (Year 5). It falls as the Model Foundry and the Verifier Foundry absorb volume. Compute as a
share of revenue falls from about 31% to about 6%. From Year 2, a standing target keeps it at or below 18%, so that
revenue growth mechanically unlocks capacity.

**If the ambition misses**, the misses are diagnostic, not fatal:

| Symptom | Diagnosis | Response |
|---|---|---|
| Probes on target, revenue under half | Finds demand, cannot convert | Invest in Guild (human sellers) and Keystone assets (brand, distribution) |
| Probes under target | Mandate or brand infrastructure is the bottleneck | Widen the Probe Mandate; build shared probe infrastructure |
| FTE-equivalents on target, revenue off | Accepted work on the wrong things (busywork at portfolio scale) | Season Review with the founder; Sideways Reviews across ventures |
| Founder minutes rising with ventures | Policy is not compiling | Standing Order compiler audit; Decision Supply Bench |

## 6. The founder's week at the destination

Illustrative — a Year-3 week, 40 working hours, of which about 4.5 are decisions. The rest is his choice: sales calls,
customer time, learning, deep product work, rest.

| When | Surface (reach) | What he does | Minutes |
|---|---|---|---:|
| Mon 08:00 | Reel on phone | Decision window: 6 Decide packets cleared by the Exchange; circles 14 takes from the weekend's dailies | 12 |
| Mon 10:00 | Mission Control → Ventures | Board meeting for Flagship 1 with the Co-founder seat: two wagers settled (one lost by the Co-founder), a pivot packet with three options | 30 |
| Mon 17:00 | Reel | Decision window | 9 |
| Tue | Office Window (ambient) | Nothing lights up; he spends the day on the first ten sales calls of a new agency, with an agent preparing and following up each call | 0 |
| Wed 03:10 | — | A production incident in a micro-venture: SCRAM safe state reached automatically, Incident Lead recovers it, Acceptance verifies restart. Class Know, reach Reel: he reads it at breakfast | 2 |
| Wed 08:00 | Reel | Clears 4 packets. The overnight incident never rang him: it was recovered and verified inside its SCRAM envelope with no customer harm, so its class was Know; a stop that was unconfirmed or harmful would have been a Halt and rung | 7 |
| Thu | Terminal | He forges a new hybrid ("Procurement Bid Tactician × Grant Economist") and sends it to the Audition Ladder | 15 |
| Fri 17:00 | Mission Control → Map | Fleet review: 80 micro-ventures, three lit off-band; he signs one Standing Order the Co-founder compiled from his last twelve identical answers | 20 |
| Sun | Wrist | Two two-way approvals under $50, each with a 1-hour held-dispatch undo | 1 |

Everything else — roughly 8,000 accepted outcomes that month — settled through policy, mandates and verifiers. He can
open any of them from the Traces page and ask *why*.

## 7. What vibe startuping is not

- **Not a tool the founder operates.** The system can act *as* a founder of a venture (principal mode Proxy, at A4) or as
  part of the company (Staff, Partner). It never acts as the founder's *person*: never his name, voice, likeness or
  signature (never-list line 2).
- **Not a swarm without a spine.** Swarms are one mission shape among four, chosen per mission; authority never comes
  from agents agreeing with each other.
- **Not a playbook library.** Learned recipes are optional advice, typed so they cannot block.
- **Not an autopilot for the founder's life.** Ventures that he drives stay at A0–A1; autonomy is his per-venture switch.
- **Not an MVP of itself.** The build is sequenced in `14-BUILD-PLAN.md`; the destination is not shrunk.
- **Not reputationally anonymous.** Every outward agent is a disclosed brand agent at first contact; accountability for
  every effect names a legal actor of record.

## 8. Ideas the founder did not ask for

1. **Three definitions of "billion-dollar"** (value, output, capability) so that none can be gamed alone.
2. **Seconds per outcome** as the founder's headline metric: it must fall every quarter, or the organisation is not
   compounding his judgment.
3. **A Season Review** each quarter: the founder re-decides a blind sample of last season's decisions without seeing what
   he chose, which measures whether his own judgment is drifting — a calibration check on the founder, not only the agents.
4. **Wager-settled disagreement** with the Co-founder: the Co-founder's win rate against the founder is public to him,
   and it should sit near but below 50% on contested calls. Far below means it only flatters; far above means he should
   delegate more.
5. **"Out-build thousands" as a scoreboard**, not a slogan: FTE-equivalents by task family, with rework debits, on the
   Map beside revenue.
6. **Original knowledge as a product line** (Frontier Program): the portfolio's pooled, privacy-safe priors are a dataset
   no single company has, and studies from it build reputation and distribution.
7. **Ventures as each other's first customers** at list price, disclosed and capped, never counted as product-market fit.
8. **Exit-ready at all times**: every venture maintains an operating-company package, so selling, spinning out or closing
   is a mission, not a scramble.
