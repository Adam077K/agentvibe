# R5 — Scenario walk (Codex)

**Verdict: all 15 scenarios have plausible operating paths, but the package does not yet define one consistent, executable path through them.** The main breaks concern authority activation, effect classification, acceptance before merge, protected reserves, founder reach, and export of sealed information. Several scenarios also assume mechanisms that their writers correctly marked as missing.

This is a design-consistency review, not verification of the illustrative outcomes or external legal claims. I traced intake → admission → execution → acceptance → effects → settlement → memory → founder contact in every scenario. No files were changed.

References use the canon’s file numbers and section names: [00 — Canon][00], [03 — Missions][03], [04 — Agents][04], [05 — Autonomy][05], [06 — Memory][06], [07 — Capabilities][07], [08 — Surfaces][08], [09a — Engineering][09a], [09b — Economics and acceptance][09b], [13 — Scenarios][13], [16 — External world][16], [17 — Practice][17].

Severity: **high** permits an unauthorised action, invalid acceptance, loss of an obligation, or prohibited disclosure; **med** leaves the execution path or evidence materially ambiguous; **low** is arithmetic, terminology, or reference drift. Proposed fixes identify the mechanism owner; 13 must then update its examples.

## 2. Cross-file contradictions found along the way

These are owner-level conflicts. Fixing the scenario text alone would leave future writers with the same incompatible rules.

### C1. The same effect receives different classifications and dispositions

**05 §3.3** says an A3 costly-reversible action is `notify`; **16 §4** says R3 inside a covering mandate is `auto` at A3+. At A2, 05 conditions the move from ask to notify on trust; 16 omits that condition.

There is also an effect-class mismatch: 05 describes R0–R1 as two-way internal and R2 as two-way external, whereas 16 assigns R1/R2 by blast below/above 40. A low-blast external action can therefore occupy different classes.

This affects S2 spending, S3 deployment, S5 publication, S8 concessions, S10 delivery and S12 recruitment. **16 must own consequence classification; 05 must own level/grant disposition; 09a must combine them once.** Delete competing decision tables or generate them from the same rule source.

The hard outgoing-money floor also needs reconciliation with routine paid probes and task procurement. A broad mandate may pre-authorise a class, but cannot silently turn a one-way effect into a two-way effect.

### C2. P2 terminates before P3’s promised continuity path

Canon ranks safety narrowing above obligations while giving the example of a refund continuing under a freeze. **02 §5.2** repeats that expectation. **09a §5** says a denying P2 rule terminates evaluation.

These can coexist only if the safe state already contains a bounded continuity exception or separate permitted route. That scope is not specified sufficiently to execute S4’s corrections under SCRAM.

**00 owns the precedence clarification; 09a owns the compiler implementation.** Do not solve this by generally putting obligations above safety.

### C3. Venture pivots are founder-only and autonomous simultaneously

**05 matrix row 15** gives the founder the decision to kill, pivot or persist a venture. Its state diagram defines pivot as new intent and a new Charter. Canon reserves Charter edits to the founder.

**17 §11** permits A3 pivot with a 24-hour veto and A4 pivot automatically. Those paths can change venture intent without the required constitutional signature. S7 uses the conservative founder path, but the mechanism it cites permits a broader action.

**05 owns the rights; 17 must distinguish a strategy adjustment inside existing intent from a venture pivot requiring a new Charter.** Silence cannot activate the latter.

### C4. Reach rules do not produce a single answer

The conflict is larger than G2/G-B4:

- **08 §2.1** excludes Know from Buzz and permits Know at Tap only by override.
- **08 §2.2** gives every CCIR match a Buzz floor.
- Its prose lets floors beat ceilings only for Halt and one-way doors.
- Its focus ceiling explicitly exempts floors.
- Its diagram says ceilings never go below floors.
- Its property test requires **every** combination to return reach at least its floor.
- **05 §10.1** describes focus as “Halt only between windows,” conflicting with S5’s focus-time Know/CCIR Buzz.
- **16 §4** makes an ask Ring whenever its deadline is under one hour; 08 requires the Decide ring-price condition.
- **09b §6** independently specifies Hold-to-Buzz escalation.

S4’s quiet-hours Reel is a plausible interpretation, but would fail 08’s written property test. S5’s Buzz is supported by one rule and excluded by another.

**08 must own one ordered routing table**, with explicit CCIR, quiet-hours, focus, travel, one-way and obligation interactions. 05, 16 and 09b should provide inputs, not independently choose reach.

### C5. Automatic narrowing versus founder-only constitutional writes

Canon says only a fresh founder passkey writes the Constitution. **05 §2.1** allows automatic Regulation narrowing, including adding a CCIR line and shortening validity, while those records live in the Constitution.

Automatic stopping is clearly authorised. Automatic rewriting of signed constitutional source is not clearly distinguished from it.

**05 must separate an immediately effective narrowing overlay from amendments to founder-signed records.** This matters to initial CCIR defaults, demotion, imported Charters and incident policy changes.

### C6. “No method may gate” conflicts with named-method guards

Canon P8 and **01’s principles** prohibit method-dependent admission. **03 G2** blocks a named Research move based on sequence; **G4** mandates a particular mixed-family vote and red-team procedure.

Calling these “consequence” rules does not remove their method predicates. S12’s novel mission therefore remains bound to procedures while claiming to lose every recipe.

**03 owns the repair:** express the invariant being protected and accept equivalent evidence-producing methods, or explicitly request a canon exception. A type label alone cannot make the rule consistent.

### C7. Shared record schemas are not actually shared

**06** uses `data_class`, `taint`, `authority` and `exportable`. **09a** uses `dclass: D0–D4`, `tainted`, `permission`, and `permission: non_exportable`. No complete mapping is provided; 06’s `confidential` and `sealed` categories do not map mechanically to 09a’s personal/client/secrets classes.

S6 adds an unsupported human origin; S12 adds a duration where 06 specifies a retention-class enum. Those are symptoms of the same missing contract.

**06 owns semantics; 09a owns one versioned wire schema and mapping.** Classification, boundary, retention class, retention deadline and authorising permission must remain distinct.

### C8. “All model routes” can silently undermine mandatory cross-family review

**09a §10** says single-family mode routes every role to one family and flags every verdict. **09b §6** forbids substituting a same-family judge when a required edge is unavailable. S10 correctly follows the stricter rule.

**09a must state that flagged same-family outputs are provisional and cannot satisfy missing coverage.** A provider-exit drill can measure degraded production without manufacturing accepted settlement.

### C9. HumanTask’s type catalogue excludes actual scenario work

**16 §13** explains human procurement broadly but its `kind` enumeration contains signatures, notarisation, physical work, calls, licensed review, taste panels, KYC and local presence. S7’s fallback human grant writer fits none cleanly. S12’s participants are also not represented.

**16 owns a bounded general contribution/task type and the participant protocol extension.** Do not misclassify writing as licensed review merely to pass schema validation.

### C10. Numbers and counts need explicit parameter provenance

Some differences are harmless only if identified as overrides:

- S9 uses a 15% surprise reserve; 03’s initial parameter is 10%.
- 03 names six investment sleeves but its table also contains Core: six named sleeves **plus** a residual Core allocation.
- 17’s illustrative $18k probe month exceeds its displayed $6k monthly mandate unless a different signed envelope is stated.
- S15’s 12% model-release floor agrees with 04’s ND-04-1 and the ≤15% cap, but its actual dollar spend does not demonstrate compliance without the investment-capacity denominator.

**09b owns denominators and accounting; each mechanism owner owns its tunable parameter.** Illustrations may choose values, but must expose the authority and denominator that make those values admissible.

| **G-B3 — Human-subject research** | Confirmed and broader than adding an origin enum. | **16** | Define participant principal, protocol, accountable reviewer, permitted deception/debrief, consent, pay, withdrawal, approved sample and amendment handling. **06** defines participant labels and release scope; **17** adds protocol-approved vitals. Reconcile “zero deception beyond the original” with 16’s no-deception procurement rule. |
| **G-B4 — Know at Buzz/Tap** | Confirmed across 04, 06, 08 and 17. | **08 §2** | Define whether CCIR and explicit founder overrides can raise Know to Tap/Buzz. Then remove hard-coded reach from other owners. “SCRAM tripped” should be a signed, scoped CCIR choice, not an automatic conversion of every trip into Halt. |

Sweep is new. | **05 §10**, with **08** rendering | Define the device-bound signed gesture and the event that resets Clock 2, separately from positive authorisation. Rotation, historical exposure, scrub and adoption eligibility need separate states. |
| **G-B7 — Async board under travel** | Confirmed scheduling gap. | **05 §8.2–§8.3** | Define asynchronous delivery, minutes accounting, packet deadlines, carry-forward and default expiry. **08** still applies independent reach floors: “async” must not turn every one-way Decide into a Reel-only item or hide an approaching obligation deadline. |

**Ownership consequence:** 13 owns none of these mechanisms. Its gap tables are useful issue discovery, but their recommendations do not become operative rules merely because the scenarios use them.

## 4. What held up well

The following traces survived at the architectural level, subject to the specific breaks above. “Held” means the owner defines the mechanism; it does not mean the illustrated business result was measured.

| Scenario | Trace walked | What held |
|---|---|---|
| **S1 — Agency from zero** | Genesis → blind readers → approved work → audition → Offer/contract → payment → escrow → learning | Founder handles personal introductions and first sales calls; customer money is independently observed and protected for delivery; one audition item is not treated as promotion. |
| **S2 — 48-hour validation** | Pain Index → framing → template arms → real contact → count oracle → graduation → fleet proposal | A count-based graduation criterion is distinguished from an underpowered positioning claim. Refundable pre-orders stay E3 and do not become earned revenue. |
| **S3 — Overnight feature** | Launch Sheet → reserved coverage → isolated workers → collision/rework → security failure → canary → held email → wrap | Tool leases forbid hidden reviewer spawning; mixed authorship gets component and end-to-end coverage; delayed email dispatch provides real undo. |
| **S4 — Production incident** | Independent read-back → partial SCRAM → incident grant → funded correction → target check → restart → residual duties/AAR | The founder need not operate the incident. Scope, expiry and restart evidence are explicit; a target mismatch stops effects before patient information is sent. |
| **S5 — Competitor launch** | Sensor → CCIR → initiative admission → forecasts/challenge → alternatives → cells/option → settlement/wager | Competitor activity does not itself become a command to match price. Rejected initiatives retain reasons and revisit conditions; dissent remains visible. |
| **S6 — New field** | Genesis → dual oracles → capability discovery → research fan-in → human correction → invoices/exam → priors | Fan-in counts independent sources rather than agreeing agents. Human corrections become contested facts; skill admission is evaluated separately by family. |
| **S7 — Pivot** | Missed vitals/debt → court → freeze new sales → competing cases → signed new intent → old deliveries → memory carry-over | The shown A2 decision stays with the founder. Old obligations remain funded, old goal versions remain available, and losing wagers are not erased by pivot. |
| **S8 — Founder absent** | Declared state → sweep/board → forced leave → heartbeat → incident → churn/contract/price → report → re-entry | Forced leave has a concrete hidden-dependence test. Unapproved legal terms remain pending; the re-entry brief separates completed work from decisions still owed. |
| **S9 — Collision** | Funding/shape → footprints → optimistic leases → first landing → rework → expiry → stale push → reacquisition → fan-in | Storage checks fences independently of the coordinator. The design honestly treats leases as ordering/staleness controls, not a substitute for semantic integration. |
| **S10 — Exhausted budget** | Capacity pressure → Conserve → Essential → unavailable route → Acceptance waiting → bounded packet → delivery | Same-family sign-off and impermissible subscription borrowing are refused. A spend-cap error is distinguished from a transient rate limit; work is checkpointed. |
| **S11 — Founder disagreement** | Goal amendment → own/founder views → Shadow correction → overrule → wager → execution → independent settlement | The Mind can disagree without owning settlement. Abandoned outcomes are listed; calibration may change presentation/default routing but does not grant authority. |
| **S12 — Novel research** | Framing → research/design/challenge → locked Bet → ethics → pilot/main → independent analysis → null → publication | Novelty does not require inventing a recipe. Objections have dispositions, analysis is independently recomputed, and a negative result remains publishable in principle. |
| **S13 — Counterparty injection** | Signed arrival → quarantine → typed fields → Offer-bound counter → escalation → payment → account/incident records | Signature establishes identity, not entitlement. Claimed founder approval cannot authorise a discount or refund destination; checkout binds to a specific Offer version. |
| **S14 — Fleet import** | Census → deduplication → excavation → obligation registration → sort → hygiene/adoption → baseline → reuse/archive | Obligations precede commercial classification. PR-only adoption, provenance-bearing reuse and measured baselines give import a concrete conservative path. |
| **S15 — Model release** | Registry admission → pin both → judge/capability tests → re-audition → fingerprint → option/replays → report | Unknown price blocks admission; the old default remains pinned; family name does not transfer calibration. A better disagreement becomes a finding rather than an automatic authority change. |

Four design choices are especially strong across the walks:

- **Independent observation:** Custody’s receipts are assertions; Acceptance reads external systems separately. This consistently gives payments, deliveries and wagers a defensible settlement path.
- **Durable obligations:** Duties survive pause, pivot, kill and founder absence. Latest safe times and funded fallbacks are stronger than calendar-only continuity.
- **Explicit uncertainty:** Underpowered results, unknown provider capacity, pending effects and unqualified judges have named states instead of being treated as success.
- **Evidence limits are usually acknowledged:** S9 explicitly distinguishes canned SP2 results from unrun live arms; S15 labels its model hypothetical. The report should retain that discipline while fixing the places where illustrations bypass the actual rules.
