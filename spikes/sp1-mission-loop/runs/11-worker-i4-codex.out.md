## q3 — Price point & offer structure that closes inside 30 days

I narrowed to the two strongest niches and hunted for *transacted* or *posted* prices rather than SaaS list prices. Findings below; the offer is in the artifact.

**What the evidence actually supports**

| Anchor | Niche 1 — freight carrier vetting | Niche 2 — PI intake/lead response |
|---|---|---|
| Software floor (have) | $99/mo (Carrier411); $2–10/carrier/mo + $50–500/seat/mo | $99–499/mo platform + $0.10–0.30/min AI-first |
| Human-labour substitute | Freight-broker VA $1,299/mo part-time, $1,999 full-time | Human intake $1.50–4.00/min or $300–2,500/mo retainer |
| Value/outcome anchor | Liability exposure (Montgomery) — no public $ | **$284 avg cost per lead, $468 per signed case** |
| Nearest consulting comparable | A&A "Freight Broker Carrier Vetting & Compliance Audit" — custom T&M, no published SMB price | Per-qualified-lead $25–150; per-signed-case $500–2,500 |
| Commodity build price | — | Upwork AI-receptionist gigs from $35–39; live posted job at **$500 fixed** |

Two things matter most. First, **PI is the only one of the two with a public per-unit value number** ($284/lead), which lets you price off recovered revenue instead of arguing about your hourly rate. Second, **the build is a commodity** — a real Upwork posting for an AI voice receptionist sits at $500 fixed price, so selling "I'll build you an agent" caps you at four figures once and invites price shopping. The money is in the ongoing outcome.

On the 30-day constraint: the one deal-size/cycle benchmark I could bind to a source says sub-$1,000-ACV deals average 25 days. That argues against an annual contract as the first commitment and in favour of a small, single-signature first step. Encouragingly, freight compliance consulting already transacts fast — A&A sends its agreement within 24 hours of mutual interest and can start within two weeks of signature — so the space itself is not procurement-bound.

**Recommendation:** lead with PI intake. Paid diagnostic → 21-day pilot → month-to-month retainer at **$1,000/mo**, with the pilot priced at $500 and credited. Full offer in the artifact.

**Honest caveat on source quality:** most price *ranges* here come from vendor/aggregator buyer's-guide pages, not audited transaction records. The two hardest datapoints are the live $500 Upwork posting and Wishup's published VA rate card. I did not find an invoice or named-client case study of a consultant billing a specific small brokerage or small PI firm.

## Artifact — First Offer

### Primary: "First-Call Guarantee" for small PI firms

**The claim:** *You will never lose another case to a slow phone or an unanswered web form. If you do, you don't pay that month.*

**Structure — three steps, each signable by the owner alone:**

1. **Free diagnostic (day 0–2).** Submit their own web form and call their own line after hours. Deliver a one-page timestamped record: time-to-first-response, calls unanswered, forms never replied to. This is evidence they can verify themselves, not a pitch deck. (27% of firms never respond at all — the diagnostic converts itself.)
2. **21-day paid pilot — $500 fixed, credited to month 1.** Live 24/7 capture on one number and one form, <5-minute callback SLA, qualified-lead summary into their inbox or case management. Fixed fee, single invoice, card payment, no contract beyond the pilot.
3. **Month-to-month retainer — $1,000/mo**, cancel anytime, plus **$75 per qualified lead** above 15/mo against a written qualification rubric the firm controls.

**Why these numbers:**
- $1,000/mo sits inside the $300–$2,500 human-intake retainer band while replacing a service that also charges $1.50–$4.00/min, so it reads as a substitution, not a new line item.
- At $284/lead, the retainer pays for itself on **3.5 recovered leads/month**; at $468/signed case it pays for itself on ~2 signings. That's the whole ROI argument, and it's one sentence.
- Hybrid (low fixed retainer + per-qualified-lead) is the structure the buyer-side guidance itself recommends as cleanest, which means you're proposing what an informed buyer would ask for.
- $500 pilot keeps the first commitment under the sub-$1,000 fast-close threshold and under any plausible signature limit for a firm owner.

**What NOT to sell:** the build. A fixed-price "build me an AI receptionist" job is publicly transacting at $500 and gigs start at $35. Never quote a build fee.

**30-day path:** days 1–10 run diagnostics on 30 firms from the FJA directory → days 8–20 send each firm its own timestamped failure record + pilot offer → days 15–30 close 1–2 pilots at $500. Target: first dollar by day 30, first retainer by day 45.

### Alternate: freight-broker carrier vetting (run second)

**Structure:** **$1,500 fixed Carrier Vetting Readiness Review** (14 days, deliverable = written gap assessment against the post-*Montgomery* standard of care + remediation checklist), then **$850/mo continuous monitoring retainer** — re-check every active carrier's authority/insurance/fraud signals weekly, alert on lapse, monthly evidence file for the firm's insurer or defence counsel.

**Why:** $850/mo undercuts the $1,299/mo part-time VA that is doing this work manually today, while sitting well above the $99/mo software floor because the deliverable is a defensible evidence file, not a dashboard. The $1,500 review mirrors A&A's audit product at a price a 70%-family-owned TIA membership can approve without a scoping cycle — A&A quotes custom time-and-materials, which is exactly the gap a fixed-fee solo offer fills.

**Why second:** it's a liability sale with no public per-unit value number, so ROI must be argued rather than calculated — slower to a first "yes" than the PI version.
