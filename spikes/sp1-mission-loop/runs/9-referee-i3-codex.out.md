## Referee verdicts

**Claim 0 — DialIQ $49/mo, veterinary — SUPPORTED.** The page exists and search results show dialiq.ai carrying that exact framing ("$49/month for unlimited simultaneous calls, 24/7"). Caveat worth flagging: "unlimited" refers to *concurrency*, not volume — the $49 Essential tier includes 50 calls/mo with $1.50 overage. The claim's phrasing ("unlimited simultaneous 24/7 calls") mirrors the vendor's marketing, so it passes, but a reader could take it as unlimited volume.

**Claim 1 — Smith.ai $150/mo for 75 calls, month-to-month — SUPPORTED.** smith.ai/pricing/ai-receptionist carries the Pro tier at $150/75 calls, $2.00/call in-plan, month-to-month, no contract. The load-bearing parts check out.

**Claim 2 — Legal answering $199–$360+/mo — SUPPORTED, with a stretched upper bound.** The quote is on the cited getnextphone page ($199/mo flat, unlimited inbound). The `$360+` top of the range is not in the quote; the page's adjacent content (traditional per-minute services at $200–$400 base plans, Smith.ai at $255–$900) puts it in the right neighbourhood, so the claim isn't inflated — but the quote alone only establishes the floor. Also note the source is a vendor blog ranking itself first.

**Claim 3 — COI three-tier ladder — UNSUPPORTED (misattributed).** The cited Vertikal RMS 2026 page does **not** carry that ladder. It prices per vendor per year: $3–$10 self-service, $10–$30 full-service, CertFocus at $13–$19/vendor/yr. The quoted "$29–$50 / $80–$300 / $7,500–$10,000+" breakdown appears on **coverwarden.com**, a different vendor. The numbers exist on the web; the citation points at a page that says something materially different.

**Claim 4 — Dental verification $6.50–$8.25 — UNSUPPORTED (misattributed).** The quote is verbatim from **dentalclaimsupport.com**, not the cited dentalbilling.com pricing page. The cited page publishes different rates: $4.25 standard / $6.75 extensive / $7.25 specialty / $8.25 premium, plus monthly plans at $250 / $650 / $825. Ironically the cited page *does* support the claim's second half (several-hundred-dollar monthly retainer) — but not via the quote given.

**Claim 5 — Carrier411 $99/mo — SUPPORTED.** carrier411.com carries $99.00/month flat to monitor all carriers, no contracts, 30-day free trial. The quote is a paraphrase rather than verbatim, and "one of the most affordable dedicated carrier vetting platforms" is the worker's editorial, not the site's — but the price fact stands.

**Claim 6 — Agency setup fee + retainer — UNSUPPORTED (numbers wrong).** The structural point is on the taskip page, but the quoted figures are not. The page says one-time setup fees of **$3,000–$10,000** (not $500–$3,000) and small-business retainers of $500–$1,500 (not $500–$2,000). The claim also asserts "not a pure project fee," while the cited page's whole premise is six co-existing models *including* project-based at $1,500–$20,000+.

**Claim 7 — SMB decides within a week, owner as sole decision-maker — UNSUPPORTED (misattributed, and the cited page cuts the other way).** The quote is from **pipeline.zoominfo.com**'s SMB sales strategy piece. The cited FitSmallBusiness page's actual guidance is that SMBs should form a *multi-departmental tech purchasing committee including IT* — close to the opposite of "no formal procurement, sole decision-maker." The "2–100 employees" band and "no formal procurement process" are additions not in the quote at all.

**Claim 8 — Inference — UNSUPPORTED.** It leans on two things that didn't survive: the same-week decision speed (claim 7, misattributed) and an anchor stated as "$97–$300/mo" where no verified source produces $97 — the supported anchors are $49, $99, $150, $199. The narrower sub-point (no-contract monthly is the norm at two of the vendors) does follow from claims 1 and 5, but the inference as written is broader than its base.

**Summary overreach:** yes. Three problems. (1) "All sold month-to-month with no annual contract" is verified only for Smith.ai and Carrier411; the COI full-service tier the worker itself cites is quoted *per year*, and dental verification is sold per-verification or in monthly plans with no contract terms established. (2) "SMBs… decide fast, usually within a week, with the owner as sole decision-maker" rests entirely on the misattributed claim 7 — and the page actually cited recommends a purchasing committee. (3) The "$97–$497/mo" recommendation's endpoints aren't anchored in any verified source. Four of eight facts clean, one clean-with-a-caveat, three misattributed to pages that don't carry the quote — the summary is written with the confidence of eight.
