## Referee report — q1

I checked all 10 `fact` claims against their cited URLs (and looked for independent corroboration where the source was a vendor). Nine sources exist and carry the claimed content; one number could not be found on its page.

**Sound work.** Most claims are honestly scoped — several are deliberately unquantified ("significant time", "a large share"), which means the vendor-marketing sources behind them can't inflate anything. The freight-broker claim is the strongest in the set: *Montgomery v. Caribe Transport II, LLC* is real, decided by the US Supreme Court on 14 May 2026 (unanimous, Barrett), holding that state-law negligent-hiring claims against brokers fall within the FAAAA safety exception — corroborated well beyond the cited Averitt page (Cornell, SCOTUSblog, Crowell, Benesch, Gordon Rees). The Hennessey 1,400-firm / 27% figure and the CALSSA CPUC complaint both check out against independent sources.

**The one bad number.** Claim 1's quote ("Nearly 50% of denied claims in small practices are never followed up on and age quietly into write-offs") does not appear on the Medical Economics page. Three searches, including a domain-restricted one, surfaced that article's actual figures — 15% initial denial rate, ~$25–30 to rework a claim, "many denied claims are never resubmitted" — plus a *different* industry figure elsewhere ("up to 65 percent … never resubmitted"). The directional pain is real; the specific "nearly half, at small practices" is not attributable to that source.

**Provenance caveat the summary doesn't carry.** Seven of the ten sources are vendors or agencies selling the exact solution being scoped (TrustLayer, Henry Schein One, PupPilot, Aqore, The Coyle Group, Averitt, HOAmanagement). Their numbers are marketing copy with no disclosed methodology. That's acceptable as a pain-point signal but is weaker than "documented cost evidence" implies — notably the vet $100K figure, where the site conditions it on "clinics with inefficient phone systems" while the claim generalises to "the average veterinary clinic."

**Quotes are paraphrases, not quotes.** Claims 0, 6, 7 and (partly) 1 and 4 present stitched or reworded text inside `quote` fields. Claim 7's page says "that silence is your answer," not "that silence is a common complaint." Claim 6's "quote" is a synthesis of facts drawn from several outlets, not a CalMatters sentence. The substance survives in each case, but a `quote` field should be verbatim or it can't be audited.

**Inference 10 doesn't follow.** "Owner-level buyers close faster than committees" is a reasonable sales prior, but not one of the ten facts touches buyer structure, decision-maker count, or sales-cycle length. It's imported, not derived — and the summary elevates it to "the main signal for a sub-30-day close."

**Inference 11 mostly holds**, since three of the sources *are* the incumbents, so "not total whitespace" follows directly. The narrower part — that the gap is specifically the small end incumbents under-target — has no support, and I found mild counter-evidence: TrustLayer publishes a "Starter" free tier explicitly for small teams.

**Unverified numbers smuggled into the artifact.** The artifact carries roughly eight quantified figures with no corresponding claim or source_url: the Wolters Kluwer ~2,000-firm survey, ">90% of solar applications have errors", "$475 truck rolls", "21x qualification odds", "$7.11 vs $1.48/verification", "~23 hrs screening per hire", "72% cite high-volume applications", "$120k–$300k/yr per provider". These bypassed verification entirely. If the artifact is downstream input, those need claims of their own.

Three specific overstatements: "acute, **quantified** pain points" (4 of 11 niches have no number — COI tracking, HOA, commercial-insurance renewal, and the medical figure that failed verification); "**Each** has … an owner-level (not committee) buyer" (the artifact's own entry #3 concedes the HOA budget holder can be the board); and "which is the main signal for a sub-30-day close" (asserted, evidenced nowhere). The closing caveat about incumbents is honest and appropriately scoped — credit where due.
