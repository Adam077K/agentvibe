## Referee report — q4

**Verified independently via search. Six facts, one inference.**

**Claim 0 — Ruby $12M class action — supported (weak citation).** The cited classaction.org page exists and its headline is the quote verbatim. But that article covers the November 2018 *filing*, not the settlement, so the quote carries neither the $12M nor the 18,000 figure. I corroborated both elsewhere: settlement approved July 2021 (D. Or., *McKenzie Law Firm, P.A. v. Ruby Receptionists*), ~$12M total = **$8M in customer credits plus up to $4M attorney fees**, class certified at ~18,000. Worth noting: the customer-facing portion is credits, not cash, so "$12 million" is the gross figure. Hold-time billing, 30-second round-ups and hangup minimums all appear in the complaint.

**Claim 1 — G2 value-for-money / "churn and burn" — unsupported.** The "quote" is a synthesized analytical sentence, not text on the G2 page. Both elements actually trace to **Capterra**, not G2: Capterra breaks out Value for Money (3.8/5, Ruby's lowest sub-score) and a Capterra reviewer is the one who calls the operation "churn and burn." G2 shows only an overall 3.7/5 from ~11 reviews and no such sub-score breakout. The claim's "every platform that measures it separately" is also an overgeneralisation — one platform was found doing so. Substance is roughly right; the source is wrong.

**Claim 2 — 2+ year Smith.ai churner — unverifiable.** `cloudtalk.io/blog/smith-ai-alternative/` exists, but after three searches (including domain-restricted) I could not surface that sentence or a near-equivalent on it. Other Smith.ai complaints are findable (calls going to voicemail, billing-on-cancel, a BBB complaint on billable-call redefinition) — but not this one, as quoted.

**Claim 3 — LEX Reception complaints — supported.** The page exists and carries near-verbatim content: per-minute billing creating unpredictable overages ("the clock keeps running" on emotional/verbose callers), and rigid scripts producing "long, frustrating calls." **Caveat the worker did not flag: this is Smith.ai's own competitive sales page.** "Firms that have switched away" is a vendor's assertion about a rival, not independently documented customer testimony.

**Claim 4 — Answering Legal Trustpilot — supported.** 1-star review titled "Trial was a disaster" on the cited page: scripts not properly drafted, agents robotic and unable to think outside the script, trial cancelled before it ended.

**Claim 5 — guarantee structures — supported.** Smith.ai: 30 days, full refund of plan and add-ons regardless of calls/chats used, capped at $1,000 (on smith.ai/pricing/receptionists and docs.smith.ai). Ruby: 21-day money-back guarantee. Two caveats the claim omits — Smith.ai excludes overage, and Ruby's window also ends at 500 receptionist minutes/50 chats, whichever comes first, first-time users only. The quote is a paraphrase, and the Ruby half isn't on the cited Smith.ai URL.

**Claim 6 — per-lead pricing is agency-side only — unsupported as an inference.** It doesn't follow from the supported facts; it *introduces* facts. Nothing in the claim set establishes FWD Lawyer Marketing's pricing model (I confirmed fwd-lawyermarketing.com is a PI law-firm marketing agency, and that agency-side pay-per-lead PI is real at ~$100–$600/lead, but not FWD's own model), and no claim documents Answering Legal's pricing basis at all. This is an unsourced fact claim wearing an inference label.

**Summary overreach — yes, three places.** (1) "Worst-in-class value-for-money scores" inflates *Ruby's own lowest sub-score* (3.8/5 on Capterra) into a cross-vendor ranking never measured. (2) "None of the four incumbents offers per-qualified-lead or outcome-based guarantee pricing — all bill per-minute/per-call" is a universal negative resting on no exhaustive survey; Answering Legal's model is undocumented in the claim set (it is per-minute per my own check, which rescues the substance but not the evidence). (3) The LEX and Smith.ai legs are weaker than presented — LEX sourced entirely from a competitor's sales page, and the Smith.ai churn anecdote unverifiable. Ruby and Answering Legal are the only legs standing on independent evidence. The directional conclusion (niche not saturated-and-satisfied) survives; the "four incumbents, all dissatisfied, wedge provably open" framing does not.
