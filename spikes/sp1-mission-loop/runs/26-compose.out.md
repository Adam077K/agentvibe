# Solo AI Agency: First Client in 30 Days — Personal Injury Lead-Response Recovery

## 1. The Niche and Why It's Underserved

The niche is **after-hours and web-form lead-response recovery for small personal injury (PI) law firms** — not building AI receptionists (a commodity, see below) but selling a *guaranteed-response* retainer to solo and small-firm PI attorneys who are currently losing signed cases to slow or missed intake.

This is underserved in a specific, narrow way. It is not a greenfield market — Smith.ai, Answering Legal, LEX Reception, and Ruby Receptionist are all established vendors selling into this space. The opportunity for a solo operator is the segment those incumbents serve poorly: small/solo PI practices where the *service quality* of the incumbent — not its existence — is the failure point. Three independent, named-practitioner complaints describe the incumbents' current offerings failing on exactly the dimensions a leaner, guarantee-backed offer can fix: unpredictable per-minute billing (<https://smith.ai/blog/lex-reception-alternative-law-firms>), robotic/poorly-scripted call handling that caused a firm to cancel mid-trial (<https://www.trustpilot.com/review/answeringlegal.com>), and a $12M class-action settlement over deceptive billing practices at one major vendor (<https://www.classaction.org/news/ruby-receptionists-sued-over-allegedly-deceptive-billing-practices>). This is a market with real, paying demand and real incumbent dissatisfaction — the wedge is reliability and pricing transparency at the small-firm end, not technology novelty.

## 2. Evidence

**Buyer pain.** 27% of law firms surveyed (n=1,400) never respond to online leads at all (<https://hennessey.com/press/hennessey-digitals-lead-form-response-time-study-of-1400-law-firms-identifies-the-fastest-responding-personal-injury-firms/>). Two independent, non-vendor-hosted accounts confirm this pain specifically for PI practices: PI/criminal-law firm owner Richard Gutierrez describes being overwhelmed by client calls routing to his personal cell phone (<https://clutch.co/profile/answering-legal>), and PI attorney Ryan McKeen documents losing a two-case client because he returned a call 15 minutes late (<https://aconnecticutlawblog.com/ruby-receptionist-review/>). A third Clutch review describing the same failure mode could not be confirmed as PI-specific and is not counted (<https://clutch.co/profile/answering-legal>). Industry body PILMMA reports 67% of legal leads call outside business hours (<https://www.pilmma.org/blog/how-law-firms-lose-at-least-500k-annually-in-after-hours-leads/>), and West Coast Trial Lawyers' open after-hours intake role is a firm-authored signal the gap is real and unfilled even at larger firms (<https://www.ziprecruiter.com/Jobs/Legal-Intake-Specialist/-in-Los-Angeles,CA>).

**Reachability.** The Florida Justice Association's member directory is searchable by practice area and location, giving a filtered, enumerable list of PI firms, plus chapter-level lists for local targeting (<https://www.myfja.org/member-benefits/>). Directories yield firm names and phone/web contact but rarely named-decision-maker emails, so first touch is phone or web form — which conveniently doubles as the diagnostic itself.

**Willingness-to-pay / price anchors.** Human legal intake runs $1.50–$4.00/min or $300–$2,500/mo retainer (<https://www.layer3labs.io/guides/legal-intake-services>), with buyer-side guidance recommending a hybrid low-retainer-plus-per-qualified-lead structure at $25–$150/lead (<https://www.layer3labs.io/guides/legal-intake-services>). PI firms pay ~$284/lead and ~$468/signed case on average in 2026 (<https://rankings.io/blog/personal-injury-lead-costs/>), giving a hard ROI anchor. Legal-specialized answering services price $199–$360+/mo (<https://www.getnextphone.com/blog/best-answering-service-for-law-firms>). Deals under $1,000 ACV close in ~25 days on average versus an 84-day median across all B2B SaaS (<https://b2bsalestraining.org/average-sales-cycle-length>), which is why the first commitment must stay small.

**Competitive gap.** Beyond the billing/quality complaints above, a live Upwork posting shows AI-receptionist *builds* transact at $500 fixed (<https://www.upwork.com/freelance-jobs/apply/Voice-Receptionist-for-Service-Company_~022056519076716738893/>) — confirming implementation is commodity and cannot be the revenue basis; the retainer and guarantee are the product, not the build.

## 3. The First Offer

**"First-Call Guarantee"** — pitched as: *you will never lose another case to a slow phone or an unanswered form; if you do, you don't pay that month.*

- **Free diagnostic (days 0–2):** Submit the firm's own web form and call its own after-hours line; deliver a one-page timestamped record of response failures — self-verifying, not a pitch.
- **21-day paid pilot — $500 fixed, credited to month 1:** live 24/7 capture on one number + one form, sub-5-minute callback SLA, qualified-lead summaries delivered to inbox.
- **Retainer — $1,000/mo, cancel anytime, + $75/qualified lead above 15/mo**, against a rubric the firm controls.

At $284/lead, break-even is 3.5 recovered leads/month; the hybrid structure mirrors what buyer-side guidance itself recommends, so the pitch reads as informed rather than sales-y. The $500 pilot stays under the sub-$1,000 fast-close threshold.

**30-day path:** Days 1–10, run diagnostics on ~30 FJA-directory firms; days 8–20, send each its own failure record plus the pilot offer; days 15–30, close 1–2 pilots. Target: first dollar by day 30.
