# Principles

**UNANSWERED.** This file is a question, not a placeholder. Nothing has been decided.

## The question

What does this product look like, and — the half that is usually skipped — **what does that
forbid?**

## What this file will hold when it is answered

- The locked visual decisions, stated as rules a later step can be measured against.
- For each one, what it rules out. A principle that forbids nothing is a preference.
- The hierarchy mechanism set actually in use, chosen from: size · weight · colour/contrast · case ·
  letter-spacing · whitespace · position · surface · border · fill · shadow · optical alignment.

## Why it is empty rather than seeded with something plausible

A plausible default survives to launch. `docs/03-system-design/DESIGN-CAPABILITY.md` §7.2 records the
one diagnosis available today and it is not a decision: mission-control's hierarchy is **four greys —
pure down-pop on a single axis, with zero up-pop anywhere.** Erik Kennedy's rule is to emphasise on
one axis while restraining on another. Writing that observation in as a principle would turn a
finding into a decision nobody took.

## What has to happen first

The three irreducible human seed judgements (§7.5) — base colour, the increment, brand adjectives →
typeface. Two of the three are open. `design/system/type.md` decides the increment and explicitly
does not claim the other two.
